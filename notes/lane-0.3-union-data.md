## Rules and judgements, with what each rejected

0. **The start is 5 March 1861** (lead ruling after the first review request): Lincoln's cabinet is confirmed that day and 0042 starts the situations on it. Everything below is as of 5 March.
1. **State governments on 5 March (a modelling rule; accepted by the lead).** The seven states the research notes call "the seven states" (South Carolina, Mississippi, Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana, Texas) are Confederate departments. The eight slave states that had not left the Union (Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee, Kentucky, Missouri, Arkansas) are departments with **no faction** (F6, 0050): their side is a situation's work. The other 19 are Union. *Rejected:* Union for the eight (it makes Letcher's Virginia a Union government on 5 March); a pack-wide "neutral" label for Kentucky alone (the same evidence covers all eight).
2. **Control (lead ruling: loyalty never stands in for control).** The format has no unknown for control, so the table writes: Confederate 1000 in the seven states; Union 1000 in free states, territories and the District; Union 1000 at Fort Sumter, Fort Pickens, Key West and Fort Monroe (the drafts' own notes and posts); and **no one's** (the state government's, which has no faction) in all eight uncommitted states and the Indian Territory nations, the reason in each row. Federal installations in those states stay with their Union posts (the St. Louis arsenal, Harpers Ferry), not with region control. *Rejected:* Union 1000 in the eight (an overclaim); the research note's loyalty shares as control (removed from the five states that used them); the note's two Virginia proposals (they split at the Blue Ridge, which the draft's regions do not mark).
   **Loyalty proposals kept for a loyalty field** (the research note secession-winter, "Border states: starting control and loyalty", all [I] proposals; union / confederate / neutral-contested, summing to 1000, as of 4 March): Maryland (rest) 350/250/400, Baltimore 150/400/450, Delaware 650/80/270, Kentucky 330/250/420, Missouri 450/200/350, Virginia east of the Blue Ridge 350/300/350, northwest Virginia 650/100/250. Each border-state row's note in `control.csv` carries its own numbers.
3. **People's faction at the start (decision 0055, ruling S1).** A man's side is the faction of the department of the post he holds on 5 March. With no post it is his home state's government: Confederate for a seceded state, **no faction for one of the eight**, Union for a free state; a man holding a US commission on 5 March starts Union (Lee and Stuart among them). The importer holds a table of the **44 Confederate-coded men with no start post** (`START_SIDE` in `import-people-oob.mjs`): 12 Union (US commissions: Lee, Stuart, Longstreet, Magruder, Pemberton, Ewell, Gorgas, Holmes, Loring, E. K. Smith, G. B. Crittenden; Lovell, a New York official), 12 Confederate (a seceded home state or office: Bragg, Cobb, McCulloch, Northrop, Polk, Reagan, Semmes, Slidell, Tattnall, Twiggs, Wigfall, Yancey), 17 no faction (home or office in one of the eight: Jackson, Mason, Hunter, Floyd, Vance, Buckner, Breckinridge, Forrest, Pillow, Pike, Price, Seddon, Randolph, the two Hills, Daniel, Morgan) and 3 `unknown` because the draft names no home state (Hardee, Bulloch, Van Dorn; `faction` is on their `unknowns` list). **Each man's note carries the reason and his eventual side** (`[start side …; his eventual side in the draft is …]`) for the history run. Eight post-holders also differ from the draft's side (Cooper, both Johnstons, Franklin Buchanan: Union by post; Letcher, Harris, Jackson (C. F.), Hicks: no faction). Where I judged from a home state the reason names the draft row it came from. *Rejected:* `unknown` for all 44 (it throws away what the drafts do say); leaving them Confederate (the 5 March pack would show Lee as Davis's man).
   **The 89 Union-coded men with no start post, audited (round 3)** by the same rule, from each man's `prewar` and `posts_1861_62` cells (the table `START_SIDE_UNION_CODED`, each entry with its reason; 133 men in all with the 44 above): **75 stay Union** (a free state's seat, newspaper, firm or office, or a US commission on 5 March; Farragut, Nelson and G. H. Thomas, born in slave states, are Union by commission); **8 have no faction**: Blair (Missouri), Clay and J. J. Crittenden (Kentucky), Holt (Kentucky; his Secretary of War tenure ended on 5 March, leaving no post), Andrew Johnson (Tennessee), Sherman (president of a St Louis railroad from January 1861) and, **by inference from a state office held later because the draft's prewar cell is needs-source**, Gamble (Missouri) and Pierpont (Virginia); **6 are `unknown`** because the draft names no residence for a civilian on 5 March: Burnside, Frémont, Hitchcock, Hooker, McClellan and Sigel. Four more Union entries rest on the same kind of inference (Tod: Ohio; Eckert: Ohio, from the prewar cell's "Ohio;"; Wallace: Indiana; Lane: Kansas), marked `[inferred]` in the note. Overall: 87 Union, 25 no faction, 12 Confederate, 9 unknown among the 133 men with no post. The three foreign ministers and Lord Russell keep no faction (foreign), as before. *Rejected:* guessing a residence for the six civilians (Hooker's and Frémont's California years, McClellan's Illinois Central, are not in the draft's rows); calling Holt, Clay and the Crittendens Union from their eventual loyalty (the rule, and 0055, say the 5 March post or the home state).
4. **Tenures.** A tenure counts when it certainly covers 5 March: its start is on or before it (or empty and unflagged) and its end is after it (or empty and unflagged); `to` equal to the start means the man has left (Holt's and Toucey's tenures end on 5 March, the day their successors begin). A later explicit start orders two tenures. **Fort Pickens (ruling S2):** the holder is Slemmer, who commanded the garrison from 10 January; Vogdes was senior on the station from 6 February but stayed aboard Brooklyn (the draft's own rows), so he is not the holder; his record is kept (`vogdes_israel`, a man on a post without the post) and the row's note says why. **The five cabinet holders (S3)** (State, Treasury, Interior, Attorney General, Postmaster General) cited only research notes, which 0050 does not accept alone. Each now also cites `NYT-1861-03-06`, the New York Times Washington dispatch of 5 March (confirmations), registered as a primary source with its archive.org copy; **That citation is UNVERIFIED: I took it from the march-1861-facts note's question 10 and did not fetch or re-check the newspaper.** It stays as cited until the verification research (lead's ruling) has checked it, together with the Senate Executive Journal. **The Department of Texas (round 3)** is held by Waite: the oob draft's own rows order the two tenures although neither has a date cell, because the row for Twiggs says he "relinquished command 19 Feb 1861 (before the window)" and "not a holder in the window", and Waite's says he "assumed command 19 Feb 1861 (General Orders No. 7, Department of Texas)", both citing OR I-1 pp.502-503, 521, 591. Twiggs keeps his record (Confederate by his table entry). The ruling is in `HOLDER_RULING`, with its reason in the holder row's note. *Rejected:* an acting marker or an unknown holder, since the draft does order them. 111 posts have a holder, **all seven of Lincoln's cabinet among them** (Seward, Chase, Cameron, Welles, Smith, Bates, Blair); 24 are `unknown`, with the candidates in the row: Confederate bureaus and commands whose start is needs-source or later (Baton Rouge and Mount Vernon arsenals, Morris Island, Fort Morgan, the Charleston naval command, the Pensacola navy yard with three candidates, the Adjutant and Inspector General and three other bureaus); state posts (Ohio's adjutant general, the Texas governorship with Houston and Clark, Jackson and St. Philip, two Texas bodies); and Union posts with no usable tenure (Utah, Harpers Ferry, Sackets Harbor, the Revenue Marine, the St. Louis arsenal, three ships' commanders and the Richmond's). Tenures outside the start are not written; they stay in `drafts/oob/people_in_posts.csv`.
5. **Posts and blocks formed after the start are not in it.** Eight posts: the Confederate Pensacola command (7 March), Texas command (11 April), Department of Florida, Department of Washington, the two relief expeditions, Water Witch (an April commissioning), and the Pensacola navy yard under the Union (abolished January). Twelve blocks go with them or have no usable place: the Pensacola force (1,045, 31 March), the Texas riflemen, both expeditions' troops, and four blocks with no place (Harriet Lane, John Adams, Release: needs-source; Levant: lost at sea). The Pickens expedition and the Sumter relief are situations' work (temporary posts, 0040). **No scheduled Confederate commands (lead ruling):** Pensacola and Texas stay out; the Confederate rule seat creates such commands in play, and a history run reproduces them through the historical default. So the post kinds `harbour_command` and `temporary_command` are now creatable by directive (with `navy_squadron` and `army_department`), where they were by appointment.
6. **Strengths are the draft's nearest return, dated** (answering the oob draft's question 6): 11 blocks carry a return dated after 5 March, with the date in `strength_date`. *Rejected:* a bring-forward rule now (no attrition rule exists yet); dropping the 11 (the draft chose them as the nearest return).
7. **Installations.** One record per flag, named from the region's notes ("Forts Barrancas and McRee" is one record where the notes group two forts). Two flags are not installations: `lowcountry` naval_base (Port Royal, Union from November 1861; a naval base must be a key point) and `britain_canada` naval_base (a foreign station). Port Hudson's fort_mode has no fort flag, so no fort. 21 forts on water carry the draft's passage or reduction mode and **command the water edges touching their region** (a default rule, since the draft names only obstructed edges). Posts link to their installation (20 do).
8. **Obstructions.** 12 water edges are obstructed at the start, each by a named installation. Not entered: the Cape Fear and Fort Macon edges (federal and unmanned in March, as the map README says) and Vicksburg (the draft marks it unconfirmed).
9. **Rail lines.** The first line the census parenthesis names is the edge's line; 30 edges name several (only the first is used; the notes keep the rest). Fifteen edges name no census line: the line is the first one their notes name; two are **composite lines** ("Lines from Indiana into Chicago", "Lines from Chicago into Wisconsin and round the lake") because the draft names none. 31 edges are breaks of bulk (ferries, horse cars, a break of gauge), found by keywords in the notes, not by the draft's own flag.

10. **River seasons.** Months from the draft's text (`Aug–Oct (low water)` is low water, months 8-10). "High water only" texts (3 edges) are written as the complementary closed months, marked inferred in the row. Texts with no months are left without a window, the text kept in the notes.
11. **Ships on foreign stations** go in the off-map box nearest their station (the draft's open question 8): Africa, Brazil and the River Plate and Cape Town: `south_atlantic`; China: `eastern_seas`; the Mediterranean: the new `mediterranean` box (lead ruling: the draft has none; role `distant_theatre`, one sea leg to `north_atlantic` of 15 days at capacity 5, a design parameter equal to the draft's transatlantic legs, since no source gives the route); Panama: `panama`; the rest of the Pacific squadron and Mare Island: `pacific_coast`; Vera Cruz: `france_mexico`. Troops afloat are `aboard` their ship (Vogdes's company aboard Brooklyn).
12. **People's evidence.** Stat evidence comes from people-sources Part 3; a direction with no admissible citation became unknown (12 cases, listed in the importer report). Pointers (research notes) never count; Bates's diary (licence unconfirmed, 35 citations) and the `WP1848` key (7, no entry in the map draft's source table) are dropped. 68 men who hold a post at the start but are not in the roster are people records too, with unknown centres, a kind taken from their post, and the tenure's sources; three roster/oob id pairs are one man each (`beauregard`, `toombs`, `morton`).
13. **Fields the format lacks stay in notes.** `posts_1861_62`, `west_point_class` and `prewar` are in each person's CSV note and in the drafts. Edges have no unknown marker, so the draft's wholesale "road existence needs-source" is in the `MAP-LANE` source's title and the 12 river edges with no seasonal text say so in their notes.
14. **Format change.** A sea edge may join two key points on one harbour (Charleston to Fort Sumter, Pickens to Pensacola); two ordinary land regions still fail. *Rejected:* dropping the two edges (authored data), or retyping them (they carry obstructions).

15. **Supply sources and depots (decision 0055).** The drafts have none, so without them every traced region is a pocket on day 1. `source/world/depots.csv` has 24 rows, **every capacity and every choice of region a design parameter** (each row cites `LANE-0.3`):
   - 8 Union sources in the coarse northern regions: New England 4, New York 5, New Jersey 3, northern Pennsylvania 4, northern Ohio 4, northern Indiana 3, northern Illinois 4, Michigan and Wisconsin 3.
   - 6 Confederate sources: Montgomery (the capital) 4, and 3 each at the arsenals in regions its states held on 5 March (control rows): Baton Rouge, Mount Vernon (Mobile), Augusta, Charleston, San Antonio. The arsenal rows cite the installation's own sources as well.
   - 10 depots, capacity 3, at installations the drafts name: the Washington Navy Yard and Arsenal, Gosport, Philadelphia, Brooklyn, Charlestown, Portsmouth, Sackets Harbor, Mare Island and Pensacola navy yards. Under 0054 a depot supplies only while a source's path reaches it, so the Gosport depot (Virginia, no one's) and the Pensacola yard (Confederate region) are as the control rows leave them.
   - **No off-map source.** The ruling asks for Union sources "in off-map boxes standing for the North's railways and industry", but the draft has no such box (its off-map boxes are the Pacific coast, Panama, foreign powers, blockade ports and raider zones), and 0054 as written cannot use one: off-map boxes are never passed and have no control rows, so a source there would supply nothing. I added none. If the North's industry should be off-map, the lead needs a box (and a rule) first.
   *Rejected:* Confederate sources at every state capital (nothing in the drafts says what they fed); a source at each arsenal in the eight uncommitted states (they are no one's); a capacity from any census figure (the drafts give no throughput).
16. **Neutral passage (decision 0055) is lane 0.5's code, not data here.** For the hand-over to 0.5: **Maryland, and with it the rail line from Baltimore to Washington, is open on 5 March 1861.** Maryland is a no-faction state government, so its regions are passable to all sides unless a situation closes them (`baltimore`, `frederick_md` and `southern_maryland` carry no control).

## Design parameters, not sourced facts

The supply sources and depots (regions and capacities, rule 15); the season effects and default months in `pack.json` (mud 1500 length and 600 capacity, low water 1300 and 500, flood 1200 and 800, ice 2000 and 300); `creatable` per post kind (squadrons, departments, harbour commands and temporary commands by directive; ships' commands and fort commands by appointment; the rest at a start only); the Mediterranean leg (15 days, capacity 5); the rank seniority tiers (a brevet is one below its grade); the terrain road multipliers (the map README's, themselves design parameters); the trait gate flags (the people README's). All are in `vocab.mjs`.
