# 0039: Positioning, price and the go or no-go bands

Status: accepted (2026-11-14) as the working basis for the owner's spending decisions, which remain the owner's (0037). From the market-research lane (`docs/research/2026-11-14-market.md`); all sales figures there are estimates from review multipliers and trackers. Extends 0037 and amends how 0022 and 0038 present AI. Amended by 0040 (first-month walkthrough revisions).

## Context

- Estimated Steam sales: Civil War strategy games that are not tactical stay small (Grand Tactician roughly 20–70k, Civil War II 6–18k, No Greater Glory's re-release under 1k); only tactical games pass 100k. A president-premise game sold to wargamers alone looks like 5–50k copies.
- Games whose hook is politics or characters reach further: Suzerain about 500k on Steam, with a 12-hour median and a $20 price. A realistic upside for this game is Suzerain-class reach, carried by curation, awards and streamers rather than advertising.
- Headlining AI hurts: AI-forward games drew mixed reviews, AI-disclosed games collect fewer reviews, and core gamers report strongly negative attitudes. Steam's disclosure covers both pre-generated and live-generated text.
- Wishlists are worth roughly half what they were in 2020–22. A public demo well before Next Fest earned about 2.5 times the wishlists.

## Decision

**Positioning.** Sell the constraint and the people, not the map and not the AI: "You can't move a division. One of your generals may run against you." Tag the game as political, narrative and historical strategy. The desk, the letters and the generals lead the store page and the trailer; the arrows come second. AI-written voice, where offered, is an optional extra and is never in the headline.

**Price.** A working target of $24.99 (range $20–30) for a campaign of about 15–25 hours. Final pricing is the owner's.

**Go or no-go bands** for art and marketing spend, measured on organic wishlists with the Steam page live for six months or more and a public demo:
- under 3,000: no-go for spend;
- 3,000–7,000: hold and improve the hook;
- 7,000–20,000: go, modestly;
- over 20,000: go fuller, or approach publishers.

Two hook tests sit beside the bands: demo players converting to wishlists at 20% or more, and organic wishlist additions of 10 or more a day.

**The demo** shows the desk and the letters (the audience, plan review, a general who shades his report), so it tests the actual hook. A console-only demo would under-test it. It goes public well before a Next Fest.

Rejected: marketing as a Civil War wargame (the niche's ceiling); headlining AI characters (the evidence says it costs reviews); pricing at the niche's $40–45 (the reach-beyond comparables sit at $20–30).

## Consequences

- Phase 2's demo-ready build (0037) means the desk with the audience and plan review, not the console.
- Steam's AI disclosure, covering pre-generated text too, is settled before the store page goes up (with 0038's question to Valve).
- The English-only review counts used in 0037 understate the niche somewhat; the conclusion holds.
