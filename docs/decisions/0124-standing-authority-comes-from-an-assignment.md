# 0124: Standing authority comes from an assignment to command, read with the regulations

Status: accepted (2026-11-23). From S117's research (`docs/research/2026-11-23-S117-command-authority.md` and `docs/research/S117/` on `sprint/research-authority` at `0cb5af51`). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0121 (where a mandate's root and standing fields come from). Clarified by 0126 (a neutral governor's undertaking offers his regiments; the transfer completes on receipt) and 0129 (a received assignment to command opens the post).

## Context

- **What 0121 requires of a mandate.**
  - A mandate names its actions, subordinates, regions, modes of movement, ceiling, guard, expiry, reporting duties and onward grant. Anything it doesn't name is denied.
  - Its root is a grant the pack declares, sourced and reviewed.
- **What S117 read.** It read 38 instruments of 1861 command and found 5 more referenced but unread. None enumerates all eleven fields.
  - **Scott's command.** The presidential orders of 5 July 1841 direct Scott to assume command of the Army (Richardson IV p.73).
  - **The 1857 Regulations.**
    - A commission alone doesn't put an officer on duty, and command of a department or the army needs an assignment (pp.2–3, paragraphs 7–14).
    - Orders pass through intermediate commanders, and a staff signature carries the commander's order (pp.57–60, paragraphs 422–447).
  - **Patterson's department.** War Department GO12 of 27 April creates the Department of Pennsylvania (Pennsylvania, Delaware and Maryland outside the Washington and Annapolis departments) and assigns Patterson to it. Virginia is not in it (OR I-II p.607).
    - His operations toward Harpers Ferry and across the Potomac rest on Scott's specific directions, such as that of 24 May (p.652).
    - Restraints and recalls followed (pp.657–717).
  - **Wallace.** Patterson's letter of 6 June allows him a conditional safe detachment, with no number for a ceiling or a guard (p.668).
  - **Lincoln's written delegations to Scott** concern civil security: habeas corpus and the Maryland legislature (Richardson VI pp.17–19). They are not a military root.
  - **The Confederate side.**
    - Lee held a grant of command from the State of Virginia.
    - Virginia's forces passed to the Confederacy on 6 to 8 June (OR I-II pp.775–776, 827, 911–912).
    - Johnston's assignment by the Secretary of War is referred to but unread. His assumption of 24 May and the government's letters to him as commander are read (pp.871–872, 894, 897, 910, 923–925, 934–935).
  - **A discrepancy.** GO12 excludes twenty miles either side of the Annapolis railroad. Patterson's own GO23 announces twelve (pp.607, 611).

## Decision

1. **Standing authority comes from a read assignment to command, never from rank alone.** An instrument that assigns a person to command an army or a department is the basis of his standing mandate.
   - **The Union's two.** Scott's comes from the order of 1841, read with the Regulations. Patterson's comes from GO12.
   - **What the pack declares.** Each standing mandate is pack data. It cites its instrument and the Regulations, and it stays visibly separate from S117's catalogue of historical instruments.
2. **An assignment carries what commanding means under the Regulations, and no more.** Each field is fixed this way:
   - **Subordinates and ceiling:** the troops assigned to the command, as `forces` and `command` hold them. The ceiling is a reference to the force, never a number chosen for it.
   - **Regions:** the territory the instrument defines, mapped to the pack's regions. Command doesn't extend beyond it without a specific direction.
   - **Actions:** the field goals the kernel already offers, within the territory and over his own troops. 0121's exclusions stand: no appointments, levies, political verbs, or compulsion of a peer.
   - **Onward grant:** only to his own subordinate commanders, inside his own mandate. That is what the Regulations' chain of orders is. A wider grant needs an instrument that says so.
   - **Guard:** none, unless an instrument names one.
   - **Expiry:** none of its own. The mandate lasts until he is relieved, superseded or leaves the post (0121).
   - **Reporting duties:** those the Regulations state, such as copies of orders to the Adjutant-General, and those an instrument names.
3. **Specific instruments widen or narrow a standing mandate, with their read terms only.**
   - **A direction beyond the territory** is a specific mandate for that operation, holding only what its text says. An example is Scott's of 24 May toward Frederick, Hagerstown and Cumberland, to threaten Harpers Ferry.
   - **A restraint or a recall** narrows the mandate once received (0121 item 2).
   - **Civil-security delegations aren't military mandates,** and aren't gameplay now.
4. **A staff signature carries the commander's order.** A paper signed by Townsend or Porter for his general is that general's order, as the Regulations provide. The staff officer is never a granting principal.
5. **A referenced assignment may stand on the read papers that attest it, for what they state.** Johnston's command at Harpers Ferry is attested by his own assumption and by the government's letters to him as commander. Its scope is only what those papers state.
   - The original stays open for recovery.
   - Nothing is reconstructed from a later account.
   - Jackson's refusal pending Virginia's instructions is kept as a receipt and an answer.
6. **A governor's post is the root for his state's forces until they transfer.** The pack already gives governors their state forces. A state grant is a mandate rooted in that post, such as Letcher's to Lee. A transfer instrument moves those forces to the national chain once the receiving command has received it.
7. **Where witnesses disagree, the issuer's text governs, and the disagreement stays visible.** For Patterson's boundary, the pack follows GO12, the War Department's own text, and records GO23's twelve miles. If the two readings give the same regions, nothing turns on it.
8. **Numbers still come to the lead with their basis.** No instrument gives a numeric ceiling, guard or duration for a sortie or a demonstration. 0121 item 5 and 0123 item 2 stand: their pack rules come to the lead first, never fitted.

Rejected:
- **Filling a mandate from rank or commission alone.**
- **Treating a blank field as unlimited,** or treating it as a historical prohibition.
- **Treating Lincoln's civil-security delegations, or the War Department's unsigned letter of 20 June, as a military root.**
- **Treating a staff officer as the issuer.**
- **Reconstructing Johnston's assignment from later accounts.**
- **A numeric ceiling, guard or expiry chosen to reproduce the record.**

## Consequences

- **The mandates build.** It follows S112's letters and receipts and declares the standing mandates of the June chain:
  - Scott and Patterson on the Union side;
  - Lee under Virginia, and Johnston after the transfer, on the Confederate side.

  Each comes with its instrument, and specific mandates follow from S117's catalogue.
- **The unread originals stay open for recovery.** These are the five references S117 couldn't read, the full chain of Scott's commands, and Johnston's assignment.
