# 0025: The president's attention is a weekly budget

Status: accepted (2026-11-14). The foundation review found that attention (proposal P9; `attentionPerTurn` in the M2 kernel) vanished when turns became real time (0017). Recommended by the lead. Amended by 0040 (first-month walkthrough revisions). Amended by 0041 (clarifications).

## Context

- P9: micromanagement creeps back whenever doing it yourself is free and better. M2 gave the player a few attention points per turn.
- The games survey's strongest warning: *No Greater Glory*'s players spent "about 60–80% of the game" on reassignment and transport.
- Under real time with pause there are no turns to hang a budget on.

## Decision

- Lincoln has an **attention budget that refills weekly** (pack-set; health, staff and crisis can change it).
- Verbs that reach into the war cost attention: setting a goal, editing a plan's arrows, transferring divisions, an audience, appointing or relieving, sending an observer. Approving a commander's draft as written, reading reports and pausing cost nothing.
- **A visit** costs days away from Washington instead of points: while away, the cabinet's and Congress's business resolves by default, as P9's "ignored".
- Decisions that reach the president can be decided (costs attention), delegated (the holder's bias applies) or ignored (resolves by default and is logged), as in P9.

Rejected: no budget (micromanagement wins); a per-day budget (fiddly under real time); a real-time cooldown per verb (invisible and unexplainable).

## Consequences

- The default path, approving a good draft, stays free, which is what keeps bookkeeping from eating the game.
- Attention spent and refused is logged, so the liveness gate can see whether players are starved or flooded.
