## Decision

1. **S13 is a notice in 1A.** `black_volunteer_offers` has no options or effects. It reports Dodson's offer and Cameron's refusal, quoted and attributed from OR III-1 pp. 107 and 133. 2. **Law and practice are a narrow exception to named proposers.** An option may carry `proposedBy: { basis: 'law-and-practice', cites }` only when it is gated and the register supersedes it or the option is watched. Its sources must resolve in provenance. It does not invent an adviser. The recorded S17 use is the return option's basis in the 1850 law and Major Cary's demand; its watched status, policy floor and expiration remain. Sources: `ef440f3`, commit message, validator and tests, explicitly attributing the exception to the lead's S17 ruling; `f1f61b8`, S17's implementation and gate notes. The ordinary rule still requires the people who argued for an option.

3. **The principal's initiative is provenance.** `principal-initiative` is accepted only on an option of a gated decision, with that side's principal as author and a document dated on or before the situation's earliest date. When every option rests on that basis, there is no staff recommendation: the decision is told by a plain report, and no presenter turns the provenance into advice. Sources: `4a3c09e`, implementing the gate reviewer's three conditions; the hand-over's “Where it stopped / Rulings, as built”; and the signed `habeas_on_the_line` gate note at `d9c10c1` as preserved in `95cdecd`.

4. **A notice checks the person whose words or official act it gives.** Its `speakers` declare the required post, or null for a person who must still be serving. Every speaker must qualify before it fires. A person named without speaking or acting is explained in `namedOnly`; the validator requires one of those declarations when the notice names a World person, excluding places named for people. Sources: “The post-holder check (the lead's rule 7)” and `f1f61b8`, which adds the runtime conditions, validation and both-way tests. An old historical attribution does not authorize an act by a man who no longer holds the post in this playthrough.

5. **A call is addressed to a state government.** It may reach a governor on the calling side or a neutral state's governor. It may not reach the other side's governor or a self-governing nation's post. The validator refuses the latter as `self-governing-call`; the kernel refuses it as `not_raising_post`. Calls and replies can travel to and from governments on no side, without assigning them allegiance. Sources: “Calls go only to a state government (the lead's ruling),” “Kernel added by this lane,” and `80512eb`; 0069 and 0070 continue to govern the nations' own acts.

6. **A dated letter can remain a report.** Townsend's and Bates's July letters are standalone dated reports, not a renewed S17 decision. Patterson's, Thomson's and Judge Nelson's calls for martial law or arrests are attributed reports with no effects; their words do not make their authors an option's proposer. They retain the speaker or named-only checks and gate review. Sources: the hand-over's S17 and S18 rows; `f1f61b8` (OR II-1 pp. 760–761); `46f8595` (OR I-2 pp. 586, 596 and 630). S17's recurrence belongs to 1B.

The view projects the tag without its research citations; both qualified desks display “No recommendation from the staff” once for the whole decision.
