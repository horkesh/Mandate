# 0136: An order is read when its terms are read, in the paper or in its issuer's or addressee's report

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). From S151's research (`docs/research/2026-11-24-S151-july-army-orders.md` and `docs/research/S151/` on `sprint/research-authority` at `ea14ce8e`). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0131 (what a read instrument is).

## Context

- **What 0131 requires.** A regiment goes where a read instrument sends it, and an uncovered regiment stays where it is. 0131 never says what makes an instrument read.
- **What S151 found.** Most of the orders that built the July armies survive only through reports and letters that state their terms:
  - **New York's sending.** Sandford's report of 30 December attests his orders of 19 April (the Sixth, Twelfth and Seventy-first to Washington), 20 April (the Eighth and Sixty-ninth to Annapolis) and 27 April (the Fifth to Annapolis). Morgan's letters of 18, 20 and 22 April report others (NY AG report for 1861 p.109; OR III-I pp.85, 95, 100).
  - **The crossing of 24 May.** It is known from Sandford's report of 28 May, made "pursuant to" Scott's order, with the named positions (OR I-II pp.37–39). The original is unread.
  - **The handover to McDowell.** It is known from McDowell's letter of 30 May, attesting the Government's direction to relieve Sandford (OR I-II pp.653–655).
  - **Sandford's sending to Patterson,** 6 and 7 July, is in his own report (NY AG p.110).
- **The OR prints are themselves copies.** S151 rightly kept every such reading as an attestation, not a recovered original, until a ruling.

## Decision

1. **An instrument is read when its terms are read.** The terms are:
   - its issuer;
   - its addressee or scope;
   - its destination or receiving command;
   - its date.

   They may be read:
   - **in the paper itself,** as printed in the Official Records or in a letterbook or order-book copy;
   - **in an official report or letter by its issuer or its addressee** that states those terms.

   An instrument read through a report is marked `[I]`, with the report's page. The report's date and the order's date are kept separate.
2. **Three things are not instruments:**
   - **A statement that orders were given, without their terms.** Farnham, for example, reports that he lacks the details of his regiment's orders.
   - **A later register, history or roster,** such as Phisterer's 1912 register or a battle's order of battle. These witness performance and identity only. Their departure and arrival dates never trigger a movement.
   - **An organisation order's listing of a body its issuer doesn't hold.** An onward assignment takes effect only on the bodies actually under his hand (0131).
3. **Where readings disagree, the earliest official reading of the terms governs.** The others stay recorded beside it. There is no midpoint, and no choosing whichever fits.
4. **0131 otherwise stands.**
   - The order's date constrains when it is eligible.
   - Delivery and movement are causal, through ordinary latency, agency, journey and receipt.
   - Missing terms are gaps.

Rejected:
- **Requiring the autograph original or the addressed copy.** Most 1861 orders survive only in reports, letterbooks and acknowledgements. This would leave the armies unbuilt for want of paper that does not exist.
- **Accepting a later register or roster as an order.** It would build the armies from their outcome.
- **Accepting a bare statement that orders were given.**
- **Choosing among disagreeing witnesses by the result.**

## Consequences

- **The build** declares S149's and S151's instruments under this rule, each with its page and `[I]` mark. They include:
  - Sandford's orders of April;
  - Scott's SO89½;
  - the crossing as Sandford reported it;
  - the handover attested by McDowell, with GO26;
  - GO1 and the July organisation orders;
  - the successive Lynchburg rules;
  - Sandford's July sending to Patterson;
  - Curtin's five regiments of 13 July.
- **The remaining gaps stay named.** These are the unnamed selections, missing receipts and identity holds that S151 lists.
- **What it may give.** The armies may form. Their size and the date of any battle are measured, never targeted.
