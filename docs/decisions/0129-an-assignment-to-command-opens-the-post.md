# 0129: A received assignment to command opens the post, and a lot keeps its read identity

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). From S126's hand-over (`docs/audit/s126-evidence/` and `docs/research/S126/` on `sprint/july` at `4b45f4e1`), with S117's catalogue (rows C01 and C02). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0124 (what a read assignment does).

## Context

- **What S126 built.** A general path for assigning regiments onward, once they've been received.
- **The two historical orders it couldn't declare:**
  - SO119 of 25 May, sending Bate's regiment to Fredericksburg to report to Ruggles (OR I-LI.2 pp.107–108);
  - Lee's direction of 1 June, sending Turney's regiment to Johnston (OR I-II pp.897–898).
- **Why:**
  - **Both orders are Lee's, and Lee's post was never opened.** S117 read two grants:
    - **Letcher's** gave Lee command of Virginia's forces (C01);
    - **Walker's of 10 May** told him to assume control of the Confederate forces in Virginia and assign them to the duties he indicates (OR I-II p.827, C02).

    Nothing in the model turns a received assignment into a held post.
  - **SO119 names Ruggles,** and the model has no post for him.
  - **The regiments' identities were lost.** S119 gave two of Tennessee's April-call lots the identities of Turney's and Bate's regiments, from Harris's telegram of 2 May (OR IV-I p.275). S124's path of offer and receipt left those lots anonymous.

## Decision

1. **A received assignment to command opens its post.**
   - A read instrument that assigns a person to command, delivered and answered through the ordinary path, makes him the holder of that command's post on receipt. It also gives him 0124's standing mandate. No separate appointment is invented.
   - **Lee's two posts:**
     - Letcher's grant opens his Virginia command, under the governor's post until the transfer (0124 item 6);
     - Walker's of 10 May opens his command of the Confederate forces in Virginia.
2. **An assignment the Secretary of War issues in his office is the government's, rooted in the principal.**
   - This is as 0124 item 4 treats a staff signature: the Secretary is never a granting principal of his own.
   - The build cites the Confederate act creating the War Department if it reads it. Otherwise the declaration carries `[I]`.
3. **An onward order whose named receiver has no post in the model is received by that command's post, whoever holds it when the regiment arrives.**
   - For SO119, that is the command at Fredericksburg.
   - Where the model has no post for the command at all, the gap stays named.
   - No holder is invented and no person is substituted.
4. **A lot keeps an identity assigned from a read link through every later path:** offer, receipt, transfer or onward order.
   - S124's loss of Turney's and Bate's identities is a defect, and is restored.
   - An identity is still assigned only where a read paper completes the link (S118).

Rejected:
- **Appointing Lee by date, or treating his unopened post as held.**
- **Making the Secretary of War a principal, or treating an unread instrument from the President as read.**
- **Addressing SO119 to Holmes,** who was assigned on 5 June, after the order.
- **Adding regiments on top of the generated lots, instead of restoring their identities.**

## Consequences

- **The build.** It restores the identities and opens Lee's posts from their read assignments. It then declares SO119 and the direction about Turney as onward orders, and measures.
- **C25's path.** Holmes, assigned on 5 June, receives at Fredericksburg only what the command there holds when his assignment arrives. C25 is measured, not promised.
