# 0139: An order to send the surplus keeps what its own words name

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). From S156's research (`docs/research/2026-11-24-S156-onward-papers.md` and `docs/research/S156/` on `sprint/research-authority` at `e9a30b2a`). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0135 (what a holder keeps) for a read order whose scope is the holder's surplus.

## Context

- **What S156 read.**
  - **Scott to Butler, 25 April.** Retain a competent force at Annapolis and select a regiment to guard the railroad, then send "all spare troops" to Washington as transport becomes available. Butler acknowledges it on 27 April (OR I-II pp.600, 604–605; Butler I p.42).
  - **Townsend for Scott to Cadwalader, 15 May.** Send the whole surplus within his command, after retaining reasonable guards for Baltimore, Relay House, the Junction, Annapolis and their railroads. The four regiments mentioned are an estimate, not a floor (OR I-II pp.638–640).
- **The difference from 0135.** 0135 keeps only for a need the holder's planner states, and an undetermined need is no reason to keep. These orders instead name what the holder retains. While the enemy is unknown, 0135 alone would send everything, against the paper's own words.

## Decision

1. **A read order whose scope is the holder's surplus is lawful.** "All spare troops" or "the whole surplus within his command" is a held-force scope, executed by the holder's own selection under 0134's ordinary compliance.
2. **The paper's own retention clause governs what he keeps.**
   - Where it gives a number, he keeps that number.
   - Where it names a guard for a place or duty without a number, he keeps one body for each named place or duty, of his own choosing. This is `[I]`: the smallest body that is a guard.
   - Where it names a body ("a regiment to guard the railroad"), he keeps that kind and quantity.
   - An estimate stated in the paper is not a floor.
3. **Beyond the named retention, 0135 still applies.** He keeps more only for a need his planner states. An undetermined need is no reason to keep more.
4. **It acts on actual holdings at each lawful execution,** as transport becomes available. It is never a standing sweep of later arrivals, unless the paper says "as they arrive", which falls under 0138 G8. A later paper that changes the terms supersedes it on receipt (0138).

Rejected:
- **Sending everything** because the need is unknown.
- **Keeping everything** because a guard is unquantified.
- **A fitted garrison size,** or the estimate taken as a floor.
- **A standing subscription** that drains every later arrival.

## Consequences

- **Build A carries it** as a 0138 G2 onward act for Annapolis, with focused tests:
  - a named number kept;
  - one body per named place kept;
  - an estimate not kept as a floor;
  - an unknown need keeping nothing beyond the named retention.
- **The named gaps stay open:**
  - the Eighth's and Sixty-ninth's exact Washington papers;
  - the Fifth's chronology, an intention against a register date;
  - the July brigades' crossing papers;
  - a separate handover paper;
  - the unnamed Lynchburg pairs.
