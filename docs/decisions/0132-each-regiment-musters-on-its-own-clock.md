# 0132: Each regiment musters on its own clock

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). From S140's research (`docs/research/2026-11-24-S140-basis-of-muster-timing.md` and `docs/research/S140/` on `sprint/research-military` at `f6b7c702`), with S137's cases (`203ebd77`). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0127 (the recruitment rule's timing).

## Context

- **Why fourteen regiments leave together.** Fourteen of McDowell's named regiments expire on the same model day, 19 July. Every lot of the call of 15 April musters on day 46, because the recruitment rule gives a whole levy one muster day (`musterDays`, 5 after receipt). Each lot's term counts from that shared muster.
- **What the record shows.** Musters within one call were staggered:
  - New Jersey's three May regiments mustered 4, 9 and 18 days after the receipt of 17 May (Stryker I pp.[67], 68, 102, 150);
  - Iowa's two came after 10 to 11 days and after 22 to 24 (OR III-I pp.220–221; Iowa AG I pp.91, 98, 101, 283).
- **How terms ran.**
  - A federal volunteer's term ran from his officer's muster (OR III-I pp.332, 403).
  - Some contracts named their own endpoints: the Twelfth New York's expired on 13 August, 92 days after its muster (p.391).
  - Second Maine and the Twelfth and Thirteenth New York are separate contract cases.
- **How much the sample covers.** It is sparse: 28 of 40 cells of side and state have no observation. S140 found no basis for a numeric profile.

## Decision

1. **A call's regiments muster in separate tranches** (S140's form).
   - A received call starts each eligible regiment's preparation, and each regiment musters at its own readiness and opportunity.
   - Musters on the same day are allowed.
   - An instruction about readiness is not an activation. Prepared troops may wait for acceptance or a mustering officer.
   - Each delay records its reason.
2. **Each accepted component keeps its own clock.**
   - A new federal volunteer's term runs from his actual muster, in the units of his contract and to its named endpoint.
   - A transferred state force keeps its remainder.
   - A continuation needs its own received authority and acceptance.
   - No blanket extension.
   - Second Maine and the Twelfth and Thirteenth New York are entered as their sources state them.
3. **A named lot whose identity has a read receipt and muster-in gets that interval,** counted from the model's own receipt.
   - It is the sourced readiness of that organisation, never an absolute date from the archive.
   - Like 0127's establishment, it is labelled `rule`, with its source.
4. **Every other lot keeps `musterDays`, declared as a provisional rule** until paired evidence for its profile suffices. Its provisional status is visible in the pack and the evidence.
5. **Nothing is set to reach 19 or 21 July.**

Rejected:
- **One interval for everyone, or one for each state.**
- **A call's lots all completing together.**
- **A uniform spread, a fitted midpoint, or New Jersey's median imported everywhere.**
- **Archived absolute dates, extensions for the fourteen, or days added or removed to reach the battle.**
- **Starting terms at proclamation, or restarting transferred regiments at Confederate muster.**

## Consequences

- **The build.** It changes when each lot musters, not its term arithmetic. It runs S140's paired cases on fictional fixtures, enters the sourced intervals for named lots, and measures.
- **Research.** A research lane recovers dated receipts and muster-ins for the named lots of 1861, both sides, from the state rosters, the federal register and OR. Dyer is used for identity and place only.
