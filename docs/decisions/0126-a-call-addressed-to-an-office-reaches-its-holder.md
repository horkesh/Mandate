# 0126: A call addressed to an office reaches whoever holds it

Status: accepted (2026-11-23). From S119's timing diagnosis (`docs/audit/s119-evidence/april-timing.md` on `sprint/july`). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0124 (a governor's undertaking offers his regiments; the transfer completes on receipt).

## Context

- **What the model does with the call.**
  - Walker's call of 22 April for three Tennessee regiments is sent on 3 July (day 120).
  - Harris receives it and answers on 5 July.
  - The regiments muster on 10 July.
- **What the record shows.**
  - Harris had the telegram by 30 April, and was organising three regiments for Lynchburg (OR IV-I p.272).
  - Turney's regiment left for Lynchburg the night of 1 May (p.275).
- **The cause is the path the call was authored on.**
  - The call is a doctrine row. It may go only to a post whose holder the issuer has received evidence of, and Davis learns that Harris holds Tennessee's office only from a report stamped day 120.
  - The Union's call of 15 April reaches the same office in April, because a situation addresses it to the office (S65's path).
  - The same gate delays Davis's call to Arkansas until day 71.
- **What happens to a neutral levy.** It musters under its governor and serves no side. Nothing moves it toward the call's destination until a separate transfer is requested.

## Decision

1. **A call may be addressed to an office, where the read paper shows it was.**
   - The dispatch goes to the office. Whoever holds the office when it arrives receives it and answers, and the issuer needs no knowledge of the holder.
   - The call keeps its identity, terms and ordinary compliance through dispatch and response, so its pushback rows and the lineage of its lots still refer to one request.
   - Where no read paper shows an office address, the call stays on the path addressed to a holder.
2. **A telegraph route between two offices is added only where a read paper shows the telegram.** An example is Walker's to Harris (p.272). It follows the pack's latency policy, with no promise of receipt the same day.
3. **A neutral governor who undertakes a call offers his regiments.**
   - His raised regiments go to the call's destination under his own authority (0124 item 6). They remain his until the receiving command receives them, and the transfer completes then.
   - He may still recall them before that receipt, as Harris reserved the right to.
   - His reservations go with the offer.
4. **No general address book of office holders is adopted.** Every government knowing every office's holder would change all its correspondence, not only calls, and needs its own ruling.
5. **No calendar.** No departure is scheduled by date. `musterDays` is unchanged, and no holder is placed in a menu the seat hasn't learned.

Rejected:
- **Advancing Tennessee's side, or inserting Harris into Davis's menu.**
- **Shortening the shared muster or courier delay to fit the record.**
- **Scripting Turney's departure, or a named muster.**
- **Delivering a neutral levy to the Confederacy at the call's receipt.** The governor's offer and the receiving command's receipt are separate events.
- **A general address book now.** It is a wider change than this record's evidence supports.

## Consequences

- **The build.** After the 0125 build, it adds the address to an office, the telegraph routes the read papers show, and the offer and receipt of a neutral governor's regiments. Each call of 22 April to a governor whose paper is read is moved onto the office path:
  - Tennessee's (OR IV-I p.272);
  - Arkansas's (OR I-I pp.687–688);
  - the others only as their papers are read.

  It measures the result.
- **C05 stays pending.** Harris's disclaimer of authority stays a question under 0112, since the request he sent about who would receive the troops grounds no reservation of authority.
