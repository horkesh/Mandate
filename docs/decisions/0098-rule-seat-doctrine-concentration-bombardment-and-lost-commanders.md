## Decision

1. **The principal's doctrine is pack data.** `rules.ruleSeats.principals` supplies a doctrine for each side, with no kernel default. It starts acting on the morning after that side enters the war, within the pack's weekly attention costs. It answers commanders first, then issues dated offensives, fills posts that lost their opening holders, makes dated calls, transfers levies to its concentration post, and makes inquiries on its cadence. The other capital is no longer a generic objective. The drafting tool may retain that objective as an explicitly labelled benchmark. Source: “What changed in Part A,” “The doctrine” and “Retired”; implementation `ea764cd`.

2. **Neutral passage and policy refusal retain their political meaning.** `crossNeutral` is false for both sides: entering a neutral government's ground must come through a political decision. A carrier's refusal records his grievance and stance but does not suspend the policy in his department. Both choices are attributed to the lead in “Choices, and the alternatives rejected.”

3. **A levy has one source of calls.** Calls have their authored dates, and the doctrine calls a governor once. A situation taking over a call requires removal of its matching doctrine row. A retained answer also prevents a later second call, whoever made the first; call answers survive ordinary response pruning. The Union doctrine omits the quota rows for Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee, Arkansas, Kentucky and Missouri, whose governors were outside its opening service side; the situations handle those requisitions. Source: “Calls happen once” and “Choices, and the alternatives rejected.” Removal of rows taken over by situations is still Part B integration work, not work completed by R48.

4. **Doctrine appointments and inquiries are limited.** Automatic appointment fills a post that lost its scenario-given holder, leaving opening vacancies alone. The weekly inquiry goes to the doctrine's armies, meaning its concentration and offensive posts. Part A has no doctrine for consult, audience, observer, promise, pressure, address or relieve. The lead left promise and address QUIET with driving tests; consult waits for situations, and the other verbs have no sourced trigger. Source: “Choices, and the alternatives rejected.” This limits automatic doctrine, not the player's existing verbs.

5. **A transfer includes an order to join the receiving post.** `forceJoins` is State owned by `movement`, in `movement/join.ts`. Each transferred block heads for the receiving post's pack-defined seat. Operations starts its next permitted quickest leg each day; blocks travelling together to the same seat march together. The order ends on arrival or when the block leaves the post, surrenders, disappears or enters one of that post's operations. With no route it waits, records `no_route`, and tries again each day. This supplies movement to a named command rather than a new planner assault. A hold goal holds its named objective, or the post's seat if none is named. Source: “A transfer concentrates (the lead's Fix 1).”

6. **Commanders choose battles; the player remains the principal.** Battle requests offer commit, night, cut-off and pursuit choices, including `judge`. A command must come from the holder's seat and can be given once a day. A human-driven seat receives no battle menu and its battle command is refused as `player_seat`, even if the post is a commander's. The guard reads `seatDriver`, not a fixed post identity. Rule seats choose `judge`, leaving battle decisions to traits. Source: “What changed in Part A,” “Battle orders are seat requests,” citing hard rule 1 and 0016.

7. **Bombardment is a planner method with a fortification guard.** It requires a take goal, no land route to the objective, a water edge within the pack's battery range, friendly shore guns, and a believed force or known works at the target. It uses the shore with the most guns, concentrates there and fires without crossing. In addition, the objective must appear in `combat.bombardment.objectives`, with a source for each fortification. Both planner and execution refuse an unlisted target; record terms must name a listed fort. The Union pack lists Sumter and Pickens; the testbed list is empty. Source: “The bombardment,” including the lead's ruling and G-1.12a; `ea764cd` and `0fd0f26`.

8. **Bombardment preserves the distinction between evacuation and capture.** Each day's fire applies the pack's gun ratio, morale, stores, works and casualty terms, with a breakdown. The defender's morale and boldness govern surrender; a breakout cannot cross the water. A cited `recordTerms` row may give evacuation under terms: the garrison leaves play and its commander leaves the post while still serving. Without that row, surrender captures the men and commander through the existing owners. Control receives a proposal, and both principals receive a typed `fort-surrendered` report. The permitted target kinds are force and works; the model neither acts on a town's people nor supplies text about who built the works. Source: “The bombardment,” including its three gate conditions. The signed Sumter notice is the later state recorded in `38587de` and the hand-over's opening review history; its older draft paragraph is not its final status.

9. **The menu follows news of a lost commander.** The lead's ruling on `review-lane-1.13.md` item 2, under 0052 and 0027, keeps a lost commander's post commandable until the principal receives the loss report. Reappointment waits for that news. An order sent to the believed holder travels to the headquarters. If the post is vacant or has changed holder on arrival, it returns by its route with `order-returned` and `vacant_post` or `holder_changed`, rather than receiving a compliance answer. A known vacancy is refused immediately. Source: “Posts as the principal believes them.”

10. **Loss belief covers each route of loss.** R48's shared reporter observes holder/status transitions after each phase, including appointment followed by capture on the same day. The report carries the lost holder's identity, so belief can survive battle-record pruning and work through a filter. A post losing all its blocks remains addressable until news arrives. This supersedes Part A's reliance on the battle record without adding a stored belief table. Source: “For Part B,” the lead's addition on `believedHolder`, and “Part B (R48) / People messages and loss belief”; `ba76d27`.

11. **People messages follow the same delivery rule.** Inquire, transfer, relieve, audience and observer accept the believed holder and return undelivered if that holder is absent on arrival. Failed delivery has no compliance response, grievance, transfer, audience counter or observer posting. Audience effects occur on arrival over the existing in-person route, with counters and cooldown checked then; an observer's commission carries the expected host separately from the observer. Source: “For Part B,” the lead's people-verbs addition, and “Part B (R48) / People messages and loss belief”; `ba76d27`.

12. **The capital has one State owner.** `command` owns `State.factionCapitals`, initialized from the start and moved by a validated `faction_capital` proposal. Use 29 May 1861, Davis's arrival at Richmond, as the effective routing date. Principal locations, his own post, capital fallbacks, menu route origins and the drafting benchmark read that State. Do not assign the ministries simultaneous moving dates. Source: “Part B (R48) / The capital”; `ba76d27`. Its research source is `docs/research/_parts/I-richmond.md`, item 2: the Richmond-dated telegram, OR IV-1 p. 361, and the arrival report in *Rebellion Record* 1, Diary p. 84. Selecting that date for routing is the design inference; it does not make the earlier authorization or department order an arrival date.

13. **The remaining stand-ins name their owners.** The bombardment parameters and concentration posts are [I] choices owned by lane 1.14. The gun-arm men's count used in place of a gun count is owned by 1B. R48 changes the labels without recalibrating the numbers. Source: “For Part B,” the lead's stand-in addition, and “Part B (R48) / Stand-ins, generated files and checks.”

Rejected:

- Routine permission to cross neutral ground, or a policy carrier's departmental veto. The lead rejected both in “Choices, and the alternatives rejected.”
- Calls on the morning after war instead of their dates; a second per-row owner field; appointment to every empty field post; inquiries to every fort and yard or a new unsourced men threshold. The alternatives and reasons are in that same section.
- Holding at the strongest block's current location rather than the goal's objective or post's seat. “A transfer concentrates” records the incorrect New York hold that Fix 1 corrected.
- Bombarding any populated place merely because a force is believed there. G-1.12a requires the cited fortification list and separately authored civilian consequences before extending it.
- a second capital owner; 20/21 May authorization, the War Department's 25 May order, or Congress's 20 July sitting as the president's routing move. “The capital” gives these alternatives and the research distinguishes them.
- an immediate audience that reveals a true vacancy, or a dispatch following a captured host to his side's capital. “People messages and loss belief” gives these rejected choices.
