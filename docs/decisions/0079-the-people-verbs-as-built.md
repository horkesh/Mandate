## Context

Lane 1.5 built the president's verbs on people, the audience recast for plans, the weekly attention budget and the weekly people step. Its reviews settled three questions:
- where an audience reads a general's terms during a plan;
- how the order-to-value map is protected;
- what is left for its follow-up, lane 1.5b.

## Decision

**Every verb is an order to a person,** accepted only from a principal and answered through his compliance with typed reasons, which `proxy` records. No verb names a place except as part of an order to a person (hard rule 1).
- **Appoint:** an optional rank, checked against the vocabulary and its kind. A man may decline the post. An appointment keeps a pending promise of advancement.
- **Relieve** has three modes: to a quiet department, at his own request, or outright. Each carries its own grievance, regard and standing change, as pack rules. A relief always takes effect, and "at his own request" becomes outright if he won't ask.
- **Transfer:** the sending commander picks the blocks by his own answer:
  - strongest first if he complies;
  - weakest first if he drags his feet;
  - half if he reinterprets;
  - nothing, with a memorandum, if he objects or refuses.

  This replaces the weakest-first stand-in (0064).
- **Inquire:** past a week's free inquiries, a man holds a "pestered" grievance (0040).
- **Consult:** the answers become stances on record. The cost of reversing one is 1B's. Consult goes by courier.
- **The observer:** a man posted at a headquarters (`observerPostings`, owned by `command`). The watched man holds a "monitored" grievance, and the observer writes letters to whoever sent him. His oversight is its own typed term, added on top of the base, never overwriting it.
- **Promise, pressure and address** act at once in Washington. A promise that falls due unkept becomes a grievance and costs regard. `address` only proposes to politics (lane 1.6).

**The audience** is M2's, recast for plans, with its eight counters.
- **Before a plan:** an audience reads the goal's terms. Goals carry no required strength, because the planner is the one owner of force ratio.
- **During a plan:** an audience reads the chosen candidate's own terms, as the edit path builds them, so "lacks resources" and the reinforcement counter work. A promised reinforcement counts only as far as the principal's credibility makes it believed.
- **M2's nine audience cases** are moved onto the new State, case for case.

**Attention** is owned by `seats` and priced by the pack's cost table: approving as drafted and sending back are free, an edit costs 1, inquire 0, consult 1. It refills to the cap weekly, with no carry-over. A command is paid only if it is accepted, and one it can't pay is refused.

**The weekly people step:**
- grievances fade, and due promises break;
- the ladder moves one rung a week, and a discontented man with standing becomes a rival;
- seat initiatives fire through compliance and reach the principal as memoranda.

**0074's gate rules, as built:**
- **`costOnly`:** honouring a cost-only value adds nothing and gives no reason, while offending one still costs.
- **The order-to-value map** is pack data. Its rows carry `gateTopics` and a `notCheaperThan` floor, which counts the pushback of every member of the side who holds an offended value. A chain of floors that loops is refused at validation.
- **Lane 1.5b** wires the map into play before any `fugitive-policy` row is authored. It also closes the options' own `honours` path, types the floor's difference, pins gated rows to their reviewer, adds the practice-clash term and the lever sweep's reading of delegation, and fixes the edit path's scale for available strength.

Rejected:
- **A required strength on goals.** It would be a second owner of the planner's force-ratio number.
- **The audience reading goal terms during a plan.** It never saw the plan it was about.
- **Weakest-first transfers whatever the man's answer.** That ignored the sender's will, which is the point of the verb.
- **Overwriting the base oversight with an observer's figure.** Both terms explain a deterrence figure.
