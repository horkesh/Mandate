# 0134: A superior may answer a request by ordering a subordinate who holds the troops

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). From S150's trace (`docs/audit/s150-evidence/trace/` on `sprint/allocate`, staged). Decided by the lead under the working style in `CLAUDE.md`; the owner can overturn it. Clarifies 0133 (what a superior may grant). Clarified by 0135 (a holder keeps what his own mission needs, and an unknown is not a need).

## Context

- **What S150 traced.** It followed sixteen declared sending instruments and sixteen received offers to day 140.
  - Seven of Cameron's papers of 3 June delivered 17 lots to Mansfield's Washington post (OR III-I p.246), about 14,700 men. All were still at Washington on day 140.
  - McDowell's own requests reached Scott, who answered `no_eligible_force`: the lots are Mansfield's, not his.
  - The onward paper of 23 June names two regiments Mansfield doesn't hold (OR I-II p.716).
- **What 0133 allows a superior.** He "cannot grant what he doesn't hold". He may relay the need upward or ask a governor, but 0133 says nothing of a subordinate who holds the troops.
- **What 0124 already gives Scott.** His standing mandate covers the troops assigned under him, and lets him grant onward to his own subordinate commanders. Historically, Scott assembled McDowell's army by directing the department commanders below him, as in the direction of 23 June that Mansfield conveyed.

## Decision

1. **A superior's means include his own subordinate commanders' holdings, through orders to them.**
   - A superior who receives a request may answer it by ordering a subordinate commander under his mandate to send a scoped cohort to the requester. For example, Scott may order Mansfield to send regiments to McDowell.
   - The order is ordinary command:
     - it is delivered and answered under the subordinate's own compliance;
     - the subordinate chooses the lots by his own planner, against his own missions;
     - it completes on the receiving command's receipt.
2. **It is never a direct reach into another command's lots.**
   - The superior names the scope, never the lots.
   - Only a subordinate within his mandate can be ordered. A peer receives an ordinary request and may decline (0121).
   - A governor still receives an ordinary request (0126, 0133).
3. **Everything else in 0133 stands:**
   - the quantity is the requester's stated shortfall;
   - one request is outstanding per mission, task and scope;
   - no daily retries, no target size and no roster.

Rejected:
- **The superior reaching into his subordinates' lots directly.**
- **Treating the whole faction's lots as the superior's.**
- **Leaving held troops stranded because the superior doesn't hold them himself.**

## Consequences

- **The build.** S150 builds it with focused tests and measures.
  - McDowell may now draw on the regiments at Washington, by Scott's order to Mansfield and Mansfield's own compliance.
  - Whether a July battle follows is measured.
- **Named gaps.** These stay named:
  - transport by sea and the Potomac, for the First Connecticut;
  - the conditional terms of Lynchburg's station;
  - the missing live cohorts.
