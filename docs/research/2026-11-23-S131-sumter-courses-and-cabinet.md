The central finding is that **the course's stated purpose and an adviser's
prediction about its consequences are different evidence**. The March papers
support qualified clause readings, not one unconditional list of favourable
and unfavourable values for everything called relief or abandonment. The
starting-cell findings are **13 held, zero not held, 36 unknown**. Unknown does
not mean opposition, neutrality, or a proved absence of conviction.

## How the readings are separated

Decisions 0085/0087 require a contemporary leg **before 5 March 1861** for a
starting value. A period letter printed verbatim later can supply that leg;
its editor's description cannot. Under 0122 a personally signed undertaking
can state its signer's motive; office, party, nomination, service and someone
else's policy cannot replace his own evidence. Under 0113 unknowns supply no
invented judgment. These are cell proposals, not instructions to change the
cast or to apply an average where a page is missing.

The March opinions are read **only to identify the courses and their clauses**.
They supply no cabinet starting cell. Several replies to the 15 March question
are actually dated **16 March** in the printing: Chase, Cameron and Smith.
Their dates are retained in the clause CSV. The Scott memorandum printed in
OR I-I pp.200–201 has no date in its own heading. The lead identifies it as
28 March; S131 does not turn its placement among the 15 March enclosures into
a composition or receipt date. [Question and replies, Lincoln II
pp.11–22](S131/read/L-II-11.txt); [OR enclosure](S131/read/OR-I-I-200.txt).

## The four courses

| Course | Read purpose and possible value meaning [I] | Clauses that prevent an unconditional mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provisioning | Sustaining a retained federal garrison can honour `union` and `duty`, read with Lincoln's instruction to maintain retained military places. | The 15 March question assumes possibility and asks wisdom. It neither orders an attack nor guarantees peaceable passage. The offences to `conciliation` and `caution` depend on the predicted armed collision, loss and escalation. |
| Relief | Supplying **and reinforcing** the garrison serves the same maintenance purpose; lawful assertion also touches `order`. | The March inquiries ask what means and preparation are needed. A major expedition capturing batteries differs from boats bringing temporary provisions. On 29 March, a peaceable attempt followed by reinforcement if resisted is another qualified course. |
| Abandonment | Leaving Sumter can honour `caution` and `conciliation`. Several proponents explicitly retain `union` and `order` as the wider purpose. | Leaving this fort while defending Pickens and enforcing law elsewhere differs from surrendering federal authority. Blair predicts the former will still lead to prolonged conflict; Smith makes the political consequence depend on how the act and concurrent measures are understood. |
| Evacuating both forts | Scott explicitly seeks `union` through `conciliation`, while avoiding an expedition he considers unavailing (`caution`). | He names **both Sumter and Pickens**, but expressly retains Jefferson and Taylor. Giving up held positions conflicts with the earlier maintenance instruction (`duty`), without making his purpose disunion or abandonment of every fort. |

These summaries are supported by Lincoln's maintenance instruction and inquiry
of 9 March, pp.9–10; the 15 March question, p.11; the cabinet papers,
pp.11–22; the preparation order and 29 March opinions, pp.25–27, with the end
of Bates's opinion on p.28; and Cameron, Totten and Scott, OR I-I pp.196–201.
Every separate reading has its exact page, attribution and condition in
[course-clauses.csv](S131/course-clauses.csv). The `honours`/`offends` columns
must be read with `mapping_kind` and `limit`; **they are not executable lists**.

Three distinctions matter particularly:

- **Provisioning is not automatically reinforcement.** Chase expressly adds
  reinforcement and a liberal but firm proclamation (pp.14–15). Smith gives
  an exception for adequate force and reinforcement (pp.18–19). These added
  clauses cannot be silently included in the President's bare question.
- **Conciliation is an end as well as a proposed means.** Seward expects
  restraint to preserve reunion (pp.12–14); Blair expects firmness to restore
  fraternity (pp.20–21). A shared desired end does not prove a shared forecast.
- **The two-fort proposal expressly aims at Union preservation.** Coding its
  purpose as simply offending `union` drops Scott's own explanation on
  OR p.201. Conversely his forecast of permanent adherence is not established
  truth available to every adviser. [Scott, pp.200–201](S131/read/OR-I-I-201.txt).

`party` and `reputation` are audited in the matrix because the discussion
touches party demoralization, personal reputation and national honour. They
are not recommended as unconditional course terms. Seward expressly weighs
party against country on p.12; Anderson's reputation is **Anderson's**, quoted
by Cameron (OR p.197), and national credit or the expedition's sailors' honour
does not establish a cabinet member's personal good-name motive. No new
`honour` value is proposed.

The course does not itself order emancipation, fugitive return, or a change
in slavery law. No slavery-value offence or reward is inferred merely from
its being a Sumter course. This does not remove the papers' context: Bates's
fear of a servile war (p.22), Blair's discussion of claims about abolition and
racial equality (p.20), and Scott's concern for the eight slaveholding states
(OR p.201) remain attributed in the retained pages. They are not narrator
judgments or invented terms of Lincoln's question.

**Seward:** the task he describes is reconciling men bent on disunion to the
incoming administration. This independently retains the two cells already
present in the specified S128 cast. It supplies no March-specific stance or
generic low-risk disposition. [Own letter, p.481](S131/read/SEWARD-481.txt).

**Chase:** his own policy expressly combines maintenance of the Union,
enforcement of law, present official duty, and goodwill and concessions bounded
by principle. He rejects weak concession and intemperate denunciation. The
letter supports `order` independently of the existing cast's February-26
locator. Its specific warnings about finance and interference with state
tribunals are retained without promoting them to a general `caution` cell.
His fugitive compensation/colonization proposals and separation of the federal
government from slavery remain in the complete letter; they are not omitted
to make a simpler Union argument. [Own letter,
pp.293–295](S131/read/CHASE-293-295.txt).

**Cameron:** Savage's quoted 1846 tariff remarks concern labour and
Pennsylvania; his friends' praise is not his own motive. They do not establish
these cells. His later March advice supplies none of them. **Smith:** his
seconding of Lincoln's nomination (Chicago proceedings p.108), campaign
correspondence, or convention attendance does not establish these starting
values. The substantial Smith speech in Chittenden's conference report is
identified in its index as **J. C. Smith**, not Caleb B. Smith; it is excluded.
Search boundaries and exclusions are in [searches.csv](S131/searches.csv).

**Welles:** the actual first manuscript page was viewed as a downloaded image,
headlessly. Its desired end is strength for country and Union under the new
administration. A dealer's undivided transcript suggests further duty and
conciliation language, but manuscript pages 2–3 were not recovered. Those
cells remain unknown, with that exact page gap. This is not permission to
use his retrospective diary introduction. [First-page transcription and scan
receipt](S131/read/WELLES-FOGG-1.txt);
[original first page](https://pictures.abebooks.com/inventory/32376714856.jpg).

**Bates:** the signed 20 August 1859 letter printed on pp.43–44 seeks sectional
peace, harmony, law and order. Its earlier use of *union* denotes an opposition
party coalition; it is not silently read as a commitment against national
secession. The quoted policy letter on p.41 urges pausing to estimate costs
and contingencies before foreign war: `caution` is a labelled interpretation
of that own motive. Its precise date is unresolved, but the **1860 printing**
establishes a pre-start upper bound. The earlier narrative says a committee
wrote him in February 1859; that does not establish his reply's exact date.
The promising 23 January 1861 Rollins letter was located in a manuscript
catalog, but its pages were not successfully read; secondhand quotations do
not fill his `union` cell. [Own text, p.41](S131/read/SAVAGE-41.txt);
[signed letter, pp.43–44](S131/read/SAVAGE-43.txt).

**Blair:** his complete letter supports the existing `union` and `order`
cells. It also expressly desires permanent fraternity and reconciliation:
`conciliation` is proposed as that end, **qualified by his belief that force
may be its means**. Willingness to risk blood does not prove the absence of
all caution. His disparaging comparisons and opposition to abolitionists
remain his attributed words in the complete source.
[Own letter, pp.2–4](S131/read/FOX-I-2-4.txt).
