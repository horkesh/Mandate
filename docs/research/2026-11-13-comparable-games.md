# Research: command through proxies — comparable games and theory

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: research note (not canon). Items marked (gk) come from general knowledge, not a fetched source; verify before relying on them.

## Game by game

| Game | Proxy mechanic | Works | Fails | Steal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decisive Campaigns: Barbarossa | Political Points; named relationships (Hitler, Brauchitsch, army/panzer group cdrs); stance choice; delegate a decision for 1 PP; poor relations → "half-hearted performance or insubordination" | "Playing a card doesn't guarantee the order is executed" | thin characters, tight PP economy feels like a tax, repetitive decision text | **Delegation priced in political capital** |
| DC: Case Blue / Ardennes | Off-map high command issues shifting intermediate objectives; HQ chains | link between high command, HQs and units | Ardennes: C2 realism without politics "feels dry" | **Imposed objectives you can meet, negotiate or defy** |
| Shadow Empire | Leaders (Technical/Command/Interpersonal), relation score, council directors generate Strategic Stratagem cards; seniority makes firing costly | politics emerges from staffing | opaque ("digging deep enough"), repetitive staff events | **Proxies generate the player's options**: a bad minister means bad cards, not −10% |
| Command Ops 2 | Order delay through the HQ chain; subordinates plan their own execution; bypassing costs coordination | micromanaging is a mistake by design | players can't tell deliberate friction from AI weakness | **Show the delay and cost estimate before sending** |
| AGEOD Civil War II | Per-turn activation check vs strategic rating; seniority; sacking political generals costs national morale/VP (gk) | "armies without commanders", 1861–62 | dice feel like cheating; can't tell why a stack didn't move | **Sacking cost scales with constituency, not competence** |
| Grand Tactician: Civil War | Fame boosts morale; feuding leaders refuse commands | — | clunky; feuds feel arbitrary | **Fame as the proxy's own political capital** (McClellan problem) |
| Ultimate General: Civil War | Reputation drives funding; govt sacks you | — | **government is a vending machine, not an actor** | negative lesson |
| Imperator: Rome | Loyalty < 33 = disloyal; if 33% of the army is under disloyal generals a civil-war timer starts; senate parties | "the rebels were the most competent people in my country" | bribe-and-monitor whack-a-mole; menu spam | **Structural threshold: share of force held by personally loyal men** |
| CK3 / Victoria 3 / HOI4 | Councils, vassal factions with a power threshold; interest groups whose demands mutate with leadership; HOI4 generals as stat blocks | factions as coalitions | interchangeable opinion currency; Legitimacy opaque (reworked in V3 1.1); HOI4 AI execution is worse than doing it yourself → micromanagement | **Coalitions act only above a power threshold; demands change with the bloc's boss** |
| Old World | Orders: one per-turn command budget, scales with Legitimacy | "biggest success" | characters can't do much | **Unified attention budget gated by legitimacy** |
| Suzerain | Ministers argue before you decide; personal goals | debate framing | linear; pushback is scripted | **Debate scene as the decision UI** |
| Terra Invicta | Councilors, enemy factions can turn them | — | "gopher-hammering micromanagement"; losing a councilor snowballs | **Rivals can compromise your proxies** |
| Total War: Rome II | Intrigue layer | — | "utterly opaque", ignored | negative lesson |
| Field of Glory: Empires | Decadence as structural decay | prevents snowball | "arbitrary, gamey" | **Dysfunction should come from player choices** |

## Board and professional

- **GMT COIN (Fire in the Lake, Andean Abyss, A Distant Plain)**: asymmetric factions; allies on the same side score differently (ARVN wins on Patronage). Steal: **allied proxies with their own victory conditions**.
- **Twilight Struggle** (gk): using a card carrying the opponent's event fires the event anyway. Steal: **resources that come with someone else's agenda**.
- **Here I Stand**: mandatory events push history whether you like it or not.
- **Churchill (Herman)**: conference issues debated with staff cards; your staff limits which issues you can win.
- **For the People / A House Divided / Lincoln (Wallace)**: Strategic Will; "Room at the Top" forced leader replacement; Lincoln's deck destruction simulates pressure, not re-enactment.
- **Defiance: 2nd Russo-Ukrainian War (GMT)**: four political tracks (Zelenskyy, NATO, Lukashenko, Putin) feed Russian Strategic Morale. An end-of-turn Unity/In-Fighting roll can add **Mutiny / Inaction / Infighting HQ chits** to the activation cup, so political rot becomes command dysfunction. The game was criticised for leaving out Ukrainian internal friction.
- **Matrix games (Engle)**: argument + counter-argument + umpire odds. Weakness: inconsistent adjudication. **Free Kriegsspiel**: umpire judgement replaces tables.
- **Pol-mil seminar games**: Blue/Red/Green teams with a White control cell.

## Theory

- **Feaver, *Armed Servants***: agents "work" or "shirk" by degree. Shirking is driven by preference divergence × (monitoring probability × punishment credibility). Monitoring is costly and causes resentment. Punishment credibility drops when the agent is popular or irreplaceable.
- **Cohen, *Supreme Command***: the "unequal dialogue": blunt, iterative, civilian has the final word. "It is always right to probe."
- **Clausewitz friction / Auftragstaktik**: orders as intent + constraints + resources; interpretation varies with initiative, competence and alignment. Friction should come from the agents, not from noise.
- **Selectorate theory (Bueno de Mesquita)**: survival depends on a winning coalition; small coalitions need private goods, large ones public goods.
- **Coup-proofing (Quinlivan; Talmadge; De Bruin)**: counterbalancing, rotation and loyalist promotion reduce coup risk and also reduce effectiveness; counterbalancing can provoke coup attempts.

## Synthesis: transferable mechanics

1. Orders as intent + constraints, answered on a **compliance spectrum**: comply, comply-with-modification, slow-walk, reinterpret, object with counter-proposal, refuse.
2. **Delegation as a priced choice**: decide yourself (costs attention), delegate (the proxy's bias applies), or ignore.
3. **Visible, reasoned pushback, previewed before commit** (Into the Breach-style telegraphing).
4. **Proxies generate options**: a portfolio holder's competence and agenda shape your menu.
5. **Allied proxies with their own victory conditions.**
6. **Political capital earned from results**; sacking cost scales with constituency/Fame.
7. **Structural rebellion thresholds**: share of force under personally loyal men + coalition power vs yours.
8. **Political rot becomes command dysfunction.**
9. **Filtered reporting**: every report has an author with a bias; probing and independent channels reveal the gap.
10. **Seniority, Fame and constituency constrain appointments.**
11. **Loyalty vs competence trade-off** (coup-proofing).
12. **Coalition maintenance**: survival of the leader is the clock.

## Failure modes

- Pushback that feels random → make it deterministic, caused, and telegraphed.
- Opaque systems → every number must be explainable in one click.
- Micromanagement creeping back → doing it yourself must cost scarce attention, and delegation must not be strictly worse.
- Loyalty whack-a-mole → grievances are specific and not interchangeable (gold does not fix a denied promotion).
- Event spam → events arise from agent state, not random draws.
- Patrons as vending machines → patrons are actors with goals and memory.
- Scripted linearity, stat-block proxies, snowball loss of one key proxy, inconsistent adjudication.

## Market gaps

1. **Political fog of war**: information filtered by self-interested reporters. Almost nobody does this.
2. **Negotiable, readable pushback**: matrix-game argument settled deterministically.
3. **Generals as political competitors**: McClellan 1864, Caesar, Zaluzhnyi 2024, Prigozhin 2023.
4. **Same-side asymmetric scoring** in digital grand strategy.
5. **An escalation ladder of insubordination**: slow-walk → reinterpret → leak → go over your head → public defiance → armed action.
6. **External patrons as proxies** (client kings; Britain and the Confederacy; the West and Kyiv; red lines on deep strikes).
7. **Deterministic agency**: friction that is fully caused and readable to a player who probes.
8. **Losing the coalition as the primary fail state.**
