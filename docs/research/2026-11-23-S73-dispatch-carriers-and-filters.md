# S73: dispatch carriers and presidential information filters, March–July 1861

Research drafts, unapplied. Window: 5 March–31 July 1861. No pack, kernel,
post, person, rule, band or signature changes. **[I]** denotes an inference.
The reader must distinguish a dated paper, its physical journey, its receipt,
and the simulation's report about its subject.

## What was inventoried

Both historical factions live in `packs/union-1861`; there is no separate
Confederate pack here. The fictional testbed supplies no historical evidence.
[S73/pack-snapshots.json](S73/pack-snapshots.json) pins the situation files read
in this worktree, main, `sprint/b2`, and `sprint/politics-gaps`, with their hashes,
authors and citations **as authored**. Other worktrees were read solely to find
the modeled papers. `sprint-july` was not accessed. No worktree was modified
except this one; the sole closing coordination append is separately authorized.

[S73/modeled-papers.csv](S73/modeled-papers.csv) gives all **70 distinct situation
contexts** a disposition: **51 notices and 19 decisions**, including B2's
`pickens_refusal_received` and `pierpont_aid_requested`, and main's
`butler_at_fort_monroe`. These are coverage counts, not 70 authentic dispatches.
Each inspected variant remains in the snapshot register; the union of IDs does
not assert that all variants are loaded together.

The source-backed context and the actual paper are often different. A treaty,
ordinance, election, public proclamation, statutory opinion or decision may cause
a modeled cabinet summary without identifying any original delivery to either
president. The register does **not** assign its report's author as historical
writer or bearer. Unestablished hand delivery remains unknown; an event date is
not a departure or arrival date. The pack's existing citations are retained as
retrieval leads and model evidence, not promoted to historical facts read in S73.

The generated families are recorded in [S73/coverage.json](S73/coverage.json):
orders and answers, recruiting requisitions and state referrals, recurring
military/naval/press reports, staff summaries, and operational/appointment/return
notices. They have no finite historical paper for every possible player order,
seed, governor response, battle or alternate history. A generic report template
does not authorize inventing a historical messenger [I]. The April requisition
was notified to governors by telegraph on its date, according to the editorial
footnote in **OR III-1 p.68**; Letcher's acknowledgment of Cameron's telegram of
the 15th is dated the 16th (**p.76**). This establishes a wire exchange, not a
named hand carrier for every generated governor/adjutant chain. No requisition
or reply is silently assigned a bearer.

Two comparison cases prevent false joins. Jerome Closson delivered Patterson's
papers at Cumberland on the morning of 11 June; the writer's own march via
Grafton is not Closson's route (**OR I-2 p.676**). Beauregard's 7 July covering
letter requests personal delivery by Col. Thos. H. Taylor to Lincoln, but
McDowell's 9 July answer says the paper was transmitted through the
General-in-Chief (**p.738**). Neither is a new modeled situation or a reason to
invent one. No carrier is established for the actual 3 June Beauregard letter
in **OR I-2 pp.901–902**, or Cooper's 15 May Johnston assignment in **pp.844–845**.
Johnston's instructed travel via Lynchburg is the commander's route, not evidence
of a bearer delivering his own appointment to the president [I].

Two other explicit papers remain carrier holds. **OR II-1 p.760** prints
Townsend's communication to Mansfield, but its author is not thereby a bearer.
Bates's 23 July answer acknowledges a letter of the 11th received on the 19th
under Senator Lane's frank (**p.761**). A postal frank is not evidence that Lane
personally carried the letter [I]. No carrier itinerary is drafted from it.

## Unapplied S53 drafts

[S73/latency-dispatches.partial-drafts.json](S73/latency-dispatches.partial-drafts.json)
uses S53's `to`, `carriers[{post,route}]`, `terms` and `source` object shape. It is
explicitly **partial and not loadable**. `terms.boldness` remains null; empty
honours/offends mean unassessed. Missing targets stay null. Invented average
judgments or calibrated delivery demands would exceed this research request.
The `s73_draft_*` labels are proposed research identifiers, not cast records,
appointments or claims that offices existed. Existing ship posts in C16 retain
their own unresolved locations/holders and opening leg.

Three format limits matter before apply [I], established by reading
`packages/kernel/src/messages/latency.ts` and `tick/context.ts` on this branch:

- S53 selects an undated itinerary by destination alone. Distinct journeys to
  Sumter or Sabine cannot all become permanent requirements; duplicate targets
  are invalid. The format has no departure, arrival, document ID or validity dates.
- Its carrier array is serial. People traveling together are recorded as
  alternatives for a lead's future representation, never stacked as independent
  delay gates. C16 alone has successive vessel legs. Sealed or undelivered
  dispatches must retain those distinctions.
- Carrier routes allow courier or ship. A rail journey, a river crossing, a boat
  to a fleet and a local harbor leg cannot acquire authentic times merely by
  choosing one of those words. Route classifications in the drafts are [I].

No complete journey duration has been entered as a willingness term. No
historical delay is counted as resistant behavior, and no pushback floor is
supplied or waived. The lead owns calibration.

## Offices that screened papers

[S73/filters.csv](S73/filters.csv) gives eleven positive, qualified, later or held
records. **No universal March–July presidential incoming-wire filter holder is
established for either side by the passages read.** This is a finite evidence
limit, not a claim that no screening occurred before a particular date.

For Lincoln, Bates describes the early War Department instruments in Chief Clerk
Sanderson's room and the operators taking orders directly from the Secretary of
War. His footnote dates Strouse's appointment to 15 May; his account names Wilson
as manager during Bull Run (**Bates pp.25–27,87–88**). He describes personally
delivering Ellsworth's news to Lincoln on 24 May (**p.29**), and Lincoln waiting
in the office for dispatches on 21 July (**p.88**). These are affirmative access
cases, not proof of unrestricted access to every wire [I]. Bates calls the
operators' arrival both Thursday and 27 April (**p.25**); this inconsistent
weekday/date does not establish an exact activation date.

Scott's 8 July rule, confirmed by Cameron, requires the Commanding General's
permission for telegraph conveyance of army-operation dispatches (**OR III-1
p.324**). That is an in-window security rule. It does not identify a holder
authorized to hide Lincoln's incoming copies or prove that Lincoln was its
restricted audience [I]. Taylor's July paper establishes forwarding, with no
documented hold or trim (**OR I-2 p.738**).

The State Department supplies a narrower in-window example. Seward's 15 March
memorandum describes the commissioners' incoming request, received by the
Assistant Secretary through Banks on the 13th, and says he submitted his paper
to Lincoln, who sanctioned the decision (**Richardson I pp.85,87–88**). This
establishes diplomatic screening and a summary reaching the principal through
Seward. The precise date Lincoln saw each original letter is unknown. The later
delay to the commissioners, by their consent, is an outward diplomatic process;
it is not a demonstrated incoming-wire hold on Lincoln [I].

Hay's 1866 account says he opened, read and answered letters and selected
newspaper material; Nicolay handled visitors, congressional messages and general
supervision. Hay describes delegation developing over the administration,
without dating the mail handover to March–July (**Herndon and Weik II
pp.224–226**). The mail-screening function is attested; an exact in-window start,
end or general wire role is not. This preserves R92's distinction.

The named withholding incident is later. Bates places Eckert at McClellan's
headquarters in the September context and dates Stone's withheld dispatch to
**21 October 1861**, under Cameron's general-first instructions (**pp.93–96**).
The instruction's original issue date remains unknown. Stanton's final office
move belongs to **March 1862** (**pp.38–40**). Neither may be backdated to fill
July's filter floor [I]. Nor does a first dated example prove absence before it.

For Davis, Jones's **19 May** entry describes installation beside Major Tyler
in Walker's office: Jones opened and read incoming letters, abstracted their
contents on the backs, then Walker pencilled directions for replies which Tyler
and Jones drafted (**Jones I pp.36–37**). His June and July entries describe
continued processing, including July 11–13 and Bledsoe acting during Walker's
July 15–16 absence (**pp.48,50–51,60–62**). These establish **departmental mail
screening**, not a general presidential wire filter [I]. In his 22 June entry,
Davis searches for a letter he had already referred to Walker; that example
has Davis seeing it first (**p.54**). Jones's descriptions and criticism supply
no temperament values here.

Pickens and Beauregard intercepted official outgoing Sumter mail on 9 April,
forwarding private letters and sending official ones to the Confederate
government (**OR I-1 pp.248–249,291–294**). That is enemy-mail interception,
distinct from screening Davis's ordinary inbox [I]. The Rice *Papers of Jefferson
Davis*, volume 7 index gives Josselyn retrieval locators at pp.55 and 330, but
the underlying pages were not retrieved. F10 remains a retrieval hold: no
appointment date, duty or tenure is asserted from the index. A dated duty order
or endorsed incoming papers would settle the presidential-mail question.

## Proposed rulings [I]

1. Retain the named journeys and unknowns as document-specific evidence. Reject
   treating landfall, a dated letter, a contemplated departure or an AGO receipt
   as an exact personal delivery; reject false identity expansions and serial
   processing of companions.
2. Keep these partial drafts unapplied until dated scope, actual posts/holders,
   route admission and delivery terms have been resolved. Reject a permanent
   destination rule inferred from a one-off journey or invented average terms.
3. Retain the in-window departmental/mail/security evidence and the presidential
   filter holds. Reject importing October or 1862 restrictions, equating every
   cabinet author with a filter, or declaring a proven absence from silence.

For the lead: review the packet under 0032/0058 and decide any later carrier/post
or dated-route work. Whether any 1861 pushback floor is justified remains the
lead's calibration question; no criterion is changed here.
