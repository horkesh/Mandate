In  such  cases  they  are  popularly  distinguished  by  the  nam>=>s  of  :he  command- ers, as  Turney's  First,  Maney's  First.  The  principal  cause  of  this  duplication  was  the  fact that  several  regiments  early  in  the  war  were  mustered  directly  into  the  service  of  the  Con- federate States.  These  were  Tennessee  regiments  in  fact,  but  were  generally  cailed  Confed- erate. Thus  Turney's  was  styled  First  Confederate;  Maney's,  First  Tenner-see.  To  make this  confusion  as  little  perplexing  as  possible,  I  have  placed  these  Confederate  regiments  at the  beginning  of  the  long  defile  of  Tennessee  commands. On  August.  13,  1SS2,  Judge  Peter  Tarney,  in  answer  to  iny  application  for  a history  of  his  very  distinguished  regiment,  says:  "  I  have  written  to  one  of  my  old captains,  who  is  a  walking  diary  of  occurrences  in  my  regiment.  I  am  sure  I  can get  his  assistance,  and  therefore  think  I  may  promise  what  you  want.''  Accord- ingly, Captain  W.  E.  Donaldson,  of  Jasper,  soon  entered  upon  the  task.
