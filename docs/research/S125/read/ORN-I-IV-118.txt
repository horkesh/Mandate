Report of. Lieutenant Worden, U. 8. Navy, of his detention as prisoner of war.
MONTGOMERY, ALA., April 15, 1861.
Sir: I started from Pensacola on my way toward Washington on the
evening of the 12th instant; was arrested near this place on the 13th,
and am now held here as a prisoner of war.
Respectfully, your obedient servant,
JOHN L. WORDEN,
Lieutenant.
Hon. GIDEON WELLES,
Secretary of the Navy, Washington, D. C.
Letter from Captain Vogdes, U. S. Army, commanding Fort Pickens, to Captain Adams, U. 8.
Navy, senior officer present off Pensacola, requesting additional force of laborers.
ForT PICKENS, FLA., April 16, 1861.
SIR: Will you please let me have 100 seamen, as there are evidences
of great activity on the opposite shore which should be met on our
side. I do not anticipate an assault, but I want more laborers to put
the fort in a proper state of defense. If you have any bags please let
me have them. If you have any means of cooking that can be trans-
ported, please send them with the men. But for the fact that I have
no means of cooking for more men and very poor accommodations for
them I should ask for 300 seamen. If the Atlantic has troops for this
place and sand bags, it will obviate the necessity of your sending
seamen and bread bags from your fleet.
Very respectfully, your obedient servant,
I. VOGDES,
Captain, First Artillery.
Captain H. A. ADAMS,
Commanding Naval Forces off Pensacola.
P. S.—I hope, however, if there are any troops on board the Atlantic
for this place, that you will make every effort to land them to-night.

U. S. FRIGATE SABINE,
Off Pensacola, April 18, 1861.
SIR: I have the honor to inform you of the arrival here of the steamer
Atlantic, on the 16th instant, with Colonel H. Brown, U. S. Army, and
a number of troops and military stores on board. At the request of
Colonel Brown the soldiers were immediately landed by the boats of
the squadron, which are now employed getting the stores, etc., on shore.

it important. The Sabine and St. Louis are getting short of water.
