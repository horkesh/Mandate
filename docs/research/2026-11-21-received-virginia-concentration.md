## Implementation choices proposed to the lead [I]

- Use 26 April as the earliest stamp for a ministry report of the convention,
  conditioned on Virginia's secession cause. Its department-side proposal is
  idempotent: Virginia has already changed side through its own existing act.
  It supplies a Confederate receipt, without deriving a side from control,
  a call answer or an appointment.
- Carry the two command papers as ministry summaries through the existing
  report routes. The author is the serving secretary's post, not a newly
  invented Cooper post or fabricated personal speech. The Manassas summary
  also waits for the game's named commander to hold its command; a delayed
  appointment delays this summary instead of asserting an arrival that did
  not happen. Source dates are earliest stamps, never forced receipt dates.
- Retain the existing 6 June transfer paper and its ordinary route. No men,
  regiments, arrival, march, July junction or battle are added by these notices.
- Preserve received institutional acts through pruning: bounded latest
  appointment and vacancy evidence per post, service choice per person and
  official notice per subject. Keep original stamps and source identities;
  derive the seat's picture on read. Ordinary dispatches still expire.

Rejected: taking Virginia's side from the live department table, equating
territorial control with government allegiance, treating an appointment reply
as a government-side announcement, inventing historical receipt stamps,
opening a post from a dated notice, or importing the source letter's strength
as current simulation truth. Nothing here signs a gated item or certifies the
model's military realism.
