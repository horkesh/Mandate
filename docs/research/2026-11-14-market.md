# Research: market for a politics-and-people strategy game on the American Civil War (niche sales, reach beyond the niche, wishlists, AI characters, price)

Date: 2026-11-14. Status: research note (not canon). Input to the go or no-go in decision 0037.

**Conventions.**
- Citation keys in [brackets] resolve in the Sources list at the end.
- **(snippet)** = seen only in a search-result snippet; the page itself was not read.
- **[I]** = our interpretation. **[gk]** = general knowledge, unverified.
- **Every sales figure here is an estimate unless it says "stated by the developer or publisher".** Where I give a range, it is the spread between the methods named beside it, not a confidence interval.

**Provenance and method.**
- The lead's brief was answered by me directly (searches) and by four read-only helper sessions that fetched pages. Their fetch tool summarises pages through a small model, so every number is a relayed reading, not a raw copy. Re-check against the live page before quoting any figure outside the repo.
- **Review-multiplier ("Boxleiter") method.** Estimated copies = Steam reviews x a multiplier. Published working ranges are about **20x to 60x**; Steam Page Analyzer recommends about 30x for recent releases and warns the multiplier is "essentially a guess", that cheaper games collect more sales per review, and that bundles, deep discounts and review bombing distort it [BOX-SPA]. Another site cites GameDiscoverCo's "NB number" averaging about 63 in 2025 (third-party claim, unconfirmed) [BOX-PROF]. Sources disagree on who originated the method [BOX-SPA], [BOX-PROF]. I apply 20x to 60x to the **all-language** review counts that trackers report, because Steam's store page shows English reviews only (stated on the store page [STEAM-CK3]), and then compare against tracker estimates. Where the tracker and the multiplier disagree I say so.
- **Trackers.** steamdata.ai publishes no method on its pages (a snippet says it uses review volumes, player counts, pricing history and regional signals) [SD]. Datahumble says "modeled from Steam, Twitch and player-review signals" [DH]. Gamalytic blends review multiples, concurrent players, sales rank and profile polling and claims 76.9% of games land within 30% error on a ~120-game test set [GAMALYTIC]. SteamSpy gives bucketed owner ranges that include free copies and giveaways, and currently shows a banner that estimates from 7 June 2025 to 9 November 2026 "may repeat older values" [SS]. Sensor Tower/VG Insights figures came from snippets only. The trackers disagree by up to 3x on the same game (Grand Tactician, below), and the studio-stated Suzerain figure is about 2.5x steamdata.ai's.
- **Not reachable:** steamdb.info (403), Gamalytic's API (403), gamesensor.info (403), HowLongToBeat (blocked), Business Wire (403). Playtime below is SteamSpy's average/median, which is a Steam-account average, not time to beat.
- **No developer-stated sales figure exists** for Grand Tactician, either Ultimate General game, Civil War II, Strategic Command: ACW or Victory and Glory in anything the helpers found. Every number for the niche is third-party.

### 1.2 Reading [I]
- **Two tiers.** The Civil War titles that reach **100k+ are the tactical, battle-focused ones** (Ultimate General). The campaign and grand-strategy titles sit at **roughly 1.5k to 70k units** at $30 to $45 over years. Grand Tactician, the best of them and the one closest to our scope, is probably in the **tens of thousands, not hundreds of thousands**, after five years and a long Early Access.
- **A Civil War strategy title without a tactical hook** (Strategic Command: ACW, Civil War II, Victory and Glory) lands around **2k to 18k**.
- **The president-as-player premise did not sell the 2024 re-release** of No Greater Glory, but that is a $10, DOSBox-wrapped 1991 game with no visible marketing (11 reviews). It shows the premise alone is not a draw, and says little about a new game [I].
- **The niche has a ceiling near 20k-70k units for a well-reviewed, feature-rich title.** If *By Order of the President* sold only to Civil War strategy players it would plausibly land between Civil War II and Grand Tactician: **~5k to 50k units, or about $0.1m to $1.2m gross at $25 over several years** [I, from the table].

### 2.2 How far they reached [I]
- **Two orders of magnitude separate the groups.** The Paradox games and the survival-morality games (Frostpunk, This War of Mine, Papers, Please) sold in the **millions**. The small political and character-driven sims sold in the **hundreds of thousands (Suzerain, Democracy 3/4) or tens of thousands (Through the Darkest of Times, Cultist Simulator in its first year)**. A setting-bound historical title with a political hook is closest to the **second group**.
- **Suzerain is the best single precedent for our hook**: a political-decision game with a small team and no war chest [SUZ-AV], priced at $19.99, with a **12-hour median playtime** and a **93% rating**. It sold roughly **200k to 500k Steam copies in about six years**, reaching 500k only after sales, Humble Choice, Prime Gaming and a Switch port [TORPOR].
- **Frostpunk and This War of Mine sold on a moral-pressure hook, not a genre.** That is evidence that "you carry the decision and the cost" travels beyond a niche, but both are survival games with strong art, a funded publisher or studio, and lots of marketing, which this project does not have [I]. 11 bit says Frostpunk's PLN 12m development and marketing budget was recouped in under three days (snippet) [FP-GWO].
- **The playtime pattern matters.** Suzerain (12h median) and Democracy 4 (8.5h median) are short for the money; Paradox games are long (30-46h median). A 15-25 hour campaign sits **between** the two groups.

### 2.3 What drew players beyond the core audience
Developer and press statements, not controlled evidence:
- **Suzerain:** the founder frames it as an "empathy machine" and says players act on "gut feeling or their roleplay"; the team was "small" with no war chest and ran out of funds, which forced the release date [SUZ-AV]. Studio timeline credits Humble Choice (Apr 2022) with reaching "thousands of new users", Prime Gaming, the Switch port, awards (Games for Change, German Game Awards), a Financial Times feature (Apr 2024) and Steam front-page placement (Dec 2024) [TORPOR].
- **Papers, Please:** savings-funded; a public demo played by YouTube streamers cleared Steam Greenlight within days (snippet) [PP-WP].
- **Reigns:** the "Tinder meets Game of Thrones" pitch and shared-screen play that suited YouTube and Twitch [REIGNS].
- **Cultist Simulator:** its author lists luck, community, a Kickstarter, Humble support and streamer-friendliness [CULT-WF].
- **Pattern [I]:** a one-line pitch that needs no genre knowledge, a mechanic that is easy to watch or retell, and **distribution and media help** (bundles, subscriptions, features, awards), not paid ads. No game here reached beyond its niche through the *setting*.
- **Not found:** any Zukowski, GameDiscoverCo or Game Developer analysis of a politics-or-narrative-strategy crossover audience. Zukowski's general advice (snippet) is that linear narrative indies are a hard sell outside horror and visual novels [ZUK-10STEP].

### 3.2 What counts as a promising signal
- **Zukowski (updated June 2026) [ZUK-HOW]:** the old rule of 5,000-7,000 wishlists at launch becomes **about 7,000**, which is roughly where a game reaches Steam's Popular Upcoming list. "If you have 3,000 after six months of effort, release and move on." Popular Upcoming (one surveyed day): 30th percentile 8,470, median 13,270, 70th percentile 20,000. His tiers at launch: **Bronze 5,000, Silver 8,000, Gold 50,000, Diamond 90,000** [ZUK-BENCH]. Weekly velocity by tier: Bronze 0-40, Silver 15-120, Gold 100-700, Diamond 300-3,000 per week [ZUK-BENCH]. Festivals give about 1,000-3,000 each.
- **GDCo 2021 [GDCO-2021]:** organic daily wishlists predict launch week: **under 10 a day gave under 3,000 first-week units**, 100-199 a day gave 15,000-60,000.
- **Not found:** a published 20,000 or 50,000 "alarm" line from GDCo; those numbers appear only as Zukowski's tier ranges.

## 4. AI characters as a selling point

### 4.1 Steam's rules
- Valve's content survey page [STEAM-AI]: **pre-generated** AI content ("ships with your game and is consumed by players") and **live-generated** content ("created with the help of AI tools while the game is running") are both disclosed. Live-generated content additionally requires you to "tell us what kind of guardrails you're putting on your AI". Live-generated Adult Only Sexual Content is not allowed. Content must be consistent with marketing materials.
- **January 2026 clarification** (reported 17 Jan 2026): the section concerns AI content "consumed by players", including "artwork, sound, narrative, localization", not efficiency tools behind the scenes [GOL-JAN26]. The form has separate pre-generated and live-generated tick boxes. A player-facing overlay button for reporting illegal live-generated content was reported in a snippet and not confirmed on any page.

### 4.2 Did AI disclosure hurt sales? [mixed, partly snippet-level]
- **Share disclosing:** about **1 in 5 of 2025 releases** (Steam Labs' former head, Ichiro Lambe, 7,818 titles, 7% of the library, July 2025) [TOMS-LAMBE]; 21% (Game Oracle) [GAMESGG-AI]; roughly one in three new games by mid-2026 (a census of 53,597 releases) [FRAGWYZ].
- **Game Oracle study, 2026 (snippet-level):** 9,879 Steam releases Jan-Oct 2025; disclosed games had **about 53% fewer reviews** after controlling for publisher, developer experience and genre. Reviews are a proxy for sales, the study is correlational, and the label spans concept art to code to translation [NBC-STUDY], [PCG-STUDY]. The census [FRAGWYZ] agrees in order of magnitude (AI-flagged games reach 100 reviews at about 55% of the non-AI rate) and uses the same multiplier method, so the two are not independent confirmation of sales.
- **Backlash cases tie to a studio's own AI move or an undisclosed use**, not to the label alone: *The Alters* (11 bit) after undisclosed AI-written placeholder and translations [ENG-ALTERS]; *Party Animals* after a generative-AI contest sent reviews from Very Positive to Mostly Negative in under 24 hours (May 2026) [NBC-PARTY]; *Big Walk* after House House said chatbot output was a starting point for under 0.1% of its code, with one day's reviews falling from about 98% to about 54% positive (Nov 2026) [DEXERTO-BW]. One snippet says no cited report documents a measurable review-bomb caused by the label alone.

### 4.4 Player and industry surveys
- **Quantic Foundry (fieldwork Oct-Dec 2025, N = 1,799, 74% core gamers):** 85% negative on generative AI in games, 63% the most negative option; negativity about AI-generated quests and dialogue rose from 46% (2024) to 77% and 83% (the article does not say which figure belongs to which). Self-selected [QF-GM].
- **GameDiscoverCo Steam player survey (N about 3,800, reported 8 Jul 2026):** "no problem" 23.4%, "fine" 19.6%, neutral 25.6%, "not super keen" 23.3%, would not consider playing any game using AI **8.1%**. Headline: 31% have a problem, 43% fine. 44.4% check disclosure labels in detail, 44.7% glance, 10.9% ignore them [GAMESGG-SURVEY], [GAMESRADAR-SURVEY].
- **The two conflict in tone** (hardcore creative-focused vs broad Steam audience). Neither isolates NPC dialogue [I].
- **Developers (GDC 2026, N about 2,300, snippet):** 52% say generative AI is harming the industry (30% the year before); narrative and design staff 63% negative [TECHSPOT-GDC]. The Big Walk case also reports 19 IGF jurors saying they would not vote for games using generative AI [DEXERTO-BW].

### 4.5 Reading [I]
- **AI characters are polarising, not a safe mass-market hook.** Where AI was the hook (Vaudeville, Suck Up!, Whispers from the Star) it **drew attention and split the ratings**; where it was an add-on (Where Winds Meet, inZOI) it was a talking point, not a reason to buy.
- **The penalty in the data attaches to the label and to undisclosed use**, more than to optional features kept out of marketing. No study isolates optional, authored-fallback AI text. Evidence of players' reaction to "optional", "local" or "bring your own key" framing is thin and anecdotal.
- **Disclosure is likely required** if AI-written letters ship, whether pre-generated or live, per Valve's page; The Alters shows hiding it is worse than the label.
- **What survives from decision 0022** (AI text is voice, never state; every line has a written fallback): it limits harm, because the base game is authored. It does not remove the Steam label if the feature ships.

| Paradox grand strategy | $49.99 | 30-46h |

- **A 15-25 hour campaign game in this space sells for $20-$30 [I].** Suzerain's $19.99 for a 12h median, Democracy 4's $26.99 for about 8.5h, and Frostpunk's $29.99 for 8-24h set the band; the Civil War strategy titles that charge $40-$45 are 75-hour games with deep simulation. GDCo's median first-week ratio is **0.10x for games priced over $10** (vs 0.15x overall) [GDCO-STATE], so higher price lowers conversion; a secondary summary of GDCo says conversion is lowest at $15-$30 and highest at $1-$10 [GDCO-MONTH1] (filtered sample, caution).
- **The brief's full campaign is 15-25 hours.** The median Suzerain player plays 12h; the same ratio would put the median of our players near 8-12h of a 15-25h campaign [I]. Price on the campaign's length, but set expectations by median play.
- **Net per copy:** at $24.99 and 30% off Steam's cut [gk], about $17 per copy before tax and refunds.

## 6. Recommendation

**1. Realistic audience.**
- **Core:** Civil War strategy and wargame players, a market of **roughly 5k to 50k copies** at $25-40 over several years (section 1). It is not enough on its own to support a funded production [I].
- **Reach:** players who liked Suzerain, Democracy 4, Through the Darkest of Times, Papers, Please and Crusader Kings' court intrigue, and Civil War and American-history readers who do not buy wargames. The best precedent (Suzerain) sold **200k-500k Steam copies in six years**, with outside help. Plausible targets for a small studio with a proven hook: **20k-100k units, or $0.3m-$1.7m gross at $25** [I, from sections 1-2]. Anything above that needs a hit, which is luck and distribution, not planning.

**2. Positioning.**
- **Lead with the constraint and the people, not the war.** The one-line pitch is "You are Lincoln. You can't move a single division. Your generals can lie to you, and one of them may run against you in 1864." It needs no wargame knowledge, is easy to retell and to watch, and has the "constrained role" shape of Papers, Please and Suzerain's "empathy machine" [I].
- **Tag and describe it as political strategy, narrative, historical, management, not as a wargame.** The capsule art and trailer should show a desk, letters and faces before arrows; keep the war map secondary [I].
- **Keep the war honest and the story ahead of the map**: the Civil War gives American and history audiences a recognisable setting, and the slavery framing (0021) is part of the pitch, not a risk to hide.
- **Do not make AI a selling point.** Do not mention it on the store page headline, in the trailer or in the demo. If AI-voiced letters ship later, make them **optional, disclosed on Steam, and secondary to the authored letters** (0022 already guarantees a fallback for every line). Section 4 shows AI as a hook splits ratings (Vaudeville 47%, Suck Up! 62%) and the label correlates with **about half the reviews** in the data we have (a correlation, not proof).
- **Distribution is part of the product.** Suzerain's growth owed more to Humble Choice, Prime Gaming, awards, press features and sales than to ads [TORPOR]; Papers, Please and Reigns relied on streamers. Plan a demo that streamers can play in 20 minutes, a press kit and bundle outreach, which cost time, not money [I].

**3. Wishlist threshold for go or no-go.** The numbers below combine Zukowski's 7,000 floor and "3,000 after six months" release-and-move-on line [ZUK-HOW], our 0.4-0.7 first-year conversion [INVEN-INFL] and GDCo's 10-a-day line [GDCO-2021]. They are my thresholds [I], not published standards.

| Wishlists (organic, Steam page live 6+ months, demo public) | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Under 3,000** | The hook has not travelled. About 1,000-2,000 first-year units | **No-go for art or marketing spend.** Rework the hook or finish as a small, self-funded release |
| **3,000-7,000** | Below the Popular Upcoming line; roughly 1,500-4,500 first-year units | **Hold.** No new spend; change the pitch, the demo or the capsule and re-measure |
| **7,000-20,000** | Reaches Popular Upcoming; roughly 3,000-14,000 first-year units | **Go for modest spend**: key art and capsule, hired UI polish, a trailer, a Next Fest slot |
| **Over 20,000** | Zukowski's median Popular Upcoming game; roughly 8,000-28,000+ first-year units | **Go for fuller production**; consider a publisher |

Add two **hook-test signals** that matter more than the raw count:
- **Demo conversion:** Zukowski's median is 16.3% (played and wishlisted / players); **20% or more (70th percentile) says the hook works** [ZUK-NF26]. Below 12% (30th percentile) says it does not.
- **Organic daily adds:** **under 10 a day sustained is a no-go signal** [GDCO-2021]; 20-40 a day over the months before launch is a go signal for a niche title [I].
- **Next Fest alone should not trigger go:** it adds a median of about 1,000 wishlists for a game starting at 1,000-9,999, which convert at half the organic rate [ZUK-NF26], [GDCO-QUALITY]. Count organic wishlists separately from festival ones.
- **Re-measure before spending.** The conversion data is old and the market moves: Zukowski's study says a wishlist is worth about half of what it was in 2020-22.

**4. Price.** Launch at **$24.99** for a 15-25 hour campaign; $19.99 is the floor and $29.99 the ceiling. The $40+ band belongs to 75-hour simulation games [I].

## Findings outside my scope

- **The games survey's market-signal table and decision 0037 use English-only store review counts.** The survey cites Ultimate General: Civil War at 5,662 reviews and Grand Tactician at 2,037; steamdata.ai's all-language counts are 8,550 and 2,420. The conclusion (niche) stands, but the "proxy" in 0037's context understates it by up to a third, and the survey did not apply a multiplier. Nothing edited; flagged for the steward.
- **AI disclosure is a design constraint, not only a marketing choice.** If AI-written letters (0022) ship, Steam's form requires disclosure of pre-generated or live-generated content, and live generation requires stated guardrails. Both the design (a live LLM call vs bundled text) and the privacy and cost model (Vaudeville's Inworld bills arrived before Steam payouts) should be settled before the Steam page goes up.
- **The demo's timing is a design input.** A demo released well before a Next Fest earned about 2.5x more wishlists (no sample size) [ZUK-NF26]. Decision 0037 puts the demo at "Phase 1 console loop at the earliest, Phase 2 desk more likely". Since the hook is people and letters, a console-only demo may under-test it [I]; the earlier the page and demo go up, the longer the wishlist clock runs before any spend decision.
- **Game World Observer mislabels GDCo's 1.9% as a 90th percentile** [GWO-DEC24]; use GDCo's own text if the figure is quoted.
- **The Frostpunk 2 milestone date on WNHub reads "09.07.2026"**, which could be 9 July or 7 September; not needed for any conclusion.
- **Developer-community risk:** 52% of surveyed developers and 63% of narrative and design staff say generative AI is harming the industry (GDC 2026, snippet) and 19 IGF jurors said they would not vote for games using it. If awards and peer press are part of the distribution plan, any AI feature costs goodwill there.
