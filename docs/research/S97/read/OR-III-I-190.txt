EXECUTIVE  CHAMBER,
Frederick  City,  Md.,  May  13,  1861.
Hon.  SIMON  CAMERON:
DEAR  SIR:  I  herewith  transmit  a  copy  of  a  letter  from  Brigadier-
General  Stites,  tendering  his  brigade  for  special  service.  So  soon  as  I
can  get  the  necessary  papers  ready  I  shall  call  out  the  four  regiments
for  which  the  President  has  made  a  requisition  upon  me.  They  will  be
composed  of  volunteers.
Very  respectfully,  your  obedient  servant,
THO.  H.  HICKS.
[Inclosure.]
HDQRS.  FIRST  BRIG.,  SECOND  Div.,  MARYLAND  MILITIA,
Elkton,  May  10,  1H61.
His  Excellency  THOMAS  IT.  HICKS:
DEAR  SIR:  Your  proclamation  of  the  Oth  instant  reached  me  this
morning,  and  as  it  was  not  my  purpose  to  issue  a  brigade  order  in
obedience  to  General  Tilghmaii's  orders,  it  was  quite  gratifying  to  know
that  I  was  not  liable  to  censure.
I  entirely  approve  of  Your  Excellency's  course  in  relation  to  the  posi
tion  our  State  should  occupy  at  the  present  juncture.  Secession  would
be  ruin  and  desolation  to  two  thirds  of  our  dear  old  State,  and  I  oiler
you  my  most  cordial  congratulations  on  the  position  you  occupy.  The
opinions  of  all  our  correct-minded  citizens  will  indorse  you,  and  Cecil
County  will  sustain  you  fully.
If  it  should  meet  your  approbation,  I  would  request  as  a  favor  that
you  offer  to  the  Government  of  the  United  States  the  volunteer  force
of  my  brigade — say  500  men,  or  1,000  if  necessary — to  guard  the  Phila
delphia,  Wilmington  and  Baltimore  liailroad  from  the  Delaware  line
through  Cecil  and  Harford  counties;  and  it'  the  offer  is  accepted  I  can
muster  the  men  into  service  in  three  days  after  receiving  the  order.
The  German  troops  who  are  at  present  stationed  here  could  be  with
drawn,  and  I  will  pledge  my  own  individual  honor  and  the  honor  of
the  whole  brigade  for  the  fidelity  of  the  guard.
I  have  this  moment  had  a  consultation  with  Colonel  Howard  and  other
officers  of  the  brigade,  who  most  cordially  approve  of  my  making  the
offer,  and  are  ready  at  a  moment's  notice.
Believe  me,  sincerely,
H.  S.  STITES,
Brigadier-  Genera].
I  should  be  glad  to  know  the  result  of  the  offer  as  early  as  may  suit
your  convenience.
WASHINGTON,  May  13,  1861.
Hon.  SIMON  CAMERON,
Secretary  of  War:
SIR  :  In  the  new  requisition  upon  the  States  for  troops  will  companies
of  cavalry  be  received'?  If  so,  I  am  authorized  to  tender  at  least  one
company,  who,  under  the  care  of  Capt.  James  Starkey,  have  already  a
partial  organization  and  have  requested  me  to  tender  their  services  to
the  President.  Other  companies  are  organizing  for  the  same  purpose.
Very  respectfully,  your  obedient  servant,
ALEX.  EAMSEY,
Governor  of  Minnesota.
