# S21: the 203 S14 unknown strengths

20-21 November 2026. **Research drafts, not applied data or acceptance.** The [row register](2026-11-20-S21-strength-dispositions.csv) joins to the original S14 IDs. The [handover](../../notes/sprint-S21-unknown-strengths.md) records verification and remaining work. Scans, extracts, numerical-table images and working scripts stay outside Git under `C:/Pyrrhic/mandate-research-sources/S4/S21/`; original S4/S14 scans are reused in place.

All **203 original unknown IDs** have a disposition: **40 quantified observations / 163 still unknown**. The 40 comprise **19 reported observations, 13 estimates, four source reconstructions and four upper bounds**. The original 213 numerical observations are preserved, giving 253 numerical entries among the same 416 force observations in 62 snapshots. Quantified does not mean an exact, full all-arms battle-day duty return.

## Reading and interpretation

Each register row names the passages searched, its result and a next source. A search is bounded evidence, not proof that a return does not exist. Keyword hits are finding aids; only read passages supply facts. No whole-volume reading or exhaustive muster-roll census is claimed. Citations are printed pages. Source images control numerical table columns where OCR loses their alignment.

Numbers retain their date and measure. Duty strength, duty equipped, aggregate present, infantry alone, marching men, reported men in action, a source's estimate and a source's reconstruction remain different observations. Rounded statements are not promoted to exact returns. Less than and not over are different bounds, identified explicitly in the measure and note. An interval figure has no invented exact return day.

A nearby return is related evidence, not a battle-day replacement. No parent is apportioned, no casualty ratio is inverted and no regiment count is multiplied. Livermore's own estimates remain attributed reconstructions, including where he uses regiment averages; no new estimate is made from those averages. Source arithmetic is marked **[I]**. Inclusive parents, children and successive snapshots remain non-additive. Actual stores remain unknown.

## Eastern results

The certified April 30 abstract supplies Yorktown army and corps duty counts (OR I-XI.3 p.130; PDF136). April 13 is a separate observation (p.97; PDF103). The differently scoped April total in McClellan's general report (OR I-V p.13) is preserved as a discrepancy in coverage, not averaged with either abstract. The read corps abstracts do not allocate strength to each division.

McDowell's August 16 return separates present for duty, duty equipped and aggregate present (OR I-XII.3 p.580; PDF586). King's duty count is **[I] 541 + 9,804 = 10,345**, compared with 10,066 equipped. Ricketts is **[I] 482 + 9,652 = 10,134**. Porter August 21 supplies Morell **[I] 312 + 6,159 = 6,471** duty (p.619; PDF625); the rightmost entries for Sykes and Reynolds are not duty strengths. Kearny's 7,000 marching men (p.614) and Pope's rounded August 20/September 1 corps claims (p.603; OR I-XII.2 pp.16,42,44) keep their different dates and categories.

At Gaines Mill, Slocum reports 8,000 entering the field (OR I-XI.2 p.433). McCall's June 30 less-than-6,000 and D. H. Hill's campaign little-less-than-10,000 remain exclusive bounds (pp.391,629). Magruder's about 13,000 covers his three divisions together (pp.660-661); A. P. Hill's about 14,000 includes his brigades and batteries (pp.834-835). These are attributed reports, not certified daily returns. Livermore's Seven Days totals are reconstructions (pp.84-86). His combined Manassas/Chantilly count (pp.88-89) covers August 27-September 2, wider than the Confederate roster window; it is kept as related evidence with that row still unknown.

Greene reports **2,504 aggregate present** at Antietam, versus 1,727 for two brigades after detachment (OR I-XIX.1 pp.504-505). D. R. Jones reports 2,430 men in six brigades on September 17 (pp.885-886); J. R. Jones gives an upper bound of 1,600 (pp.1007-1008). D. H. Hill's 3,000 is infantry only; no artillery personnel are inferred from his guns (p.1022). Humphreys' about 6,000 is a September 14 marching observation (p.338), not men fighting at Antietam. McLaws' engaged table is incomplete: Cobb has blank engaged cells (pp.861-862). Its printed component sum cannot stand for the full division. The September 20 corps statement (OR I-XIX.2 p.336) is not backdated into the September 14-17 roster.

## Western results

Reed's official Shiloh commission compilation gives McCook 7,552 and Crittenden 3,825, explicitly estimated from adjacent-date returns and comparison with Nelson (pp.99-102,111). Reed p.100 prints McCook 7,553, while the p.102 recap and the Buell quotation on p.111 give 7,552; the draft selects the latter explicitly and preserves the variant. Nelson's own report gives 4,541 entering action (OR I-X.1 pp.325-326). Reed includes only Wagner's brigade, estimated 2,000, for Wood; the full Sixth Division remains unknown. Reed's commander-engaged column gives Clark 6,036, Cheatham 4,576, Ruggles 6,484 and Withers 6,482 for the two-day battle (pp.103-106). The earlier duty returns and the compiler's category warnings remain separate (OR I-X.2 pp.377-378,382; Reed pp.111-112). None of these figures is presented as a newly discovered battle-morning duty return.

Carr's Pea Ridge report supplies **[I] 1,790 infantry + 469 cavalry + 204 artillery personnel = 2,463** brought onto the ground (OR I-VIII p.261). Asboth says the First and Second division commands were combined and exchanged during the fighting (p.240); the read passages supply no independent split. No portion of the army total is allocated to them.

Davies' Corinth count is **[I] 2,283 infantry + 233 artillery = 2,516 officers and men** for October 4 (OR I-XVII.1 p.258). His October 3 narrative contains an unresolved 10-man infantry discrepancy: brigade components total 2,934, while its printed infantry summary says 2,924 (p.251). McKean's enlisted component figures and Maury's September 30 departure figures remain partial/nearby evidence (pp.336,393); they do not establish full October division strengths. September 30 and November 10 Grant returns remain at district/wing level (OR I-XVII.2 pp.246-249,337-340).

At Perryville, Rousseau's about 7,000 is a rounded entered-action statement (OR I-XVI.1 p.1049). Jackson's 5,577 is expressly enlisted men marching from Mackville on October 8, including battery personnel but excluding the detached 101st Indiana and without officers added (p.1059). The nominal October 1 return has the compiler's explicit dating/organisation warning (OR I-XVI.2 pp.562-564); it is not an October 8 substitute. Hardee's two-division bound and Bragg's army estimate do not establish Cheatham's or Anderson's share (OR I-XVI.1 pp.1092,1120).

The approximate November 20 East Tennessee return permits a First Division brigade duty sum of **[I] 6,966** and a Third Division sum of **[I] 7,097**, the latter explicitly including an estimated 650 for Smith's Legion without an invented officer increment (OR I-XX.2 pp.412-414). The Second Division stays unknown as a full command: its three named brigades sum to **[I] 5,391**, while an estimated First Cavalry Brigade 1,500 follows it in the table but is separately listed after the divisions in the roster. The register retains that attachment ambiguity instead of silently choosing 5,391 or 6,891. Bragg explicitly calls his army return partial and says Smith's corps is en route (p.423); the department is not that corps. November 29 returns cover selected divisions and reserve artillery, not all Polk corps (pp.430-432).

## Coastal and naval results

Branch says he had never been able to put 4,000 men in the field at New Berne and was further weakened by re-enlistment furloughs at the battle (OR I-IX pp.241-242). The draft records **less than 4,000**, an exclusive field-men upper bound on March 14, without a numerical lower bound or invented duty category. Burnside's narrative and Foster's staged Roanoke landing do not allocate a full brigade strength: Foster first landed 1,400 and then returned for more; part of his brigade was still aground (p.86). The brigade totals remain unknown.

Hatteras demonstrates why a nearby quantity cannot simply fill a blank. Martin reports about 350 at his post on August 27, before the draft's August 28-29 window, then lists reinforcements with incomplete officer coverage. Reports after surrender give about 580, 550 and 615 prisoners with different provenance (ONR I-6 pp.119,138,140). These are retained as related evidence; no prisoner number is equated with the starting garrison. Stringham's squadron list gives vessels, not crews (pp.120-121). Ammen likewise describes the Port Royal naval landing party without a headcount in the read passage (p.27).

Buchanan's report names the Hampton Roads vessels, guns and officer/volunteer roles but no complete actual Virginia or squadron crew total in the read pages (ONR I-7 pp.44-48). Rodgers's requisition for three gunboats gives united crews of 198 **on June 7**, not on the August 12 or September 6 roster dates (ONR I-22 p.282). Foote's January manning-shortage statement is also distinct from the February 6 battle reports (pp.515,537-539). None becomes a later fleet strength.

At Memphis, Davis lists five naval gunboats and treats Ellet's rams separately. His report supplies no total crew strength (ONR I-23 pp.119-120). Ellet reports partial Confederate crews, but leaves Queen's own crew number blank; General Lovell 86 and General Price 81 cannot establish all eight enemy vessels (p.135). Mound City's reported 175 officers and men remains the unchanged S14 observation for that one ship, not Kilty's entire flotilla (p.171). Fitch's report identifies his regiment and the later replacement crew but no June 17 land headcount (pp.173-174); no regiment-size estimate fills it.

Porter's March 18 report counts mortar vessels crossing the bar, not men (ONR I-18 p.71). Farragut's May 31 list identifies ships and gunboat divisions, while his passage report records operations and losses without a complete April 24 personnel return (pp.151-153). The first division of ships and Bailey's first division of gunboats are different scopes. September instructions to set complements and report necessary ratings are authorizations/requests, not an October 1 actual muster (ONR I-23 pp.350-351,355).

## State reports and muster evidence

The Massachusetts Adjutant-General's report for the year ending December 31, 1862 (published 1863), prints Kimball's regimental narrative. For Antietam it records 24 officers and 582 noncommissioned officers/privates entering the fight in the Fifteenth Massachusetts (p.176; PDF178). This is one regiment under Sedgwick, not the division's strength; no regiment-count or average-strength estimate is made. Its campaign passages also preserve service and detachment context (pp.172-177).

Fox explains the company muster-out rolls, their dates/categories, publication in state reports and manuscript holdings, then gives sample entries (pp.57,59). His p.34 warns that morning-report duty and men taken into action differ. Livermore discusses retrospective muster-roll casualty abstracts and their limits (pp.5-6), and statewide lifetime roster totals (pp.34-38). These passages do not supply a division's dated battle strength. This run does not claim to have examined every individual muster roll, original manuscript or state series; those remain named research paths. Missing men are not reconstructed by counting lifetime names and subtracting known deaths.

## Proposed rulings

1. **[I] Preserve dated source measures in the drafts.** Reject replacing an unknown battle-day duty count with an earlier/later return without disclosing the date, or treating a marching count as a duty return.
2. **[I] Keep bounds and reconstructions visible.** Reject treating an upper bound as equality, or calling a later compiler's estimate a contemporary field return. A quantified limited measure is not necessarily a complete all-arms strength.
3. **[I] Preserve the full 203-row research obligation.** Reject deleting unresolved formations, assigning zero to blank cells, allocating parent totals or counting incomplete brigade tables as whole divisions. Whole-branch and gate review remain with Claude under 0106.
