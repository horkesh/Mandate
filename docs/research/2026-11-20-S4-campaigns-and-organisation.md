# Campaigns and command organisation, July 1861–November 1862

S4 research, 20 November 2026. Draft for review under 0106. Read this with the [strength and loss register](2026-11-20-S4-military-strengths-and-losses.md) and [source register](2026-11-20-S4-sources.md). All page numbers are printed pages. OR = *Official Records of the Union and Confederate Armies*, series I unless otherwise specified. L = Livermore's 1900 *Numbers and Losses*; D = Dyer's 1908 *Compendium*. This note supplies evidence and labelled proposals, not accepted mechanics, personality scores, situations or gate clearance.

## The changing organisation is part of the evidence

**[I]** Use a stable person's identity and dated command edges, not one timeless army hierarchy. A title in a retrospective report's heading may be the writer's later rank. A compiled order of battle can cover several days and list successive holders. None of these conventions establishes that all listed troops were present together or fought simultaneously.

The U casualty table marks Runyon's division as in reserve on the Potomac and Richardson's brigade guarding Blackburn's Ford. The Confederate Potomac return of troops engaged gives 9,977, but its editorial note says other casualty-bearing organisations are not in that return. Rhett's 23 August list of Shenandoah units explicitly says he cannot furnish their strengths. **[I]** The table is evidence for organisation and omissions; it cannot supply a complete engaged-strength total by itself. [OR I-II pp. 327, 568–569.]

Beauregard attributes the delayed arrival of roughly 5,000 of Johnston's troops to inadequate railroad transportation. That is his explanation in a later report, not an independent observation of every delayed train. **[I]** A Manassas reference should test that troops were brought through their actual command and transport chain, with arrival times, rather than require a battle merely because its date was reached. [OR I-II p. 486.]

Dyer dates the Northeastern Virginia department from 28 May and its merger into the Potomac department to 17 August 1861. His ensuing tables distinguish the post-battle brigades from divisions organised on 3 October. **[I]** Do not carry July's five-division organisation unchanged into winter or manufacture July corps to match a later interface. [D pp. 271–275.]

### The Peninsula: two snapshots, not one

The Yorktown organisation is explicitly compiled for **5 April–4 May**. It places Sumner's II Corps over Sedgwick and Richardson; Heintzelman's III over Porter, Hooker and Hamilton/Kearny; Keyes's IV over Couch, W. F. Smith and Casey; Sykes's regular brigade, Cooke's cavalry reserve and Hunt's artillery reserve are separately listed. **[I]** This is an interval roster. The Hamilton/Kearny succession is not two divisions available together. [OR I-XI.1 pp. 279–283.]

At Fair Oaks / Seven Pines, the casualty organisation has II Corps under Sumner with Richardson and Sedgwick; III under Heintzelman with Hooker and Kearny; IV under Keyes with Couch and Casey. This is the dated organisation used for the strength register's 31 May–1 June comparison. [OR I-XI.1 pp. 757–761; L p. 81.]

Johnston's account describes intended converging movements by D. H. Hill, Longstreet, Huger and G. W. Smith. It says Johnston was wounded around sunset and Smith succeeded him. His report blames delay on Huger and praises other subordinates; the printed correspondence includes Huger's objection. **[I]** The existence and terms of the orders, the succession and the disagreement are evidence. Johnston's assignment of blame is not an independently established trait score or verdict on a subordinate. [OR I-XI.1 pp. 933–935.]

For the Seven Days, Lee describes a plan for Jackson, A. P. Hill, D. H. Hill and Longstreet to sweep down the north bank of the Chickahominy, while Huger and Magruder held the Richmond side and Stuart guarded the outer flank. Lee reports delayed arrival and movement by Jackson and that A. P. Hill crossed about 3 p.m. on 26 June. **[I]** Those are attributed descriptions of command execution, not proof that one scalar “initiative” explains the campaign. [OR I-XI.2 pp. 489–491.]

McClellan's compiled account prints messages between the corps on 27 June: Slocum sent to Porter, calls for further support, and Sumner and Franklin reporting constraints on what they could spare. It describes the movement of the trains and army across White Oak Swamp toward the James. **[I]** A delivered order, a division being released, its bridge crossing, and its entry into combat are distinct operational events. [OR I-XI.1 pp. 58–61.]

The later compiled Union organisation/casualty table covers **25 June–2 July**, rather than the Yorktown interval. It now puts Morell and Sykes under V Corps and Slocum and W. F. Smith under VI. Its IV Corps note extends that corps's loss window back to **2 June**. **[I]** Even neighbouring rows of the same compilation may require different casualty windows. [OR I-XI.2 pp. 24, 28, 30–36.]

[L pp. 88–89.]

### Corinth and Perryville

Corinth's revised casualty organisation places Stanley's Second and Hamilton's Third divisions in the Army of the Mississippi, and Davies's Second and McKean's Sixth in the Army of West Tennessee, all within Rosecrans's battle command. McArthur appears as a brigade successor within McKean's division. **[I]** Do not make McArthur an additional fifth division because Rosecrans thanks him alongside division commanders. [OR I-XVII.1 pp. 169, 172–176.]

At Perryville, Buell's account identifies McCook's I, Crittenden's II and Gilbert's III Corps. The casualty organisation identifies Rousseau's Third and J. S. Jackson's Tenth divisions in I; Schoepf's First, R. B. Mitchell's Ninth and Sheridan's Eleventh in III. It says the only II Corps organisations appearing as actually engaged are the Fortieth Indiana and Tenth Indiana Battery; Buell describes Wagner's brigade of Wood's division joining late. **[I]** A corps being on the march or near the field is not evidence that all of its divisions entered the action. [OR I-XVI.1 pp. 1026–1028, 1034–1036.]

Bragg's report says the C force at Perryville comprised three infantry divisions, about 14,500, plus two small cavalry brigades, about 1,500. It says Withers had been sent to Kirby Smith and was absent. Hardee's report identifies Buckner and Anderson, alongside Cheatham's force in the action. **[I]** Bragg's ~16,000 is a field-force statement with an explicit missing division, not the total of all Confederate troops in Kentucky. [OR I-XVI.1 pp. 1092–1093, 1120–1122.]

## October and November are not an empty interval

The OR chronology dates Stuart's Maryland/Pennsylvania expedition to **9–12 October**, Reynolds's assumption of I Corps to **29 September**, Couch's replacement of Sumner in II to **7 October**, Willcox's replacement of Cox in IX to **8 October**, and Slocum's assumption of XII to **20 October**. **[I]** An Antietam roster is already stale for much of the October interval. [OR I-XIX.1 p. 2.]

For November, distinguish the dated acts: the chronology says Longstreet and Jackson were assigned the **First and Second Corps on 6 November**, Burnside superseded McClellan on **7 November**, Hooker superseded Porter in V Corps on **10 November**, and three grand divisions under Sumner, Hooker and Franklin were organised on **14 November**. Burnside's **8 November** letter acknowledges the order and describes McClellan handing over; his own General Orders No. 1 is dated **9 November**. **[I]** An appointment date, receipt/transfer and published assumption order can differ without being contradictory. [OR I-XIX.1 p. 3; OR I-XIX.2 pp. 555, 557.]

Burnside's later report prints Halleck's **14 November** telegram approving his plan conditionally on speed. It dates Sumner's departure to **15 November**, Franklin's and Hooker's to the **16th**, Sumner's advance at Falmouth to the **17th**, and Burnside's own arrival to the **19th**. It also supplies a command-location snapshot at the hand-over: I, II and V near Warrenton; VI at New Baltimore; IX with Stoneman and Whipple near Waterloo; XI at Gainesville, New Baltimore and the gaps; Slocum still at Harpers Ferry/Fayetteville. **[I]** These are dated positions and movements, not a timeless army roster or a claim that every man of each corps arrived together. [OR I-XXI pp. 83–86.]

The same account prints Woodbury's **14 November** message: thirty-six pontoons arrived, forty expected, one train forecast ready on 16 or 17 November subject partly to the Quartermaster's Department. A **17 November** message says Spaulding has not yet left; Woodbury's **18 November** message specifies harness, teamsters and 270 new horses as the delay. Burnside says the land train actually left on the **19th** and the pontoons arrived on the **25th**. **[I]** Preserve forecast, dispatch, arrival and assembly as distinct states. A number of boats in Washington is not a bridge usable by an army at Falmouth. [OR I-XXI pp. 84–86.]

Burnside presents the delay as a reason for not sending a separated force across first and argues his decision was vindicated. That is his defence in a later report. Woodbury's **21 December 1863** letter says Burnside moved before he was ready and should have waited five days. **[I]** The November messages are evidence for the logistical sequence; these later opposing explanations are evidence of disagreement. Neither warrants an unqualified verdict on who caused the delay, a temperament score, or a promise that an earlier crossing necessarily wins the December battle. The December operations themselves remain outside this research window. [OR I-XXI pp. 85–86, 171–172.]

In the West, the chronology dates Grant's new Department of the Tennessee to **16 October**, his assumption of department/Thirteenth Corps command to **25 October**, and discontinuance of the U Army of the Mississippi to **26 October**. It treats the Mississippi Central Railroad operations as an interval beginning **31 October** and continuing beyond S4. Johnston's assignment over a broad western command is dated **24 November**, but his assumption is dated **4 December**, beyond this note's endpoint. **[I]** A November scenario must distinguish assigned jurisdiction from an officer already exercising it. [OR I-XVII.1 p. 4.]

Buell says he relinquished his army to Rosecrans on **30 October**. His description of the retreat from Kentucky identifies forage already consumed, difficult defiles and repaired rail communications as constraints. **[I]** Campaign persistence and pursuit need dated transport and supply evidence, not an assumption that every retreat leaves a pursuer free to advance indefinitely. Bragg's account presents his own supply and road explanation for leaving Kentucky; it is an opposing report to retain, not a reason to accept all his claims of tactical success. [OR I-XVI.1 pp. 1029, 1092–1093.]

## Proposed rulings and held cells

1. **[I] Retain organisational discontinuities.** July brigades/divisions, spring corps, September redesignations and November grand divisions require dated relationships. Rejected: one permanent corps map for the whole slice, or synthetic July corps without a new, explicit representation ruling.
2. **[I] Retain every source window.** A campaign return may support an operational total; it cannot supply a battle-day deployment without arrival and detachment evidence. Rejected: distributing campaign losses among battles in proportion to an invented combat score.
3. **[I] Attribute disputed reports.** Johnston on Huger, Buell on McCook, Jackson on Garnett and naval authors' comments are evidence of claims and relationships. Rejected: deriving personal scores from their accusations or adopting a post-war author's praise as a contemporary judgment.
4. **Held:** complete Union division-by-division Second Manassas arrival strength; a reconciled division-strength series for each Seven Days date; final paired returns for every smaller action; independent engineering/quartermaster reconciliation of the November bridge arrivals. These are research residuals, not zeros or permission to invent.
