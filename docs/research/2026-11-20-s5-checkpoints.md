# S5: candidate observations for 1B clocks and the economy

Research, 2026-11-20. These are proposed research inputs, not implemented acceptance tests, accepted bands or a calibration run. The topic notes explain definitions and uncertainty: [foreign powers](2026-11-20-s5-foreign-powers.md), [blockade and cotton](2026-11-20-s5-blockade-cotton.md), [finance and prices](2026-11-20-s5-war-finance.md), [industry and rail](2026-11-20-s5-industry-rail.md). Sources: [register and scans](2026-11-20-s5-sources.md). **[I]** marks every proposed comparison, rounding interval and causal interpretation.

## Use the same quantity and window on both sides of a comparison

[I] A source point cannot become a broad acceptance band merely by assigning a convenient percentage. Four types are kept apart here:

- A **reported range**, such as the wheat quotations, is a historical range in the source's stated market/window.
- A **reported point**, such as a fiscal total, retains the source's accounting perimeter. It needs an explicit model-output mapping before becoming a test.
- A **display-rounding interval** is an arithmetical tolerance around a rounded point. It is not an uncertainty estimate or permission to fit a run.
- An **unmeasured response** has no numerical acceptance criterion here. Narrative evidence of a mechanism cannot supply its coefficient.

[I] An alternative history should change outcomes when causes change. These observations are for a declared historical/default run with comparable policy and territorial circumstances; they are not outcomes every player must reproduce. Keep a truth-side calibration comparison separate from what Lincoln has received or can know.

### What a rounding interval can and cannot do

[I] If an implementation reports a monthly gold price rounded to one decimal, a source value of 2.9 can be compared against **2.85 ≤ monthly mean < 2.95**, using the specified rounding convention. Likewise the source's 1.5 corresponds to **1.45 ≤ mean < 1.55** under nearest-tenth rounding. These are **display tolerances only**. They are not historically measured variability, daily price bands or an agreed gameplay acceptance range. Source uncertainty is larger and differently structured than last-digit rounding. Lead/research agreement is needed before any broader band is adopted.

[I] No cost-of-living band is inferred from salt and coffee, and no number of unemployment points is converted into recognition probability. Where a source gives only one observation, the table remains a point until a second series or explicit historical uncertainty supports more.

## Proposed causal explanations for the existing design

[I] For **foreign pressure**, show the Secretary's received assessments of military prospects, neutral-rights incidents, industrial distress, emancipation policy and the danger of war. An internal British plan, an agreed Franco-British initiative and an American reply are different states. No numerical pressure-clock equation is calibrated by this packet.

[I] For **blockade pressure**, distinguish readiness at stations, port access, commercial contraction, prices and foreign legal acceptance. Trade restriction can reduce customs, hard-currency remittances and imported supply through a linked explanation. Do not charge the same missing cargo once as a money penalty and again as an unrelated physical-supply penalty without reconciling the channels.

[I] For **domestic finance**, use authorized sums, receipts, obligations and settlement time. Congress permits the Secretary to raise means, while people and institutions must actually buy, deposit, issue or accept instruments. Keep domestic paper distinct from spendable foreign exchange.

[I] For **rail supply**, use a named route's working equipment, repair state and allocated use, once independently sourced. Mileage supplies geography and scale, not a throughput multiplier. The evidence does not establish a universal conversion from dollars or miles into army supply.

## Unmet research inputs

The principal gaps are a monthly consolidated Confederate finance series through November 1862; comparable Union wages and household prices; November-specific Lancashire relief; port-specific cargo/attempt series; precise 1862 public/private cargo ownership; the original French dispatch/interview chronology where the early studies conflict; and serviceable rolling-stock/throughput returns for each line chosen by 1B. No unresolved input is silently replaced by a fitted coefficient or a historical outcome forced to happen on a fixed date.
