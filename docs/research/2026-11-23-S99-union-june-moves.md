# S99: Union June moves near Harpers Ferry, and Johnston's received picture

23 November 2026. Research and proposals only, `sprint/research-military`, base
`c7aa662d96f1950ddfa57da25e092909c39bcb9b`. S99 alone. No pack, code, judgment,
accepted decision, coverage score or simulation output changes. Decisions 0114

The Union threat has a sourced command chain. Scott directed concentration to
threaten Harpers Ferry; Patterson proposed turning it through Williamsport and
Martinsburg; Scott restrained his advance until supported. Patterson's subsequent
orders brought troops to Williamsport and conditionally across. Wallace chose the
Romney raid under Patterson's standing authority for limited surprise attacks,
rather than under a recovered target-specific Scott or Patterson order (OR I-II
pp.652,657-661,665,668,670-671,679,685-687,691-695;123-124).

**Two limits matter for causation.** The June 15 permission to cross responds to
reports of Confederate evacuation. In Johnston's October account, the report
that Patterson had crossed reaches him on June 16, after his stated June 15 main
departure. Neither may be moved backwards to manufacture an earlier threat.
Earlier concentration and approach reports, and the separate Romney report,
are the possible earlier information. The recrossing is also a delivered-order
sequence with failed communication, delayed execution and an alarm, rather than
one army moving instantly on Scott's issue date (OR I-II pp.471-472,687,691-695,
696-704,895-896,934).

## 2. Recall, recrossing and the proposals remaining before June 25

**Scott recalled troops; Patterson ordered the recrossing.** Scott's June 16,
11 a.m. telegram asks what pursuit Patterson proposes and, if none, orders all
regular horse and foot and the Rhode Island regiment to Washington. Patterson
answers that pursuit is impossible and asks to retain the regulars temporarily;
his June 16 long letter explicitly says the recall telegram is at hand.
Scott's second message challenges the usefulness of a Winchester detachment,
prioritizes the capital, orders the regulars/Rhode Islanders sent as disengaged
and requires Patterson to remain within the stated limits until he convinces
Scott otherwise. Patterson's June 18 account places receipt of the recall/limits
that night; June 16 night is an inference from his sequence, without an exact
time (P25-P27; OR I-II pp.691-695,701).

Porter, for Patterson, orders the Rhode Island regiment/battery back on June 16,
stops the Cumberland-bound cavalry and tells Cadwalader to be ready to fall back
to Maryland. On June 17 he directs Thomas's regulars via Frederick, Miles's
Second/Third/Eighth Infantry via Hagerstown-Harrisburg, and withdrawal of the
command to Maryland. Scott separately repeats that the capital is pressed and
the twice-requested troops must come without delay. These are actual orders;
the papers do not establish that every subordinate received them together
(P28-P30; OR I-II pp.695-696).

**Do not certify one clean recrossing clock.** Patterson's June 17 telegram says
all his force is back on the Maryland bank and Miles left at 8 p.m. Cadwalader's
June 18 report instead says seven regiments and the First Philadelphia City
Troop remained across overnight despite positive orders, probably through
failed brigade communication. A messenger's undelivered order was brought back
after he was shot; Cadwalader reports the troops withdrawing as safely possible
through a difficult ford. Patterson's June 18 papers also describe delayed
volunteer withdrawal and report the command now on the Maryland bank. Retain
the conflicting completion claims; the last unit's crossing time is not proved
(P31,P33-P34; OR I-II pp.697,701-704).

The June 17 alarm is another cause, rather than a silent exception to the recall.
Cadwalader's 7.5 p.m. report repeats accounts of Johnston with 15,000 men near
Martinsburg threatening Williamsport; Patterson temporarily diverts Thomas and
Miles and calls for guns and supporting brigades. His June 18 report explains
the delay and says the infantry was detached once the force was safely back.
The alarm is believed information; it is not proof Johnston was physically at
Martinsburg with that force. Patterson says Burnside went off despite recall,
so the order to halt him cannot be treated as successful delivery (P32-P33;
OR I-II pp.698-701).

Scott's June 18, 9.15 p.m. reply says he expected temporary retention/recall of
the regulars while the importance of Johnston's appearance was determined and
permits retention of the siege guns and Perkins battery. Patterson explicitly
receives this on **June 19 at 12.15 p.m.** His reply describes holding Williamsport
ford under Cadwalader, with a Doubleday gun, and continuing to ask to retain
Thomas. Scott on June 19 at 9.45 p.m. permits two Thomas cavalry companies and
directs Thomas with the other two to Washington; receipt is not dated in the
read pages (P35-P37; OR I-II pp.703,707-708).

Burnside's June 22 regimental report independently supplies his command's route:
Chambersburg June 11, Williamsport June 15 at noon; part of the battery across
on June 16, recalled June 17; via Hagerstown, Boonsborough and Middletown to
Frederick, rail departure June 18 at 7 p.m., Washington June 19 at 6 a.m.
This is the **Rhode Island volunteer regiment and battery**, not proof that all
Regular Army units had arrived. Nor should a later retention permission be
treated as having undone an already completed movement (P44; OR I-II pp.715-716).

The register retains the subsequent unaccepted or revised plans rather than
discarding them:

- June 16: Patterson proposes a Harpers Ferry base, B&O communications, staged
  Winchester/Strasburg advance and a reinforced Cumberland-Romney/Moorefield
  column. He continues his views while requesting approval and regulars'
  retention; this is no read acceptance of the entire plan (P26; pp.691-694).
- June 17-19: he proposes the Harpers Ferry bridge/base, then abandons the
  Maryland Heights occupation plan when the railway company refuses repair
  without protection of the line to Cumberland (P29,P33,P36; pp.696,701-702,707-708).
- June 20: Scott requests examination of Maryland Heights and a plan for a
  Leesburg-Alexandria sweep in cooperation, absorbing Stone. Patterson reports
  receipt at midnight at the June 20/21 boundary and proposes Heights garrison,
  Frederick depot and crossing near Point of Rocks to join Stone. It remains a
  submitted plan, without a read pre-June-25 acceptance. The June 20 Sharpsburg
  defensive order follows reported enemy reappearance (P38-P40; pp.709,711-712).
- June 21/24: Wallace is ordered to join; route remains to his judgment, with
  the later letter specifying shortest route unless strong unanticipated reasons
  intervene. His actual receipt/arrival is not established here (P41,P43;
  pp.712,715,717).
- June 23: Patterson reports partial Heights reconnaissance/water problems and
  proposes a renewed Winchester advance once battery harness is ready, subject
  to Scott's approval. No such approval is read inside the window (P42; p.717).

## 3. Romney: authority, commander, force, return

**Wallace decided and commanded the raid.** Patterson's standing instructions,
sent by Porter on June 6, directed the Eleventh Indiana at Cumberland to halt,
secure bridges, gather information and await further orders, while authorizing
surprise capture or rout of armed offensive bodies weak enough to be overcome
by a force safely detached. Wallace on June 11 acknowledges delivery of
dispatches that morning by Jerome Closson. Patterson's June 11 report says he
is acting on the instructions of the 6th; this identifies the authority without
pretending Wallace supplied an itemized receipt list. Army headquarters received
Patterson's report that afternoon and forwarded it to Stone at 9 p.m. (R01-R02;
OR I-II pp.105,668,676).

Wallace's June 14 report says he was informed of several hundred armed men at
Romney and determined to disperse them. He took **eight companies, about 500 men**
of the Eleventh Indiana from Cumberland at **10 p.m. June 12**, went by rail to
New Creek Station, then marched over the mountains and approached Romney about
**8 a.m. June 13**. He describes the bridge fight and an attempted flank against
two enemy guns. He says he had no horse for pursuit. These are Wallace's account,
not a full independently reconciled battle return (R03; OR I-II pp.123-124).

He then entered and searched the town and returned along the same road, reporting
arrival at camp at **11 p.m.** The June 13 return date is **[I]**, inferred from
the departure, June 13 action and his roughly 24-hour round-trip description.
He says route vulnerability forced his return and already acknowledges
Patterson's approval. The original approval and its delivery clock were not
read. This is a raid, not a permanent Romney garrison (R04; OR I-II p.124).

Keep the source limits visible. Page 123 really prints a departure from New
Creek a little after 4 o'clock, a projected 6 o'clock arrival and about 8 o'clock
actual approach over a stated 23 miles; page 124 gives 87 total miles/46 on foot
in 24 hours. These timings/distances were not repaired to fit one itinerary.
Wallace's whole-regiment strength of over 800 on June 11 is different from the
roughly 500 raiders; Johnston's report of 2,000 at Romney is a third, enemy-belief
quantity. Patterson's June 11 statement of Cumberland occupation yesterday and
Wallace's June 11 wording of arrival that morning are also retained rather than
quietly certifying one arrival day (OR I-II pp.105,123-124,471,676).

The ensuing relief chain is separate. Wallace's June 15 alarm describes a feared
4,000 at Romney; Patterson's contemplated reinforcement and Hancock/Bedford
fallback, Cadwalader's transport-based inability to detach, the recall of the
relief force, and Wallace's June 18 report of no enemy seen by scouts remain
attributed papers. None proves a permanent 4,000-man opponent or a completed
relief march (P21,P24,P28-P30,R05; OR I-II pp.687-689,692,694-697,704).

### What the pinned grammar can and cannot express

- `PlanGoal`/`GoalTerms` carry objective, posture, permissions, committed blocks,
  risk/deadline and nested discretion. `planner/axes.ts::holdingAxis` explicitly
  moves nobody; `direct`/`buildAxis` can produce a physical march. Do not replace
  an advance/recross with a distant stationary hold. No risk, deadline, assembly
  delay or trait value is selected here.
- `PrincipalDoctrine.offensives` has `id`, `fromDate`, `requiresNotice`, posture
  and discretion. `seats/requests.ts::doctrineView` makes a notice-gated row ready
  only from a received own-side situation report. That is useful **only if a real
  resulting received report supplies the gate**. It is not a generic matcher for
  arbitrary dispatches, joined regulars, multiple paper prerequisites or another
  commander's private knowledge.
- The current sent-order suppression marks an identified offensive issued if
  any goal was sent to that post on/after its source bound. Multiple G02-G06
  letters to the same post therefore need independent identity/supersession
  treatment in a later build. Merely adding several rows could suppress later
  letters. `proposalAnswers` correlates a received planner proposal with an
  earlier doctrine order and supports the three answers; it cannot be used to
  invent a commander proposal for every ordinary reconnaissance or recall.
- `packages/rule-seats/src/index.ts` gives doctrine to the principal; a commander
  chooses among planner candidates. `tick/orders.ts::issueOrder` authorizes the
  principal's field-post orders. The historical Scott-Patterson-Cadwalader and
  Patterson-Wallace initiative must retain issuer/relay and responsible chooser.
  Existing relayed principal letters can carry authority; silently attributing
  every local Patterson/Wallace choice to Lincoln or scripting their plans would
  erase the sourced chain. Separate local issuing/delegation support needs a
  bounded design ruling, not a fabricated source.
- Ordinary `transfer` names posts and an amount, with the sender selecting blocks;
  it is not exact named-regiment recall, nor a completed march/arrival. Actual
  retained force after a delivered recall must drive subsequent judgment. No
  uncertain source strength is converted to a known production strength.

**Recommended smallest later build [I]:** retain the existing goal/proposal/
message/plan/operation grammar and add only the missing receipt predicates,
independent letter identity and bounded local-authority/raid-return support that
the lead chooses. Start with G01-G03 and G07 source chains, then G04-G06 so the
threat is not permanent after recall. Require paired observations with the same
world truth and different received papers, a lost-paper control, and a control
in which reported evacuation never occurs. None is an S99 implementation or a
request for a new campaign. A truthful partial mapping is preferable to claiming
all this can be encoded by current `requiresNotice` alone.

## Proposed rulings and rejected alternatives [I]

1. **Accept the sourced sequence, with separate receipt clocks and motives.**
