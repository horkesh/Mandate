We  have  had  great  difficulty  in  equipping  the  four  regiments  we  are
now  sending.  The  arms  furnished  to  them  by  the  United  States  are  of
inferior  quality,  being  flint-lock  muskets  percussioned.  It  is  earnestly
desired  that  they  may,  if  possible,  on  arriving  at  Washington  be  pro
vided  with  arms  superior  to  those  they  now  have.  The  United  States
have  provided  us  with  little  besides  these  arms,  yet  our  troops  are  on
their  way,  prepared  to  defend  the  Government  of  their  country.
I  learn  by  yours  of  the  20th  instant  that  it  is  contemplated  to  accept
men  for  three  years.  If  so,  I  would  be  glad  to  be  informed  if  there  is
n  certain  number  allotted  to  the  several  States,  and  if  so,  what  will  be
the  quota  of  New  Jersey,  with  any  instructions  you  can  give  me  respect
ing  the  raising  of  them.
I  am,  sir,  very  respectfully,  your  obedient  servant,
CHS.  S.  OLDEN.
