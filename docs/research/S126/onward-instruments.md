The read instruments identify Lee as issuer. They do not make the Confederate War Department, Davis or Cooper the author of these particular orders. A staff signature does not become a granting principal (0124item4). S117C02 additionally keeps the President-to-Walker instrument unread; it is not reconstructed here.

S124's rendezvous receipt supplies actual national ownership, not a historical regiment identity. S118T01/T02 and correctionC06 distinguish Turney, Bate and Vaughn and explicitly warn against adding historical units on top of generated April-call lots. [I] Renaming or assigning ordinal generated lots would invent that linkage. No additional troops, appointments, root mandate or received papers are created to bypass the gaps. The accepted state-root template cannot authorize troops after national receipt (0124item6; S123 standing-mandates note).

The S126 generic assignment behavior is demonstrated only with fictional fixtures. The historical instructions are source rows, not executable declarations. Rendezvous troops remain there until a real authorized order exists. These historical acceptance gaps remain open; a passing generic test or integration measurement does not retire them.

Rejected representation choices: attribute Lee's directions to the national principal; open a post by calendar; treat possession of a historical paper as a runtime grant; rename anonymous lots; send Bate directly to the later Holmes command; equate sending with receipt.
