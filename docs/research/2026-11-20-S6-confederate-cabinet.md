## The sequence the read record supports

Wright's *General Officers of the Confederate Army* (1911), printed pp151-154, is a public-domain reference work. Under 0087 it can source offices and dates. It cannot alone source character, regard or gifts. The contemporary Senate proceedings and correspondence below take precedence where they conflict with its directory.

## What the confirmation record says about reputation

The Senate confirms Benjamin, then rejects reconsideration. The printed names on pp73-74 give eight for reconsideration and thirteen against. Mallory's nomination is initially laid on the table; after debate it is confirmed **13-6** on p74. Memminger, Randolph, Watts and Reagan are recorded as confirmed, but those entries supply no equivalent roll-call measure of enthusiasm.

[I] Mallory's divided confirmation supports an **average, institution-specific reputation observation on 18 March 1862**, following the kind of evidence 0087 accepted for his earlier confirmation. It does not make average a measured population statistic. Benjamin's opposed reconsideration shows a contested cabinet appointment, not an uncontested high reputation. His general reputation centre remains HOLD in S6. Never turn the confirmation counts into permille competence, candour or popularity. Appointment also establishes neither Davis's affection nor the appointee's regard toward Davis. [JCCS II pp73-74](https://archive.org/details/journalofcongres02confiala/page/73).

## Benjamin and military command

On **25 January 1862**, Benjamin urges Johnston to risk generous furloughs now rather than lose the twelve-month men altogether. He acknowledges danger on both sides, limited arms and competing demands. [I] This is a specific argument about timing and reenlistment incentives, not evidence of uniformly reckless or timid temperament. OR I-5 pp1045-1046.

Benjamin's **30 January** telegram directs Jackson to recall Loring immediately. Jackson's **31 January** reply says he has complied, then asks for reassignment to the Virginia Military Institute or, failing that, acceptance of his resignation. Johnston forwards it with regret on **7 February**. The endorsements have their own dates. [I] A request to leave after complying is not itself proof of disobeying this order. Nor is a Secretary's intervention, merely because unwelcome to a general, proof of incompetence. The command conflict belongs in a dated relationship observation. [OR I-5 p1053](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730194/page/1053).

Johnston's **1 February 1862** protest describes War Department furloughs and personnel details that bypass his command. His account must remain attributed to him. [I] It is direct evidence that he objected to the administrative practice. It does not establish every alleged consequence, or automatically establish Benjamin's `micromanages` trait. The department's own instructions and the different statutory responsibilities must be read alongside it. OR I-5 pp1057-1058.

Loring's **31 January** appeal describes sickness, exposure and supply problems at Romney. His earlier enclosure is dated **28 January**; it continues onto p1055. [I] This is relevant counterevidence to treating the recalled command as a mere personal grievance. His estimates of enemy strength remain estimates. [OR I-5 pp1054-1055](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730194/page/1054).

## Randolph and the governors

Randolph's **17 July 1862** circular says depleted army ranks are a serious problem and asks governors to use public opinion to bring absent men back. It expressly acknowledges that trying to repair the weakness risks disclosing it to the enemy. [I] This is an unusually useful dated disclosure of an administrative tradeoff. It supplies neither an innate gift nor proof of a general reputation. OR IV-2 p7.

Vance's **13 November** letter to Memminger complains that delay in providing bonds may have cost his purchasing agent a sailing. Vance offers two possible explanations, including the conduct of subordinates, and requests investigation. [I] This is Vance's complaint, not an adjudication that Memminger was incompetent or obstructive. It does not establish Vance's personal regard for Davis. OR IV-2 p178.

Pettus's **15 November** letter, continuing across pp178-179, argues for keeping State units intact while filling Confederate regiments, and explicitly says he wants to strengthen rather than embarrass the Confederate government. The day is checked on the scan: the OCR is unreliable here. [I] An institutional objection can coexist with a declared commitment to the common military effort. Do not assign hostility to Davis from the objection alone. OR IV-2 pp178-179.

## Proposed rulings

1. Prefer **15 November** for the recorded acceptance of Randolph's resignation. Reject silently retaining the directory's 17 November as the date of acceptance. Separate acceptance from an acting successor's appointment.
2. Preserve Seddon's **20/21 November** evidence conflict. Reject an exact assumption date inferred from either an address on a letter or a later directory alone. A dated appointment/assumption instrument is still needed.
3. Retain the March cabinet's different acts and its acting overlap. Reject one date copied to every appointment, confirmation, oath and assumption.
4. Keep every post-start judgment out of the accepted 1861 starting cells. A future 1B snapshot or dated update needs the lead's temporal ruling before application. Do not change 0085/0087's accepted boundaries in this research lane.
