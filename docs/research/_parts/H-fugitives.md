# H. Fugitives at Union lines, May-July 1861: is anyone on record urging return? (S17)

Agent: gaps-H-fugitives. Window 1 May-31 July 1861; later items are noted by date only.

Sources read in OCR from archive.org (page numbers are the printed running heads):
- **OR I,2** = archive.org `warofrebellion02secrrich` (Series I vol 2).
- **OR I,3** = `warofrebellionco0003unit_v7h9` (Series I vol 3, Missouri).
- **OR II,1** = `warofrebellion0201rootrich` (Series II vol 1; the chapter "Military Treatment of Captured and Fugitive Slaves", pp. 749-822, is printed in date order).
- **OR III,1** = `warofrebellion0301rootrich` (Series III vol 1).

Labels: STRONG = contemporary primary or official. RETRO-OFFICIAL = later official compilation or editor's note. WEAK = memoir or retrospective. NONE = nothing found.

## Bottom line

**Yes, several people on the Union side are on record urging or ordering return, but not for the three Fort Monroe men.**

- For the Fort Monroe men, the only claimant on record is the Confederate Maj. John B. Cary. Butler refused. Cameron approved the refusal (30 May).
- Nobody on the Union side asked for the three men to be returned. The nearest Union voice is Scott's military secretary writing for Scott (16 July), who relays Lincoln's own question about owners fetching slaves who had crossed the Potomac with the army.
- Officers' orders returning or excluding fugitives in the window:
  - McDowell's HQ (26 June);
  - Schenck (6 July);
  - Miles (15 July);
  - Scott's HQ to Mansfield (16 July);
  - Mansfield, General Orders No. 33 (17 July).
- Requests from loyal-state officials:
  - Rep. Calvert of Maryland to Cameron (8 July);
  - Attorney General Bates (23 July).
- No order from a Union general or the War Department in the window ordered Butler's department to return them.

## 1. The claim at Fort Monroe; Butler's reply; Cameron's reply

**The claimant: Maj. John B. Cary, Virginia Volunteers (Artillery), Confederate. Date 24 May.**

- STRONG, Cary's own letter to Col. J. B. Magruder, headed "MAY 24, 1861 -- 7.15 p. m.", OR I,2 pp. 870-871 (also OR II,1 p. 753). He wrote that Butler granted him an interview at "3.30 p. m." and that Butler "has taken three of Colonel Mallory's negroes, which he has refused to give up on application."
  - OCR prints his name "Gary"; the OR II,1 index (p. 1024) lists him as "Cary, John B." with the page given as "75!" (753). The OR I,2 index also reads "Cary, J. B."
  - Cary's own letter does not mention the Fugitive Slave Act.
- STRONG, Butler to Scott, headed "Fort Monroe, May 24, 1861", OR I,2 pp. 648-651; abridged in OR II,1 pp. 752-753.
  - The three men "belonging to Col. Charles Mallory" are described at p. 649 ("three negroes, field hands, belonging to Col. Charles Mallory ... delivered themselves up to my picket guard" on "Thursday night", i.e. 23 May).
  - On p. 650: "Major Cary demanded to know ... if I did not feel myself bound by my constitutional obligations to deliver up fugitives under the fugitive-slave act."
  - Butler's reply, same page: "the fugitive-slave act did not affect a foreign country, which Virginia claimed to be ... in Maryland, a loyal State, fugitives from service had been returned". He added that if Mallory "would come to the fort and take the oath of allegiance ... I would deliver the men up to him." Cary answered that "Colonel Mallory was absent."
- **Date anomaly.** The paragraph on the interview sits under a dateline "Saturday, May 25" inside a letter headed 24 May. Cary's own 24 May letter puts the interview at 3.30 p.m. on the 24th. Use 24 May, and note the dateline conflict.
- **"Contraband of war."**
  - The phrase is not in Butler's 24 May or 27 May letters as printed (OR I,2 pp. 648-651 and 53; OR II,1 pp. 752-754).
  - It is first recorded in these sources as a quotation in a letter of 1 June. The Haytian Bureau of Emigration, Boston, wrote to Cameron about the Department's "decision ... that 'the slaves held by rebels who may seek protection in our camps are to be reported as contraband articles of war'" (OR III,1 pp. 244-245; STRONG that the phrase circulated by 1 June; signer not read).
  - The usual account puts "contraband of war" in Butler's mouth at the Cary interview. I found no contemporary source for that wording (the claim rests on Butler's 1892 memoir, WEAK, not read here).
- **Names Baker, Townsend, Mallory (Frank Baker, James Townsend, Sheppard Mallory).** They do not appear in any OR page read. Treat them as unsourced here.
  - I looked for them in the Atlantic Monthly of Nov 1861 (Pierce's article), but the OCR is unreadable.
- STRONG, Butler to Scott, 27 May, OR I,2 p. 53 (duplicated to the Secretary of War). OR II,1 p. 754 prints it as addressed to Scott.
  - Quote: "I am in the utmost doubt what to do with this species of property."
  - He proposes to employ the able-bodied and to keep an account.
  - He asks about women and children: "can I receive the services of the father and mother and not take the children?"
- STRONG, indorsements on Butler's 24 May letter, 29 May, OR I,2 p. 652. Scott: "There is much to praise in this report, and nothing to condemn. It is highly interesting ... particularly in its relation to the slave question." Cameron: "I agree with the Lieutenant-General in his entire approval of the within report."
- **STRONG, Cameron to Butler, 30 May, did not order return.** OR II,1 pp. 754-755; also OR III,1 p. 243.
  - "Your action in respect to the negroes who came in your lines from the service of the rebels is approved. The Department is sensible of the embarrassment which must surround officers conducting military operations in a State by the laws of which slavery is sanctioned."
  - Then: "you will permit no interference by the persons under your command with the relations of persons held to service under the laws of any State, you will on the other hand so long as any State within which your military operations are conducted is under the control of such armed combinations refrain from surrendering to alleged masters any persons who may come within your lines."
  - Then: "employ such persons ... keeping an account ... The question of their final disposition will be reserved for future determination."
  - The text says "embarrassment" (OR II,1) / "embarrassments" (OR III,1); both prints appear.
- Cameron to Butler, **8 Aug 1861** (after the window): OR II,1 pp. 761-762. Date only.
- Butler to Scott, 6 June, OR I,2 p. 668: "no disaster has befallen us, except the great influx of slaves." This is a report, not an argument for return.

**Verdict, item 1:** sourced. The only person pressing return in this affair is the Confederate Cary. Union approval of Butler is on record. The Fugitive Slave Act is cited by Butler's own account of Cary.

## 2. War Department and cabinet letters, May-July

- **Cameron.** The only War Department instruction in the window is 30 May (above). It is scoped to Butler's department, to States under armed combinations. Nothing from Cameron orders return in the window. I found no Cameron reply to Calvert (below).
- **Lincoln, via Scott's secretary: STRONG, official transmission (not the note itself).**
  - Lt. Col. Schuyler Hamilton, military secretary, to McDowell, headed "CONFIDENTIAL", 16 July, OR II,1 p. 760.
  - Text: "the general-in-chief ... has received from the President of the United States a second note dated to-day on the subject of fugitive slaves in which he asks: 'Would it not be well to allow owners to bring back those which have crossed' the Potomac with our troops?"
  - It continues: "Of course it is the general's wish the name of the President should not at this time be brought before the public in connection with this delicate subject."
  - Lincoln's own note is not printed in the OR. I did not find it elsewhere. A web search did not locate Nicolay and Hay's text, and Gutenberg was down for maintenance, so that edition was not read. The "first note" of the same day is not located either.
  - A question phrased as a suggestion, not an order, and the President's name kept out of the record.
- **Scott's HQ, undated in print, placed between 16 and 17 July.** Asst. Adj. Gen. E. D. Townsend to Gen. Mansfield, Dept. of Washington, OR II,1 p. 760: "The general-in-chief directs that you take stringent measures to prevent any fugitive slaves from passing over the river particularly as servants with the regiments ordered over."
  - STRONG, and a direct instruction from the General-in-Chief.
- **Attorney General Edward Bates, 23 July, to J. L. McDowell, U.S. Marshal, Kansas.** OR II,1 p. 761. STRONG.
  - He answered a question on whether to give "official services in the execution of the fugitive-slave law": "It is the President's constitutional duty to 'take care that the laws be faithfully executed.' That means all the laws. He has no right to discriminate ... Missouri is a State in the Union."
  - He called refusal by a ministerial officer "an official misdemeanor".
  - Bates urges the execution of the Act in a loyal State. He does not mention camps or soldiers.
- **Rep. Charles B. Calvert (Maryland) to Cameron, 8 July.** OR III,1 p. 324. STRONG, but a congressman, not an official. He wrote:
  - "Various complaints have come to me relative to the difficulties of our citizens retaining their slaves at home in consequence of the tempting offers made to them by some of the volunteer regiments. I know that it is not the desire of the Government to encourage the escape of this species of property from the lawful owner ..."
  - He asked for "an order to the commander of the Maryland Division to exclude from all the camps in his division such slaves as may be found therein at this time, and hereafter to permit none to enter their camps."
  - The OR III,1 index says "Exclusion of slaves from camps, 324, 326". Page 326 is a transport-rates table, so the second page reference is probably an index error. No Cameron reply found.
- **Seward and Blair: nothing found.** Their names do not appear in any fugitive-slave document read in this window. I did not search their papers, so this is "nothing found in the OR chapter", not "nothing exists".
- Others in the window, recorded in the OR: the Haytian Bureau of Emigration (1 June, OR III,1 pp. 244-245) proposes an emigration depot for "contraband articles". It is not about return.

**Verdict, item 2:** sourced. Lincoln, via Hamilton, plus Bates, Calvert, and Scott's HQ via Townsend.

## 3. Officers' orders in the window

- **Slemmer, Fort Pickens, 12-13 March** (before the window): returned eight to Pensacola authorities. OR II,1 p. 750. Date only.
- **Butler, 23 April** (before the window), to Governor Hicks: "ready to co-operate with your excellency in suppressing ... any insurrection against the laws of Maryland." OR II,1 p. 750.
- **Harney (St. Louis), 14 May, to T. T. Gantt.** STRONG, OR II,1 p. 751 (also OR I,3 pp. 313-314). He described Union practice so far: slaves who "have sought refuge in the camps of U. S. troops from Northern States and commanded by a Northern general. They were carefully sent back to their owners." Description of practice, not an order.
- **McClellan, 26 May, "To the Union Men of Western Virginia."** STRONG, OR I,2 pp. 48-49; OR II,1 p. 753. Wording: "not only will we abstain from all such interference but we will on the contrary with an iron hand crush any attempt at insurrection on their part." He did not mention fugitives and did not order return. Same day to Col. Kelley: "repress all attempts at negro insurrection" (OR II,1 p. 753).
- **McDowell's HQ, 26 June.** STRONG, OR II,1 p. 758. Fry's indorsement to Schenck: "Brigadier-General Schenck will please have this matter investigated and return the negro to his owner." It follows Townsend's 25 June letter to McDowell, "Please aid him in recovering the negro belonging to his mother ... they have been practicing a little of the abolition system in protecting the runaway" (OR II,1 p. 757).
- **Schenck (Camp Upton, Va.), 6 July.** STRONG, OR II,1 pp. 755-756: "This camp will not be permitted while I have command to be made a harbor for escaping fugitives, but persons owing labor or service to loyal citizens of loyal States if they resort to us shall always be surrendered when demanded on proper order or authority by the lawful owner." McDowell's indorsement, 7 July (p. 756): "An officer of the army will be sent to General Schenck's camp with the owners of the slaves whenever it is desired to make further search for them."
- **Miles, 15 July.** STRONG, indorsement on Col. Davies's report of 14 July, OR I,2 pp. 299-300 (OR II,1 pp. 759-760). Davies had reported that the negroes from John A. Washington's farm near Mount Vernon had driven wagons into his camp. Miles ordered Davies to "send back to the farm the negroes his troops brought away." These were not fugitives crossing lines but plantation slaves taken to camp; the order is still a return.
- **Mansfield, General Orders No. 33, Dept. of Washington, 17 July.** STRONG, OR II,1 p. 760: "Fugitive slaves will under no pretext whatever be permitted to reside or be in any way harbored in the quarters and camps of the troops serving in this department. Neither will such slaves be allowed to accompany troops on the march."
- **Col. Harvey Brown, Fort Pickens, 22 June.** STRONG, OR II,1 p. 755: "I shall not send the negroes back as I will never be voluntarily instrumental in returning a poor wretch to slavery." This is the counterexample.
- **Butler, 27 July.** STRONG, OR I,2 p. 764; OR II,1 p. 761. His complaint that Col. Duryea, 5th New York, took "certain negro slaves to Washington ... against my express orders, and after a portion of them had been detained by my provost-marshal"; he asks instructions for "departing regiments."
- **Butler's own statement (24 May):** fugitives in Maryland "had been returned" (OR I,2 p. 650), with Butler at Annapolis in April.
- **Not found in window.** Patterson, Banks, Dix, Rosecrans, W. T. Sherman, Prentiss, Pope, and Halleck. Searched OR I,2 (negro/slave/fugitive: no Patterson, Banks or Rosecrans hit; the only Union hits are Butler, McClellan, Davies/Miles, Duryea), OR I,3 (Missouri; the only window item is Harney's 14 May letter), and OR II,1 (index). Dates, dated later, for the record:
  - Dix, 4 Nov 1861 (OR II,1 pp. 763-766);
  - W. T. Sherman, 8 Nov (OR II,1 p. 774 and nearby);
  - Halleck, General Orders No. 3, 20 Nov (OR II,1 p. 749; the OCR reads "No. '6'");
  - Grant, 25 Dec.

**Best single citation for the SENSITIVE-HISTORY claim that "officers went on returning fugitives" (the [SW 6.3] practice claim):** the 6 July Schenck report with McDowell's indorsement of 7 July, plus the 26 June Fry indorsement and the 17 July Mansfield order, OR II,1 pp. 755-760 (one chapter, dated, with exact words). If a single page: **OR II,1 p. 760** (Hamilton to McDowell on Lincoln's question; Townsend to Mansfield; General Orders No. 33).

**Verdict, item 3:** sourced.

## 4. The 4 March inaugural as standing policy in May-July

- The inaugural text is on record (STRONG, the speech; text read in the Avalon mirror, `avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/lincoln1.asp`, a finding aid; print locator: Nicolay and Hay, *Complete Works* vol. 2, not read; Wikisource shows it as *Life and Works* vol. 6, pp. 155-168):
  - "It is scarcely questioned that this provision was intended by those who made it for the reclaiming of what we call fugitive slaves; and the intention of the lawgiver is the law. ... slaves whose cases come within the terms of this clause 'shall be delivered up' their oaths are unanimous."
  - "I have no purpose, directly or indirectly, to interfere with the institution of slavery in the States where it exists."
- **Restated as policy in the window by others:**
  - Harney, 14 May: "my general acquaintance with the statesmanlike views of the President makes me confident" (OR II,1 p. 751). This is his inference, not an official statement.
  - Cameron, 30 May: "The Government cannot recognize the rejection by any State of its federal obligations nor can it refuse the performance of the federal obligations resting upon itself" (OR II,1 pp. 754-755). Strong, but for States under armed combinations.
  - Hamilton, 16 July: "His Excellency's desire to carry out to the fullest all constitutional obligations" (OR II,1 p. 760). The President's desire as relayed by Scott's secretary, STRONG as to what Scott's office said.
  - Bates, 23 July: "all the laws" (OR II,1 p. 761).
  - Cameron, 8 Aug (after the window): "It is the desire of the President that all existing rights in all the States be fully respected and maintained" (OR II,1 p. 761).
- **Lincoln's 4 July message to Congress: NONE.** I read the full text (American Presidency Project, `presidency.ucsb.edu/node/202522`; Basler vol. 4, pp. 421-441). It has no fugitive-slave policy; "slave" appears once, in "the States commonly called slave States."

**Verdict, item 4:** sourced. The inaugural is a standing statement (March), and May-July administration restatements exist, but none cites the inaugural by name.

## Congress, July-August 1861 (Globe 37-1)

- **House, 8 July, p. 24.** Lovejoy's three resolutions, including "it is no part of the duty of the soldiers of the United States to capture and return fugitive slaves", were laid on the table 88-62. **9 July, p. 32:** the single resolution on soldiers and fugitive slaves was adopted 93-55. The tabling vote on 9 July failed 66-81 (OR II,1 p. 759 prints the 93-55 roll). No debate. No one urged return in speech. The 55 nays included Mallory (Ky.), Calvert, Crisfield, Crittenden, Carlile and Vallandigham. Voting nay is not the same as urging return.
- **Senate, 18 July, p. 189.** Lane (Kansas) asked Carlile: "will the Senator ... expect ... the armies of the North, to become the servants of the traitors, and return those slaves to their traitorous masters?" Carlile (Va.) did not answer yes; he cited McClellan's 26 May proclamation. No express call for return.
- **Senate, 22 July, pp. 218-219.** On Trumbull's amendment (forfeiture of a master's claim where slaves were employed in the rebellion), Wilson (Mass.) spoke against returning such slaves ("cease to return to traitors their fugitive slaves"). Breckinridge asked for the yeas and nays and called the amendment "very objectionable". No express call for return.
- **Crittenden (House, 22 July, p. 222) and Johnson (Senate, 25 July, p. 243):** the war-aims resolution says the war is not waged "for any purpose of ... overthrowing or interfering with the rights or established institutions of those States." It does not mention fugitives. NONE.
- **Appendix:** I grepped the Appendix (same file) for "fugitive slave law" and read the one hit. It is a Senate speech on the causes of secession, noting that the 1850 Act was executed; it urges no return of anyone. The full Appendix was not read.
