# I-richmond: when the Confederate government moved from Montgomery to Richmond, 1861

Keys: JCCS = Journal of the Congress of the Confederate States, vol. 1 (Senate Doc. 234, 58th Cong.; archive.org `journalofcongres00conf`, OCR read). CSA-Stat = Statutes at Large of the Provisional Government (Matthews, 1864; archive.org `statutesatlarge00conf`, OCR read). OR IV-1 = Official Records Ser. IV vol. 1 (archive.org `2warofrebellion0401rootrich`, OCR read). RR1 = Moore, Rebellion Record vol. 1 (archive.org `rebellrecord01moorrich`, OCR read). Davis, *Rise and Fall*, not used. Page numbers are the printed running heads; OCR doubts marked.

## Item 1. The Provisional Congress's act

**Verdict: the research note is right on substance but cites it loosely.** The act is a resolution, "A resolution to provide for the removal of the seat of government", agreed to 20 May 1861, approved 21 May 1861. OR IV-1 prints it at pp. 342-343 (pp. 337-343 is the run of 21 May acts around it, not the resolution). It does more than adjourn to Richmond: its second clause authorizes the President to move the Executive Departments. STRONG (official, contemporary).

Text, CSA-Stat Provisional Congress Sess. II, Res. No. 9, p. 165 ("Approved May 21, 1861"); same text OR IV-1 pp. 342-343:
- "Resolved by the Congress of the Confederate States of America, That this Congress will adjourn on Tuesday next, to meet again on the twentieth day of July, at Richmond, Virginia; and that a committee of three members of this Congress be appointed to make suitable arrangements for the accommodation of this Congress, and of the several executive departments."
- "Resolved, further, That the President be and he is hereby authorized to cause the several executive departments, with the archives thereof, to be removed at such time between this and the twentieth day of July next, as he may determine, to Richmond: Provided, however, That in case of any public emergency which may, in the judgment of the President, render it impolitic to meet in Richmond, the President shall have power by proclamation to call the Congress together at some other convenient place to be selected by him."

Companion act: "An Act making appropriation to defray the expenses of removing the seat of government to Richmond, Virginia", 21 May 1861, ch. 67, CSA-Stat pp. 131-132: $40,000 for "rent of executive buildings and President's house, furniture, expenses of packing books and records, railroad freight ... of the government, from Montgomery to Richmond". JCCS p. 264-265 (reported by Toombs, passed and signed 21 May). STRONG.

Legal reason it needed a vote: Provisional Constitution Art. VI cl. 3, "Until otherwise provided by the Congress, the city of Montgomery, in the State of Alabama, shall be the seat of Government." (CSA-Stat, Provisional Constitution; quoted by Davis, JCCS p. 243).

So: **Congress last sat at Montgomery on Tuesday 21 May 1861** (adjourned, p. 265). **First sat at Richmond on Saturday 20 July 1861**: heading "THIRD SESSION, JULY 20, 1861 ... Richmond, Va., July 20, 1861": "In pursuance to a notice of adjournment, Congress met at the Capitol in the city of Richmond, State of Virginia, at noon on the 20th day of July" (JCCS p. 271). STRONG. The volume's title page also lists "Third session. Held at Richmond, Va., July 20, 1861, to August 31, 1861."

Davis, message to Congress at Richmond, 20 July 1861 (JCCS p. 272; signature "Richmond, July 20, 1861"): "I deemed it advisable to direct the removal of the several Executive Departments, with their archives, to this city, to which you had removed the seat of government, immediately after your adjournment." STRONG (official, contemporary; Davis's message, not the banned memoir). Note "immediately after your adjournment" is loose: departments left in the week of 25-29 May (below), not on 21-22 May.

A Northern paper reported the adjournment in the Diary: "The Confederate Congress in session at Montgomery, Ala., adjourned to meet at Richmond, Va., July 20th. -- N. Y. Herald, May 28." (RR1 Diary p. 74, under 21 May). STRONG for the report; it does not say that the seat was moved.

## Item 2. When Davis and the departments moved

### Davis
- Left Montgomery: **no contemporary primary read gives the day.** Walker's telegram of 25 May (below) says he himself leaves "Monday night" (27 May). Modern finding aid (Rice University Davis chronology snippet) says Davis left 26 May: WEAK/unread, do not cite. The Montgomery Cabinet was still at Montgomery on 27 May on the evidence of Walker's letter (below). Treat departure as **about 26-27 May, unproven**.
- **Arrived at Richmond Wednesday 29 May 1861, morning.** Evidence:
  - Davis telegram, OR IV-1 p. 361: "RICHMOND, VA., May 29, 1861. Governor JOSEPH E. BROWN, Atlanta, Ga.: Troops, armed and equipped, ammunition included, are much needed. ... JEFF'N DAVIS." STRONG: his first Richmond-dated item in OR IV-1; Brown's answer the same day is addressed to him.
  - RR1 Diary p. 84, 29 May: "President Davis reached Richmond this morning, accompanied by his nephew, Mr. Joseph Davis, Col. Northrop, of the Confederate Army, and Col. Wigfall. Gov. Letcher and the Executive Council met and received the President at Petersburg. An immense assemblage welcomed his arrival at Richmond ... -- N. O. Delta, May 30." STRONG (contemporary Southern newspaper as printed by Moore; the heading prints "May 20", plainly a misprint/OCR for 29 between the 28 and 30 headings).
  - Richmond *Dispatch*, 30 May 1861: "He made his advent into Richmond at 25 minutes past 7 o'clock yesterday morning, having arrived from Petersburg in an extra train". STRONG in kind, but I read only a modern website transcription (civilwarrichmond.com); the page image on Chronicling America / Virginia Chronicle is blocked (virginiachronicle.com returned 403). Treat as unverified wording; the date agrees with the two items above.
  - Brown to Davis, Atlanta, 31 May, addressed "President of the Confed. States of America, Richmond, Va." (OR IV-1 p. 363); Virginia's Secretary of the Commonwealth, Richmond, 31 May, proposes an interview with Davis "at 10 o'clock to-morrow morning, in the council chamber at the capitol" (p. 364); Davis speech "at Richmond, June 1" (RR1 Doc. 222, pp. 322-323). STRONG.
- Richmond was also already the capital of Virginia and the seat of Lee's command; Letcher telegraphs Walker from Richmond 24 May (OR IV-1 p. 354). That is the Virginia capital, not the Confederate one.

## Suggested wording for the pack
- Seat of government moved by resolution of the Provisional Congress agreed 20 May and approved 21 May 1861; Congress last sat at Montgomery 21 May, first at Richmond 20 July.
- Davis at Richmond from the morning of 29 May 1861 (Davis telegram datelined Richmond that day, OR IV-1 p. 361). War Department bureaus ordered from Montgomery 25 May; Richmond-dated War Department papers from 3 June. For the other departments say "by late May-July 1861" and cite the 21 May resolution plus Davis's 20 July message; do not give a day.
- A scenario starting 4 March 1861 needs Montgomery as the Confederate capital until late May. A date after 29 May 1861 needs Richmond.
