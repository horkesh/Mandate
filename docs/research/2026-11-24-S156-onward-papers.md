## Packet and reading rule

[The CSV](S156/instruments.csv) has **28 records**: 11 for Annapolis, 5 for
Washington-to-Virginia, 5 for the handover, 7 for the unnamed Confederate bodies.
These include repeated readings of existing instruments, official reports,
intentions and negative controls; **28 is not a count of new orders**. Each row
gives issuer/authority, the paper's recipient separately from its executing
addressee, sending/receiving commands, selected scope words, conditions and
means, issue/report dates, receipt limits, page citations, and identity links.
Scope excerpts sometimes omit intervening wording or expand named lists;
the retained full page governs.

**[H]** denotes read historical text, **[M]** a repository/identity inventory,
and **[I]** interpretation. Under **0136**, `paper_itself` means a printed copy
of the contemporary paper, not an autograph. An issuer/addressee report of
an earlier direction is marked **[I]** and keeps the report date separate.
An intention, suggestion, bare reference or battle witness does not become
a completed sending order. Missing exact dates and terms remain missing.

Butler's *Private and Official Correspondence*, volume I, privately issued
in 1917, reproduces the 1861 letters/telegrams used here. Their terms are read
as papers; the collection's later publication does not make every editorial
statement an instrument. Only selected OCR pages were read, with two static
facsimile checks. No full-volume collation or general absence claim is made.

[Source readings](S156/source-reading.json) pin **86 selected extracts**, with
hashes and locators; [input receipts](S156/input-receipts.json) pin the handoff's
decisions, S154 design and earlier research. Existing S149/S151/S138 evidence
is reused explicitly. Catalogue and S138 keys are distinct CSV columns.
`candidate_keys_not_assigned` is a comparison list, **never an order's selected
force**. The Twenty-fifth and Twenty-eighth NY Militia retain catalogue keys
and no invented S138 keys. No generated lot fills an unnamed regiment.

## 1. Annapolis to Washington, April and May

**[H] A01:** Scott's **25 April** letter to Butler tells him to retain a
competent force at Annapolis and select a regiment to guard the railroad.
Its final clause directs him to send **all spare troops from Annapolis** to
Washington as transport becomes available. Butler's 27 April report says he
received the 25 April command letter that morning. This is a read general
onward direction, with a corresponding receipt acknowledgment, not three
named selections (OR I-II pp.600,604–605; Butler I p.42).

**[H/I] A03–A04:** Butler reports directing **Corcoran's Sixty-ninth** to guard
the railroad from near the Annapolis depot to the Junction. Corcoran's
29 April report describes the resulting guard duty and reaching the Junction
the previous evening. Neither passage directs a service arrival at Washington
(OR I-II pp.604–605,610–611).

**[H] A05–A07:** The sequence changes. On **29 April**, Scott says later
instructions will address further forwarding. On **30 April**, he refers to
Hamilton's telegram stopping further troops for the present, then states the
capital's need for at least eight more regiments. On **1 May**, his letter to
Patterson, sent open through Butler, insists on additional regiments despite
equipment deficiencies, some potentially spared from Annapolis. Butler's
telegram says the open letter was forwarded. The Hamilton original and
precise regimental selections remain unread (Butler I pp.54,61; OR I-II
pp.611,614–615). These are changing instructions, not additive eight/nine
quotas or an unlimited future subscription.

**[H/I] A08:** Butler's **1 May telegram to Scott** says he will send the
**Fifth New York**, if possible, by another train, after separately describing
Ellsworth's intended forwarding tomorrow. This supplies named prospective
terms and rail means, but does not reproduce an issued order to the Fifth's
commander, certify departure, or acknowledge Washington receipt (Butler I
p.61). **Fifth Militia is a contextual candidate only**: this telegram supplies
neither the militia qualifier nor its colonel. Sandford's report had sent
Fifth Militia to Annapolis on 27 April, while Phisterer gives Washington muster
on **1 May**, earlier than Butler's intended next-day forwarding. Keep both
readings; do not silently change either date or use the later register as a
sending paper (NY AG1861 p.109; Phisterer I p.531).

**[H/I] A09:** Butler's **6 May** report says he occupied **Relay House** with
Lyons's **Eighth New York** in obedience to Scott. It gives no exact date for
Scott's originating instruction. Relay House guard duty cannot substitute
for the missing Washington leg (OR I-II p.623; Butler I p.66).

**[H] A10–A11:** On **11 May**, Hamilton's Fifth New York guard-concentration
proposal is expressly a **suggestion, not an order** for that arrangement.
On **15 May**, Townsend for Scott directs **Cadwalader**, Butler's successor,
to send the **whole surplus within his command** after retaining reasonable
guards for Baltimore, Relay House, Junction, Annapolis and their railroads.
Townsend's 16 May letter acknowledges Cadwalader's acknowledgment of the
previous day's letter, without a precise receipt time. The supposed sufficiency
of four retained regiments is an estimate, not a mandatory numerical floor
(OR I-II pp.633–634,638–640).

**Result [I]:** G2 now has read general onward authority and the qualified
Fifth intention. The actual surplus selection, each body's lawful holding,
means, onward receipt and exact Eighth/Sixty-ninth Washington directions remain
unrecovered. Arrival at Annapolis still cannot automatically mean Washington.

## 2. Washington to Virginia, June and July

**[H] V01** reuses the explicit **23 June** Scott direction through Mansfield:
**Third Connecticut and Tompkins's Second New York** to McDowell that evening.
It rejects steamers for this movement, specifies wagon/knapsack arrangements,
and asks McDowell to name the crossing and provide guides. Its predicted
crossing before eight is not receipt (OR I-II p.716). Tompkins's Second is the
Militia/later Eighty-second identity, not Second Volunteers at Fort Monroe
(Phisterer IV p.2897).

**[H] V02–V04:** **SO167, 2 July**, constitutes Burnside's and Richardson's
brigades. **SO169, 3 July**, constitutes Blenker's. The read terms name bodies
and brigade commanders under Mansfield; **they contain no river-crossing or
report-to-McDowell clause** (OR I-LI.1 pp.409–410). The CSV preserves the Rhode
Island battery component, Twelfth NY Volunteers versus Militia, and Eighth NY
Volunteers versus Lyons's Militia. Garibaldi/Thirty-ninth is an identity join
from Phisterer III p.2188, not a movement trigger.

**[H] V05:** McDowell's **4 August report** says regiments crossed late for
want of transport, many eight or nine days after the time fixed upon. This
supports a delay witness; it supplies no brigade-by-brigade dispatch list or
receipt date (OR I-II pp.317,323–324).

**Left:** addressed crossing/transfer papers and receipts for the three July
brigades. No equivalent to V01 was recovered for them in the bounded OR
correspondence search. GO13 organisation cannot retrospectively supply that
journey, and its appearance on 8 July is not a scheduled movement instruction.

## 3. The handover, 27–29 May

**[H] H01–H05** reuse and distinguish five readings:

- **GO26, 27 May:** the Government establishes McDowell's department; no
  predecessor or regimental transfer list is specified (OR I-II p.653).
- **GO1, 28 May:** McDowell explicitly assumes command in compliance with
  GO26 and organises the named brigades (OR I-LI.1 pp.389–390).
- **29 May report:** he arrived too late on 27 May to assume formally that
  day, received Sandford's information, and inspected troops on 28 May
  (OR I-II pp.653–654).
- **30 May letter:** he attests being ordered **by the Government** to relieve
  Sandford. It does not name Scott as author of that relief instruction.
  The postscript's reported Scott instruction concerns protection of the Lee
  house, a different subject (OR I-II p.655).
- **Sandford's 30 December official report:** he dates his relief and return
  to his Washington command to **28 May** (NY AG1861 pp.109–110).

**Result [I]:** the G5 handover interpretation remains supported under 0136.
No separate Scott handover instruction of 27–29 May was recovered in the read
sections. Its date, addressee and wording must not be manufactured from GO26,
the house-protection postscript or GO1. Sandford's Virginia holding is not all
his remaining Washington holding; a complete per-body handover list is absent.

## 4. The unnamed Lynchburg bodies

**[H/I] L01–L02:** Johnston's **12 June** letter acknowledges Lee's **10 June**
notice that two Lynchburg regiments had been ordered to report at Harpers
Ferry. The notice's original remains referred to but unread. Lee's **21 June**
reply identifies a President-selected **Georgia/Tennessee pair**, sent from
Lynchburg **without passing Richmond**, but gives no regimental numbers or
exact underlying dispatch dates (OR I-II pp.922,945).

**[H/I] L03–L05** are separate named controls: Turney's **First Tennessee** is
at Richmond in Lee's 1 June report; SO63 of 4 June sends Bartow's regiment;
SO65 of 6 June sends Gartrell's **Seventh Georgia** (OR I-II pp.897–898;
I-LI.2 pp.128,135). Evans's battle report identifies Bartow's **Eighth Georgia**
(OR I-II pp.558–559). None of those papers equates its body with the June10
notice's pair. Do not substitute Turney/Gartrell, Bartow/Vaughn or another
ordinal combination from the July roster.

**[H/I] L06:** Davis's **13 July** letter reports an unnamed North Carolina
war-service regiment arriving yesterday and going to Johnston's column today.
It separately describes a twelve-month mountaineer regiment sent to Garnett
and an imperfectly equipped Danville body ordered to Richmond. No regimental
number is printed for any of these three in that passage (OR I-II pp.976–977).

**[H] L07:** Beauregard's battle report names **Sixth (Fisher's) North Carolina**
at Manassas. Its heading prints **26 August [14 October]**; the covering letter
is 14 October. This is a numbered battle witness, **not a read equation with
Davis's July13 dispatch** (OR I-II pp.484,493,495). `nc_6_infantry` is therefore
only a candidate for L06. The unnamed pair and Davis direction receive no
assigned catalogue or S138 keys.

**Left:** the June10 original or an official numbered attestation, station
dispatch/arrival records that actually identify the pair, and a paper joining
the July13 North Carolina dispatch to a number. No exact pair was recovered.
