## Strengths and their limits

The [observation register](S79/strength-observations.csv) has **ten dated observations: one named-force observation and nine broader-command, place-subset or unmatched observations**. They are separate records, not ten cleared blocks. No parent total is divided, no companies are multiplied by an establishment, and no July strength is inferred from a May report.

**Garland.** Cocke's report of **14 May** identifies Garland's force, arrived on Sunday, as **490 men** (OR I-II p.841; printed page inspected). The report explicitly identifies the preceding Sunday as the 12th: the observation date is **12 May [I]**. Its measure is men in the arriving force, not a labelled present-for-duty return. The four-company battalion ordered on 10 May is documented separately (pp.821,826). This supplies a dated force observation; growth, detachments and the complete service-cohort join through July remain held.

**Preston.** The 10 May order identifies seven companies. Bonham says on **25 May** that seven companies of Preston's command had gone to Harpers Ferry, leaving only 600 infantry at Manassas overall (OR I-II pp.42,826). The 600 is not Preston's strength. A later numbered-regiment report is explicitly **Robert T. Preston's Twenty-eighth Virginia**, not proof that the early battalion had the same identity or headcount (pp.549-551). The source's J.F./R.T. Preston distinctions must be resolved before joining them; the early battalion's own strength stays held.

**Harpers Ferry.** Jackson's reports forward May 4 and May 7 strength statements, but the printed editor marks those enclosures not found (OR I-II pp.809-810,814). A **28 May** consultation describes about **5,000 effectives** for the whole force then at the Ferry (pp.889-890). Garnett's **30 May** estimate is **5,500**, expressly excluding Maryland troops and Point of Rocks (p.895). These different scopes do not identify the original Virginia lot still in the ledger. Johnston's May 23 description includes numerous formations; Rhett later explicitly cannot furnish their regimental strengths (pp.470-471,569). No missing enclosure was acquired from an archive.

The same 30 May statement gives **Norfolk 7,000, conjectured; Richmond/Ashland including Confederate troops 5,500; Williamsburg/Yorktown 3,500**, with its warnings about missing returns (OR I-II p.895). Hill's **15 June** Yorktown report says scarce 3,000 men there while other troops occupy separate places (p.927). These are retained as observations with their own measures and coverage, not the strengths of unidentified Virginia-only cohorts.

**Charleston units.** Pickens's **30 June** report says Hagood's First had declined Confederate muster and been discharged for the present. Rion's Sixth partly declined: seven companies were accepted and others were being received. Its regimental scope is not the earlier Morris Island battalion. The report also describes infantry and artillery positions without their individual strengths; 1,000 regular men armed is not the First Infantry's return (OR IV-I pp.413-414). Pickens's **18 July** telegram gives 876 men sent the previous day and 916 that day but does not name either regiment (p.487). Neither number is assigned to Rion or another opening block.

Kershaw's report places his Second South Carolina at Bull Run. Its referenced enclosure is cross-referenced to No.121, whose published table gives casualties; the brigade abstract is not a regimental return (OR I-II pp.524-527,568,570). That evidence cannot quantify the opening Charleston component. Texas company dispositions and Ford's partly fielded regiment likewise give no complete headcount for the requested lots (OR I-I pp.575-577,617-618,630-632,635-636). The June 12 Texas coast-defense memoir recommends guns and companies, rather than reporting Galveston's actual battery personnel (OR I-IV pp.92-93). Fort Morgan's earlier expected transfers remain distinct from an actual May-July return (OR IV-I pp.192-193,209).

**Disposition:** one block has a dated named-force observation; **24 still lack their own matched May-July strength**. The nine unidentified branch/raising lots need identities before a historical return can be their own. For every held row the register names the missing scope or original record. ## Charleston and South Carolina command

The [command register](S79/charleston-command.csv) keeps local Confederate command, State authority and a fort artillery command separate:

- **27 May:** the published chronology says Beauregard relinquished State volunteers to **Governor F.W. Pickens**, and transferred Confederate forces in Charleston Harbor and vicinity to **Colonel R.H. Anderson** (OR I-VI p.1, footnote; inspected). The chronology cites a May 27 letter; that letter itself was not located/read here.
- **30 June:** Pickens identifies **Anderson in command of all forces in and about Charleston**. Pickens directs State dispositions and seeks Confederate agreement. **Lieutenant-Colonel R.S. Ripley** immediately commands the specified Sumter/Moultrie artillery companies (OR IV-I pp.413-414).
- **25/27 July:** a **10 February 1862** State military communication reproduces July 25 correspondence to Mills under Anderson and describes Anderson's July 27 request for promotions in the State artillery and infantry (OR IV-I pp.913-915). This is a later account quoting July papers, not an original manuscript inspected here.
- **21 August:** Ripley's assignment to the **Department of South Carolina** is in the published chronology (OR I-VI p.1). It must not be backdated into July.

**[I] Proposed July coverage:** Anderson for the local Confederate succession, with Pickens's separate State authority. The late-July documents support continued institutional responsibility; they do not establish Anderson's daily physical headquarters or an exact departure/acting-command date. July 31 is this request's horizon, not a sourced relief date. No single officer is invented as commander of every South Carolina force regardless of authority.

## Rail comparison

The [eight-line register](S79/rail-register.csv) copies the relevant `rail_lines.csv` records and attaches opening evidence, gauge observations and qualifications. The [edge audit](S79/rail-edge-audit.csv) copies **21 exact source edges**, including shared and connecting alignments. These are comparisons to the current research-worktree pack, not a claim that S78's mutable tree has been validated.

Opening evidence: Carter's *Railroads of the South before 1860* gives the **East Tennessee & Virginia completion as 14 May 1858**, the **Virginia & Tennessee reaching Bristol by 30 September 1856**, **Petersburg completed before the end of 1833**, **Richmond & Petersburg operating 13 February 1838**, **Wilmington & Weldon completed in 1840**, and the **Orange & Alexandria reaching Gordonsville in 1854 and completing its Lynchburg extension in January 1860** (Carter pp.105-106,111,121,123-126,135-136). These are secondary chronology, not every line's opening-day company record. The Manassas Gap account supplies completion bounds for Strasburg and a later extension, not an exact Mount Jackson opening day (pp.127-128). Those unresolved days remain held.

The North Carolina company's own **1856 annual report p.10** records joining the track **29 January**, followed by through trains **30 January** between Charlotte and Goldsboro. The printed page was inspected. This supersedes an imprecise year-only opening for this comparison, without editing the pack.

**Gauge evidence needs its date.** Ashcroft's **1862** directory lists East Tennessee & Virginia and Virginia & Tennessee at **5 feet**; North Carolina, Wilmington & Weldon, Richmond & Petersburg, Orange & Alexandria and Manassas Gap at **4 feet 8.5 inches**; Petersburg at **5 feet** (pp.108-109,111,113,115,117,136). Its unnumbered introduction warns that Southern entries use the most reliable obtainable information after mails were cut off. It is contemporary evidence, but not a June-July 1861 survey. The register explicitly marks carrying those gauges into that window **[I]**. Carter's early Petersburg construction gauge differs; **the conversion date is unresolved**, not silently chosen.

Comparison findings and limits:

1. **No missing main corridor is demonstrated for the eight named lines at the pack's region resolution.** The Gordonsville-Charlottesville connection is already the `charlottesville -> culpeper_orange` **Virginia Central** edge, explicitly noted as shared with Orange & Alexandria. A search by line ID alone initially suggested a gap; checking all edge types/owners rejected it. Do not add a duplicate connection.
2. **[I] Bristol is not a demonstrated gauge break:** the two directory entries both give 5 feet. The pack note's question remains a source-review item. Same gauge alone never proves common terminals, continuous operation or unrestricted through running.
3. **[I] Transfers to review:** Lynchburg between 5-foot Virginia & Tennessee and 4-foot-8.5-inch Orange & Alexandria; Charlotte between 5-foot Charlotte & South Carolina and North Carolina; Wilmington between 5-foot Wilmington & Manchester and Wilmington & Weldon; Weldon between Wilmington & Weldon and the directory's 5-foot Petersburg; Petersburg between that road/South Side and Richmond & Petersburg. Connecting-road gauges come from Ashcroft pp.109,111,113,118-119. These are comparisons, subject to the directory's date limitation. No handling delay is invented.
4. The audited edges have blank `break_of_bulk`, and their schema has no opening-date or gauge columns. **[I]** Towns grouped within one region can conceal a terminal transfer; a blank edge field cannot certify its absence. The coast/Raleigh edge also combines the Goldsboro North Carolina approach and the Weldon Raleigh & Gaston approach. This is a resolution limit, not proof of an extra railway or a wrong regional adjacency.
5. A Danville-Greensboro extension was contemplated but not built before hostilities in Carter's account (p.119). Its absence is not evidence of a missing June-July shortcut. Exact later opening is not certified here. Johnston's actual account describes the march from Winchester through Ashby's Gap to Piedmont and the subsequent rail movement, not an all-rail Winchester-Manassas journey (OR I-II pp.473-474). The pack must not gain that shortcut from a concentration goal.
6. Existing track does not establish operating access. The Alexandria end suffered bridge destruction on 24 May (OR I-II pp.41-42). No continuous June-July Confederate access or every bridge's reopening is certified. The [topology illustration](S79/topology-check.json) finds existing source-graph rail connections into Manassas from five origins, without applying side, trace, capacity or operation rules. Its source-weight sums are **not travel-time predictions**. Rail permission/holder causes remain S78's work.
