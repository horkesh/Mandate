# Proposed record: distinguish authorized delivery and outward diplomacy from pushback

Status: **proposed, unsigned** (22 November 2026), S53. For the lead's ruling under 0106. This draft would amend the case classification accompanying 0099 item 6; it changes no accepted record, source catalogue, counter or numerical band on this branch.

## Context

0099's relay-hold floor names N3-27, Harney's relief dispatch. Its incoming-filter floor names N3-06, Seward's filed refusal to the Confederate commissioners. S37 reread the sources and sorted both as category (c): the cited act belongs to a different mechanism. The kernel's newly built carrier and disclosure paths do not change what these sources establish.

## Proposed decision [I]

1. **N3-27 is authorized conditional delivery.** Special Orders 135 is dated 16 May 1861. Lincoln's letter to Colonel F. P. Blair of 18 May explicitly leaves delivery or withholding to Blair's discretion and asks him to withhold unless urgent necessity requires otherwise. Harney's letter of 31 May reports receipt the previous evening and immediate relinquishment of command. These documents establish an authorized delivery condition, not a proven reluctant intermediary resisting his principal. Exclude this case from the verified *resistance* cases supporting relay holds; retain its dated dispatch and receipt as evidence for a future discretionary-delivery contract.

   Sources actually reread: *Official Records*, series I, volume III, **pp. 374, 381** ([edition OCR](https://archive.org/download/warofrebellionco03unit/warofrebellionco03unit_djvu.xml)); Nicolay and Hay, eds., *Abraham Lincoln: Complete Works*, volume II, **pp. 47–48**, letter of 18 May ([edition OCR](https://archive.org/download/abrahamlincolnco02linco/abrahamlincolnco02linco_djvu.txt)). Harney's subsequent intention to resume command in his letter is a different act and does not establish that Blair had withheld contrary to instructions.

2. **N3-06 is outward diplomatic handling with a negotiated delay.** The State Department memorandum is dated 15 March; its 8 April postscript directs delivery to the commissioners and describes their consent as understood. It also records presidential concurrence in declining official intercourse. It is not an incoming military wire screened before Lincoln receives it. Exclude this case from the verified cases supporting incoming-filter holds. Retain both the direction of the correspondence and the postscript's qualification about consent.

   Source actually reread: Richardson, *A Compilation of the Messages and Papers of the Confederacy*, volume I (1905), **pp. 85–88**, especially the postscript on **p. 88** ([edition OCR](https://archive.org/download/compmessages01richrich/compmessages01richrich_djvu.txt)). The postscript's account is evidence of what the department stated, not proof of every participant's understanding [I].

3. **Keep the existing table binding pending a separate quantitative ruling.** Reclassification corrects the kind of an act; it does not silently replace a missing sourced floor with a simulation count. The lead must separately decide whether new verified cases can support the current floors or whether a later accepted record should amend the numbers and their citations.

## Rejected alternatives [I]

- Count the interval between issuance and receipt as resistant holding. Neither an elapsed interval nor an authorized condition proves resistance.
- Treat Seward's outward reply as an incoming report or appoint him to an invented military-wire filter office.
- Reclassify both cases directly as slow walks. That still mistakes authorized or negotiated acts for demonstrated shirking.
- Lower the numerical floors in code now, broaden counters to absorb these cases, or declare the criterion passed because its old examples were misclassified. The accepted limits remain binding until the lead rules explicitly.

## Consequences if accepted

The lead records the amendment and routes the source-accounting question. Updating checkpoint citations, the research catalogue and any numerical limits would be a distinct, explicit follow-up; this draft applies none of them. Entrusted discretionary delivery and outward diplomatic correspondence remain separate contracts, not substitutes for incoming-filter traffic.
