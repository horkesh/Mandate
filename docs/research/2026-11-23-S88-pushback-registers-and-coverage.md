## Registers and reading dispositions

The [Union register](S88/union-register.csv) has 47 candidate rows and the
[Confederate register](S88/confederate-register.csv) has 27. Each includes the
issuing side, named actor, office, role, bounded matter, act, date, page
references, classification, reason and proposed model matter. `eligible`
means proposed for acceptance, not already accepted by the lead.
The [41-row disposition](S88/catalogue-disposition.csv) preserves every ID
from S87's inherited Appendix C list and links each to its register rows.
Every inherited row now has a source-bounded disposition or an explicit
exclusion; unverified allegations have not become verified negative facts.

The unit is side/actor/role/matter/act. Supporting letters and restatements
are not additional acts. A materially new instruction can create a new
matter. One letter can answer several distinct requests; compliance with one
does not supply the undertaking required to call a different answer a
reservation. Side follows the issuing principal, so governors' replies to
Lincoln's April call remain Union-side observations.

Material Union findings and exclusions:

- Five individually signed March 15/16 cabinet answers replace a group
  allegation: Seward, Cameron, Welles, Smith and Bates (Nicolay and Hay,
  *Complete Works of Abraham Lincoln* II, 1894, pp.11–22; OR I-I pp.196–197).
  Seward's March 29 advice and Blair's March 29 challenge concern renewed
  consultations. Blair opposes **abandonment**, not relief (Lincoln II
  pp.26–27). Scott's two-fort memorandum is distinct from his relief
  assessment, but the exact composition/receipt date is unsettled: OR files
  it among the March 15 attachments; do not silently certify the catalogue's
  March 28 date (OR I-I pp.200–201).
- Scott's March 5 endorsement is a separate first assessment, not another
  copy of the March 12 inquiry quoted in Cameron's answer. Read the complete
  LoC/Knox transcript of the six-image Holt/Scott document, digital document
  pp.1–6, including the endorsement. This cites the complete document, not
  separately inspected scans or invented printed pagination
  ([LoC manuscript 0779200](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mal.0779200/?st=text)).
- Adams invokes standing Navy instructions and requests orders; Porter
  invokes unrecalled Lincoln orders against Seward's countermand. Neither
  automatically supplies a reservation or plan objection against Lincoln
  (ORN I-IV pp.109–112,237–240). Authorized substitutions by Summers and
  conditional delivery entrusted to Blair do not establish resistance
  (Burlingame II ch.22 pp.2382–2383,2387; Lincoln II pp.47–48).
- McCauley's repeated postponement of Merrimack's removal is tied by the
  named engineer's report to the actual decision to retain her. The removal
  order and conflicting readiness accounts are retained; subsequent
  destruction is not a second delay (ORN I-IV pp.275,281,288–289).
  Patterson's depot request, later attack/demonstration dispute and response
  to fresh directions remain distinct acts (OR I-II pp.164–169).
- Harney's agreement is **excluded**, revising S87's nearest-type
  interpretation: May 21 precedes the May 27 presidential direction printed
  with it. These pages do not establish undertaking an identified earlier
  request with an imposed qualification (OR I-III pp.374–376).
- Morgan's question and undertaking remain a qualified mechanism
  interpretation, with no inferred private reluctance. The undertaking is
  p.85, not p.86. Garrett's reported carriage refusal is on p.84; Harris's
  April 17 reply is on p.81, with April 20 repetition on pp.91–92
  (OR III-I pp.72,81,84–85,91–92). Troop-call refusals are retained outside
  the seven bands, not converted to plan objections.
- The broad summer-neutrality allegation has no bounded act verified here;
  Lincoln II pp.76–77 are August correspondence, outside the window.
  Cadwalader answers a court writ, not a Lincoln order (Taney, *Reports*,
  pp.250–252). The unpaginated Cameron-contract secondary supplies no
  verified presidential refusal; OR III-I pp.327–328 concern a different
  House inquiry and cannot corroborate it. These exclusions do not prove
  that no other qualifying act existed.
- N3-06/N3-07 retain the 0109/0111 exclusions. The outward memorandum and
  Campbell assurances do not identify an incoming-report claim cut.
  Disclosure to Lincoln stays unresolved (Richardson, *Messages and Papers
  of the Confederacy* I pp.85–88,93–97; Burlingame II ch.22
  pp.2349–2351 n.87,2392). No trim or relay floor is rescued by a different
  mechanism or by elapsed time alone.

The Confederate search covers 5 March–31 July 1861 in the specifically read
correspondence, not an assertion of exhaustive coverage of every document
in that period. Governor replies supply ten proposed reservations; Bragg
and Holmes supply two more. Examples include Georgia's organization and
muster terms, Pickens's organization/consent terms, Harris's legal-authority
qualification, the July appointment protests, and Holmes's undertaking to
dispatch Bate while disputing the expedition's force and feasibility
(OR IV-I pp.135,207,214,232–236,419–424,478; OR I-II p.959).
Bragg's undertaking under supply constraints is included (OR I-I
pp.456–458). These are interpretations of stated qualifications, not
personality or covert-delay judgments.

Alabama's July 4 reply is **excluded** as a reservation on the extra armed
pair: its separate reserve/cavalry undertakings cannot supply an undertaking
of that same demand (OR IV-I p.420). Georgia's July 14/15 refusal concerns
the later actual brigade-regiment demand, distinct from the earlier
conditional reply; a troop-call refusal is not plan advice
(OR IV-I pp.424,466,477–478). Johnston's June 17 endeavor already falls
within discretion granted and confirmed by the government; it is not an
imposed reservation (OR I-II pp.924–925,934).

The three proposed Confederate plan objections are Johnston's Harpers Ferry
garrison counterproposal, Holmes's proposal to reduce the Fredericksburg
force, and Holmes's challenge to the conditional Mathias Point withdrawal
(OR I-II pp.896–897,907–910,929,932,959,975–976). Criticism without an
identified government instruction is excluded; Holmes's separate June 18
disagreement with Beauregard is not substituted for resistance to the
government (OR I-II pp.901–903,932–933).

Three source-qualified extras are **not in the recommended 33**: Welles and
Bates on blockade, read in Burlingame II ch.23 pp.2459–2460, and Toombs's
Sumter warning, ch.22 p.2403 n.264. The secondary's cited later letters,
diaries and reminiscences were not separately read; the dramatic Toombs
wording is itself disputed there. Lead acceptance of these source
qualifications would add two Union and one Confederate plan obligations,
bringing the total to 36. No exact quoted wording or private motive is
required to match them.

Match by named actor, role and bounded matter, including the direction of
the advice. Require the same requisition, not merely a nearby governor's
response. An observed model act is credited at most once; contradictory
responses are mismatches, missing acts are not observed, and insufficient
evidence is unresolved. Candidate IDs may recur when several historical
acts face one undifferentiated model consultation, but no credit is reused.
If the model never exposes the historical demand, retain an exposure
mismatch separately; do not infer behavior under an unissued instruction.

and [summary](S88/score.json) reuse the frozen S82 reference identified in
S87: commit `e2b98c818b6e5e0c2e81256e5cba4955bb7feaa2`, final hash

- Of 33 recommended obligations: **one qualified match** (Morgan), eight
  mismatches, nine unresolved, four exposure mismatches and eleven not
  observed. The three source-qualified additions are also not observed.
- Eight Sumter advice cases are unresolved because S87's retained response
  packet exposes consultation participation, not delivered advice or the
  exact proposed course. `comply` does not establish agreement. No single
  consultation is allocated to multiple Scott or Seward acts. Pickens's
  organization/consent case is the ninth unresolved case: generic transfers
  do not identify those terms.
- Three Patterson cases face model hold goals rather than the recorded
  depot/fresh attack instructions. Johnston's model goal is to take
  Manassas, not the June garrison question; S84's day 142/143 commands verify
  that objective and posture. These are exposure mismatches.
- Eight governor requisitions have contrary model responses. Exact call
  identifiers, not dates or actor-only similarity, identify the matters.
  South Carolina's reserve response cannot stand for the distinct later
  armed pair. Alabama's model reservation remains visible but gets no
  historical credit for the excluded C08.

The [complete raw diagnostics](S88/raw-response-diagnostics.csv) preserve
208 addressee and 11 relay responses, with actor/side/role/kind/actual and
declared rung, order and day; [grouped counts](S88/raw-counts.csv) sum to
219. Reservations remain Union 11 / Confederate 5, addressee slow walks
9 /9, plus two separate Cameron relay slow walks. Filter holds/trims both
have zero recorded fires. These totals are diagnostics, never compared to
33 historical cases. All 34 S84 reservation/slow-walk responses remain in
the [disposition](S88/s84-response-disposition.csv); 33 supply no coverage
credit. Unmatched does not mean historically impossible or automatically
excessive.

Reject actor-ever deduplication, Union-only counting, counting every
`object` as plan advice, borrowing another request's undertaking, and
matching by date or outcome. Reject counting all repeated accounts as acts,
using a consultation's cooperation rung as advice content, fitting ceilings
to 16/18, inferring motive from capacity, or lowering traits to fit a row.
