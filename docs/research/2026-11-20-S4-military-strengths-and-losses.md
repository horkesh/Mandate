## What the numbers mean

The numerical spine is Thomas L. Livermore, *Numbers and Losses in the Civil War in America, 1861–65* (Boston: Houghton, Mifflin, 1900), here **L**. Pages below are printed pages, not PDF image numbers. This copy is the Internet Archive scan `numberslosses00liverich`; printed page 77 is PDF page 89. Its OCR drops most of that page's table; the figures here were read from the image. Official Records citations use series, volume, part and printed page, never an Internet Archive item's apparent volume alone.

Livermore explains that the reports count different things: present for duty, present for duty equipped, or effectives. Some Confederate accounts omit officers or an entire arm. His usual reconstruction takes 93 percent of infantry/artillery present for duty and 85 percent of cavalry, but he sometimes accepts a return's effective strength instead. He also excludes some troops on returns, or even on the field, if he thinks they could not influence the fighting. These are his analytical conventions, not a uniform contemporary muster category. [L, pp. 66–70.]

**[I] Proposed use:** retain the reported basis and the author's reconstruction as separate fields. A field army's return, men available on one day, men who opened fire, and the forces accumulated across several days cannot share an unqualified `strength`. Never apply Livermore's percentages to a number he has already converted. A missing return remains unknown, not zero. Rejected: treating every number below as an exact count of men firing, or using a later strength to fill an earlier date without recording the reconstruction.

Losses are **killed / wounded / missing or captured**, in that order. Totals in parentheses are **[I] arithmetic**, the sum of those columns, not an additional source count. They are battle-return categories, not a medical statement about final deaths or permanent manpower losses. Livermore warns that some returns' missing include wounded or dead, notably in the Seven Days and Shiloh. A captured wounded man must not generate two people lost. [L, p. 64 and its note; OR I-X.1, p. 101, note to the Shiloh casualty table.]

## Bull Run as a 1A reference

Livermore's Confederate 32,232 is 21,883 for the Army of the Potomac, 8,884 for the Army of the Shenandoah and 1,465 for Holmes's brigade. The last includes his estimate of 200 for officers and artillery; he says Shenandoah's number probably includes officers. The Union 30,594 excludes Runyon's division and includes Blenker's brigade. His 28,452 is the 93-percent conversion, not a separately observed return. [L, p. 77, image.]

The OR's departmental abstract for **16–17 July**, several days before the battle, lists 1,707 officers and 35,614 men present for duty. It lists Tyler, Hunter, Heintzelman, Runyon and Miles as division commanders. Its editor explicitly warns that Tyler's fourth brigade's total and aggregate present were omitted on the original return, making its apparent present total smaller than its pfd. **[I]** The departmental 37,321 pfd and Livermore's selected 30,594 pfd answer different questions; neither establishes how many fought at a single point on 21 July. [OR I-II, p. 309; [page transcription](https://ehistory.osu.edu/books/official-records/002/0309).]

**[I] Proposed checkpoint pair, not accepted bounds:** a pre-battle departmental return may be checked separately from a force actually delivered to the Manassas operation. Use 30,000–33,000 as a coarse selected-force comparison around Livermore's 30,594 U / 32,232 C, with the exact bases attached; use 27,000–30,000 only for his converted Union effectives around 28,452. These are analyst tolerances, not sourced confidence intervals and not a claim that the simulation must reproduce his exclusions. Rejected: making 37,321 Union departmental pfd versus 32,232 Confederate effectives a single strength ratio; labelling 32,232 the men actually firing; changing 1A bands in this research lane.

The loss reference for Livermore's edition is 2,708 U / 1,981 C. The OR's compiled Confederate table instead prints 25 officers plus 362 men killed, 63 officers plus 1,519 men wounded, and **one officer plus twelve men missing**: **[I] 387 / 1,582 / 13 = 1,982**. Thus Livermore's missing column is one lower. Likewise, the OR's First Corps/Potomac return prints **21,863**, twenty below Livermore's **21,883**. Its component aggregates are 19,569 infantry, 1,468 cavalry and 826 artillery, which **[I] sum to 21,863**. Keep both discrepancies visible. Using the OR Potomac figure with Livermore's other two command figures would produce **[I] 32,212**, but that hybrid is not a newly verified contemporary total. [L, p. 77, image; OR I-II, pp. 568, 570, images.]

**[I] Preferred reference for the primary-source casualty series:** 2,708 U / 1,982 C, with the OR categories cited; retain Livermore's 1,981 as a variant. Do not silently “correct” a transcription without saying which source changed. A later revised return can supersede an earlier report for a total, while both remain evidence of what headquarters had reported on their dates.

## Cases that need explicit handling

### Donelson: reporting lag and a surrender

Grant's **16 February** report announces roughly 12,000–15,000 prisoners, describes leaving Fort Henry with about 15,000 men in two divisions on 12 February, and names McClernand, C. F. Smith and Lew Wallace in the eventual battle. It initially puts U losses at not far short of 1,200. The printed editor points to the later casualty table. **[I]** That first dispatch is evidence of Grant's knowledge and claim at the time, not the final loss count. [OR I-VII, pp. 159–160, 169.]

The compiled casualty table separates officers from enlisted men: 22 + 478 killed, 87 + 2,021 wounded, and 3 + 221 captured/missing. **[I]** These sum to **500 / 2,108 / 224 = 2,832**, agreeing with Livermore. The scan's column headings matter: 478 is enlisted dead, not total dead. [OR I-VII, p. 169, image; L, p. 78.]

Livermore rejects Pillow's approximately 13,000 total and adopts about 21,000 largely through Grant and a reconstruction of the component forces. He expressly says the basis cannot be securely classified as pfd, equipped pfd or effectives. **[I]** A Donelson strength band should therefore be a source-choice proposal, not a precision target. A surrender also requires a capture/escape state transition; treating its 14,623 missing as 14,623 battlefield deaths would fundamentally misread the evidence. [L, pp. 78–79.]

### Shiloh: two days and two Union armies

The pre-battle Army of the Tennessee abstract lists 44,895 officers and men pfd, dated by division to 4–5 April. The third division's 7,564 are explicitly noted as not in the battle of 6 April. Division staff are excluded; the sixth division's note says one regiment and one battery were not reported on the original. **[I]** A day-one battle cannot begin with Livermore's two-day 62,682 U strength. [OR I-X.1, p. 112, image; L, p. 79.]

Livermore estimates Tennessee effectives as **[I] 0.93 × (44,895 + 1,000), rounded to 42,682**; his added 1,000 covers the missing regiment/battery group. His Ohio contribution is about 20,000 in the second, fourth and fifth divisions. He excludes the sixth Ohio division because it did not arrive in time to influence the decision. The Confederate before/after return gives 40,335 before and 29,636 after, but the editor warns those columns do not agree with the detailed field returns immediately following. [L, pp. 79–80; OR I-X.1, pp. 396, 398–399.]

**[I]** The reduction of 10,699 between the OR return's columns is not automatically the published casualty total of 10,694: it also depends on which return is used and whether other absence/return movements are included. Preserve the five-person discrepancy rather than inventing a cause. The source itself warrants caution. A campaign comparison should preserve arrival times and the ability of each division to enter the battle, not infer superior commander traits from the two-day aggregate.

### The Seven Days: overlapping windows

The 91,169 / 95,481 whole-campaign strengths and 15,849 / 20,614 losses already include the subsidiary actions. The 83,345 / 86,748 row applies to the grouped 29 June–1 July withdrawal battles, not to Malvern Hill alone. **[I]** Adding those rows together would double-count people and casualties. [L, pp. 82–86.]

Livermore reconstructs Confederate forces from the **20 July** return by exclusions, added-back losses and estimates for Jackson and Ewell. His footnotes discuss whether artillery/officers are included and even whether July's pfd fraction was too low for late June. **[I]** It is usable as an attributed later estimate; it is unsuitable as an exact contemporary report Lincoln received before the campaign. Nor does the grouped table establish a distinct loss count at Glendale or Malvern Hill. [L, pp. 83–86.]

### Cedar Mountain and the Second Manassas window

At Cedar Mountain, Livermore intentionally excludes forces arriving after he judges the battle decided, and excludes their casualties too. His U 8,030 and C 16,868 are a deliberately restricted comparison. The note discusses the later-arriving Union division, whose commander name is corrupted in the scan's OCR. **[I]** That name is not transcribed into the cast from OCR alone. [L, pp. 87–88.]

His **Manassas and Chantilly** total is not a 30 August snapshot. It includes Taylor's brigade, Hooker and Kearny, the Ninth Corps, four Kanawha regiments and the Fifth Corps group; it excludes Banks's corps for the specified fighting window. Losses include some earlier Rappahannock casualties. **[I]** Keep separate event keys for the campaign total, Second Manassas and Chantilly; do not debit the entire 16,054 Union loss a second time after using a Second Manassas loss table. [L, pp. 88–89.]

### South Mountain and Antietam: a contested reconstruction

South Mountain's Union strength is explicitly built from I and IX Corps; **[I]** it must not silently absorb Franklin's separate Crampton's Gap action. Livermore's Confederate South Mountain killed, wounded and missing rely partly on inferred proportions and prisoners. He discusses several different strength computations. These numbers are not direct headcounts. [L, pp. 90–91 and footnotes.]

At Antietam, Livermore's U 75,316 includes about 23,000 in VI Corps and Sykes's division that did not open fire in line of battle; Morell's division is excluded by his convention. His C 51,844 is built from a September return, later cavalry strength and added-back estimated losses. He quotes Lee's claim of fewer than 40,000 and argues against it. His 2,700 Confederate dead, 9,024 wounded and 2,000 missing are also reconstructed, not a complete Confederate casualty return. [L, pp. 92–94.]

**[I] Proposed ruling:** retain both the commander's attributed report and the later reconstruction, with one chosen only for a named purpose. A single narrow Antietam C band would prejudge this dispute. Rejected: averaging “under 40,000” and 51,844 into a supposedly neutral exact number; turning Livermore's dispute into a proven accusation of a commander's dishonesty; using losses alone to establish the combatants' competence or courage. The latter would import Livermore's evaluative argument as a game stat without the contemporary leg decision 0087 requires.

### Corinth and Perryville: selected men versus the army nearby

At Corinth, the revised U table gives the same 2,520 total used above. Rosecrans's own report initially says 315 killed, 1,812 wounded and 232 missing, and the editor directs the reader to the revision. It also claims 38,000 enemy, substantially different from Livermore's approximately 22,000 based on field returns. **[I]** Keep Rosecrans's account as an attributed report; use the revised return for the selected casualty series. [OR I-XVII.1, pp. 170, 173–176; L, p. 94.]

Livermore deducts losses at Hatchie's Bridge, 5 October, from the Confederate 3–5 October figures. Thus his C 4,233 is a Corinth 3–4 October figure; **[I]** Hatchie's Bridge needs its own event and must not be removed from an army twice. [L, pp. 94–95, footnote.]

For Perryville, Buell reports about **58,000** effective infantry, artillery and cavalry advancing on the town, including about 22,000 raw troops. He describes how much of Crittenden's corps did not enter the battle. Livermore's selected U 36,940 already excludes more of the nearby army, and his note says another six brigades could be excluded to produce fewer than 25,000. **[I]** 58,000 nearby, 36,940 selected and under 25,000 more narrowly engaged are not rival census claims about the same set. [OR I-XVI.1, pp. 1026–1028; L, p. 95.]

The revised OR table's final columns are 39 officers + 806 men killed, 94 + 2,757 wounded, and 13 + 502 missing: **[I] 845 / 2,851 / 515 = 4,211**, agreeing with Livermore. Its grand-total cell nevertheless prints **4,241**. Buell's narrative gives an earlier 4,348, with an editorial direction to the revision. **[I]** Prefer the explicit component sum for this series and preserve the printed total as an arithmetic discrepancy; do not invent thirty additional casualties. [OR I-XVI.1, pp. 1031, 1036, latter image; L, p. 95.]

## Suggested checkpoint design inputs

These are **[I] proposals for the owner of the 1B plan**, not changes to accepted criteria and not statistical confidence intervals.

| Checkpoint question | Evidence usable now | Recommended comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Does a force reach a battle in time? | Shiloh division arrival distinction; grouped Second Manassas forces | Compare dated arrivals and an entered-battle predicate before a total strength. |
| Does a field army survive a defeat? | Seven Days and Perryville reports distinguish retirement from destruction | Compare field-army continuity, route/base and subsequent orders separately from tactical result. |
| Are battle losses on an intelligible scale? | Attributed killed/wounded/missing series above | Bind a source, window and category to each criterion; broad tolerances require a stated representation reason. |
| Does headquarters know the true number? | First dispatches at Donelson and Corinth differ from revised returns | Validate received reports against their dates; do not give a principal the post-war reconstruction. |
| Are captured men still treated as people? | Donelson's surrender and Shiloh's captured/missing categories | Research establishes categories only; exchange, parole, prison conditions and consequences stay with S7 and the gate reviewer. |

## Remaining source work and limits

The companion notes extend the reading into smaller actions, dated organisation and naval operations. This numerical spine does **not** supply reliable division-by-division headcounts for every day, a reconciled contemporary Antietam C return, or separate losses for every Seven Days battle. Those cells must stay qualified or held until the relevant reports are read. An exact-looking number inherited from an older research note is not automatically verified by this note.
