## Reading rules

Citations name the Official Records (OR), series I, volume and part, with printed pages. Original scans, selected extracts, numerical images, acquisition records and logs stay in `C:/Pyrrhic/mandate-research-sources/S4/S55/`. S4/S14/S21/S31 scans are copied read-only into that folder; their inventories remain intact. Only selected passages are read, not whole volumes. OCR searches are finding aids. Images control numerical columns.

The formation register gives the army, corps/wing where the source has one, division, brigade and commanders for each campaign window. It deliberately adds no missing echelon. A semicolon in a commander field records successive holders in an interval roster, not simultaneous command or a complete tenure chronology. The window is the source's campaign observation, not a daily strength validity interval. Attachments, columns and territorial titles keep their source character. Corps of Observation is Stone's contemporary title, not a later numbered army corps. An inherited unit title does not prove its namesake remained in command.

The strengths register is one observation per date/category. Its `strength_date` may be an interval or an expressly relative date when the source gives no exact day. Narrative reported men, bounds and estimates are separated from abstracts/returns. No report statement is certified as a duty return. An absent formation quantity remains explicitly unknown in `strength_status`; a partial measure does not establish a complete command headcount. These unknowns remain integration holds. No regiment count is multiplied, no parent is allocated, and no casualty ratio is inverted.

Inclusive army/corps/division/brigade totals overlap and must never be added together. Successive snapshots and temporary attachments likewise must not duplicate manpower. Ordered forces, enemy intelligence, prisoners, casualty totals, gun counts and establishments are not own-force strength. Stores are not inferred. No live pack input, generated output, checkpoint, band, cast/service row, gate placement or signature changes.

## First batch: August 1861?April 1862

Wilson's Creek uses the report's columns and brigades, not invented corps. Schofield's main-column about 4,000 excludes about 250 mounted Home Guards; Sturgis's 3,700 is retained as a separate statement. Sigel's 900 describes infantry only. McCulloch's 5,300 infantry and 6,000 armed horsemen remain separate; unarmed horsemen have no count. Price's State force and Pearce's Arkansas State command retain their distinct identity. [OR I-III pp.60,65,69,86,100-106.]

At Ball's Bluff, Stone's two routes and Baker's discretion keep operational command distinct from which troops actually crossed. Evans reports a force of 1,709 aggregate in the engagement; the read report is not a certified whole-brigade duty return. His reserve at Edwards Ferry is not silently counted as men fighting on the bluff. Union strength remains unknown as a complete command in the read passages. [OR I-V pp.293-296,348-350.]

Tilghman's Fort Henry brigade statements, 1,444 and 1,215 officers and men, coexist with his separate grand-total statement of 2,610 and Gilmer's about 3,200. They are not reconciled by invention. Grant's about 15,000 leaving Henry on 12 February is a departure observation, not his final Donelson total after reinforcements. Wallace says the nominal Second Brigade of his Third Division never assembled as such; it is not instantiated. Donelson's borrowed Baldwin regiments and sortie wings overlap administrative divisions. [OR I-VII pp.135,137,140,159-160,192,236,329,338-339.]

At Pea Ridge, Sigel's First/Second division commands were combined and exchanged. Osterhaus's task force contains troops from several formations; it is not a second copy of his division. Carr's two-day report gives 1,790 infantry, 469 cavalry and 204 artillery personnel: **[I] 2,463**, retaining the original arm counts. Price's 6,818 is the earlier advance force; Van Dorn's less-than-14,000 is an exclusive entered-action bound, not equality. No adversary estimate of Pike's command is promoted into its strength. [OR I-VIII pp.208-209,217-218,240,261,285,305.]

Shiloh's Union abstract supplies brigade duty aggregates for 4?5 April, excluding division staffs. It calls the First Division's original document a statement of effective force; its own duty-column label is retained with that warning. Lew Wallace's return is 4 April and his division did not fight on 6 April. Prentiss's original omits two regiments and a battery. Neither these abstracts nor the two-day roster certify simultaneous engaged strength. [OR I-X.1 pp.100-107,112.]

Jordan's Confederate before-battle summary explicitly disagrees with the detailed returns. The separate 3 April table gives **[I] 2,587 + 37,011 = 39,598** duty officers and men, **38,773 effective total**, and **49,444 aggregate present**. Its infantry, artillery and cavalry columns remain distinct from the summary's 40,335. No synthetic casualty total is made by subtraction. Hardee and Breckinridge have directly subordinate brigades; no invented division is inserted. [OR I-X.1 pp.382-384,396,398.]
