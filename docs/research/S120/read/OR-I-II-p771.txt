Extract  from  the  Executive  Journal  of  the  State  of  Virginia.
APRIL  18,  1861.
*******
The  following  order  was  issued,  through  the  adjutant-general,  to  Maj.
Gen.  William  B.  Taliaferro :
Yon  will  forthwith  take  command  of  the  State  troops  which  are  now  or  may  be  as
sembled  at  the  city  of  Norfolk.  Your  immediate  presence  there  is  necessary.
The  governor  appointed  and  commissioned  the  following  officers  for
the  State  Navy:
Eobert  B.  Pegram,  captain,  to  rank  as  such  from  18th  of  April,  1861.
George  T.  Sinclair,  captain,  to  rank  as  such  from  19th  of  April,  1861.
Catesby  Ap  E.  Jones,  captain,  to  rank  as  such  from  20th  of  April.
1861.
James  H.  Eochelle,  lieutenant,  to  rank  as  such  from  the  18th  of  April.
1861.
The  following  order  was  issued  to  Capt.  Eobert  B.  Pegram  :
SIR  :  You  will  proceed  to  Norfolk  and  there  assume  command  of  the  naval  station,
with  authority  to  organize  naval  defenses,  enroll  and  enlist  seamen  and  marines,  and
temporarily  to  appoint  warrant  officers,  and  do  and  perform  whatever  may  be  neces
sary  to  preserve  and  protect  the  property  of  the  commonwealth  and  of  the  citizens  of
Virginia.
Co-operate  with  the  land  forces  under  the  command  of  Maj.  Gen.  William  B.  Talia
ferro,  and  report  all  important  acts  which  may  be  done  or  performed  under  your  orders
promptly  to  the  executive,  through  the  general  in  command.

JOHN  LETCHER.
PETERSBURG,  April  20,  1861.
L.  P.  WALKER  :
Governor  Letcher  has  stopped  three  steamers  on  James  Eiver,  and
may  stop  two  more.  They  can  put  seven  thousand  men  in  Baltimore  in
twenty-four  hours  from  here  by  our  connections  with  the  railroads  front
Lynchburg  to  Dalton.  We  can  carry  from  five  to  seven  thousand  men
daily  at  the  rate  of  three  hundred  and  fifty  miles  per  day.  Georgia  car&
can  be  run  through  without  unloading.  The  South  Side  Eailroad  is  at
the  service  of  the  Confederate  States.
H.  D.  BIED,
Superintendent.
PETERSBURG,  VA..,  April  20, 1861.
L.  P.  WALKER  :

just  reached  here  from  Baltimore  by  way  of  Norfolk.  He  witnessed  the
butchery  of  Baltimore  citizens  by  the  Massachusetts  regiment  yester
day.  He  states  the  city  is  in  arms  and  all  are  Southern  men  now.  He
