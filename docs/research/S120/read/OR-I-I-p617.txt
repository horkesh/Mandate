J.  H.  Eeagan,  Postmaster- General,  C.  8.:
Dear  Sir  :  I  returned  here  late  yesterday  evening  from  Powderhorn.
Colonel  Van  Dorn  has  not  succeeded  in  engaging  many  of  the  oflicers
or  soldiers  to  join  the  Army  of  the  Confederate  States.
There  are  some  five  hundred  soldiers  assembled  here,  and  two  men-
of-war  and  five  sea-steamer  transport  vessels  lying  outside  our  bar  to
receive  the  troops  here  and  as  they  arrive,  and  the  Fashion  is  chartered
by  Captain  King  to  remain  here  and  lighter  the  men  to  the  sea  vessels.
I  very  much  fear  the  plan  of  Lincoln  is  to  delay  delivering  up  Fort
Sumter  until  the  whole  Texas  Army  can  be  concentrated  for  an  attack
on  Pensacola,  and  by  a  brilliant  stroke  arouse  Northern  enthusiasm  in
favor  of  coercion.
I  shall  start  to  Austin  to-day  to  petition  the  legislature  to  give  State
aid  to  establish  a  line  of  steamships  to  run  on  the  Gulf.
Ought  not  President  Davis  to  giVe  immecKate  orders  that  all  the
steamships  in  the  Gulf  should  be  sei2;ed  at  the  ports  where  they  may  be
found  upon  the  first  spark  of  war  ?
Our  towns  are  entirely  undefended,  and  those  now  carrying  the  mails
are  at  the  mercy  of  an  enemy  having  steamers  that  can  cross  our  bars.
Morgan  and  Harris  are  both  at  Powderhorn.
Yours,  in  haste,
HUGH  W.  HAWES.
