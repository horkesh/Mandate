Confederate  States  of  America,  War  Department,
Montgomery,  March  8,  1861.
His  Excellency  Jefferson  Davis,
President  of  Confederate  States  of  America:
Sir:  Since  my  eommunication  of  the  4th  instant,*  in  which  I  had
the  honor  to  submit  the  Army  estimates  for  twelve  months,  the
Congress  has  passed  an  act  authorizing  the  President  to  call  into
the  service  of  the  Confederate  States  any  ii'nmber  of  volunteers,  not
exceeding  100,000.  The  estimates  heretofore  submitted  by  me  were
based  upon  the  bills  pending  before  Congress  and  afterward  passed,
and  were  only  intended  for  the  provisional  forces  and  the  Army.
Under  these  circumstances  I  deem  it  proper  to  call  your  attention  to
this  fact,  and  to  suggest  an  additional  appropriation  by  Congress  of
$5,000,000  for  the  volunteer  service,  in  the  event  it  should  become
necessary  to  organize  such  a  force.
I  have  the  honor  to  be,  very  respectfully,  your  obedient  servant,
L.  P.  WALKER,
Secretary  of  War.

troops  now  in  the  service  of  your  State  to  the  Provisional  Army  of  the
Confederate  States.  The  third  section  of  that  act-refere  to  the  troops
already  in  the  service  of  the  State  governments,  who  must  be  tendered
by  the  respective  State  authorities,  and  also  to  such  troops  not  in  the
service  of  the  States  as  may  volunteer  with  the  consent  of  the  States.

Army  of  the  Confederate  States  must  necessarily  be  slow  and  unsatis-

situation.  Under  these  circumstances  the  main  reliance  of  this  Gov-
