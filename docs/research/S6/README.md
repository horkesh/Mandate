## The opening-cast candidate: McClellan

Sherman's letter to John of **20 May 1861** explicitly assesses McClellan as professionally qualified and refers to the opportunities he had in Mexico and Europe. The letter starts on printed p120; the appraisal is on p121. This is someone else's contemporary judgment, not McClellan praising himself, an appointment order, or the editor's later verdict. **[I] competence above** is a restrained candidate for the innate exception; one informed voice does not warrant high. The date and appraisal were checked on the scans. It is the sole row marked `START_INNATE_CANDIDATE`. [*The Sherman Letters*, pp120-121](https://archive.org/details/shermanlettersco00sher/page/120).

That does **not** rescue McClellan's opening reputation. John Sherman's **15 February 1862** letter harshly describes the military view of him, while William's reply, printed with an editorial **23 February** date, warns John against underrating him. These are opposed, particular voices, and are both too late for starting reputation. General reputation remains HOLD in S6 rather than turning one hostile or favorable correspondent into a national consensus. [*Sherman Letters*, p140](https://archive.org/details/shermanlettersco00sher/page/140).

McClellan's policy letter of **7 July 1862** explicitly prioritizes preservation of Constitution and Union. Its opposition to forcible abolition is qualified: the passage also recognizes statutory protection of people reaching Union lines and discusses compensated action. **[I] union** and a **gated draft slavery_noninterference** observation follow those actual words, with the qualifications carried in the row. It does not establish a universal policy of returning fugitives or an abolition value. [OR I-11-1, pp73-75](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609560/page/73).

The **28 June** telegram printed in his report does include, on p61, the accusation against Stanton and the government that the earlier people README marked page-unverified. The scan resolves **what this printing contains**. It does not independently resolve what the telegraph office transmitted or struck out, nor certify his enemy-strength claims. **[I] regard toward Stanton below** is tied to the attributed words. Neither the retrospective connective narrative nor his own battlefield appraisal is a new leg for his competence or nerve. [OR I-11-1, p61](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609560/page/61).

Lincoln's **13 October 1862** letter criticizes over-caution and makes a detailed case for movement. The OCR misreads the heading; the scan reads the 13th. Its closing explicitly says it is not an order. **[I] boldness below** is therefore a dated operational assessment, not proof of cowardice, or of disobeying an order in this letter. [Lincoln, *Complete Works*, II pp245-247](https://archive.org/details/completeworksofa02linc_0/page/245).

## Halleck, Grant, Buell and Sherman: preserve the argument's two sides

Halleck's **16 February 1862** message insists on Donelson and Clarksville rather than dissipating effort toward Nashville; p625 expressly accepts sacrifice to take Donelson. His **16 March** instruction to Grant bars an engagement until strength makes success secure, but leaves local detail to Grant. **[I] average boldness**, with a dated **caution** priority, preserves both instead of making him uniformly timid. His **9 March** demand for order and system supports **[I] order**. None of these orders proves his own organising gift. [OR I-7, pp624-625](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609545/page/624); [OR I-10-2, pp22, 41](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077728230/page/22).

The **March 1862** Grant dispute is not a verdict that Halleck's allegations were true. Halleck's adverse official view on the **6th** supports a dated **[I] regard toward Grant below**. Grant's replies describe the reporting route and disputed delays. On the **7th** he offers to be removed if unsatisfactory; on the **14th** he resumes command after clarification and explicitly commits his efforts to the cause. **[I] candour above and duty** attach to that conduct. They do not establish every factual claim, permanent resentment, insubordination, drunkenness or fraud. [OR I-10-2, pp4-6, 13-15, 36](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077728230/page/4).

Grant's **16 February** demand for immediate unconditional surrender says he will move against the works. **[I] boldness above** rests on that operative demand. His own Shiloh report is not a leg for his own innate gifts. [OR I-7, p161](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609545/page/161).

Buell's **6-10 March** messages discuss rivers, transport, supply and the position from which to unite and advance. Halleck's impatience is a countervoice, not an adjudication that the obstacles were imaginary. Buell's own explanations do not justify below nerve. Lincoln's **6 and 13 January** messages distinguish his political urgency over East Tennessee from a formal order and expressly allow Buell's contrary clear military judgment unless ordered. The relief instruction printed with an editorial **24 October** date is an office event, not a gift rating or proof of the actual hand-over date. Buell's core centres remain HOLD in this pass. [OR I-10-2, pp10-11, 22-23, 27](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077728230/page/10); [OR I-7, pp927-928](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609545/page/927); [OR I-16-2, pp640-641](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730228/page/640).

Grant's **9 April 1862** report explicitly credits Sherman's judgment and skill, describes his wound and three horses shot under him, and his continued presence. **[I] competence above and nerve above**, limited to the division-command evidence, therefore have a third-party contemporary leg. Sherman's **16 April** letter to John expresses confidence in Halleck, Buell and Grant by name. The three **[I] professional-regard above** rows use the letter, not the editor's narrative above it. These are all later observations, not July-1861 innate evidence or March-1861 relationships. [OR I-10-1, p110](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730160/page/110); [*Sherman Letters*, p141](https://archive.org/details/shermanlettersco00sher/page/141).

## Pope, Burnside and Stanton: words, actions and gifts

Pope's **14 July 1862** operational message urges cavalry forward to destroy rail communications. His address on the same date declares an offensive policy and explicitly promises distinction and glory. **[I] boldness above and glory** have a conduct/declaration leg. His own boasts cannot establish his competence, nerve or reputation. Nor does rhetorical dismissal of bases prove that all his actual supply arrangements were absent. Those cells remain HOLD. [OR I-12-3, pp473-474](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730145/page/473).

Burnside's detailed Roanoke report begins on p75, dated **14 February 1862**, and expressly distinguishes the hasty dispatch of the **10th**. Its commendation of Goldsborough and the fleet on p80 supports **[I] professional regard toward Goldsborough above**. Reno's independent **10 February** report describes Burnside ordering the landing to wait for boat howitzers, followed by landing under gunboat cover. **[I] joint_minded** has a specific coordination act as its leg, alongside Burnside's own arrangements. Own-operation success is not automatically an innate competence appraisal. A December admission cannot establish November self-doubt and is excluded. His competence, nerve, general reputation, principal regard and organising disposition remain HOLD here. [OR I-9, pp75, 79-81](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion09unit/page/75), [pp97-98](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion09unit/page/97).

Stanton's **24 January 1862** request seeks discretion to change the ordnance bureau for greater activity; Lincoln authorizes that discretion. Permission does not prove an organising gift. The signed **29 January** order demands written and filed supply contracts, revokes outstanding foreign-purchase authorities, and sets a deadline for notice and copies of claims. **[I] order** is supported by this administrative act. Do not turn the order's prima-facie treatment of unfiled claims into a finding that a named contractor was guilty of fraud. Candour, personal regard and the competence/organiser centres remain HOLD. [Lincoln, *Complete Works*, II p118](https://archive.org/details/completeworksofa02linc_0/page/118); [OR III-1, pp869-870](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079622175/page/869).

## Lee, Jackson, Johnston and the western Confederate commanders

Lee's **3 September 1862** Maryland proposal discloses limits in clothing, shoes, transport and ammunition and explicitly does not propose besieging Washington. **[I] boldness above and candour above** describe proposing a risky movement while stating its constraints. His thanks to Davis on the following day is ordinary official courtesy; personal regard remains HOLD. His proposal is not his own competence certificate. [OR I-19-2, pp590-591](https://archive.org/details/cu31924080772233/page/590).

Jackson's **31 January** answer reports obedience to Benjamin's recall instruction and then asks for reassignment or resignation. **[I] candour above** is the explicit adverse disclosure. `insubordinate` and `rank_proud` are held: this episode establishes neither disobedience nor a precedence-of-rank dispute. Johnston's **7 February** endorsement regrets the request and his inability to replace Jackson; **[I] professional regard above** is qualified by the shortage of general officers. Loring's **31 January** account and **28 January** enclosure preserve the countercase about sickness, supply and exposure. [OR I-5, pp1053-1055](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730194/page/1053).

Johnston's **29 January** regret at Beauregard's transfer supports a separate **[I] professional-regard above** observation. His **1 February** administrative protest supports **[I] candour above**, because he directly states disagreement about furloughs and personnel control. His self-description as frank is not the leg, and his asserted consequences remain attributed. The cabinet note discusses Benjamin's side. [OR I-5, pp1051, 1057-1058](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730194/page/1051).

Beauregard's **11 April 1862** Shiloh report explicitly names **Polk, Bragg, Hardee and Breckinridge**, credits ability and initiative in the absence of instructions, and describes their personal leadership under fire. It supplies **[I] competence above and nerve above** for those four, bounded by the corps/reserve roles and the report's official, interested nature. Bragg's horses and Hardee's and Breckinridge's exposure provide specific detail. It does not award army-level genius. Beauregard's own report is not a leg for his own gifts. [OR I-10-1, date p385 and appraisal p389](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730160/page/389).

Bragg's short **12 October** report records the decision to attack and the subsequent retreat. **[I] boldness above** is bounded by those two acts and the circumstances he reports. His complaint that Kentuckians did not rally does not alone establish a persistent `shifts_blame` disposition. Polk's opposing account is dated only **November 1862** in the printing; it is not assigned a day. The long Bragg report beginning on p1088 and the circulated Buckner exchange elsewhere in the volume are dated in **May 1863** and are excluded as contemporary legs for the November cast. [OR I-16-1, pp1087-1088, 1109](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079609594/page/1087).

## Governors: read the signatures, not a generic loyal-governor label

The **24 September 1862 Altoona address**, pp582-584, declares commitment to national preservation and confidence in Lincoln. Its printed signers are Curtin, Andrew, Yates, Washburn, Salomon, Kirkwood, **Morton by D. G. Rose his representative**, Sprague, Peirpoint, Tod, Berry and Blair. **[I] union and principal regard above** are dated public commitments. They do not prove private affection, universal abolition commitments, innate organising ability or battlefield nerve. Its endorsement of emancipation is the conditional preliminary policy, not a statement that every signer favored every later form of abolition. [OR III-2, pp582-584](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079575241/page/582).

Morgan and Buckingham are **not** in that printed list. A different, printed **28 June** reinforcement address on p180 includes them and other names. The **30 June** proposed memorial and circulation instructions on p181 make exact individual signing dates unsafe without further research. Do not silently copy the Altoona roster or date onto that document. The seven additional governor records drawn from it remain HOLD for judgments. J. B. Temple's p180 title is President of Kentucky's Military Board, not governor; he is not added as a governor. [OR III-2, pp180-181](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079575241/page/180).

Brown's **22 April 1862** protest to Davis combines constitutional/State authority objections to conscription with explicit concern for maintaining plantation supervision and control of enslaved people. **[I] state_autonomy plus slavery_defence**, both as gate drafts with the slavery term costOnly, retains the motive instead of sanitizing it into constitutional language alone. His declared refusal of State enrolment assistance and threatened protection of the legislature support **[I] candour above and political boldness above**. He also expressly says he does not impugn Davis's intentions: principal regard remains HOLD. His manpower estimates are claims, not S6's audited checkpoint quantities. [OR IV-1, pp1082-1085](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079579532/page/1082).

Vance's **13 November** complaint to Memminger, Pettus's **15 November** State-unit proposal, and Shorter's **20 November** address to Seddon are handled individually in the cabinet note. Their administrative objections alone do not establish below regard toward Davis, a competence band, or an autonomy motive detached from slavery. No governorship title is a leg for an organising gift. [OR IV-2, pp178-179, 196](https://archive.org/details/cu31924080778909/page/178).

Milton's opening, recovered on the p56 scan, dates his letter to Florida's congressional delegation **18 August 1862**. He asks for local manpower and explicitly seeks the ability to control enslaved people and prevent their reaching Union forces. **[I] slavery_defence** is therefore a dated, costOnly gate draft. The local-defense request does not by itself prove a separate constitutional State-autonomy motive or a below relationship toward Davis. The copy is certified on p59 by his private secretary. His allegations about trading fraud, ships and economic capacity remain his allegations, not S6's findings about the blockade or the enslaved population. [OR IV-2, pp56-59](https://archive.org/details/cu31924080778909/page/56).

## Proposed rulings and rejected alternatives

1. **Keep the dated research separate from the start apply.** Let the lead decide how 1B observations attach to a snapshot or an update. Reject moving 0085's boundaries, backdating late evidence, and allowing S6 HOLD to erase vetted 1861 cells.
2. **Send McClellan competence above to the data/review lane as the sole temporal opening candidate.** Reject treating the May letter as March reputation or multiplying it into nerve, organiser and principal regard. The candidate remains subject to the whole-branch review.
3. **Use restrained, role-bounded bands.** Reject high centres inferred from office, victory alone, self-appraisal, an appointment expectation, or the arithmetic of confirmation votes. Reputation needs an outside voice and the audience must stay visible.
4. **Keep office facts separate from motive and gifts.** Retain the cabinet note's Randolph acceptance correction, March acting overlap and Seddon date conflict. Reject inventing dates or resolving provisional people from names remembered rather than read.
5. **Keep the four sensitive-history value recommendations as drafts.** Route Brown's two linked motives, Milton's coercive motive and McClellan's qualified policy to the gate reviewer. Reject placements, signatures, a slavery benefit, or dropping the stated motive to make a Confederate objection look only constitutional.
6. **Retain contested and negative results.** HOLD a disposition when the act does not establish it. Reject labeling Jackson insubordinate from a resignation request, Bragg blame-shifting from one report, Buell cowardly from transport objections, or every public supporter Lincoln's private friend.
