## 2. Union cast

### 2.1 Abraham Lincoln (principal)
- **Posts:** President, 4 Mar 1861 – (whole scenario). Commander in chief; issues War Orders directly (Jan–Mar 1862). [CWN-GWO], [OCWC-WO3]
- **Forces:** none directly; acts via general-in-chief / Secretary of War.
- **Traits [I]:** boldness 650 (ordered general advance by 22 Feb 1862 over McClellan's objections [CWN-GWO]); candour 750 (n/a as reporter; kept emancipation draft secret until a victory: see timeline, verify); competence 750; standing 550 at start (minority 1860 president; lost House seats 1862 [Wiki-1862]); regard n/a.
- **Values [I]:** union, party.
- **Notes:** player character. Lincoln's own pressure tools seen in sources: direct War Orders; personal visits to the army (Harrison's Landing 8 Jul 1862 [CWM-HL]; Antietam early Oct 1862 [MLWH-McC]); private letter urging McClellan to move, 13 Oct 1862 [MLWH-McC]; living in the telegraph office from 1862 [WETA-Tel], [ECWC-Tel].

### 2.2 Winfield Scott
- **Posts:** General-in-chief to 1 Nov 1861 (retired at own request, General Orders No. 94). [EV-McC], [ECWC-McC]
- **Forces:** nominally all US land forces; in practice bypassed by McClellan from Aug 1861.
- **Traits [I]:** boldness 250 (author of slow strangulation "Anaconda" approach: verify source); candour 750; competence 600 (aged 75, infirm [OCWC-McC]); standing 600 (Mexican War hero); regard 400.
- **Values [I]:** duty, order.
- **Pushback / friction [D]:** Scott resented McClellan bypassing the chain of command and dealing directly with cabinet members [EV-McC]; McClellan worked to undermine and replace him [MLWH-McC]. Whether Scott's retirement was voluntary is disputed (ill health vs pressure from McClellan's allies) [OCWC-McC], [Smith-Nov61].
- **Game note [I]:** a short-lived actor in Aug–Oct 1861; his main mechanical role is an obstacle McClellan lobbies to remove.

### 2.3 George B. McClellan
- **Posts:** commander, Division/Army of the Potomac 26 Jul 1861 (army named 20 Aug); general-in-chief 1 Nov 1861 – 11 Mar 1862; Army of the Potomac to 7 Nov 1862 (field command interrupted in late Aug 1862 when most troops went to Pope; Washington defences 2 Sep; field command again 5–7 Sep). [EV-McC]
- **Forces:** Army of the Potomac, roughly 100,000–120,000 effectives on the Peninsula (figure: verify; see section 3). Lincoln withheld McDowell's corps (~one third) in spring 1862 [EV-McC].
- **Traits [I]:**
  - boldness 150: repeatedly overestimated enemy numbers and delayed (Yorktown, Seven Days, post-Antietam) [EV-McC], [MLWH-McC].
  - candour 200: inflated enemy strength reports; blamed Washington ("you have done your best to sacrifice this army") [EV-McC]; private letters contemptuous of Lincoln while publicly deferential [MLWH-McC].
  - competence 650: Lincoln would "back General McClellan against any general of modern times" as organiser, but he "will never get ready to go forward" [MLWH-McC].
  - standing 800: popular with troops; leading Democrat; restored in Sep 1862 despite cabinet and congressional opposition [MLWH-McC].
  - regard −400: called Lincoln "the original gorilla" (17 Nov 1861), cabinet "the greatest geese" (10 Oct 1861) [MLWH-McC].
- **Values [I]:** reputation, conciliation (Harrison's Landing letter opposes "forcible abolition" and confiscation [RUG-HL]; Democrat opposed to abolition [EV-McC]).
- **Pushback episodes [D unless marked]:**
  1. Aug–Oct 1861: bypasses Scott, deals directly with cabinet, undermines Scott [EV-McC], [MLWH-McC].
  2. Fall 1861: resists Lincoln's push for an advance on Manassas, citing inflated estimates of Johnston's strength [EV-McC].
  3. 13 Nov 1861: snubbed Lincoln by going to bed while the president waited at his house (John Hay diary) — *not found in sources consulted; needs research*.
  4. Jan 1862: when Lincoln consulted his generals during his illness, McClellan came in person to reassert his command (6 Jan meeting context) [MLWH-McC].
  5. Jan–Mar 1862: did not meet the 22 Feb general-advance date; pretext for War Order No. 3 removing him as general-in-chief [CWN-GWO], [OCWC-WO3].
  6. Apr–May 1862: siege at Yorktown against inferior force; Lincoln's "slows" [EV-McC].
  7. 28 Jun 1862: telegram accusing Stanton/government of trying to "sacrifice this army"; sentence struck by the telegraph supervisor so Stanton never saw it [EV-McC]. (Game mechanic precedent: an intermediary filters an insubordinate report.)
  8. 7–8 Jul 1862: Harrison's Landing letter — unsolicited political advice opposing emancipation and confiscation [RUG-HL], [CWM-HL].
  9. 3 Aug 1862 onward: protests order to leave the Peninsula as "disastrous"; slow withdrawal [HoW-OR11].
  10. 29 Aug 1862: suggests to Lincoln the option "to leave Pope to get out of his scrape" [HoW-OR11], [Arch-Scrape]; his generals' animosity toward Pope contributed to Second Bull Run [MLWH-McC].
  11. Sept–Oct 1862: after Antietam, repeated demands for men and supplies, excuses in reply to Lincoln's 13 Oct letter [MLWH-McC]. Horses: McClellan forwarded a cavalry report (128 of 267 horses unfit, "sore tongue", grease, sore backs); Lincoln's telegram of 24/25 Oct 1862 asked what the horses had done since Antietam "that fatigue anything"; a 26 Oct follow-up noted 7,918 replacement horses had been sent [HD-Horses], [CWN-Horses]. (Classic "excuse report" template for the engine.)
  12. Relieved 5 Nov 1862, the day after the elections [EV-McC].

### 2.4 Henry W. Halleck
- **Posts:** Department of the Missouri (assumed 19 Nov 1861 per OR; ordered 9 Nov) [OR-Ser3]; Department of the Mississippi from 11 Mar 1862 [OCWC-WO3]; took field command after Shiloh (Apr–Jul 1862) [HN-GvH]; general-in-chief 11 Jul 1862 (in Washington and assumed command 23 Jul) [UCSB-Hal], [ECW-Pope].
- **Forces:** all western armies Mar–Jul 1862 (Grant, Buell, Pope's Army of the Mississippi; ~100,000 at Corinth: verify); thereafter staff authority only.
- **Traits [I]:**
  - boldness 200: slow advance on Corinth; after Second Bull Run "broke down—nerve and pluck all gone" (attributed to Lincoln via MLWH; attribution unverified) [MLWH-Hal].
  - candour 400: wired McClellan an unsubstantiated rumour that Grant had resumed drinking (4 Mar 1862) [Epperson-GvH].
  - competence 550 as administrator; Lincoln: "little more than a first-rate clerk" [EncyCom-Hal].
  - standing 550: "Old Brains", military author; western victories credited to him in spring 1862 [MLWH-Hal].
  - regard 150.
- **Values [I]:** caution, career.
- **Pushback episodes [D]:**
  1. Mar 1862: sidelines Grant after Donelson, complains to McClellan of Grant's unauthorised trip to Nashville [Epperson-GvH], [HN-GvH].
  2. Aug 1862: unable to integrate Pope's army with troops returning from the Peninsula [EncyCom-Hal].
  3. After Aug 1862: evades responsibility; Welles: "takes no responsibility, plans nothing, suggests nothing, is good for nothing" [ABT-Hal].
  4. Reported threat to resign rather than execute a Lincoln directive (source: Substack essay, weak) [Sub-Hal].
- **Game note [I]:** a "buffer" general-in-chief who forwards Lincoln's wishes as suggestions rather than orders.

### 2.5 John C. Frémont
- **Posts:** Western Department (St Louis), Jul 1861 – notified of removal 2 Nov 1861 (relief ordered 24 Oct) [OR-Ser3]; Mountain Department 11 Mar – late Jun 1862 [OCWC-WO3]; refused to serve under Pope when the Army of Virginia was formed 26 Jun 1862 and was relieved at his own request (exact date: verify) [MLWH-Fre].
- **Forces:** Western Department, tens of thousands (number: unsourced, needs research); Mountain Department ~ one corps (number: needs research).
- **Traits [I]:**
  - boldness 650: issued emancipation/martial law proclamation unilaterally, 30 Aug 1861 [AH-Fre].
  - candour 300: issued it "without notifying President Lincoln" [WikiFreEm]; arrested Frank Blair (twice) for reporting criticism to Montgomery Blair, who passed it to Lincoln [MLWF-Fre].
  - competence 300: rumours of mismanagement and corruption in his department were the decisive concern [MLWF-Fre].
  - standing 800: 1856 Republican presidential nominee; darling of radicals/abolitionists (and of German-American Missourians: verify).
  - regard −200.
- **Values [I]:** glory, abolition.
- **Pushback episodes [D]:**
  1. 30 Aug 1861: unauthorised emancipation proclamation [AH-Fre].
  2. Early Sept 1861: refuses Lincoln's request (2 Sep letter) to modify it himself; Lincoln must publicly order it (11 Sep) [WikiFreEm].
  3. 10 Sep 1861: sends Jessie Benton Frémont to lobby Lincoln at a late-night White House interview, which went badly (Nevins: "disastrous midnight interview") [MLWH-Jessie], [MLWF-Fre].
  4. Sept 1861: arrests Col./Rep. Frank Blair Jr. for critical letters [MLWF-Fre].
  5. Jun 1862: refuses to serve under the junior Pope; relieved at own request [ECW-Pope] (verify exact wording).
- **Game note [I]:** dismissing him costs standing with Radicals/abolitionists; keeping him risks unilateral policy acts.

### 2.6 Benjamin F. Butler
- **Posts:** Fort Monroe (Department of Virginia) May–Aug 1861 — in late May 1861 declared runaway slaves "contraband of war"; the War Department told him on 30 May, after a cabinet meeting, that his policy "is approved" [NPS-Contra], [HN-Contra] (a precedent of a general making policy that Lincoln then ratified, versus Frémont/Hunter whose orders he revoked); Hatteras expedition Aug 1861 (verify); Department of the Gulf, New Orleans, 1 May 1862 – removed 16 Dec 1862, replaced by Banks [HNOC-Butler], [Wiki-GO28], [EV-Butler].
- **Forces:** ~15,000 at New Orleans (number: needs research).
- **Traits [I]:** boldness 600; candour 400; competence 350 militarily, 600 as administrator; standing 750 (leading Massachusetts Democrat turned war hawk; first major Democratic general); regard 200.
- **Values [I]:** career, union.
- **Friction [D]:** General Order No. 28 ("Woman Order", 15 May 1862) caused international outrage; this and threats to foreign consuls contributed to his removal [CWMon-WO], [Wiki-GO28]. Lincoln's correspondence with Butler had to route via New York by steamer [CW-OR-Butler].

### 1862 midterm checkpoint
- House: Republicans lost about 22 seats and their outright majority; Democrats gained ~27–29 (sources differ); Republicans kept control with Unionist allies (R-D-U approx. 87–72–25 per one source). [Wiki-1862House], [Wiki-1862], [PAHist-1862]
- Senate: Republicans gained seats overall. [Wiki-1862]
- Governors: Democrats won New York (Horatio Seymour) and New Jersey. [Wiki-1862]
- Legislatures: Democrats flipped four Lower North legislatures including Indiana and Illinois. [Wiki-1862], [IHB-GA]
- Causes cited: slow war, inflation, taxes, corruption, habeas corpus, draft fears, emancipation backlash; McPherson: "the smallest net loss of House seats in twenty years". Contemporaries called it a "severe reproof"; historians call it inconclusive. [Wiki-1862], [PAHist-1862]
- Timing note: elections ran from October (PA, OH, IN, IA) to 4 Nov (NY, NJ, IL etc.); dates: verify per state. [I] For the game, model as a rolling checkpoint Oct–Nov 1862.
- Detailed seat maps: University of Richmond "Electing the House of Representatives" [UR-House].

## 5. Communication latency

**War Department telegraph office.**
- Anson Stager appointed head of the military telegraph in Nov 1861; Thomas Eckert headed the War Department telegraph office 1862–66 [Wiki-Eckert], [Raab-Stager].
- Oct 1861: decisive steps to create a military telegraph system; after 1 Nov 1861 McClellan ordered field generals to build lines to his HQ; by Jan 1862 commercial lines ran into his Washington HQ; weeks later Stanton moved them into the War Department and from then controlled army communications and censored press dispatches [ECWC-Tel].
- From Mar 1862 all war traffic was centralised in the War Department's old library beside Stanton's office, so Lincoln had to go there to read dispatches and send his own; from late spring 1862 he used it to give orders and in summer 1862 spent long hours there [WETA-Tel]. Bates (office manager): Lincoln spent more waking hours there than anywhere else [WETA-Tel], [Bates-book].
- Precedent for filtering: the supervisor of the telegraph struck McClellan's insubordinate sentence from the 28 Jun 1862 telegram [EV-McC].
- Scale: over the war ~6.5 million messages, 15,000 miles of line, ~1,200 operators [ECWC-Tel].
