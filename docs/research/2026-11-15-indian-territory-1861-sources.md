## 3. The Leased District and the August and October treaties

The Leased District was the Choctaw and Chickasaw land west of the 98th meridian that the two nations **leased to the United States** "for the settlement of the Wichita and other tribes" (Art. 9 of the treaty of 22 June 1855, 11 Stat. 611, read on the OSU Kappler page [KAPPLER-1855]). The Choctaw also gave up claim west of the 100th meridian. The Confederate treaties of 1861 with the peoples there, read as the Confederate Statutes print them:

| Date | Made at | Parties as the text names them | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 Aug 1861 | the Wichita Agency, near the Washita, "in the country leased from the Choctaws and Chickasaws" | the **Pen-e-tegh-ca Band of the Ne-um (Comanches)** and the tribes and bands of **Wichitas, Cado-Ha-da-chos (Caddo), Hae-cos (Waco), Ta-hua-ca-ros, A-na-dagh-cos (Anadarko), Ton-ca-wes (Tonkawa), Ai-o-nais, Ki-chais, Shawnees and Delawares**, "residing in the said leased country, by their respective Chiefs and Head Men" | The text places them "between the ninety-eighth and one hundredth parallels of west longitude" **H** [CSA-COM] |

| 4 Oct 1861 | Park Hill | the **Seneca** (formerly of Sandusky) and the **Senecas and Shawnees** (the mixed bands) | **H** [CSA-SEN] |

**Not read:** a separate treaty of 12 August with the "Comanches of the prairies" (UNL lists it; the scanned file read here is the one above); the names of each band's chiefs; and each treaty's terms beyond the parties. The Leased District's peoples are **their own**: they spoke, in 1861, through their chiefs and head men, and no single government is sourced for them.

## 4. The forts, settled from the OR

From Emory's report of 19 May 1861 and the documents beside it (OR I-1 pp. 648–662, civilwar.com transcription, OCR) [OR1-648]. **Washita:** Emory says "The day after I left Washita [April 16] the Texas occupied that place in force", so he left on or about 15–16 April and the Texans were in on 16–17 April; a later report places a regular officer at Washita on 30 April (Sturgis, p. 650), which this note cannot reconcile. **Arbuckle:** Emory: the Arbuckle troops "joined me five miles from Arbuckle" on **3 May**, "The same day Arbuckel was occupied by a large force ... from Texas"; Campbell, still at the post, wrote on **5 May** that it "was this morning taken possession of" (p. 652). **Cobb:** no occupation is recorded; Emory found the Cobb command, two companies of foot, on **9 May** thirty-five miles northeast of the post, so Cobb was abandoned **before 9 May**, after 3 May [I]; on 14 May Benning calls Washita, Arbuckle and Cobb "abandoned" (p. 653). **Fort Smith** (Arkansas, outside the box): evacuated 23 April at 9 p.m. and taken by Arkansas state troops (235 or 300 men) about two hours later.

**So against the project's notes:** the pack's draft region note ("Washita abandoned 16 April, Arbuckle and Cobb 5 May") is right for Washita, **gives Cobb a date no source has**, and gives Arbuckle the Texan date rather than the day the troops left. SENSITIVE-HISTORY 3.7 on main now carries the settled dates ("Washita left about 15–16 April; Arbuckle's troops left on 3 May; Cobb's troops were gone before 9 May 1861, the day Emory found the column"), and the patch below uses them. **The wording used:** "Washita left about 15–16 April and occupied from Texas by 16–17 April; Arbuckle's troops left on 3 May (Emory) and Texans held it by 5 May; Cobb's troops were gone before 9 May." **Not read:** OR I-1 p. 667 (Scott's 17 April order, which Emory cites for the march to Fort Leavenworth), and Special Orders No. 40 of 13 March.

**What the withdrawal told the nations:** Scott's orders (18 March, p. 656) say "good faith requires that notice shall be given" to the Indians near Cobb; whether notice was given is not recorded in these pages. Emory (p. 648) complains of "the unworthy conduct of the governor of the Chickasaw Nation" who "busily joined in an attempt to disarm and disgrace the soldiers" (the governor is unnamed). On 14 May a Texas officer reports that Arbuckle's property was turned over to the Chickasaws and that Colonel Young "formed a treaty of peace with the Reserve Indians" on condition that the Confederacy "feed and protect them" (p. 653). Which fort stood in which nation is stated unevenly: Benning puts Washita and Cobb "in Chickasaw Nation"; Townsend, 27 March, puts Cobb "on a portion of the Choctaw country, leased as a reserve" (p. 660).

### 5.2a As applied (branch `research-it-split`)

The patch was applied by hand to the source CSVs after `selfgoverning-rule` landed (decisions 0070 and 0071), and `data/` was regenerated. Where the patch left a choice open:
- **The three unsourced re-attachments keep the draft's anchor** (the old box's reference point) and are marked `[needs-source]` in each edge's notes: `tx_northeast` and `tx_west` go to `it_choctaw` (the old anchor, Boggy Depot, is the Choctaw region's, so their lengths are unchanged); `kansas_west` goes to `it_cherokee` (its old anchor lay by Tahlequah).
- **Road lengths** are derived by the map draft's own method (`ceil(1.25 × straight line × terrain / 12)` for roads, `ceil(1.5 × straight line / 80)` for the river), checked first against all ten old Indian Territory edges, which it reproduces exactly. The Arkansas and Texas roads to the Choctaw are unchanged (20, 33, 19, 9 and 41 days). The ten new internal adjacencies are derived from the new reference points and carry `[needs-source]` for the road itself (the Muscogee–Chickasaw one for its adjacency too, below).
- **Reference points are design parameters** (anchors named in 5.2; approximate coordinates), not sourced. **The Seminole point was moved twice after the gate re-check.** First from the tract's centroid (98.40°W), which made the derived road to the bordering Muscogee region 205 miles, to near the tract's east end (97.10°W, 35.55°N). The standing reviewer then found that the North Fork of the Canadian runs at about 35.44°N at that longitude, so 35.55°N fell in the Creek country; the point is now **97.10°W, 35.25°N**, between the Canadian and its north fork, labelled "design parameter: east end of the 1856 tract ... pending the Council House's sourced place". The four Seminole roads were recomputed by the same method: Muscogee 18 to 9 days, Cherokee 20 to 14, Chickasaw 16 to 8, Leased District 7 to 7.
- **A tenth internal edge, Muscogee–Chickasaw (10 days, ≈120 road-miles), was added** on the standing reviewer's finding. **The quoted treaty lines do not settle whether the two nations touch.** The Creek country's south line is the Canadian from the mouth of Pond Creek down to the mouth of its north fork (KAPPLER 1856 Art. 2), and the Chickasaw district's north line is "west along the main Canadian to the ninety-eighth degree of west longitude" from "due north to the Canadian River" (KAPPLER 1855 Art. 2); the two touch only if that due-north line meets the Canadian **east** of Pond Creek's mouth ("a few miles east of the ninety-seventh parallel", 1856 Art. 1). Neither treaty gives the longitude of that meeting or of the source of Island Bayou's eastern prong, so the edge is marked `[needs-source]` (a period map is needed), and its adjacency, not only its road, is unsourced. (By contrast the Seminole–Chickasaw edge rests on the lines: both fronts run along the Canadian between about 97° and 98°W.)
- **The done check** passes all six criteria; see the report for what moved (the Union run's final hash, from `109dd2c1e306a0f6` to `a652d5129f954a54`, because the World changed; the testbed's is unchanged; the run's arrivals, contests, control changes, supply and pocket counts and the mechanic bands are identical).

### 5.3 What the split does not fix

- The Choctaw government's election date for Hudson, and the Chickasaw governor in March 1861, stay inferred (GS 1.1).
- The Cherokee boundaries need a period map or the patent, and the region is bounded by difference (`[I]`).
- A department's side is State under decision 0065, so the nations' treaties are changes of side; this proposal creates no faction field.
- Under decisions 0069 and 0070 a region of a nation moves only to a department of the same kind and the same polity, and only a notice naming that government as the actor may move it. **The patch is applied only after `selfgoverning-rule` (with `polity`) is on main**; until then the manifest kind cannot carry `selfGoverning`.
- The Union pack's gate register needs its `indian-territory-1861` topic (refused recruitment and manpower signatures aimed at a nation's region or department, the nations' population fields as protected fields, the nations' names as protected groups) before the nations are applied; the gate reviewer drafts it.
