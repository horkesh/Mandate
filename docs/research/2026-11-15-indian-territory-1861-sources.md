# The Indian Territory in 1861: the thin items sourced, the forts dated, and a region split proposed (Mandate research)

Status: first pass complete (2026-11-15), lane `research-governments-stores`, part 2. It answers the gate reviewer's G7 (the thin items) and G1 (split the map) after decision 0069. It builds on `2026-11-15-governments-and-stores-1861.md` (call it GS) and corrects it where it was thin or wrong. **It records facts and proposes a patch; nothing here is applied.** The split waits for the gate reviewer's approval of this proposal. Native nations fall under the sensitive-history gate's open record R3.

Conventions as in GS: **H** = primary text read; **M** = one reputable source read; **L** = a search snippet only, a mirror, or a conflict. **(snippet)** = a search summary only. **[I]** = our inference. **Design parameter** = a modelling choice, never a fact. **OCR caveat:** the Confederate treaties were read as text extracted from page scans of the Confederate *Statutes at Large* (the Internet Archive scan, on dheller.org); the extract is garbled in places (letters wrong, columns mixed), so each name below is the one that reads clearly, and Indian names are spelled as the scan prints them.

**Two corrections to GS:** (a) **GS 1.1 gave the Creek chief only from a summary (L); the verbatim ratification text now fixes Kinnaird and Icho Hacho (H).** (b) **GS 1.3 says "Opothleyahola, 'leader of the Upper Creeks' ... declared neutrality" is a snippet; it still is.** What the Nation's own words say is in 2.

**A finding about the Upper Creeks:** Icho Hacho, "First Chief of the Upper Creeks", **signed** the treaty of 10 July, and Opothleyahola, "leader of the Upper Creeks" in Bahos's account [BAHOS pp. 60–61], **did not** sign. So "the Upper Creeks" are not one side: their own first chief and their best-known leader chose differently. The 17 Creek signers (a Committee "appointed by the General Council of Mus-kodvi Nation") include Chilly and Louis McIntosh, Daniel N. McIntosh, Samuel Checote and others [CSA-CREEK, **H**, OCR].

## 2. In their own words

- **The Muscogee Nation's own study guide** (Challenge Bowl 2026) prints oral histories from the 1930s. Three are on 1861 [MCN-GUIDE; the guide's own wording, **M**; the interviewers and dates are the guide's]:
  - James Scott of Greenleaf Town: the "ruthless raids and destroying of homes by the McIntosh Creeks" fell on "the Muscogees who were remaining loyal to Opothleyahola"; "we fled ... as it was during the winter time that we were on our flight to a neutral country".
  - Wallace Cook: "Part of them had fought on the North side and part on the Confederate side. That war was just like a civil war between the Indians of the Creek Nation."
  - The guide's quotation page gives Opothleyahola's words, "Letter to President Lincoln asking for protection during the Civil War": "Now the wolf has come, men who are strangers tread our soil, our children are frightened and the mothers cannot sleep for fear." I could not confirm the wording or the date elsewhere; *Now the Wolf Has Come* is the title of a 1996 book on the Creek Nation in the Civil War **(snippet)**. Two snippets date a letter of Opothleyahola to Lincoln 15 August 1861, with an answer on 10 September, and name different co-signers (Micco Hutko, or an Upper Creek "Principal Chief" Oktarharsars Harjo): **L, conflict kept** [SNIP-OPO].
- **The Muscogee Nation's culture and history page** said, in a search summary, that the majority of Muscogee people wanted neutrality, which proved impossible, and that Confederate forces attacked "a large group of neutral Muscogee led by Opothleyahola" **(snippet; the page now returns 404)** [MCN-SNIP]. That is the Nation's own phrase for the group, and it supports "neutral" as theirs, but I could not read it, so it stays L.
- **The Seminole Nation Museum** (a museum of the Nation, not its government) says "Chief John Jumper and the rest of the nation worked with the Confederacy", and that "one-third of the Seminole people chose to remain with the Union. They were led by Big John Chupco and moved to Kansas" **M** [SNM]. John Chupco is **not** among the twelve town chiefs who signed on 2 August [CSA-SEM, **H**].
- **The Chickasaw Nation's own pages:** "At first, the Chickasaws didn't know which side to choose or if they should remain neutral" (undated) [CHICK-TV, from GS]. No Chickasaw-voiced text of the 25 May act was found.
- **Ross's proclamation of 17 May** (GS): "strict neutrality between the States threatening civil war."
- **The 1 July Articles** (the nations' own copy, GS): the four nations are "free and independent".

**Names used in letters to the Creek, from catalogue titles only (snippet):** Ross wrote "to Creek Chiefs and Headmen" on 24 August 1861, to Opothleyahola and other Creek chiefs on 19 September, and "to Motey Kennard" on 4 October; Cooper wrote "to Hon. Motey Kinnard" on 21 October [GILCREASE-SNIP]. That Kinnaird was still addressed as chief in October is consistent with the treaty.

## 3. The Leased District and the August and October treaties

The Leased District was the Choctaw and Chickasaw land west of the 98th meridian that the two nations **leased to the United States** "for the settlement of the Wichita and other tribes" (Art. 9 of the treaty of 22 June 1855, 11 Stat. 611, read on the OSU Kappler page [KAPPLER-1855]). The Choctaw also gave up claim west of the 100th meridian. The Confederate treaties of 1861 with the peoples there, read as the Confederate Statutes print them:

| Date | Made at | Parties as the text names them | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 Aug 1861 | the Wichita Agency, near the Washita, "in the country leased from the Choctaws and Chickasaws" | the **Pen-e-tegh-ca Band of the Ne-um (Comanches)** and the tribes and bands of **Wichitas, Cado-Ha-da-chos (Caddo), Hae-cos (Waco), Ta-hua-ca-ros, A-na-dagh-cos (Anadarko), Ton-ca-wes (Tonkawa), Ai-o-nais, Ki-chais, Shawnees and Delawares**, "residing in the said leased country, by their respective Chiefs and Head Men" | The text places them "between the ninety-eighth and one hundredth parallels of west longitude" **H** [CSA-COM] |
| 2 Oct 1861 | Park Hill, in the Cherokee Nation | the **Great Osage Tribe**, "by its Chiefs and Headmen" | **H** [CSA-OSAGE] |
| 4 Oct 1861 | Park Hill | the **Quapaw Tribe**, "by its Chiefs and warriors" | **H** [CSA-QUA] |
| 4 Oct 1861 | Park Hill | the **Seneca** (formerly of Sandusky) and the **Senecas and Shawnees** (the mixed bands) | **H** [CSA-SEN] |

**Not read:** a separate treaty of 12 August with the "Comanches of the prairies" (UNL lists it; the scanned file read here is the one above); the names of each band's chiefs; and each treaty's terms beyond the parties. A later Wichita statement calls the Pike treaty a forgery signed by men who were prisoners, and a Caddo and Wichita removal to Kansas is described as voluntary in one account and forced in another **(snippets, L; contested, partisan)** [SNIP-WICH]. The Leased District's peoples are **their own**: they spoke, in 1861, through their chiefs and head men, and no single government is sourced for them.

## 4. The forts, settled from the OR

From Emory's report of 19 May 1861 and the documents beside it (OR I-1 pp. 648–662, civilwar.com transcription, OCR) [OR1-648]. **Washita:** Emory says "The day after I left Washita [April 16] the Texas occupied that place in force", so he left on or about 15–16 April and the Texans were in on 16–17 April; a later report places a regular officer at Washita on 30 April (Sturgis, p. 650), which this note cannot reconcile. **Arbuckle:** Emory: the Arbuckle troops "joined me five miles from Arbuckle" on **3 May**, "The same day Arbuckel was occupied by a large force ... from Texas"; Campbell, still at the post, wrote on **5 May** that it "was this morning taken possession of" (p. 652). **Cobb:** no occupation is recorded; Emory found the Cobb command, two companies of foot, on **9 May** thirty-five miles northeast of the post, so Cobb was abandoned **before 9 May**, after 3 May [I]; on 14 May Benning calls Washita, Arbuckle and Cobb "abandoned" (p. 653). **Fort Smith** (Arkansas, outside the box): evacuated 23 April at 9 p.m. and taken by Arkansas state troops (235 or 300 men) about two hours later.

**So against the project's notes:** the pack's draft region note ("Washita abandoned 16 April, Arbuckle and Cobb 5 May") is right for Washita, **gives Cobb a date no source has**, and gives Arbuckle the Texan date rather than the day the troops left; SENSITIVE-HISTORY 3.7's "Arbuckle 3 May, Cobb about 9 May" is right for Arbuckle and treats as Cobb's abandonment the day Emory *found* its column. **Recommended wording:** "Washita left about 15–16 April and occupied from Texas by 16–17 April; Arbuckle's troops left on 3 May (Emory) and Texans held it by 5 May; Cobb's troops were gone before 9 May." **Not read:** OR I-1 p. 667 (Scott's 17 April order, which Emory cites for the march to Fort Leavenworth), and Special Orders No. 40 of 13 March.

**What the withdrawal told the nations:** Scott's orders (18 March, p. 656) say "good faith requires that notice shall be given" to the Indians near Cobb; whether notice was given is not recorded in these pages. Emory (p. 648) complains of "the unworthy conduct of the governor of the Chickasaw Nation" who "busily joined in an attempt to disarm and disgrace the soldiers" (the governor is unnamed). On 14 May a Texas officer reports that Arbuckle's property was turned over to the Chickasaws and that Colonel Young "formed a treaty of peace with the Reserve Indians" on condition that the Confederacy "feed and protect them" (p. 653). Which fort stood in which nation is stated unevenly: Benning puts Washita and Cobb "in Chickasaw Nation"; Townsend, 27 March, puts Cobb "on a portion of the Choctaw country, leased as a reserve" (p. 660).

## 5. The region split, proposed (G1)

**Principle (the reviewer's):** one region per nation, with the Seminole separate, plus the Leased District; no interim folding; the old boxes stay `unknown` until the map is split. **This is a written patch for the map lane and the gate reviewer to approve; none of it is applied.**
