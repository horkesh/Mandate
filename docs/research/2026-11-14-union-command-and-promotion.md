# Union command, promotion and information, 1861–65 (Mandate people-systems research)

Status: first pass complete (2026-11-14). Written by a history research lane for the lead. It sources the claims the lead made from memory while settling the design for generals, and it builds the research base for the people systems: stats that move with battle, staff assessments that converge on the truth, junior officers who surface through reports, observers, leaks, and joint operations.

Conventions:
- Every claim ends with one URL. **(fetched)** means this lane or its helpers read the page (some very long pages came back only in part; the helper's note says so where it mattered). **(snippet)** means seen only in a search-result summary; treat it as a lead, not a fact.
- Wikipedia is used only as a flagged fallback.
- Official Records (OR) material is read through the civilwar.com and historyofwar.org transcriptions, not the printed volumes.
- "Cullum NNNN" means Cullum's Register entry NNNN at `https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Places/America/United_States/Army/USMA/Cullums_Register/NNNN*.html` (fetched).
- The Collected Works (quod.lib.umich.edu), senate.gov and Britannica refused automated fetches, so Lincoln's letters are cited through House Divided, abrahamlincolnonline.org and presidency.ucsb.edu, and anything from the blocked sites is snippet-only.
- **[I]** marks our own inference, not a sourced fact.
- Not read at all: Dyer, Fox and Livermore directly, Heitman, Gould's tables. See the gaps list at the end.

## 1. The lead's thirteen claims, checked

**a. Dana at Vicksburg — TRUE** (the pay-service cover is exact; "his reports helped Grant" is one author's judgement, not a documented fact).
- Stanton's words, as Dana recalled them in 1897–98: the "ostensible function" was special commissioner to investigate the pay service of the Western armies, but "your real duty will be to report to me every day what you see." The commission is dated 1863-03-12. Dana reached Memphis on 1863-03-23 and Grant at Milliken's Bend on 1863-04-06, told Grant of his mission, and says Grant "received me cordially." https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/42892/pg42892-images.html (fetched, chapters I–III only)
- "Helped Grant" rests on Emerging Civil War's assessment: Dana's daily telegrams relieved Grant of writing, Dana denied the drinking rumours and lobbied for Grant's promotion in Dec 1863, citing Guarneri. Dana's own text does not claim this. https://emergingcivilwar.com/2024/04/03/charles-a-dana-the-eyes-of-the-government-at-the-front/ (fetched, secondary)
- Dana's telegram of 1863-06-19 reports McClernand relieved for "palpable incompetence." https://civilwarnotebook.blogspot.com/2021/12/charles-dana-to-edwin-m-stantion-june.html?m=1 (fetched)
- Note for design: Stanton sent Dana and the reports went to Stanton, who shared them with Lincoln (the memoir's preface says they were written "for the information of Mr. Stanton and Mr. Lincoln"). The observer answered to the War Secretary first.

**b. Sheridan, Custer, Chamberlain in 1861 — PARTLY.**
- Sheridan: captain, 13th Infantry, from 1861-05-14 (1st Lt, 4th Infantry from 1861-03-01); chief quartermaster and commissary of Curtis's Army of Southwest Missouri 1861-12-26 to 1862-03-12. So he was a captain all year, but the staff job began at the end of 1861. Cullum 1612 (fetched). His memoir agrees on the 26 Dec 1861 assignment and has him in Oregon until Sept 1861 (the memoir also calls him "second lieutenant" mid-1861, which Cullum overrides). https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4362/pg4362-images.html (fetched, part)
- Custer: second lieutenant, 2nd Cavalry, 1861-06-24, graduated last in his class, led a company at First Bull Run. Cullum 1966 (fetched); https://www.nps.gov/people/george-armstrong-custer.htm (fetched)
- Chamberlain: professor of rhetoric and oratory from 1855–56, then appointed professor of modern languages in 1861. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Chamberlain (fetched, Wikipedia fallback). American Heritage says he gave up rhetoric in 1862 for a romance-languages chair and "resolved to forgo" the European leave, while Wikipedia says the leave was granted; the sources disagree. https://www.americanheritage.com/hero-20th (fetched). Correct version: a Bowdoin professor, of rhetoric earlier and modern languages by 1861–62.

**c. Lincoln's visits — TRUE.**
- Harrison's Landing: arrived 1862-07-08 on the Ariel with Stanton and others, reviewed troops that evening, left on the 10th; McClellan handed him the "Harrison's Bar Letter" and Lincoln "simply said, 'All right.'" https://civilwarmonths.com/2022/07/08/lincoln-visits-the-virginia-peninsula (fetched)
- On 1862-07-09 he questioned the corps commanders one by one; they disagreed (Porter: "Impossible—move the army and ruin the country"; Franklin: "I think we could"). https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/interview-between-the-president-and-general-mcclellan-and-other-officers-during-visit-the (fetched)
- Antietam: four days in early October 1862, about two weeks after the battle, "trying to prod" McClellan; Gardner's photographs are dated 1862-10-03. https://www.nps.gov/anti/learn/historyculture/presidential-visits-to-antietam.htm (fetched); https://civilwar.si.edu/lincoln_mcclellanlincoln.html (fetched)

**d. McClellan and Hooker leaked to friendly newspapers — PARTLY** (Hooker thinly attested; McClellan's friends yes, McClellan himself not shown).
- Hooker: Lincoln wrote on 1863-01-26 that he had heard "in such way as to believe it" that Hooker said the army and government "needed a Dictator"; no page names Lincoln's informant. https://abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/hooker.htm (fetched)
- NPS says Hooker "spoke loudly and often to newspaper reporters" (no footnote of its own). https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/hooker-takes-command.htm (fetched, weak)
- Wikipedia says a New York Times army correspondent quoted Hooker during the Mud March: "Nothing would go right until we had a dictator." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Hooker (fetched, Wikipedia fallback; the dictator remark in Lincoln's letter and this quote are not shown to be the same event).
- McClellan: his friend Samuel Barlow is credited with arranging a Feb 1862 Associated Press article praising McClellan, but only on Stanton's second-hand claim; Peninsula correspondents "favored by McClellan" and angry at Stanton's press rules fed the New York World's Manton Marble. No page shows McClellan personally leaking. https://www.mrlincolnandnewyork.org/new-yorkers/samuel-l-m-barlow-1826-1889/index.html (fetched)
- The one named leaker in a leak-focused piece is Burnside, not McClellan; the Herald leaked his 1861 expedition. https://historynet.com/northern-press-intelligence-leaks/ (fetched)

**e. Banks, Butler, Sigel as political generals — TRUE, with one fix.**
- Banks: Speaker of the House then governor of Massachusetts. Butler: a Massachusetts Democrat and prominent War Democrat. Sigel: popular in the German-American community, commissioned "for largely political reasons." https://essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/political-generals.html (fetched)
- David Work's study (reviewed): 16 political generals, 8 Republicans and 8 Democrats; Lincoln appointed them to secure their constituencies. https://www.historynet.com/?p=13681833 (fetched, a review, not the book)
- Fix: do not read "Massachusetts and Democrats" as one thing. Banks and Butler were both Massachusetts men; Butler was a Democrat. Banks's party in the verbatim ECWC text is not stated; a Wikipedia snippet lists Banks, Butler and Dix as Democrats. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_general (snippet, Wikipedia fallback)
- Lincoln to Schurz, 1862-11-10: "I have scarcely appointed a democrat to a command, who was not urged by many republicans and opposed by none." https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/lincoln/?p=977 (fetched)

**f. Grant in 1861; Halleck distrusted him — TRUE** (resignation motive differs by source).
- He resigned in 1854 "to avoid being drummed out of the service" after drinking at remote posts (American Battlefield Trust); another page says he resigned to try farming and business. In 1861 he was a clerk in the family store at Galena. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/biographies/ulysses-s-grant (fetched); https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-ulysses-s-grant-1822-1885/ (fetched)
- Halleck: found him unimpressive after an early meeting and "weighed the idea of demoting him"; wired McClellan on 1862-03-04 that Grant had "resumed his former bad habits"; yet they "worked harmoniously" later and Halleck authorised Grant to relieve McClernand. One author, uncorroborated by the other pages. https://historynet.com/the-secret-war-between-grant-and-halleck/ (fetched)

**g. Blind memorandum — TRUE.**
- Written 1864-08-23: "it seems exceedingly probable that this Administration will not be re-elected." Folded and pasted shut (LOC) or sealed in an envelope (Weber, via House Divided); the cabinet signed the outside unseen; read to the cabinet on 1864-11-11. The memo pledges to co-operate with a President-elect "to save the Union" between election and inauguration. https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2014/08/abraham-lincolns-blind-memorandum/ (fetched); https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/lincoln/blind-memorandum-august-23-1864/ (fetched)

**h. 1864 candidates — TRUE.**
- Democrats met in Chicago from 1864-08-29 and nominated McClellan with George Pendleton; McClellan rejected the peace plank. The pages disagree on who wrote it (Vallandigham on two pages, Pendleton on Wikipedia). https://civilwarmonths.com/2024/08/29/the-1864-democratic-national-convention (fetched); https://historynet.com/decision-1864-facts-about-the-election-of-1864/ (fetched)
- Election 1864-11-08: Lincoln 212, McClellan 21; McClellan carried three states. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1864_United_States_presidential_election (fetched, Wikipedia fallback)
- Frémont: nominated by acclamation with John Cochrane at the Radical Democracy convention, Cleveland, 1864-05-31; withdrew by a letter dated 1864-09-21. Reasons given differ (stop a Democratic win; unite Republicans behind Lincoln). The link to Montgomery Blair's resignation is speculation; no fetched page confirms it. https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/cleveland-convention (fetched); https://hd.housedivided.dickinson.edu/node/42766 (fetched)
- Also in Aug 1864 a group of Republicans around David Dudley Field, Greeley and others called a convention to replace Lincoln; it failed. https://www.mrlincolnandnewyork.org/new-york-politics/august-conspiracy (fetched, half in summary)

**i. Burnside and Hooker — PARTLY.**
- Burnside: planned to lead his old IX Corps against the stone wall himself, was talked out of it, and withdrew on 12-15 "with tears of frustration." https://npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/15/sec16.htm (fetched). On 1862-12-31 he suggested his own resignation and those of Halleck and Stanton; on 1863-01-23 he drafted General Orders No. 8 dismissing four generals; Lincoln relieved him instead. https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-ambrose-e-burnside-1824-1881/ (fetched). "Consumed by self-doubt" is on that page without a citation.
- Hooker: the remark that he lost confidence in himself traces to Major E. P. Halstead's 1910 letter, 47 years later, in varying wording. https://meaderingthroughtheprologue.com/hooker-at-chancellorsville-concussive-amendment-to-the-civil-war/ (snippet). Encyclopedia Virginia says "many historians" claim he lost his nerve and admitted it, but Stephen Sears calls that a myth; Pierro calls it "an enduring falsehood." https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/hooker-joseph-1814-1879/ (fetched); https://historynet.com/joe-hookers-chancellorsville/ (fetched). Treat Hooker's loss of nerve as legend, and Burnside's loss of confidence as well documented.

**j. Frémont's order; Buell and East Tennessee — TRUE.**
- Frémont's proclamation of 1861-08-30 declared the slaves of people who took up arms "freemen." https://americanheritage.com/fremont-affair (fetched). Lincoln's dates for modification and relief are in the earlier dossier.
- Buell: Lincoln wrote on 1862-01-13 that he should "menace Bowling-Green, and East Tennessee" and seize "Columbus and East Tennessee, one or both," adding "I have not offered…them as orders." https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/lincoln/?p=1039 (fetched). Relieved 1862-10-24 for "inaction"; his summer 1862 advance toward Chattanooga stalled when Forrest and Morgan cut his supplies. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/biographies/don-carlos-buell (fetched). Halleck's 1862-10-23 line that it was "the wish of the Government" that he occupy East Tennessee is in the earlier dossier from the Official Records, page unverified.

**k. Farragut and Butler at New Orleans — PARTLY** (joint, but in sequence).
- Farragut's fleet began passing Forts Jackson and St Philip at 2 a.m. on 1862-04-24; the fleet reached the city on 04-25; the forts surrendered on 04-28; Butler's army arrived and took control from the marines on 1862-05-01. Butler had about 6,000 men against the 20,000 Porter's plan wanted. https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/search-battles-detail.htm?battleCode=LA001 (fetched); https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/battles_new_orleans_1862.html (fetched). The two pages disagree on when the mortar bombardment began (16 or 18 April).
- Butler was removed 1862-12-16 after General Order No. 28 (the "Woman Order") and threats to foreign consuls. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Order_No._28 (fetched, Wikipedia fallback). His successor, Banks, is in the earlier dossier.

**l. "Six commanders before Grant" — FALSE.**
- Army of the Potomac commanders: McClellan (Aug 1861 – Nov 1862, with an interruption in late Aug–early Sep 1862), Burnside (Nov 1862 – Jan 1863), Hooker (Jan – 1863-06-28), Meade (1863-06-28 – June 1865). Four men in all, so Lincoln replaced three before Meade. Pope led a separate Army of Virginia. https://civilwarintheeast.com/us-armies/the-army-of-the-potomac/ (fetched). The pages disagree on McClellan's start (July or August 1861) and end (Oct or Nov 1862) and on Hooker's exit date (06-27 or 06-28).
- Grant never took formal command. He received his lieutenant general's commission on 1864-03-09, became general-in-chief, told Meade at Brandy Station on 1864-03-10 that his headquarters would travel with the army, and the Overland Campaign began 1864-05-04. Meade kept tactical command on paper, but by the end of Spotsylvania Grant had reduced him to "an executive officer or chief of staff." https://historynet.com/toiling-shadows-grant-meade-command-relationship/ (fetched). Another page calls Meade "the nominal head" and the pair "a good team." https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-ulysses-s-grant-1822-1885/ (fetched)
- Not supported by any page read: that Halleck became Grant's chief of staff in 1864. Verify separately before using it.

**m. Butler's "contraband" decision — TRUE.**
- Frank Baker, Shepard Mallory and James Townsend reached Fort Monroe on 1861-05-23; Butler refused to return them because they were being used to wage war against the Union. https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/ugrr/?p=3734 (fetched); https://www.nps.gov/articles/fort-monroe-and-the-contrabands-of-war.htm (fetched)
- His letter to Winfield Scott of 1861-05-27 calls it "a measure of necessity to deprive their masters of their services"; the page's text of the letter is partly model summary. https://www.americanyawp.com/reader/the-civil-war/general-benjamin-f-butler-reacts-to-self-emancipating-slaves-1 (fetched, partly summary). He did not use the word "contraband" himself, and Cameron's approval of 30 May is snippet-only. https://www.nps.gov/articles/fort-monroe-and-the-contrabands-of-war.htm (snippet)
- Within days "the flood gates opened," and hundreds were sheltering there by autumn. Self-emancipation forced the status question: First Confiscation Act 1861-08-06 (slaves used by the Confederate military forfeit the master's claim), Cameron's instruction to Butler of 1861-08-08 to receive fugitives, Second Confiscation Act 1862-07-17 (slaves of rebels "forever free"; Lincoln signed reluctantly). Army practice was not uniform, so commanders clashed (Seward and McClellan, Dec 1861). https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/emancipation/?p=703 (fetched). The Militia Act's date (1862-07-17) is snippet-only. https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/first-and-second-confiscation-acts-1861-1862 (snippet)

## 2. How Union generals were made and moved

### 2.1 Regular and volunteer commissions
- An officer could be "a major general of volunteers but only a colonel in the Regular Army" and was usually paid at the higher rank. Brevets were "temporary, honorary" ranks, and disputes about them were frequent. https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/military-organization-and-rank-during-the-civil-war/ (fetched)
- An act of 1863-03-03 allowed brevets for volunteers; many were back-dated to 1865-03-13. https://americancivilwarhighcommand.com/commanders/brevet-generals/ (snippet). Regulars later mocked that day; "isn't much of an honor if everyone gets one." https://regularcavalryincivilwar.com/2007/11/05/march-13-1865-black-friday/ (fetched, a blog)
- At war's end "more than a hundred" volunteer major and brigadier generals were mustered out in one batch (New York Times, 1866-01-10). https://hd.housedivided.dickinson.edu/node/45101 (fetched). Custer reverted from volunteer major general to regular captain, then became lieutenant colonel of the 7th Cavalry in July 1866. https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-Armstrong-Custer (snippet)

### 2.2 Senate confirmation
- The July 1861 act said the President appoints volunteer generals "by and with the advice and consent of the Senate"; governors commission regimental field and company officers. OR III/1 p. 381. https://civilwar.com/official-record/990-union-letters-orders-reports/269031-381-series-iii-volume-i-serial-122-union-letters-orders-reports.html (fetched)
- Clearest lapse: Schofield, nominated major general 1862-11-29; the Senate "declined to confirm" and the appointment "expired on March 4, 1863"; renominated 1863-05-12, confirmed 1864-05-12. Missouri's encyclopedia says Radical senators blocked him. https://www.ohiocivilwarcentral.com/john-mcallister-schofield/ (fetched); https://missouriencyclopedia.org/people/schofield-john-mcallister (fetched). The American National Biography puts the rejection in January 1863. https://hd.housedivided.dickinson.edu/node/6537 (snippet)
- Other appointments ended on 1863-03-04, the last day of the 37th Congress: Ferrero's reverted to colonel, Phelps's "expired unconfirmed," Burns's was "withdrawn." https://americancivilwarhighcommand.com/chronology-day-by-day/chronology-1863/1863-march-4th/ (snippet)
- No clear case of the Senate formally voting a Union general down was found; lapse and withdrawal were the failure modes. Lincoln nominated Grant lieutenant general on 1864-02-29 and the Senate confirmed on 1864-03-02. https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/grant (fetched)

### 2.3 Caps on the number of generals
- July 1861: at most 6 major generals and 18 brigadiers of volunteers (OR III/1 p. 381, above, fetched). 1862: at most 40 and 200 (12 Stat. 505; title and date uncertain). https://www.govinfo.gov/link/statute/12/505 (snippet). 1863-03-02: 30 more major generals and 75 more brigadiers. OR III/3 p. 88. https://civilwar.com/official-record/990-union-letters-orders-reports/270668-88-series-iii-volume-iii-serial-124-union-letters-orders-reports.html (fetched). The Senate passed that bill 22–14 on 1863-02-12. https://voteview.com/rollcall/RS0370675 (snippet)
- **[I]** With a ceiling, a star needed a vacancy, which is what made Senate lapses bite. Meade's I Corps and Upton's field promotion both waited on vacancies (section 4).

### 2.4 Seniority and combined commands
- The 1861 Regulations: when corps "happen to join," "the officer highest in rank… shall command the whole," unless the President directs otherwise. https://suvcw.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/1861-us-army-regs.pdf (snippet)
- A joint resolution of 1862-04-04 let the President assign command among officers of the same grade "without regard to seniority of rank" (War Department General Orders No. 37, 1862-04-08). https://civilwar.com/official-record/990-union-letters-orders-reports/269631-12-series-iii-volume-ii-serial-123-union-letters-orders-reports.html (fetched)
- Frémont refused to serve under Pope and was dismissed on 1862-06-27; he never commanded again. https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-john-c-fremont-1813-1890/index.html (fetched)
- At the Crater, Burnside was senior to Meade, so Grant issued separate orders to each. https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/military-organization-and-rank-during-the-civil-war/ (fetched)

### 2.5 Governors, Congress and the War Department
- Governors commissioned regimental officers and received quotas. Andrew refused to commission officers for Butler's New England "irregular troops," and on 1862-02-20 Stanton and Watson answered: "This Department recognizes the right of a Governor to commission volunteer officers." OR III/1 p. 897. https://civilwar.com/official-record/990-union-letters-orders-reports/269547-897-series-iii-volume-i-serial-122-union-letters-orders-reports.html (fetched)
- Sponsors by name: Illinois's delegation "unanimously recommended me as first on a list of seven" (Grant, memoir). https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter19.html (fetched). Lincoln to Washburne, early 1864: "All I know of Grant I have got from you." https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/visitors-from-congress/visitors-congress-elihu-b-washburne-1816-1887/ (fetched). Washburne introduced the lieutenant-general bill on 1863-12-14. https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/grant (fetched)
- Stanton decided Rosecrans's fate: in October 1863 he handed Grant two orders, one relieving Rosecrans and assigning Thomas, and let Grant choose. https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter40.html (fetched). Adjutant General Lorenzo Thomas wrote the 1861 Frémont report (OR I/3 p. 540). https://civilwar.com/official-record/571-wilson-s-creek/152803-540-series-i-volume-iii-serial-3-wilson-s-creek.html (fetched)
- No named governor lobbying for a named general was found.

### 2.6 Political generals
- Cost, in Halleck's words to Sherman, 1864-04-29: "It seems but little [better] than murder to give important commands to such men as Banks, Butler, McClernand, Sigel, and Lew. Wallace… and yet it seems impossible to prevent it." Of Banks: the President would remove him "very reluctantly, as it would give offence to many of his friends." OR I/34/3 pp. 332–333. https://civilwar.com/official-record/963-red-river-campaign-part-iii/207821-333-series-i-volume-xxxiv-iii-serial-63-red-river-campaign-part-iii.html (fetched; the transcription drops "better")
- Value: Sigel and "Fight Mit Sigel" among German Americans; McClernand's autumn 1862 recruiting drive raised much of Grant's Vicksburg army; Logan stumped for the Republicans in 1864; Lincoln on Schimmelfennig, "his name will make up for any difference there may be." https://essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/political-generals.html (fetched, the last three from the part the helper flagged as summary: treat as lower confidence). Schurz had swung "tens of thousand[s]" of German voters in 1860. https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-carl-schurz-1829-1906 (fetched)
- In spring 1864 Lincoln sent Cameron to sound out Butler's political ambitions. https://www.mrlincolnandfriends.org/the-officers/ (fetched)

### 2.7 Relief and reassignment
- McClellan: relieved by Lincoln's own order of 1862-11-05, which also relieved Porter of his corps; ordered to Trenton to await orders. https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/order-relieving-general-g-b-mcclellan-and-making-other-changes-by-abraham-lincoln-1862/ (fetched); https://www.ohiocivilwarcentral.com/general-orders-no-182-u-s-war-department/ (snippet)
- Burnside: General Orders No. 20 (1863-01-25) relieved him "at his own request," relieved Sumner and Franklin, and assigned Hooker; he was "shuffled to an administrative post" in the Department of the Ohio. https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/general-orders-no-20-relieving-general-ae-burnside-and-making-other-changes (fetched); https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/hooker-takes-command.htm (fetched)
- Pope: to the new Department of the Northwest, ordered 1862-09-06, took command 1862-09-16. https://www.mnhs.org/fortsnelling/learn/us-dakota-war (snippet). Rosecrans: relieved October 1863, then the Department of the Missouri; Dodge replaced him there 1864-12-02. https://www.nps.gov/chch/learn/historyculture/william-rosecrans.htm (fetched). Frémont: removed Nov 1861, given the Mountain Department Mar 1862 to "redeem himself." https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-john-c-fremont-1813-1890/index.html (fetched)
- Stone: held about six months in Forts Lafayette and Hamilton with "no charges ever filed"; resigned effective 1864-09-13. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/biographies/charles-p-stone (fetched). Porter was cashiered 1863-01-21. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/biographies/fitz-john-porter (fetched)

### 2.8 Joint Committee on the Conduct of the War
- Created 1861-12-09 (Senate) and 12-10 (House), chaired by Wade; final report 1865-05-22. https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/joint-committee-conduct-of-war.htm (snippet)
- Members: Senators Wade, Chandler, Andrew Johnson; Representatives Julian, Covode, Gooch, Odell (Harding and Loan replaced two in Jan 1864). Method: closed sessions that let disgruntled officers criticise their superiors; Frémont's testimony was leaked to the New York Tribune. Targets: Stone (arrested 1862-02-08, held to August); McClellan and Franklin (April 1863 report); Meade (about two months in early 1864, failed). Lincoln to Wade in March 1862: "Wade, anybody will do for you, but I must have somebody." https://essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/the-joint-committee-on-the-conduct-of-the-war.html (fetched)
- Stanton passed Thomas's Frémont report to the committee on 1862-03-12 (OR I/3 p. 540, above).

### 2.9 Lincoln's levers and what each cost
| Lever | Cost, with the example |
|---|---|
| Nominate anyone, within the caps | Senate lapses (Schofield, 1863-03-04); political generals who failed in the field (Halleck, 1864) |
| Pick a commander over seniority (1862-04-04 resolution) | The senior man can refuse and be lost (Frémont, 1862-06-27) |
| Relieve by order | Lincoln usually softened it: "at his own request" or a quiet department (Burnside to Ohio, Pope to the Northwest, Rosecrans to Missouri); a political general's removal "would give offence to many of his friends" (Banks) |
| Keep a general against Congress | A running fight with the JCCW and its leaks (McClellan, 1862; Meade, 1864) |
| Patronage for a constituency | Battlefield risk (Sigel, McClernand, Butler) |
| Delegate the choice to a subordinate | Stanton and Grant on Rosecrans: Lincoln's man does the cutting, but the decision leaves Lincoln's hands |

## 3. How Lincoln and Stanton learned the truth

### 3.1 Observers and inspectors
- Dana: see 1a. ### 4.4 How juniors were noticed and promoted (sourced)
- Under New York's militia law, officers and men elected their officers and the governor appointed them; Congress later gave governors "absolute power of appointment." https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/unit-history/conflict/us-civil-war-1861-1865/volunteer-organization-state-and-new-york-state-militia-new-york-national-guard (fetched)
- General officers: President nominates, Senate confirms, vacancies cap the number (eight brigadier vacancies in mid-May 1864). Chains in these cases: Meade's list and petitions (Upton); the brigadiers' joint telegram (Sheridan); the ranked list from Illinois's delegation (Grant); being named in official reports (Custer); a cavalry chief's recommendation (Custer, Merritt).
- The boy generals: Pleasonton told Hooker he needed officers "with the proper dash," called his candidates "disposable" because promoting junior men disrupted no working brigade, asked Meade on 1863-06-28 (Meade had authority from Halleck to appoint "as you may deem expedient"), and Special Order 175 followed in under 24 hours. The source found no evidence that Congressman John Farnsworth influenced it. https://smallbutimportantriots.com/2023/02/25/pleasontons-june-23-letter-and-the-boy-generals/ (fetched)

## 5. Credit-taking and blame-shifting

- **Iuka and Corinth (Grant vs Rosecrans).** Ord was to come from the north and Rosecrans from the south; Rosecrans ran late and Grant changed Ord's orders without telling him. Rosecrans: "Why did you leave me in the lurch?" Grant blamed couriers; Jones says no such dispatch has been found. Grant and Ord also blamed an "acoustic shadow." Grant's 1862 report was mildly critical, his 1885 memoir "utterly condemnatory." https://historynet.com/from-friends-to-enemies-grant-vs-rosecrans/ (fetched); https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/iuka (snippet); https://civilwarnews.com/issues/2022/january/a-prequel-or-grant-vs-grant/ (snippet). The Corinth pursuit is told two ways: Varney says Grant called it off, Jones says Grant was "livid" at Rosecrans's slowness. https://www.essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/william-starke-rosecrans.html (fetched). Rosecrans asked Halleck for reassignment and got Buell's army; once Grant outranked him, General Orders No. 337 relieved him on 1863-10-19.
- **Shiloh.** NPS concludes Grant was surprised: on 1862-04-05 he told Halleck he had "scarcely the faintest idea of an attack." https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/was-general-grant-surprised-by-the-confederate-attack-at-shiloh.htm (fetched); https://emergingcivilwar.com/2020/04/06/surprise-at-shiloh/ (fetched). Buell thought he had saved the day and wrote that Grant had no "marked influence" (Wikipedia snippet; "Shiloh Reviewed" not read). Grant wrote he was "not permitted to see" Buell's reports. https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter25f.html (fetched). Lew Wallace's division took more than seven hours to cover a few miles and the written order was lost; Grant's endorsement of 1862-04-25 says it did not "fully indorse" Wallace's report, and Wallace asked Stanton for a court of inquiry on 1863-07-18. https://historynet.com/why-lew-was-late/ (fetched); https://civilwar.com/official-record/578-shiloh-part-I/158823-188-series-i-volume-x-i-serial-10-shiloh-part-i.html (fetched); https://civilwar.com/official-record/578-shiloh-part-I/158824-189-series-i-volume-x-i-serial-10-shiloh-part-i.html (fetched). Halleck took personal command on 1862-04-11 and Grant became "second in command of the whole." https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/grant/chapter26.html (fetched). Grant kept his command with Lincoln's support (NPS, above).
- **Second Bull Run and Porter.** At 5 a.m. on 1862-08-30 Pope wired Halleck the enemy was "driven from the field, which we now occupy"; Longstreet routed him that day. OR XII/3 p. 741. https://civilwar.com/official-record/976-second-manassas-part-iii/165660-741-series-i-volume-xii-iii-serial-18-second-manassas-part-iii.html (fetched). Pope "scapegoated" Porter; the Cabinet refused to publish Pope's report complaining of his fellow generals. https://essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/john-pope.html (fetched); https://www.mrlincolnswhitehouse.org/residents-visitors/the-generals-and-admirals/generals-admirals-john-pope-1823-1892/ (fetched). Porter was arrested 1862-11-25; Stanton hand-picked the judges; McDowell, recalled, said a vigorous attack by Porter would have been decisive ("I think it would"); Lincoln approved the sentence 1863-01-21. https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/falling-stars-james-a-garfield-and-the-military-reputations-of-generals-irvin-mcdowell-george-mcclellan-and-fitz-john-porter.htm (fetched); https://emergingcivilwar.com/2023/01/21/the-last-casualty-of-second-manassas-the-court-martial-of-fitz-john-porter/ (fetched). The 1879 Schofield board said his actions "most likely saved the army"; Cleveland signed the restoration in 1886. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/biographies/fitz-john-porter (fetched). McDowell asked for his own court of inquiry on 1862-09-06 and was cleared but "effectively exiled to the West." No source was found of Pope blaming McDowell. Date conflict: one page says Porter was found guilty in December 1862.
- **Fredericksburg.** Burnside blamed "the fog and unexpected and unavoidable delay in building the bridges" (1862-12-17), OR XXI p. 66. https://civilwar.com/official-record/927-fredericksburg/177462-66-series-i-volume-xxi-serial-31-fredericksburg.html (fetched). His written orders to Franklin asked only for "a division at least"; the JCCW still found Franklin chiefly responsible, and Bledsoe calls Franklin, a Democrat, "a politically useful scapegoat." https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/article/failure-at-fredericksburg/ (fetched). The JCCW report was made public 1863-04-06 (snippet).
- **Chancellorsville.** In an 1877 letter Hooker wrote that Howard "criminally allowed his enemy to fall on his rear," Stoneman "kept well out of harm's way," and Sedgwick's "advance was irresolute"; his subordinates mostly blamed him. https://historynet.com/joe-hookers-chancellorsville/ (fetched); https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/hooker-joseph-1814-1879/ (fetched). Howard had ignored Hooker's warning that his flank was "in the air" (snippet). https://www.clevelandcivilwarroundtable.com/the-removal-of-joseph-hooker-the-most-unfairly-maligned-union-officer/
- **Gettysburg.** In February 1864 Sickles told the JCCW Meade had handled the battle badly and wanted to retreat on July 2; Butterfield backed him; the "Historicus" letter (1864-03-12) followed. Meade asked for a court of inquiry 1864-03-15; Lincoln declined 03-29: "you have done good services." https://historynet.com/distorted-truth-the-legacy-of-george-gordon-meade/ (fetched); https://civilwarmonths.com/2024/03/29/meade-v-historicus/ (fetched)
- **The Crater.** Ledlie's division drew the lead role by straws; Pleasants found Ledlie "cowering in the rear and nursing a bottle of rum." The court of inquiry blamed Burnside and Ledlie and exonerated Meade; the JCCW blamed Meade more. https://historynet.com/shock-awe-colossal-failure/ (fetched); https://beyondthecrater.com/resources/bat-sum/third-offensive-summaries/the-battle-of-the-crater-july-30-1864 (fetched)
- **Five Forks.** Sheridan relieved Warren as the battle ended, saying he was "too cautious"; the court of inquiry reported in November 1882, clearing him of the principal charges after his death. https://www.nps.gov/pete/learn/historyculture/gouverneur-warren.htm (fetched)
- **Inflated reports.** McClellan, 1862-06-25: enemy "stated at 200,000"; he was "in no way responsible" for being outnumbered. OR XI/1 p. 51. https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/officialrecords/vol011chap023part1/00001_p2_c2_18.html (fetched). 1862-06-28: "I have lost this battle because my force was too small"; "You have done your best to sacrifice this army." OR XI/1 pp. 60–61. https://www.historyofwar.org/sources/acw/officialrecords/vol011chap023part1/00001_p2_c2_23.html (fetched). A 1988 article asks whether McClellan exaggerated on purpose. https://muse.jhu.edu/article/420411/summary (snippet)

## 6. Generals changed by battle

- **Hooker after Chancellorsville:** before, "May God have mercy on General Lee, for I will have none." After, the "lost confidence" remark is legend (1i). He asked to be relieved over the Harpers Ferry garrison and Meade took command 1863-06-28. https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/hooker-joseph-1814-1879/ (fetched)
- **Burnside after Fredericksburg:** wanted to lead the assault himself, was dissuaded, withdrew in distress, then offered to resign (1863-01-01) and issued his ultimatum (01-23). https://npshistory.com/publications/civil_war_series/15/sec16.htm (fetched); https://civilwar.com/official-record/927-fredericksburg/178341-945-series-i-volume-xxi-serial-31-fredericksburg.html (fetched)
- **McClellan after the Seven Days:** the 06-25 and 06-28 telegrams (section 5). Adams argues he left convinced of enemy superiority, and that early defeats left the Army of the Potomac with a lasting inferiority complex. https://historynet.com/cwt-book-review-fighting-defeat/ (fetched)
- **Rosecrans after Chickamauga:** Hay's diary for 1863-10-24 records Lincoln saying Rosecrans had been "confused and stunned like a duck hit on the head"; Dana agreed he was "for the present completely broken down." https://civilwarnotebook.blogspot.com/2017/02/diary-of-john-hay-october-24-1863.html (fetched, blog transcription). ## 7. Names for invented officers

What a generator can draw on, with only what this lane could confirm.

- **NPS Civil War Soldiers and Sailors database:** over 6.3 million soldier records from 44 states and territories, from the General Index Cards of the compiled service records; it records at least full name, side, unit and company. It indexes cards, not people (about 3.5 million men actually served), and "is no longer maintained and updated" (page of 2025-04-07). Officer coverage, birthplace, residence and bulk download are not stated on the pages read. Use: collision check for invented names. https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/soldiers-and-sailors-overview.htm (fetched); https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/soldiers-and-sailors-database.htm (fetched)
- **Official Army Register of the Volunteer Force, 1861–65** (Adjutant General's Office, 1865): catalogues give seven or eight parts; archive.org holds scans (one covers New York and New Jersey). This lane could not read a part, so the fields carried (commission dates, residence) are unconfirmed. - **Cullum's Register** (West Point graduates; Thayer's site has 81 class rosters, 3,454 entries): career records of regular officers; whether birthplace and appointing state fields appear is not confirmed from the pages read. - **Census:** IPUMS offers 1850–2010 census samples and linked 1% samples (1850–1880 and 1860–1880 pairs, 7,390 and 11,293 linked males); the pages do not say whether personal names are in the public files, so use these for age, birthplace and occupation distributions, not names. https://usa.ipums.org/usa/samples.shtml (fetched); https://usa.ipums.org/usa/linked_data_samples.shtml (fetched)
- **Nativity:** secondary pages say foreign-born were about 23% of Union soldiers (about 516,000), about 216,000 German-born, with birth country unrecorded for about 43%, all from Gould (1869), whose tables this lane did not read. https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans_in_the_Civil_War (snippet, Wikipedia); https://johndailinger.omeka.net/items/show/27 (fetched, a chart pointer without numbers)
- A given-name frequency list from the 1850 census (GINAP) appeared in a search summary; no URL was confirmed, so do not rely on it. No source for 1860-era surname frequencies was found.

How to avoid real people (our design recommendation, not a sourced fact):
1. Never use a full name that belongs to a Union or Confederate general; build the blocklist from the Official Army Register and Heitman (not yet read).
2. Draw first name, surname and state separately from weighted tables, then reject any combination found as a field officer or above in the Register for that state.
3. Avoid middle initials that pair with famous generals.
4. Accept collisions at private rank and with very common names, since a real man with that name existed and the game never claims it is him. Mark every generated officer as invented in the data and on screen.

## Design implications

1. **Observers answer to Stanton first.** Dana was Stanton's man, with a cover job, and reports went to the Secretary and then to Lincoln. Model the observer as an office-holder with his own bias and error rate (Dana's "fatal as Bull Run" wire), with a delay before the player sees the report.
2. **Relief is a posting, not a firing.** Lincoln almost always moved the man to a quiet department or accepted a resignation "at his own request." The cost of relief is the faction behind the general (Banks "would give offence to many of his friends"), not the general.
3. **Promotion is capped, listed and confirmed.** Vacancies, a superior's list, then the Senate; nominations lapsed at session's end (1863-03-04) rather than being voted down. Use a lapse mechanic, not a rejection mechanic.
4. **Sponsors matter as much as battle.** Governors (Yates, Blair, Curtin) and congressmen (Washburne, Blair) put names forward; a cavalry chief's list made the boy generals in under a day. Give each junior officer a sponsor field.
5. **Leaks come from the circle, not only the principal.** Barlow and the World for McClellan; Rosecrans's staff in Cincinnati; Burnside's staff; Sickles and the Herald. Attach the leak to a staff member or friend, with the commander's culpability uncertain.
6. **Blame resolves through bodies with their own politics.** Franklin and Porter lost to Stanton-picked or Radical-led bodies; the records reversed 14 to 23 years later. Let blame land by who controls the inquiry, and allow later revision.
7. **Battle effects are not one-way.** The evidence supports changes in reputation and confidence, but not "cautious after defeat, bold after victory." Treat the change as case-specific, and separate reputation from actual nerve (Hooker's loss of nerve is probably legend).
8. **Reports overclaim in real time.** Pope's victory report, McClellan's 200,000 enemies, Dana's panic wire. Truth arrives later by another channel, which fits the settled "converging assessment."
9. **Fix the lead's "six commanders" line.** Four men commanded the Army of the Potomac and Grant never did; use "Grant directed the army through Meade."

## Findings outside my scope

- The lead's assumption that Halleck became Grant's chief of staff in 1864 was not supported by any page read; verify in a research lane before using it.
- The settled design assumes the player's staff assessment "converges" on the truth. The sources show the truth was itself contested after the war (Grant–Rosecrans on Iuka and Corinth; Porter; Warren; Hooker's "lost confidence" remark), so decide whether the sim's hidden truth is the historical record or a per-game draw, and whether post-war "verdicts" exist at all.
- Dana's own words call his Chickamauga wire too dark, and Varney says a later Dana message "never existed": the primary record on the observer's reports is contested.
- Sources that refused automated fetching (Collected Works, senate.gov, Britannica) mean Lincoln's letters here come from secondary transcriptions; a lane with a browser could verify them.
- No inspector general, and no named governor lobbying for a named general, was found; the first pass was thin there.
- This note now sources two gaps the earlier Union dossier left open: the Cameron and Lorenzo Thomas inspection of Frémont (reached St. Louis 1861-10-11, report dated 1861-10-21) and the Porter court-martial dates (arrested 1862-11-25, sentence approved 1863-01-21).
