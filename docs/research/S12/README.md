## The new contemporary witnesses

The new source is Salmon P. Chase's **dated diary**, printed in the American Historical Association's *Annual Report for 1902*, II (GPO, 1903), *Diary and Correspondence of Salmon P. Chase*, [pp45–106](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/45). The dated diary entries are contemporary legs under 0087; later editorial notes and the separate selected-letter catalogue are not themselves such legs. This batch reads the passages specified in [sources.md](sources.md), not every item in the volume.

**Grant and Buell.** In the **1 August 1862** entry, Halleck calls Grant a good general and brave in battle, but careless of his command. [I] Competence above and nerve above have a third-party leg; an organising gift remains held. Halleck calls Buell slow but safe. [I] Below operational boldness is defensible, while deficient battlefield nerve is not. Morton's **5 October** criticism of Buell concerns both command and the proclamation, in an election conversation. It remains Morton's view, not a verified finding about Buell's policy. [Chase, p52](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/52), [pp100–101](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/100).

**Halleck.** Chase's **3 August** entry explicitly credits intelligence and mastery of the business after a discussion of deployments and promotion screening. [I] Above competence and an organising observation are narrow administrative proposals, qualified by the tentative nature of the observer's hopes. On **11 October**, a speaker printed as General Hunter acknowledges ability and knowledge but criticizes Halleck's study of opportunities. This countervoice is retained; its given-name identity is not supplied from memory. The **3 August** date continues across pp53–57; it must not be changed to 5 August because the next heading is 6 August. [Chase, pp53–57](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/53), [pp104–106](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/104).

**McClellan and Burnside.** On **2 September**, Lincoln expressly compares McClellan's ability to organize Washington's defense favorably with every alternative. [I] This supports a role-limited organising observation, rather than inferring the gift merely from his appointment. Chase and other cabinet voices oppose the field command in the same discussion. Their objections are not averaged into a national reputation. Chase's preference for Burnside as an alternative is still an expectation, so competence stays HOLD. Stanton's **19 August** comment that Burnside is too partial to McClellan is a reported relationship lead, not proof of disloyalty. [Chase, pp61–65](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/61).

**Pope and Stanton.** Pope's **6 September** indignation establishes [I] antagonism toward McClellan and Porter as an attributed relationship observation. It does not establish that they caused his defeat. I. C. H. Smith's favorable **7 September** view remains beside that account. General Hunter's **11 October** criticism of Stanton is expressly from a man who says he knows little of him and met him once; it cannot responsibly clear a general competence, candour or organising centre. Stanton's **10 October** exchange about orders issued but little accomplished also distinguishes permission from execution. [Chase, pp67–68](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/67), [pp104–106](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/104).

**Morton.** His **5 October** prediction of electoral danger is an actual disclosure of adverse expectations. [I] Above candour is proposed for that conversation. Seeking soldier furloughs to vote proves neither extraordinary boldness nor an organising gift. The scan on p100 reads **Sunday, October 5**; the OCR's apparent 6 is wrong. The election result is outside this cast lane. [Chase, pp100–102](https://archive.org/details/diaryandcorrespo00chasrich/page/100).
