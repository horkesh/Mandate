# Dated officer pool for assessment and promotion

Research proposal only. [Inventory](officers.csv): **239 provisional research handles**, 139 Union and 100 Confederate, after joining the two Starke spellings on the same roster page. [Commands](commands.csv): **252 entries**. These are division and brigade commands in the Shiloh and Maryland organizations, with the four Harpers Ferry brigades named by Lorenzo Thomas added as retrospective command identifications, including temporary replacements and cavalry. This is a substantial dated pool, not an exhaustive census of every officer who commanded a brigade anywhere during 1862. The command paths are transcription aids, not proposed engine formations or accepted person IDs.

## What the dates establish

The Union Maryland organization expressly covers **14–17 September**, with footnotes identifying replacements, wounds and deaths. The Confederate organization is headed **during the Maryland Campaign**, compiled from reports. Its rows therefore have month precision; an invented 3–22 September tenure has been rejected. Shiloh's organization covers **6–7 April**; the Confederate enclosure was transmitted later. A compiler's heading does not prove that every successive commander served throughout the window. [OR I-19-1, pp169–180, 803–810](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion191unit/page/169); [OR I-10-1, pp100–107, 382–384](https://archive.org/details/cu31924077730160/page/100).

[Service records](service-records.csv) retain **128 named Heitman entry excerpts**, separating pre-1861 clauses from separable 1861–62 clauses. They preserve substantive, volunteer and brevet wording. The command-to-register joins remain proposed: the literal register identity is supplied, rather than silently treating matching initials as proof. A missing entry is HOLD, not no military experience; a military register is also not a complete civilian career. The unused automatic candidate list stays with the external research workings. Howell Cobb, Joseph Walker, James A. Walker and Thomas Morton were specifically denied unsafe homonymous matches. No 1893 medal narrative or later retrospective praise becomes a contemporary judgment.

The Maryland roster designates **George Crook colonel**; Heitman dates his volunteer brigadier commission **7 September 1862**. Both statements remain visible. John Gibbon's register gives brigadier of volunteers **2 May 1862**; Meade's major-general of volunteers dates **29 November**, so it must not replace his September grade. The date of a later brevet recognizing an earlier battle is not the officer's substantive grade at that battle. [Heitman I, pp340, 452, 700](https://archive.org/details/historicalregist01heitrich/page/340); [OR I-19-1, p178](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion191unit/page/178).

[Confederate register events](confederate-register.csv) contain **101 manually transcribed appointment/rank/confirmation records**, including December boundary entries. Wright is a later register compiler, usable for dated service facts under the sprint rules, not a contemporary character witness. Dates remain attributed to that edition where its own sequence is anomalous. [Wright, pp14–16, 21–34, 49–50, 68–77, 86–90](https://archive.org/details/generalofficers00wrigrich/page/14).

| Case | Evidence and consequence |
|---|---|
| Longstreet | Appointed lieutenant-general 11 October, rank from 9 October, confirmed 11 October. Keep appointment and seniority separate. Wright pp14–15. |
| Withers | Major-general appointment 16 August, rank from 6 April. The retrospective rank date alone does not prove an April appointment. Wright pp28–29. |
| D. R. Jones | The entry gives an October appointment while also recounting a March appointment, September rejection/reconsideration and October confirmation. The Maryland roster calls him brigadier. No continuous major-general interval is inferred. Wright p30; OR I-19-1 p804. |

| Pemberton | The p25 scan has a struck-through confirmation month and no usable day. Confirmation remains HOLD; appointment and rank dates are separately legible. |
| Pickett, J. K. Jackson, Howell Cobb, Kershaw, Maxey and others | Confirmation dates printed before appointments stay explicit anomalies. No silent emendation from memory. Wright pp69–75. |
| Elzey, Gardner, Cleburne | December major-general events are retained as year-end boundaries; they confer no earlier 1B availability. Wright pp31–32. |

## Contemporary judgments

[Observer records](officer-assessments.csv) propose three narrow observations: McClellan's **23 September** dispatch credits B. F. Davis's management of the Harpers Ferry cavalry withdrawal, supporting **[I] competence above** for that execution; Longstreet's **10 October** report specifically credits Toombs's gallant bridge defense and flank charge, supporting **[I] nerve above** for personal leadership. Recommendations and interested official reports remain attributed; neither establishes a high centre or an organising gift. [OR I-19-1, p802](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion191unit/page/802), [pp839–842](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion191unit/page/839).

[Judgment cells](pool-judgments.csv) have nine common fields for every handle: **3 CHANGE observations and 2,148 HOLDs**. A command, academy attendance, length of service, brevet, victory or selection does not itself clear competence, nerve, experience as a stat, reputation, candour, boldness, regard, organiser or duty. These are dated observation proposals for 1B, never March-1861 starting values. No exact numerical centre is invented.

The same OR passage preserves the **8 November** commission finding against Thomas H. Ford alongside Daniel Tyler's **23 September** favorable expectation of Ford's explanation. The four brigades named in Thomas?s dispatch are now included, with full-name joins for D?Utassy, Trimble and Ward still HOLD. The explicitly named Ford commission finding supplies **[I] competence below** for that command, dated to promulgation, not to the earlier surrender. His prewar history and commission chronology remain held. This is an attributed official assessment, not a universal verdict. [OR I-19-1, pp802–803](https://archive.org/details/warofrebellion191unit/page/802).
