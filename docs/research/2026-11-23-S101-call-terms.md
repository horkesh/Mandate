## Register and scope

[Call register](S101/call-register.csv): **98 entries**, with separate service
term, organisation, appointments, arms/equipment, destination, other conditions
and limits. It covers every one of **S84's 49 Confederate rows**, all **24 Union
principal-doctrine calls**, the **24 April militia quotas** and Minnesota's
separate renewal credit. Three of the Union doctrine rows concern central
Regular recruiting rather than governors. Both April situation options repeat
the same 24 militia calls, enumerated once. No historical text is invented for
the hypothetical larger/longer alternative.

[Twenty text profiles](S101/call-terms.json) distinguish incorporated law,
original call, implementing order and later reply. The May 3 national
proclamation/May 4 organisation is retained as context, not another 42,034-man
levy on top of its state allocations. Legal ceilings, appropriations and
State-force transfers in the pack catalogue are not additional governor calls.
The separate [mapping](S101/mapping.md) explains those scope dispositions and
the July laws from their read pages.

The Confederate sequence matters:

- March 9 calls for immediate infantry at named forts, muster after arrival,
  under February 28's State-consent/retained-officer basis. Arms and equipment
  responsibility is not allocated by that letter. Charleston and Texas are
  destination requirements; assignment of those quantities to the two model
  governor rows remains **[I]**, not a printed addressed quota (OR IV-I
  pp.117,134-135).
- April 8 and April 16 demand readiness; the latter adds armed/equipped troops
  **as nearly as practicable**. Pay begins at muster; conditional companies
  are not to muster until the call becomes absolute. The invoked March 6 law
  supplies the twelve-month volunteer term and State-law unit officers, with
  State **or** Confederate arms; clothing and mounted horses have separate
  provisions (pp.126-127,211,221-222,233).
- April 22 activation letters differ. Alabama/Mississippi go to Lynchburg,
  Georgia/Louisiana to Richmond, with governor-selected muster and federal
  transport/subsistence. Louisiana's second regiment is optional if ready.
  The longer Kentucky letter, adapted to Arkansas/North Carolina/Tennessee,
  expressly details twelve months minimum, State-law unit officers, federal
  staff/arms and uniforms with commutation (pp.231-233). Brown's Augusta/company
  election proposal and Walker's acceptance are later negotiations (p.234).
- June 29's two additional war regiments from Louisiana/Mississippi are for
  **Corinth**, if possible, outside the reserve. The letter does not specify
  an arms admission condition or appointment rule (p.411). Moore's later reply
  says **Memphis** and refers to June 30; both wordings are preserved (p.422).
- June 30 reserve accepts **unarmed independent companies**, with federal camp
  control and Presidential field/staff appointments; its armed/equipped
  cavalry supplement goes to Corinth. Florida has one camp, other States two.
  July 1's additional infantry regiments must be armed/equipped but choose
  their officers except federal staff (pp.412,416). These are different terms.

The Union calls also differ. April 15 is three-month militia, with specific
State rendezvous, oath, inspection/age requirements and probable repossession
of seized federal property; the proclamation explicitly limits devastation and
disturbance of peaceful citizens. Its call/April 16 organisation sheet does not
specify appointment authority or an arms supplier (OR III-I pp.67-69,76).
May 3's **New York two-year accepted tender** expressly makes the State arm and
equip and the United States assume depot pay/instruction/subsistence (p.151).
It must not inherit the national three-year volunteer term.

GO15's governor-appointed company/field officers, higher federal appointments,
clothing, horses, travel and benefits accompany the May allocation circular;
GO16's Regular organisation is separate (pp.151-157,203-204). Rhode Island's
term offer, MN/KS/NE one-regiment letters and New York's fourteen-regiment
send/hold orders have distinct profiles rather than importing Massachusetts's
whole circular (pp.204,207-208). **Indiana's June 11 original was recovered**:
six additional regiments under GO15, McMillan/Brown's and four designated by
Morton, reporting after muster to McClellan (pp.263,265). It is not reconstructed
from Morton's later report on p.290.

New York's July 30 GO78 implements the 25,000 requisition for three years or
war: 25 regiments including one artillery regiment; three State depots;
examined officer nominees and State appointments. Cameron accepts two cavalry
and two artillery regiments **in addition**. GO78, the acceptance and Morgan's
August 1 dating of his July 25 proclamation are separately identified; the
original Presidential requisition is not claimed recovered (pp.369-371,376).

## The qualifications

[Governor register](S101/governor-qualifications.csv): **27 dispositions**, all
26 governor entries in the two 0112 registers plus C06's explicit non-governor
scope disposition, so C01-C12 are all accounted for. U09/U25a/U28 concern other
matters; their metadata is not adopted as newly read historical evidence.
Existing exclusions/obligations are preserved.

Brown qualifies distant muster/medical appointments in C01 and Augusta muster,
Georgia organisation and field election in C02 (IV-I pp.207,214,234). Moore
qualifies keeping men instantly ready without camp finance in C03 (pp.232-235).
Pickens in C04 keeps regiment integrity and gubernatorial consent to permanent
service elsewhere, while accepting a Presidential Confederate general
(pp.233,235-236). Harris in C05 undertakes the three regiments while disclaiming
authority to order them outside Tennessee (pp.232-233).

Letcher in C07 undertakes the reserve but protests Presidential field
appointments (pp.419-420). Moore of Alabama in C08 distinguishes unarmed reserve,
armed cavalry and extra armed infantry, with home-defence shortage (p.420).
**The scan itself contains the tension** between not arming more than one and
not having arms to arm one: no numeric correction or stock is invented.
Pickens in C09 offers existing armed twelve-month regiments only if war-term
consent can be obtained (pp.420-421). His July 1 original is not recovered;
the printed Alabama/Georgia letter is not silently treated as his original.

Brown in C10 protests reserve appointments, requires federal reception/support,
and cannot promise reserve arms/equipment. Moore in C11 proposes existing
regiment credit and rejects State-financed camp support, reporting the
home-defence arms burden (pp.421-423). Brown C12 refuses the **suspected**
dismantling of his State brigade, undertakes other regiments and offers the
whole brigade under Phillips with a conditional extra-regiment offer (p.424).
July 1 did not expressly demand disbanding the brigade; July 12 subsequently
does demand the two regiments without brigade organisation (pp.466,477-478).
Do not backdate that later demand.

The other governor candidates retain their status: C18/C21 concern changed
terms for previously raised twelve-month troops (pp.295,302-303,355); C19 is the
later brigade dispute; C22 is **Walker attribution**, with Brown's own reply
unrecovered (p.355). U16-U21 are refusals over professed purpose/constitutional
authority, not qualified yes answers or disputes over an unprinted arms clause
(III-I pp.70,72,76,81-83,91-92,99). Hicks U36 is a temporary decline citing arms
and organisation/access (I-II p.581). Morgan U39 asks the object then takes
steps to comply; these papers do not state an arms or officer concession
(III-I pp.72,85).

## Proposed ruling and limits

**[I] Accept a per-call terms proposal grounded only in issuer text and expressly
incorporated law**, as set out in [the mapping](S101/mapping.md) and
[value rows](S101/value-mapping.csv). Use existing vocabulary only. Confederate

may have empty arrays. No blanket `duty`, new Confederate value or fitted
boldness/weight is added. Reject borrowing terms from the response to produce a
desired rung.

The mapping names the read State evidence and missing inventories, commitments,
funds, transport and supply receipts. No State has a complete dated material
balance in these pages. Reserve infantry has no arms admission deficit; cavalry
and extra armed regiments do. Legal authority, officer guarantees and unit
consent are not gun stocks. The existing `required`/`available` capacity hook
needs both inputs; recruitment now supplies zero demand and defaults to full
means. One scalar or a reservation rung cannot execute exact contractual
conditions. Reject unsourced capacity centres, outcome fitting and any claim
this research closes 0112 gaps or clears 1A.
