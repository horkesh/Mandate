This  depends  upon  the  proper construction  of  the  word  "now"  in  the  act.  What  was  the  force  and meaning  of  that  word  at  the  time  the  act  passed  ?  Was  it  not  intended to  embrace  all  such  forces  as  it  was  known  that  the  States  had  raised for  their  respective  defenses,  and  which  in  legal  contemplation  were then  in  service?  Could  any  other  idea  have  been  entertained  ?  To me  this  seems  clear.  A  narrower  or  more  restricted  construction would  prevent  the  President  from  receiving  a  single  man  who  might have  been  recruited  to  fill  up  a  vacancy  after  the  28th  of  February. This  cannot  be  supposed  to  have  been  the  intention  of  the  act.  If  a single  man  recruited  after  the  28th  of  February  can  be  received,  why may  not  a  whole  company  whose  entire  rank  and  file  has  been  similarly filled  up  be  received?  The  words  "forces"and  "now  in  the  service," &c.,  must  have  had  reference  to  the  known  military  organization  of the  States  at  that  time,  and  not  to  the  personnel  or  individuals actually  then  constituting  the  companies. These  views  I  respectfully  submit  to  your  consideration. ALEXANDER  H.  STEPHENS. Confederate  States  of  America,  War  Department, Montgomery,  April  18,  1861.
