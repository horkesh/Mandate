## Reading the evidence

The tables preserve the distinction between nomination, confirmation, commission or rank, assignment, actual assumption, relief, a request to resign, acceptance and a conditional departure. `date` belongs to the named `event`; `through` is only a date explicitly supplied by that source. A blank means unknown, not a vacancy or continued service. `precision=year` must never be converted into a guessed day. Reference tenures are the compiler’s intervals, not independently verified receipt or duty records. Wounds will not imply a duration of incapacity without evidence of that duration.

Every citation has a source key and printed page. [Sources and reading limits](sources.md) identifies the editions; [scan-manifest.json](scan-manifest.json) maps every cited page to a retained scan, URL, access-image index and SHA-256. Retaining a whole page does not mean every neighboring entry was read. Search snippets are not historical evidence.

These are factual research tables in the additive manner of S12/S38, not seven-column proposals to change judgment cells. No apply tool should consume them. ## Civil-office findings

Walker’s tender of 10 September 1861 requests retirement on the 16th; Davis accepts on the 15th. These are three different dates. [OR IV, I, pp602–603, 613](https://archive.org/details/cu31924079579524/page/602), [Wright, p153](https://archive.org/details/generalofficers00wrigrich/page/153).

The permanent Confederate cabinet is nominated in a message dated 17 March 1862 and confirmed on 18 March. Wright additionally lists Benjamin acting at War on 18–23 March beside Randolph’s 18 March entry. **[I]** Preserve this handover overlap pending an execution record. The journal supplies Watts, whom Wright’s Attorney-General list omits. [Confederate Senate journal II, pp72–74](https://archive.org/details/journalofcongres02confiala/page/72), [Wright, pp152–153](https://archive.org/details/generalofficers00wrigrich/page/152).

Davis’s 15 November letter accepts Randolph’s resignation. Campbell’s 17 November tender takes effect when a successor is appointed. A letter addresses Seddon as Secretary on 20 November, while Wright gives the 21st. **[I]** Neither an address nor a directory establishes the exact assumption; preserve both. [OR IV, II, pp178–179, 196](https://archive.org/details/cu31924080778909/page/178), [Wright, p154](https://archive.org/details/generalofficers00wrigrich/page/154).

Halleck’s general-in-chief assignment is ordered on 11 July 1862; Heitman records actual assumption on 23 July. McClellan’s 11 March order retains the Potomac command and sends department reports directly to War after receipt. **[I]** Do not invent a general-in-chief for the intervening period. [Lincoln, II, pp137, 203](https://archive.org/details/completeworksofa02linc_0/page/137), [Heitman I, p17](https://archive.org/details/historicalregist01heitrich/page/17).

## Proposed rulings [I]

Keep event dates and their legal or operational meanings separate. Prefer contemporary instruments for the act they record, while preserving reference conflicts. Reject a universal “appointment date,” invented acting tenures, backdating the almanac’s 1863 cabinet snapshot, treating a conditional resignation as immediate, and deriving character from promotion or tenure. These are proposals for the lead; nothing is applied.
