## Authority and receiving command

**[H]** Scott's assignment directs him to assume Army command, rather than
merely announcing rank (Richardson IV p.73). The 1857 Regulations deny duty by
commission alone and describe command assignments (pp.2–3, paragraphs 8,11,14).
Orders belong to the commander, receive his approval before staff issue, pass
through intermediate commanders, and are addressed to the commander present
(pp.57–60, paragraphs 422–429,438–441).

**[I]** Accordingly, Townsend, H. L. Scott, Porter, Talbot, Fry, Garnett, Deas,
Withers, Jordan and Sam Jones carry the specified superior's paper; their staff
signatures create no separate granting principal. Cameron/Walker papers and
the President's governmental staff channel are distinguished from a field
commander's orders. The authority column applies 0124/0129; it does not claim
to have recovered every departmental enabling statute. Governors' bodies and
neutral offers retain the undertaking/journey/receipt separation in 0126.
Receiving at Washington is not receiving into McDowell's department (0133).

## New York: preserve the changing direction and the service conflict

**[H]** Cameron's May 13 papers request five three-year regiments and separately
the ten returned by Read, expected for three years/war (OR III-I p.191).
Read's May 11 original report actually describes **orders to muster**, with
May 8–10 dates; they are not performed muster dates (pp.188–189). Its named
Walrath, Quinby and Davies regiments support Twelfth, Thirteenth and Sixteenth
identity links, while the term expectation remains a condition, not a rewritten
contract. Morgan promises two-year/war bodies (p.201). May 15's five Washington /
nine Fort Monroe request and contingent Fish authority concern the same fourteen
bodies in New York City (pp.204,206; U15–U19), not a new additional fourteen.

**[H]** Keyes May 27 reports Walrath/Quinby ordered from Elmira to Washington via
Harrisburg/Baltimore and conditional permission for Ninth/Seventy-ninth
(OR III-I p.239; U22). The State openings identify Walrath's Twelfth and Quinby's
Thirteenth Volunteers separately from militia (Phisterer III pp.1873,1887).

**[H]** The June sequence is especially material:

1. Franklin June 4 quotes a received June 3 direction for ready organized
   three-year regiments south of Albany to Washington by Harrisburg, but asks
   whether it includes bodies already under other orders (III-I pp.248–249;
   U25). Cameron June 5 confirms ready Elmira regiments to Harrisburg (p.255;
   U27).
2. Cameron June 12 tells Franklin to muster enrolled State bodies for **two
   years**, no more than **twenty-two regiments**, under the understanding they
   move to Patterson (III-I pp.266–267; U28).
3. Scott June 15, through Townsend, tells Du Barry to forward Third Michigan
   and the other described Harrisburg arrivals to **Washington**, and transmit
   this to Patterson (I-II p.690; U31).
4. Read June 18 reports planned departures by colonel/number, explains the
   Patterson understanding and later Washington telegram, and directs the depot
   to consult Franklin before dispatch (III-I pp.276–277; U32). Morgan June 22
   reports forwarded regiments under his Washington undertaking (p.290; U34).

**[I]** This is a sequence of received directions and choices. It cannot become
a permanent State-to-Patterson or State-to-McDowell table. The State openings
describe different performed departures, including Thirty-first June 24 and
Thirty-second June 29, after the earlier intended dates (Phisterer III
pp.2089,2103). Twenty-seventh and Forty-first first depart/arrive in July in
their own openings (pp.2039,2237); April–June eligibility is not their performed
journey. Those July edges remain recovery gaps, not permission to backdate them.

## Onward assignments and actual receipt

**[H]** McDowell's **GO1, May 28**, is a recovered early organisation:
Stone receives First Michigan, New York Zouaves and Fifth Massachusetts;
Heintzelman the Eighth, Twelfth and Twenty-fifth NY; Hunter the Fifth,
Twenty-eighth and Sixty-ninth NY, plus properly scoped nearby unassigned bodies
(OR I-LI.1 pp.389–390; U39). Eighth/Twelfth lack militia/volunteer qualifiers in
this paper: their joins are held, not silently assigned to Blenker or Walrath.
The numbered Fifth/Twenty-eighth NY in that order are not forced into similarly
numbered frozen catalogue entries.

**[H]** A direct transfer **to McDowell** is recovered on **June 23**: Mansfield
carries Scott's direction sending **Third Connecticut and Second New York under
Tompkins** that evening; McDowell must designate crossing and provide guides.
The stated wagon arrangements and expected crossing time are instructions,
not arrival (OR I-II p.716; U54). The frozen Second NY Militia join stays visibly
separate from the Second NY Volunteers sent to Fort Monroe.

**[H]** Patterson's recovered chain includes named PA regiments to Chambersburg,
SO49; present organisation SO66; First Wisconsin/Eleventh PA in SO68; Sixth
Brigade and Owen's transfer in SO76; Negley's onward Sharpsburg direction SO77;
and Yohe's First PA to Frederick SO78 (OR I-LI.1 pp.390,397–398,406–407;
U40/U42–U43/U48–U50). These organise or move existing bodies; they do not replace
the missing governor dispatches. The Fourth CT/Patterson assignment is positive
evidence against a general CT-to-McDowell rule (p.406).

**[H]** Burnside provides an unusually clear performed link: Scott SO99 of
June 8 directs his regiment/battery to Carlisle for Patterson; Burnside's June 22
report records Washington departure June 10, reporting to Patterson for duty
at Chambersburg June 11, and return to Washington June 19 after recall. Scott's
June 16 recall, Patterson's addressed instructions and the June 17 regulars
orders remain separate papers (OR I-LI.1 p.397; I-II pp.691,694–696,715–716;
U41/U45–U47/U53). This is First Rhode Island, not every Rhode Island body.

**[H]** Scott's June 30 SO109 sends Stone's remaining column to Patterson after
specified artillery, mounted troops and willing DC volunteers return to
Washington. Townsend's notice names additional components, including a
First PA under **Colonel Patterson**, not automatically Yohe's First PA
(OR I-LI.1 p.407; I-II p.734; U51–U52). The unresolved PA identity is retained.

## Confederate: requests, undertakings and onward selection

**[H]** Walker's April 22 printing supplies actual destination requests:
AL two/MS two/AR one/TN three infantry regiments to **Lynchburg**;
GA two/NC one/LA one to **Richmond**; Kentucky one to **Harpers Ferry**.
AR/NC/TN appear as a same-mutatis-mutandis editorial notice. A separate LA
telegram permits two if ready, not an additional third (OR IV-I pp.231–233;
C02–C10/C49). None names its eventual July regiments by number.

**[H]** Harris's April 22 undertaking, April 30 received-call/hold/recall report,
and May 2 Turney departure/Bate–Maney question are read separately
(OR IV-I pp.233,272,275; C10/C12/C14). The original governor sending copies and
the answer to the May 2 question remain unrecovered. **Maney is not Vaughn's
Third Tennessee** [I]; no ordinal matching fills that gap. The Virginia Council
on May 1 advises a reply providing at Lynchburg under Langhorne; advice to send
a telegram is not proved dispatch or receipt (OR I-II p.793; C13).

**[H] The Lynchburg routing changes.** Cooper's **June 14 original telegram is
now read**: armed/equipped arriving regiments go to Beauregard at Manassas;
unarmed regiments/companies are reported by telegraph and held for orders.
His **June 29** letter to Clay replaces that with holding **all arriving troops**
for orders and daily tabular reporting. It separately sends the arriving Rome,
Georgia companies to Richmond, without giving their regiment number
(OR I-LI.2 pp.140,151; C38/C43). The June 14 order is no longer merely an unread
reference in the later letter. Neither paper licenses an unlimited future
State-origin sweep; the changed direction operates only through causal receipt.

**[H]** Beauregard's June 20 GO20 organises troops already in his command by
colonels (I-II pp.943–944; C45). His **June 29 order** is a received-force onward
chain, with Third SC/Williams replacing First SC/Gregg before Gregg moves to
Fairfax Station, Eighth SC/Cash then moving to Fairfax Court-House, and
Eighteenth VA/Withers replacing it at Germantown; Withers and Twenty-eighth
VA/Preston are held ready to support Bonham against superior attack.
Transport, arrivals and departures govern that sequence (I-LI.2 p.151; C44).
GO24 and July 25 SO169 are identity controls, not backward-dated sending
authorities (I-LI.2 p.123; I-II pp.999–1000).
