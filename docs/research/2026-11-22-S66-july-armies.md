## Orders and successive organisations

**McDowell.** General Orders No. 13, 8 July, organises the five divisions and their brigades (OR I-LI.1 pp.413–414). Earlier Special Orders Nos.17 and 18, 1 July, establish the initial Franklin, Willcox and Andrew Porter brigades (pp.408–409). Mansfield had already organised Burnside and Richardson under Special Orders No.167, 2 July, and Blenker under No.169, 3 July (pp.409–410). The register gives those earlier dates with their narrower membership, and the 8 July organisation separately. It does not imply that every 21 July attachment entered on the brigade's first day.

The 21 July compiled roster is a different observation (OR I-II pp.314–315). It includes the regular infantry and cavalry battalions, Marines, batteries and howitzers; the regular battalions are represented by their listed regimental company components, **without an extra full battalion row to add to them**. Fourth Michigan is expressly not engaged, Blenker is in reserve at Centreville, and Runyon's reserve is separate (p.315). Dyer groups Runyon's three-month New Jersey men and the three-year/New York contingent into two brigades but names no commanders there (p.272). The OR roster does not provide those two brigade commanders; they remain unknown.

The 8 July order prints Second Maine under both Keyes and Howard; the later battle roster has Third Maine under Howard (OR I-LI.1 pp.413–414; OR I-II pp.314–315). Both readings remain. The order also calls Sherman's Thirteenth New York "Militia" and prints Twenty-fourth **[Twenty-seventh]** Pennsylvania under Blenker (OR I-LI.1 p.414). The bracketed Pennsylvania correction is disclosed; the Thirteenth's earlier identity remains held rather than silently borrowing a militia or volunteer regiment's path. These conflicts preclude summing the order as a deduplicated troop count.

General Orders No.17 is **16 July**, as the p.303 image shows; OCR reads 10. Its reserve/station duties and exempted New York fort regiments are pp.303–305. Its date and dispositions are not a new organisation order. The 16–17 July duty return is p.309; none of its totals is allocated to regiment rows here. Miles's division is explicitly broken up on 25 July (OR I-LI.1 p.425); a July organisation cannot be treated as permanent.

**Washington.** Mansfield's two 2 July brigades and his 3 July Blenker brigade are Washington observations before McDowell's 8 July arrangement (OR I-LI.1 pp.409–414). On 21 July he sends McCunn with the 37th, 15th, 26th and 25th New York toward Fairfax Station, then holds his last two regiments at Alexandria; Scott recalls the regiments to their Washington camps on 22 July (OR I-II pp.751,754). This is an instruction/recall chain, not proof that those four fought at Manassas. Dyer's 15th New York history places a McCunn attachment on 30 July–4 August (p.1411); it does not erase the 21 July letter.

Additional month-level station histories cover the 6th Maine (Dyer p.1220), 9th Massachusetts (p.1251), 22nd/23rd New York (p.1413), 24th (p.1414), 30th (p.1415), 33rd (p.1416), 35th (p.1417), 40th (p.1418), 42nd (p.1419), and 26th Pennsylvania (p.1585). **These are Washington-area service observations, not a complete same-day departmental garrison return or proof Mansfield commanded every station.** Later attachments to the Division of the Potomac are not backdated into July brigades. The 25th Pennsylvania's arsenal/Fort Washington companies remain distinct from the companies with Stone (Dyer p.1585). A complete same-day Washington departmental roster remains a research hold.

**Patterson.** Special Orders No.66, 10 June, names five brigades and their units. No.68, 11 June, creates Cadwalader's First Division from brigades 1,3,4 and Keim's Second from 2,5, and initially adds First Wisconsin/Eleventh Pennsylvania to Negley (OR I-LI.1 pp.397–398). No.76, 20 June, creates Abercrombie's Sixth Brigade from Eleventh Pennsylvania, First Wisconsin and Fourth Connecticut; it also orders Twenty-fourth Pennsylvania from Wynkoop to Negley when the Sixth changes location (p.406). That conditional transfer is not a calendar arrival.

No.94, 8 July, forms Stone's Seventh and temporarily attaches it to Keim. No.95 temporarily puts Nineteenth and Twenty-eighth New York with Wynkoop (p.415). No.96, 10 July, creates Sandford's Third Division from Stone's Seventh and an Eighth under the senior colonel, listing the Fifth and Twelfth New York militia alongside Nineteenth/Twenty-eighth (p.416). Dyer p.270 names Butterfield as Eighth Brigade commander; the order itself does not. These administrative observations stay separate from the 2 July action reports (OR I-II pp.179–182), 8 July movement circular (pp.161–162), and 28 June two-division duty abstract (p.187).

SO94 gives **five** Twenty-fifth Pennsylvania companies; SO96 gives **six** (OR I-LI.1 pp.415–416). Dyer also gives five field companies and five at the arsenal/fort (p.1585). The difference remains unresolved. Dyer's Twelfth Pennsylvania is in Williams's brigade but on railroad guard (pp.270,1582); SO66 instead names Twentieth Pennsylvania in its 10 June list (OR I-LI.1 p.398). Do not replace one observation with the other or count railroad guards as a field arrival. Likewise Dyer dates Nineteenth New York's joining Patterson to 6 July, while Patterson's 9 July letter reports its arrival on the preceding day (Dyer p.1412; OR I-II p.162).

**Beauregard.** General Orders No.20, 20 June, creates six brigades under Bonham, Ewell, D. R. Jones, Terrett, Cocke and Early, naming their regiments by colonel (OR I-II pp.943–944). The register preserves this arrangement separately from the 21 July roster, retrospectively returned on 25 September, where Longstreet commands the Fourth (pp.469–470,568). The order does not date Longstreet's succession or every added regiment. Where the read sources do not settle the numbered identity of Cocke's/Preston's early regiments, the register retains those colonel designations with unknown numbers.

The battle combinations overlap that return. Evans's Seventh includes Fourth South Carolina, Wheat's First Special Louisiana Battalion, Terry's cavalry and Davidson's section of Latham (pp.558–559); Alexander's Campbell Rangers also report service with Wheat (pp.564–565). Early marches with Seventh Virginia, Seventh Louisiana and the substituted Thirteenth Mississippi, while his own Twenty-fourth Virginia companies remain with Longstreet (pp.543,555–558). Ewell reports Rosser's Washington Artillery and Jenifer's four cavalry companies (pp.536–537). Kemper and Radford's cavalry serve with Bonham (pp.532–536); Jones names Flood's troop (p.538). The army's unbrigaded artillery/cavalry list is p.470. Battery sections, companies and their parent battalions overlap; **no additive army total is calculated**.

The 20 July plan allocates batteries and cavalry to tactical combinations it calls divisions, and is approved at 4:30 a.m. on 21 July (OR I-II pp.479–480). Planned attachments have their own rows; they are not assertions that the plan was executed or that a standing divisional institution had been created.

**Johnston and joining forces.** The 30 June roster gives Jackson, Bartow, Bee and Elzey, with their artillery, plus unbrigaded First Virginia Cavalry and Thirty-third Virginia (OR I-II p.470). Its organising orders/dates were not found in the passages read: **unknown**, not 30 June. The later engaged list separately names Jackson's five Virginia regiments, Bee/Bartow's combined force including Sixth North Carolina and two Eleventh Mississippi companies, E. K. Smith/Elzey's arriving brigade, five batteries and Stuart's cavalry (p.569). Rhett leaves the Maryland regiment's number blank there and says he cannot furnish strengths; its link to June's First Maryland remains [I].

Johnston says the Seventh/Eighth Georgia and Jackson's five Virginia regiments preceded his noon 20 July arrival, while Fourth Alabama, Second Mississippi and two Eleventh Mississippi companies accompanied him. This supplies arrival bounds and a partial-company arrival, not dates for every regiment of the June army. Infantry were to go by rail from Piedmont; cavalry and artillery marched. Carson/Meem militia and sick remained at Winchester (OR I-II p.473). The regimental identities of that militia are unknown in the read passages, explicitly held in the coverage register. Third Tennessee's earlier Winchester arrival is in the 13 June narrative (pp.471–472).

Holmes reports moving with Second Tennessee, First Arkansas and Walker's battery, later employing Scott's cavalry (pp.565–566). The 17 July order asked for three regiments and a battery, subject to district security, and left Ruggles there (pp.980–981). An order for three is not evidence three arrived. Holmes prints "Wednesday, the 18th"; the weekday/date conflict is retained. Eighth Virginia reports marching from Leesburg on 18 July and reaching Cocke about noon the following day (p.545). Hampton's six infantry companies arrive on the morning of 21 July after thirty hours detained in cars; his whole Legion did not thereby arrive (pp.566–567). Orders distinguish its infantry, marching cavalry and delayed artillery, McRae's Fifth North Carolina and Barksdale's regiment; the latter is identified as Thirteenth Mississippi in Early's report. Unnamed Alabama/Mississippi battalion promises stay unnamed (pp.556,980–981).

## Regiment paths and the cohort boundary

Dyer is used for unit histories, not as authority to override a different dated OR observation. Examples that constrain the mechanism:

- Second Maine was organised for three months, then mustered into US service for two and three years on 28 May (Dyer p.1219). A single original term cannot describe every observation.
- First Minnesota's April three-month entry and 10 May three-year reorganisation remain separate (Dyer p.1296).
- New Jersey's same-numbered three-month and three-year regiments are distinct identities (Dyer pp.1356–1358).
- New York militia numbers and later volunteer numbers require aliases: Second militia/82nd (p.1437), Ninth militia/83rd (p.1437), Fourteenth Brooklyn militia/84th (p.1438). The named militia and same-number volunteer regiments must not be joined by number alone.
- Twelfth and Thirteenth New York histories distinguish State terms from initial US service, as does Twenty-first (Dyer pp.1410,1413). Twenty-fourth and Thirty-third distinguish muster from credited dates (pp.1414,1416). Sixteenth Pennsylvania has a three-year **company** exception within its three-month entry (p.1584).

Most read histories give an organisation/muster or journey without identifying the raising governor and exact dated call. Those fields remain unknown. An "organised" date is not relabelled "mustered". Departure for Washington is not an arrival in the July field command. The paths retain the relevant opening text and explicitly distinguish the few arrival dates/bounds supplied by OR. No source warrants allocating the pack's statewide batches among these regiments.

**[I] Crosswalk ruling:** keep every named regiment unmatched until the state, call/transfer, date and represented scope can actually be joined. State candidates and exact model cohort dates are review aids, not positive identity matches. A model governor ID is not independent historical proof that he raised this regiment. The S64 final save omits already-disbanded cohorts; supplementing it with the earlier S64 axis does not make an exhaustive enlistment history. The three accepted partial local inclusions remain partial: Harpers Ferry Virginia contingent, Garland's four-company battalion, Preston's seven-company battalion. None becomes a complete July regiment/brigade from its commander's surname (S64 crosswalk; OR I-II pp.824,826–827,846–847,901–902).
