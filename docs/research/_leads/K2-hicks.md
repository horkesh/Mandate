# K2: Hicks, "To the People of Maryland" (the "word nor deed" address): date and citation dates

Agent leads-K2-hicks, 2026-11-16. Read in print: Rebellion Record vol. 1 (archive.org `rebellrecord01moorrich`, OCR full text, copy downloaded and searched); Appletons' American Annual Cyclopaedia 1861 (archive.org `americanannualcy00dapp`, vol. for 1861); McPherson, Political History of the Great Rebellion (archive.org `politicalhistory00mcph`); Scharf, History of Maryland vol. 3 (archive.org `historyofmarylan03scha`); Addresses on the Death of Hon. T. H. Hicks (GPO 1865, Making of America text via distantreader.org copy). Not reached: the 1860-61 newspapers themselves (Chronicling America redirects/blocked; Georgia Historic Newspapers gave a "Verify Access" gate, not bypassed; MdHS record mdhistory.org/digital-resource/2910 returned 403), and the broadside SC 295 itself.

## 1. The date of the "word nor deed" address

**Verdict: the Rebellion Record prints the text with NO date line. The date is not fixed by any contemporary primary I could read. Best-supported date: 3 January 1861 (finding aids + McPherson); one retrospective source says 7 January; one says "June 1861" and is wrong.**

### 1a. The print: Rebellion Record vol. 1 (Moore, 1861), Documents, Doc. 16, pp. 17-18 (STRONG as a contemporary printing, but undated)
- Heading as printed (OCR): `Doc.  16.— EXTRACT   FROM   GOY.   HICKS' ADDRESS.` (index: "16. Gov. Hicks' Address, 17").
- It is an "extract"; it has no date line, no place line, no heading "To the People of Maryland", and ends `(Signed)  THOMAS  H.  HICKS.` There is no source credit (no newspaper name) either.
- Page: begins p. 17 (page 17 starts after the Carrington call), runs to p. 18 (running head "REBELLION RECORD, 1860-61").
- Words (OCR, p. 17-18): opens "I firmly believe that a division of this Government would inevitably produce civil war." Closes: "In the course of nature, I cannot have long to live, and I fervently trust to be allowed to end my days a citizen of this glorious Union. But should I be compelled to witness the downfall of that Government inherited from our fathers, established, as it were, by the special favor of God, I will at least have the consolation, at my dying hour, that I neither by word nor deed assisted in hastening its disruption. (Signed) THOMAS H. HICKS." It also contains "pull down the pillars of this 'accursed Union'" and "the men who have embarked in this scheme to convene the Legislature".
- Position only: Doc. 15 (Carrington's Call) is dated `Jan. 5, 1861`; Doc. 17 (Ellis-Holt) is dated `JANUARY 12, 1861`. Doc. 14 (Buchanan fast day) is `Dec. 14, 1860`. So Moore placed it between 5 and 12 January in a loosely chronological run. That is a placing, not a date, and Moore's order is not strictly by date (Doc. 14 sits before Doc. 15 correctly, but Docs are grouped by when Moore found them).
- Identity: this is certainly an address that refuses to convene the Legislature ("The men who have embarked in this scheme to convene the Legislature, will spare no pains..."; "What could the Legislature do in this crisis, if convened"). It is the same text Appletons prints (below), so it is one address, not two.

### 1b. Appletons' American Annual Cyclopaedia, vol. for 1861 (published 1862), art. MARYLAND, p. 443 (contemporary-era annual; secondary compilation, no date for this item)
- Text: after the Commissioner of Mississippi episode ("the Governor replied on the 19th of December") and "From this time unparalleled efforts were made to induce the Governor to call an extra session", it says the friends of the Union "memorialized the Governor in opposition to such a measure ... In answer to these appeals he issued an address, stating very fully his views, in which he said:" and prints the same text, ending "neither by word nor deed assisted in hastening its disruption. THOMAS H. HICKS." (running head MARYLAND; p. 443; the next page, p. 444, then reaches 18-19 February). No date given. It places the address after the Union memorial and before the 18-19 Feb Baltimore convention.

### 1c. Dated pointers (none is the address read in a dated printing)
- McPherson, *Political History of the United States ... Great Rebellion* (1864/65 ed., archive.org `politicalhistory00mcph`), chronology under MARYLAND, running head "SECESSION MOVEMENT DEVELOPED", page 9 (the page-head number OCR reads "9"; the entry sits just below it): `January 3d, 1861. Henry Winter Davis issued an address in favor of the Union.` / `3d. Numerous Union meetings in various parts of the State. Gov. Hicks issued an address to the people against secession.` Contemporary compilation by the House Clerk, drawn from the press; a pointer, not on the pack's citable list, and not a dated printing of the text. It does give "address to the people against secession" on 3 Jan.
- Maryland State Archives exhibit "Straddling Secession" (msa.maryland.gov/msa/educ/exhibits/hicks/html/case1.html), caption (read via fetch; paraphrasing tool, marked snippet-grade): `Thomas Holliday Hicks, January 3, 1861 MSA SC 295`, title "To the People of Maryland". Finding aid only. The Maryland Center for History and Culture record (403 to me) is reported by search as a broadside "To the People of Maryland" dated 1861-01-03. Finding aid only (snippet).
- Neither finding aid shows the broadside's text. No source I could read confirms that the broadside text is the "word nor deed" text, but McPherson's description ("address to the people against secession", 3 Jan) and the Rebellion Record's position (between 5 and 12 Jan, after the 2 Jan Diary item that 5,000 Baltimoreans had signed a letter approving his refusal to convene the Legislature) are consistent with it.
- Reverdy Johnson, Senate eulogy, 15 Feb 1865, *Addresses on the Death of Hon. T. H. Hicks* (GPO 1865), p. 7: "In an address to the people of the State, of the 7th of January, 1861, he said: ... that I neither by word or deed assisted in hastening its disruption." WEAK (retrospective, memorial oratory; also misquotes "or deed"). Gives 7 January.
- Rep. Webster, same volume, pp. 29-30: "Addressing the people of Maryland by proclamation, in the midst of the dangers which encircled them in June, 1861 ... neither by word nor deed assisted in hastening its disruption." WEAK and WRONG: Hicks's June 1861 proclamation (RR Doc. 240, 7 June) is about State arms and contains no such sentence. Do not cite this.
- A New York Times item "Hicks will not convene the Maryland legislature", 7 January 1861, is cited by the Wikipedia page for Hicks (search result); I could not read it. If it prints the address, that could explain Johnson's "7th". Unread.

**Recommendation for the pack:** cite RR vol. 1, Doc. 16, pp. 17-18 for the words, and date the address "3 January 1861 (broadside, per MSA SC 295 and McPherson); printed in the press by 7 January". If a single date is needed, use 3 January 1861. Do not state the date as read from the Rebellion Record; Moore gives none. To close the gap: read the Baltimore Sun/American of 3-5 Jan 1861 or the NYT of 7 Jan 1861.

## 2. Earlier refusal: late November / December 1860

Hicks refused to convene the Legislature before the January address. There was no second *address to the people* in December that I could find, but there are two dated refusals:

- **27 November 1860**: letter to Thomas G. Pratt, Sprigg Harwood, J. R. Franklin, N. H. Green, Llewellyn Boyle and J. Pinkney, declining an extra session. Sources: McPherson chronology, MARYLAND, same page region ("November 27th, 1860. Gov. Hicks declined to call a special session of the Legislature, in response to a request for such convening from Thomas G. Pratt, Sprigg Harwood, J. R. Franklin, N. H. Green, Llewellyn Boyle, and J. Pinkney"), compilation; and Scharf, *History of Maryland* vol. 3 (1879), c. p. 366, which quotes Wallis's 1 Feb 1861 speech: "On the 27th of November, 1860, Governor Hicks addressed a letter to the Hon. Thomas G. Pratt, and other gentlemen ... 'I cannot but believe that the convening of the Legislature in extra session at this time, would only have the effect of increasing and reviving the excitement now pervading the country'" (secondary, quoting the letter; WEAK). Newspaper notices (via the Crossroads of War abstracts, snippet-grade, not read in print): Frederick Examiner 5 Dec 1860 approves "his letter"; Westminster American Sentinel 7 Dec 1860 p. 2: Hicks refused because a session "might do more harm than good". The letter's own text is not in the Rebellion Record; I did not find a primary printing.
- **19 December 1860**: reply to A. H. Handy, Commissioner of Mississippi. Rebellion Record vol. 1, Documents, Doc. 1, pp. 1-2 (STRONG, contemporary printing). Date line exactly: `STATE OF MARYLAND, EXECUTIVE CHAMBER, ANNAPOLIS, Dec. 19, 1860.` Refusal words: "I am unable to inform you when the Legislature of this State will be called together, for until I can perceive the necessity for such a step I am not willing to awake the apprehension and excite the alarm which such a call at the present time could not fail to create." Signed THOS. H. HICKS; addressed to "Hon. A. H. HANDY, Commissioner of Mississippi." Also notes he will act "in fraternal concert, with the other Border States."
- (Maryland State Archives bio: 8 Dec 1860 letter to Capt. Contee, "If the Union must be dissolved let it be done calmly, deliberately..."; not a refusal of the Legislature; snippet-grade.)
