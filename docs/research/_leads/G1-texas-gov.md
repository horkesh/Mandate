# G1: Who held the office of Governor of Texas, 5 March 1861 and after

Question: the pack's `st_gov_texas` has no holder on 5 March 1861. Who held the office, and when did it change?

Sources read (OCR downloaded from archive.org, searched and read in my own scratch dir):

- **JSC** = *Journal of the Secession Convention of Texas, 1861*, ed. E. W. Winkler (Austin: Texas Library and Historical Commission, 1912), archive.org `journalofsecessi00texa`. The Journal text is the Convention's own minutes (STRONG, official). Winkler's chronology, footnotes and appendices are editorial (STRONG as a government edition printing primaries; the editor's own words are a reference-grade secondary). Page = printed running-head page; OCR page-head numbers are sometimes garbled, so pages are +/-1 where noted.
- **OR I/1** = *War of the Rebellion*, Series I vol. 1, archive.org `warofrebellionco0001unit_l7z0` (OCR poor but legible at these passages). Official, STRONG.

Not reached: Houston's own 16 March written address (see C), Appletons' / Handbook of Texas / Biographical Directory entries for birth and death dates (see D).

## A. Summary answer

| Date | Holder | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 5 March 1861 | **Sam Houston**, Governor, in office and acting as such | JSC pp. 113-114 (his letter of 6 March replying to the Convention's committee of 5 March, signed "Sam Houston", styled "Executive"); OR I/1 pp. 612-613 (E. W. Cave, Secretary of State, 13 March, writing for "his excellency Governor Houston") |
| 16 March 1861, noon-evening | Houston declared out; **Edward Clark**, Lieutenant Governor, treated as Governor from that evening | JSC pp. 183-187 |
| 21 March | "Governor Clark" in the Convention's own resolution | JSC p. 222 |
| 26 March | "the administration of Governor Clark. General Houston, since he was deposed..." (letter of T. N. Waul to Davis) | OR I/1 p. 616 |
| 29 April | "his Excellency Governor Clark" (Confederate AIG S. Cooper to Van Dorn) | OR I/1 p. 630 |

Pack recommendation: holder on 5 March = Houston (flag him as hostile to the Convention and to Confederate control, which is what the sources show). Event on 16 March: Houston out, Clark in. A call from Davis to Texas on 5 March therefore reaches Houston, who on the record would refuse it (item B2). The nuance: Texas officially became "independent" on 2 March by the vote (JSC p. 114, committee text), and the Convention treated Texas as a Confederate State from 5 March, but the sitting Governor never recognized either step until deposed.

## B. Events and dates, with words

### B1. Ordinance of secession, vote, reassembly (context only)
- 1 Feb 1861, Friday: ordinance of secession "read a 3rd time and passed", Governor Houston present as an invited guest ("The Governor was then announced who received and accepted an invitation to a seat on the right of the President"). JSC p. 48-49. The roll printed gives Yeas "166", Nays "8" (p. 49); Winkler's appendix note says "167 in favor to 7 against" (OCR line near p. 3xx; note says so). Take 166-8 from the roll; flag the 167-7 discrepancy.
- 23 Feb: popular vote. Official vote total read to the Convention 14 March: "For secession 46,129; against secession 14,697 votes" (JSC p. 176, in the Convention's 15 March proceedings; the count is cumulative to that date).
- Convention reassembled Saturday 2 March, 2 pm (JSC p. 86, header "ADJOURNED SESSION. City of Austin Texas Saturday March 2nd A. D. 1861", 67 answered the roll, "No quorum present").
- 4 March: Houston's proclamation announcing the vote result, printed in a Winkler footnote (p. 88): "Now, therefore, I, Sam Houston, Governor of the State of Texas, do hereby issue this my proclamation declaring that a large majority of the votes received and counted, of said election, are in favor of the 'secession' of the State of Texas from the United States of America... Given under my hand and the Great Seal of the State of Texas, at Austin, this the 4th day of March, A. D. 1861. Sam Houston. By the governor. E. W. Cave, Secretary of State." Winkler cites the *Comprehensive History of Texas* II, 114-115 and the Austin *Southern Intelligencer* 13 March 1861 as his sources; I did not read the newspaper (editor's print of a primary: STRONG-ish, original not seen).

### B2. Houston refuses to recognize the Convention (5 to 13 March)
- 5 March (Tue): Convention resolution "that a committee of five be appointed to wait upon his Excellency the Governor and inform him that the Convention has reassembled, and that the ordinance of secession has been ratified by the people, and that the State of Texas is and has been, from the 2d of this current month, a free, sovereign and independent State." JSC p. 113.
- 6 March: Houston's reply, headed "Executive Department Austin Texas March 6/61", signed "Sam Houston" (JSC pp. 113-114): "when the Legislature authorized the Convention to submit the proposition to the people ... it was understood that the performance of that act, when done, would terminate the existence of the Convention ... in the opinion of the Executive its powers were then exhausted." And: "Until then, it will be the duty of the Executive, as well as all State Officers, to continue in the lawful discharge of their functions, confining their action to the sphere of Texas only." The Convention (secret session) laid it on the table and ordered the Constitution Committee to "prepare and report an ordinance suited to vindicate the majesty of the people" (JSC p. 114, Stewart of Gonzales's substitute: tabled; p. 114 text).
- 13 March: Secretary of State E. W. Cave, "instructed" by "his Excellency Governor Houston", to L. P. Walker (Confederate Secretary of War), OR I/1 pp. 612-613: Houston "cannot recognize any obligation" to the Confederate government; he "has notified the Convention ... that he does not recognize it as a convention of the people of Texas"; "Since the submission of the ordinance he has had no correspondence with the Convention other than to deny their authority." Also pp. 612-613 on Convention agents seizing the posts "without his knowledge or sanction". STRONG (official correspondence, printed in the OR).
- Context for item 5 March: also in JSC p. 399-400 (Public Safety committee report, 21 March, describing an earlier February talk) the committee calls him "the then executive of the State" and "Gov. Houston".

### B3. The oath ordinances (13 to 14 March) and the notice
- 13 March (Wed): Payne of Hopkins introduces "An Ordinance to amend the 1st Section of the 7th Article of the Constitution of the State of Texas", which requires "Members of the Legislature, and all officers under the State government before they enter upon the duties of their offices and all officers now in office" to take an oath (the draft: "...agreeably to the Constitution and laws of this State and of the Confederate States of America while Texas may remain one of said Confederation..."), JSC pp. 146-147. Adopted in amended form 14 March (Wiley's amendment "And ordinances of the Convention which assembled at Austin on the 28th day of January A. D. 1861" adopted, p. 147). The printed Journal does not give the final adopted text (Winkler: "The original draft of this ordinance has not been found" for Wiley's companion, p. 98).
- 14 March (Thu): ordinance "to provide for the continuance of the existing State government" taken up and amended (JSC pp. 164-166: Rainey strikes the proviso to section 2; Hicks adds that Convention members who hold offices take the oath "at 12 o'clock M. on the day after the adoption"; T. J. Chambers's amendment, "he shall be deemed and held to be guilty of a high crime and subject to the penalty of treason, and all his pretended official acts shall be null and void", was offered at the end of section 2 (adopted? the Journal shows it offered, then previous question; I did not verify its adoption); Portis adds the date "adopted by this Convention on the 14th day of March A. D. 1861"). The 137-1 roll at p. 165 is the oath-amendment ordinance (7th Article), passed earlier that day; the continuance ordinance's own adoption vote, which runs on from p. 166 to p. 167, I did not read to the end. The Journal is internally inconsistent: p. 183 says the continuance ordinance "was adopted on the 11th day of March"; pp. 178, 184 say 14 March. Take 14 March (three of four mentions; the 11th is a misprint).
- 14 March: President O. M. Roberts' notice to Houston, JSC pp. 178-179 (words): "An ordinance has this day been adopted by the people of the State of Texas, in Convention assembled, prescribing an oath of office for all officers of the State of Texas who held office on the 2nd day of March 1861 ... it is made my duty, as President of the Convention, to notify you ... that on Saturday the 16th day of March 1861, at 12 o'clock, M, the Convention will be prepared to receive your Excellency, and the appointed officers under said ordinance, to administer the oath of office prescribed." (The ordinance gives three days, "Sundays excepted", the Journal p. 166 says.) Roberts addresses him as "His Excellency, Sam Houston, Governor of the State of Texas".
- 15 March, Chilton's report (JSC p. 179), delivered notice to Houston "at the Executive Mansion, at 8 o'clock, P. M." on the 14th; at 1 pm on 15th "the Governor, Genl. Sam Houston, handed me the communication from yourself and also the copies of the ordinances above named, with the request that I should return them to you, and state that he did not acknowledge the existence of the Convention, and should not regard its action as binding upon him." STRONG: the Convention's own record of the refusal.

### B4. 16 March (Sat): the deposition
- JSC p. 183: "The President announced that it was in order for Genl. Sam Houston, the Governor of the State, to come forward and take the oath prescribed in the manner prescribed, should he choose so to do. Governor Houston not making his appearance, after waiting a reasonable time..." Then Edward Clark, Lieutenant Governor, qualified; so did Clement R. Johns (Comptroller), Cyrus H. Randolph (Treasurer), Francis M. White (Commissioner of the General Land Office) (pp. 183-184). "E. W. Cave, the Secretary of State being requested to come forward ... not making his appearance..." (p. 184).
- JSC p. 184: Mason's resolution: "Resolved that the office of Governor and Secretary of State of the State of Texas are hereby declared to be vacant." T. J. Chambers's amendment: "and that the Lieut. Governor Edward Clark is therefore now the lawful Governor of this State." Ireland's substitute: oath of governor to be administered to Clark "on Monday next at 12 o'clock, M". All referred to a select committee of five (Mason, Chilton, T. J. Chambers, Nelson, Devine).
- JSC pp. 185-186 (3 pm): the committee reports "An ordinance in furtherance of an ordinance to provide for the continuance of the existing State government", read three times, adopted 127 to 4 the same afternoon. The text is not printed in the Journal (title only; it is also in the list of ordinances sent for printing, 21 March, p. 224).
- JSC p. 187 (same day): the military ordinance's section 8 is amended to read "appointed by Edward Clark, Governor of the State": the Convention already treats Clark as Governor on the evening of 16 March.
- JSC p. 197 (Mon 18 March): Stapp's resolution to print copies of "the ordinance adopted by the Convention on the 1[6]th inst. declaring the office of Governor and Secretary of State vacant" (OCR "10th", but the sense and the session fix it as the 16th); laid on the table.
- JSC p. 222 (21 March): Brown's resolution adopted: Public Safety committee to "hand over to Governor Clark all papers etc. in their possession".
- Winkler's chronology (Journal front matter, a few pages in) lists "Mar. 16 Convention administered oath to State officers; Sam Houston's address to the people of Texas; Offices of Governor and Secretary of State declared vacant" (OCR scrambled but the three items are listed against 16 March), then "Mar. 18 Legislature reassembled. Sam Houston offered a message to the legislature", and "Sam Houston's farewell address" a few days later (date lost in OCR; not verified). Editor's list: reference-grade.
- Convention's own later description, "Address to the People of Texas" (dated 30 March 1861, Winkler says it was not published until after adjournment; JSC p. 257): "But the governor and secretary of state declined to take the oath when notified according to the ordinance therefor. Thereupon the Convention by another ordinance declared as consequences that each office was vacant and that the executive powers devolved on the lieutenant-governor ... And so the lieutenant-governor is performing the executive duties without consent but without resistance by the late governor, who still claims to be legally in office." Partisan (the Convention's own account), but official.
- Houston, 29 March, to Col. Waite (OR I/1 p. 551): "to sustain me in the exercise of my official functions. Allow me most respectfully to decline any such assistance of the United States Government ..." signed "Sam Houston". This is Houston himself still describing his functions after 16 March; it is the only post-deposition Houston text I read.

### B5. After
- Convention adjourned 25 March (OR I/1 p. 615, Waul to Davis 26 March: "On yesterday the Convention adjourned"); the permanent Confederate constitution was ratified 23 March (Winkler chronology; Waul: "On Saturday last, 23d instant, the ordinance of ratification was passed").
- Edward Clark remained Governor of Texas through 1861-63; Pendleton Murrah succeeded him in November 1863 (outside the window; reference-work claim only, I did not verify in print).

## C. What I could not read: Houston's own 16 March text
- Houston's address "To the People of Texas" of 16 March is cited by search results as printed in the *Writings of Sam Houston* (Williams and Barker, not public domain) and quoted in modern sources ("in the name of the nationality of Texas ..., I refuse to take this oath"; variants differ). I did not find it in any public-domain text I could download (the Journal does not print it; the *Rebellion Record* vol. 1 does not carry it, searched). Those quotations stay `(snippet)`: do not cite. What is in print and strong is only his non-appearance (JSC p. 183), his statement through Chilton (p. 179), and his 6 March letter (pp. 113-114). Suggested next place: the Austin *Southern Intelligencer* / *State Gazette*, 20-23 March 1861 (Portal to Texas History); not attempted.
- The Convention's "ordinance in furtherance" text (16 March) is not printed in the Journal; the Journal gives only title and vote. The ordinance book (*The Constitution of the State of Texas, as amended in 1861 ... The Ordinances of the Texas Convention*, Austin 1861, per JSC p. 252) would print it; not located.
- Date Clark actually took a governor's oath: Ireland proposed "Monday next" (18 March) but the committee substituted its ordinance; not found in the Journal. Treat 16 March as the effective date of the change (Convention declared the office vacant and treated Clark as Governor that evening, pp. 184, 187).
