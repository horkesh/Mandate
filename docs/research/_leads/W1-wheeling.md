# W1: The Restored Government of Virginia (Wheeling, June-July 1861)

Lead sheet, not canon. Read in OCR full text on archive.org (read in print, not through a paraphrasing mirror). OCR doubts are marked. Keys:
- JOURNAL = *Journal of the convention assembled at Wheeling, on the 11th of June, 1861*, printed by authority of the Convention (Wheeling, Daily Press, 1861); archive.org `esrp904084809` (page numbers from the running heads; the OCR of some pages is rough).
- LEWIS = Virgil A. Lewis, *How West Virginia Was Made* (1909; pack key LEWIS-HWVM); archive.org `howwestvirginiaw00lewi`. It reprints the Journal from the Wheeling *Intelligencer*; the reprint is a primary text, Lewis's own footnotes are retrospective.
- ORD = *Ordinances and acts of the restored government of Virginia ... reprinted by order of the Legislature* (Wheeling, Frew, 1866), whose first part reprints the Convention's 1861 printed Ordinances ("Printed by authority of the Convention") and then the Acts of the extra session; archive.org `esrp681482731` (Library of Congress Early State Records Project; a state government publication).
- OR I-2 = *War of the Rebellion*, Series I vol. 2 (1880), archive.org `dli.ernet.171300`.
- RR2 = Moore, *Rebellion Record* vol. 2, archive.org `rebellionrecord0002fran` (contemporary compilation; the Documents print the text).
- GLOBE = *Congressional Globe*, 37th Cong., 1st sess., archive.org `sim_united-states-congress-congressional-globe_1861-07-15_7` (the issue dated 15 July prints the Senate sitting of Saturday 13 July).

## 3. Which counties the reorganised government claimed

**Neither the Declaration nor the Ordinance of 19 June names any county.** The government was set up as the government of "the State of Virginia" and "the Commonwealth", not of a region: the Ordinance says "the state of Virginia" throughout; the Governor signs as "Governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia"; the Governor's message says the Circuit Court changes should be "confined to that part of the State in which the authority of this government is recognized" (RR2 Doc. 29, p. ~161). No count of counties appears in the Declaration or the Ordinance. The only printed county lists are:

| Item | What is printed | Source and page | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credentials report, 12 June (first report) | 28 county headings: Alexandria, Barbour, Brooke, Cabell, Doddridge and Tyler, Fairfax, Gilmer, Hampshire, Hancock, Hardy, Harrison, Jackson, Lewis, Marion, Marshall, Mason, Monongalia, Ohio, Pleasants and Ritchie, Preston, Randolph and Tucker, Roane, Taylor, Upshur, Wayne, Wetzel, Wirt, Wood | JOURNAL pp. 4-5 | STRONG |
| Roll as completed (to the Convention's close) | **32 headings, naming 35 counties**: the 28 above plus **Jefferson** (George Koontz, delegate), **Kanawha** (Ruffner, Slack), **Putnam** (Bowyer, Montague), **Webster** (H. C. Moore, seated 20 June "from the Counties of Webster, Braxton and Nicholas" per JOURNAL p. 28). Eastern Panhandle and near-Winchester: **Hampshire** (5 delegates and Senator Carskadon), **Hardy** (Michael), **Jefferson** (Koontz). Northern Virginia near Washington: **Alexandria** (H. S. Martin, Close) and **Fairfax** (Hawxhurst, E. E. Mason). | **No county from Berkeley, Morgan, Frederick, Clarke, Loudoun or Winchester sat** in this roll | by absence from the printed roll | LEWIS pp. 79-80 | inference from a printed list |
| For comparison, after the window | The Ordinance of **20 August 1861** "to provide for the formation of a new State" names **39 counties** (Logan, Wyoming, Raleigh, Fayette, Nicholas, Webster, Randolph, Tucker, Preston, Monongalia, Marion, Taylor, Barbour, Upshur, Harrison, Lewis, Braxton, Clay, Kanawha, Boone, Wayne, Cabell, Putnam, Mason, Jackson, Roane, Calhoun, Wirt, Gilmer, Ritchie, Wood, Pleasants, Tyler, Doddridge, Wetzel, Marshall, Ohio, Brooke, Hancock) and in section 3 lets the later convention add Greenbrier and Pocahontas, "and also the counties of Hampshire, Hardy, Morgan, Berkeley and Jefferson, or either of them". That is a statement of a *new-state* claim, not of the June government's claim | ORD Ordinances p. 16-17; LEWIS pp. 284-285 | STRONG |

Not printed in any source read: a statement that "the claim covered N counties"; the 40-county figure in the brief does not appear. The men of the Second Convention were chosen at "primary meetings" and by the 23 May Legislature election, not by county ballots; the credentials committee admitted those who "represent a large and substantial vote of his county" (LEWIS p. 113). So any map of regions is the credentials roll (35 counties) with the 20 August 39 as a later bound.

## 4. Earliest federal act recognising the Restored Government, to 31 July 1861

Earliest found: **Wednesday 25 June 1861**, War Department letter to Pierpont as Governor, replying to his application of 21 June. Dated and read in print.

Dated and verified: 21 June letter; 25 June Cameron letters; 26 June Smith certificate; 4 July message; 13 July Senate seating. Pierpont says (message of the opening of the session) the President "through the Secretary of War, promptly gave me very satisfactory assurances" (RR2 Doc. 29 p. ~161). Lewis's note that "letters received by him from Simon Cameron ... and Caleb B. Smith ... all showing recognition" matches these items (LEWIS p. 142 note).

Searched, nothing found: Lincoln to Pierpont in person before 31 July (no such item in OR III-1 or OR I-2 or the Globe issues read); OR III-1 contains no Pierpont correspondence (`in.ernet.dli.2015.172149` searched, none). The Senate Journal itself, the House Journal, and the Restored Government's House and Senate Journals were **not read**; the 11 July expulsion is taken from Saulsbury's words in the Globe only. The 19 June letter of Cameron to Carlile (OR I-2, about p. 709; page not pinned) predates Pierpont's election and is a letter to an individual, so it is not recognition of the government.
