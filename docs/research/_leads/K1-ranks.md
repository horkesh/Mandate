# K1: Mexican War ranks of Breckinridge and Pike (lane 1.11r leads pass)

Method: archive.org OCR full text downloaded with curl.exe, searched with Grep, read in context. Page numbers are from the OCR running heads (OCR numbers occasionally garbled; flagged where so). No Wikipedia, no snippet-only cites. Scratch: `...\scratchpad\gaps\leads-K1-ranks\`.

## Verdict in one line

Pack values `state_maj:1847` (Breckinridge) and `state_capt:1846` (Pike) are supported on rank, unit and kind. Exact commission/election dates are NOT in any strong source reached. The state-law (not Presidential) authority is supported by statute, not by a Kentucky or Arkansas document naming the vote.

## Authority rule (applies to both): STRONG

Statutes at Large vol. 9, p. 10 (Act of 13 May 1846, ch. 16, sec. 5), govinfo.gov `STATUTE-9-Pg9-2.pdf`, read as page image: volunteers "shall be accepted by the President in companies, battalions, squadrons, and regiments, whose officers shall be appointed in the manner prescribed by law in the several States and Territories to which such companies, battalions, squadrons, and regiments, shall respectively belong." So the President accepted units; the officers came from state law. That matches decision 0085's `state_` kind for volunteer officers of 1846-48.

Supporting (STRONG, official letter as reprinted): Sec. of War Marcy to the governors, 16 May 1846, as printed in Perry, *Indiana in the Mexican War* (archive.org `indianamexican00perrrich`, OCR): "all the officers with volunteers ... are to be appointed and commissioned, or such as have been appointed and commissioned in accordance with the laws of the State from whence they are taken". Indiana's own scheme there: company officers elected by the men. Not an Arkansas or Kentucky document; used only for the general rule. (Perry is a modern-era compilation; the quoted letter is the official text.)

Verified: rank captain throughout; regiment Arkansas Mounted Volunteers (Yell's); commanded a two-company squadron at Buena Vista; the regiment was received June-July 1846; Pike was discharged with his squadron about June 1847 at Monterey. Authority = state law (statute above).
Unverified: the exact date of his commission or election (1846 is the regiment's muster year, not his own date); whether the company elected him (the usual rule) or the governor commissioned him; a primary giving the vote. Not found: Arkansas Gazette 1846 (LoC blocked), Arkansas adjutant general papers, Pike's own report. Appletons gives no rank, only "commanded a squadron", so rank rests on Taylor and Robarts.

Pack check: `state_capt:1846` -- OK on kind and rank; the year is the regiment's year.
