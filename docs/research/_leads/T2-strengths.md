# T2: Confederate men under arms (31 July 1861) and the Washington Marines

Method: archive.org OCR full text downloaded and searched; every quotation below was read in the OCR, and the key pages (OR IV-1 p. 496; Welles report p. 95) were also read as page images. Labels: STRONG = contemporary official; WEAK = memoir. No memoir is used here.

OR IV-1 is on archive.org in two parts: `cu31924079579524` (pp. 1 to about 614) and `cu31924079579532` (about pp. 615 to the end, including the index). Printed page = image number minus 15 in part 1 (p. 496 = image n511).

## 1. Confederate men under arms, about 31 July 1861

### Verdict
No primary gives a count of Confederate MEN present, mustered or enrolled for 31 July 1861. The sources give (a) calls and authorisations, (b) UNIT counts, and (c) one early count of men in position. The highest primary figure for the date is a count of units, not men: **190 regiments and 34 battalions "in the field" "accepted and ready for service"** (Walker, 24 July). There is no primary figure of "upward of 200,000" in Davis's 20 July message (checked, see below); that figure is not in print there.

### What the sources support (no upper bound is proved)
- **Units**: 190 regiments + 34 battalions + "many independent companies" accepted, 24 July (Walker to Davis, OR IV-1 pp. 496-497, STRONG, official letter). Not men; "accepted and ready for service" is enrolled or tendered, not present for duty, and Walker says in the same letter that many could not be armed.
- **Men, by arithmetic (my inference, label it as such)**: the Confederate War Department itself equated 100 regiments with a force of 100,000 men (Walker's report of April: estimates "for a force in the field of 100,000 men", p. 247 and p. 248; the same estimates are headed "for 100 regiments", p. 254-255). At about 1,000 a regiment, 190 regiments is about 190,000 paper strength, and 34 battalions at roughly 300 to 500 add about 10,000 to 17,000. That gives **a rough paper estimate of about 200,000 to 210,000 for the regiments and battalions Walker counted** at 24 July [I]. It is not an upper bound on accepted men, nor on men under arms: Walker's letter puts the independent companies outside that count, leaves out twelve recently called regiments and a reserve corps of 30,000 (many already in instruction), and gives no strength for each battalion. (Corrected by the review of this branch, R44, 2026-11-19, which read OR IV-1 pp. 496-497; this paragraph had called the figure an upper bound.) A calibration ceiling of 200,000 is therefore an inferred, revisable test band [I], not a bound the sources prove. Against it: the units were accepted, not all mustered or armed; Walker's own letter says arms were short; and the later return (below) shows unit rolls unfiled.
- Do NOT put 200,000 down as men under arms. The pack's present-for-duty reading is far lower (see the lower edge).

### What the OCR does NOT contain
- No Walker report to Congress "of July 1861" beyond the 24 July letter above; Davis's 20 July message refers to one, but the report itself is not in OR IV-1 part 1 or in the JCCS pages read.
- No figure of "200,000 in the field" from Davis. If it is cited, it is not in the 20 July message; the nearest actual words are Walker's "no less than 200,000 additional volunteers" (applicants, 24 Jul).

### Beauregard and Johnston (Manassas, 21 July)
Not re-read in the OR I-2 print: the archive.org OR I-2 copy (`warofrebellionco0002unit_h8c2`) returned 401 (lending restriction, not bypassed), and `warofrebellion02unit` and `warofrebellion02unit_0` turned out to be OR III-2 and another Series, not I-2. The only Manassas Confederate strengths in hand are the earlier lane's Livermore readings (about 32,232 effectives; 33,752 present for duty, Livermore pp. 42, 77, a compilation). The Rebellion Record vol. 1 (`rebellionrecord01moor`) prints Beauregard's report and his estimate of the Federal army: "numbered in the aggregate, fifty-four thousand one hundred and forty" (an overestimate; see the earlier note). That is the enemy estimate, not his own strength.

### Recommendation for the band
Keep the lower edge at about 100,000 (Livermore's 112,040 for 1 July is an estimate he calls low). The primaries support an upper edge of **about 200,000 as accepted units on paper** (190 regiments and 34 battalions on 24 July, at about 1,000 a regiment), and no primary supports a present-for-duty count above the 25,000 + 19,000 + 16,000 early figures. If the band must be one number of men "under arms", say it is a paper band: 100,000 (estimate, low) to about 200,000 (accepted, inference from Walker's unit count). State that Davis's 20 July message gives no count.

### What was not reached
- The Colonel Commandant's own reports (Nov 1860 and Dec 1861, with the "general return" by station) are not in the Welles volumes read; they are in the Serial Set appendices and no OCR copy of those appendices was found on archive.org (the standalone "Annual report of the Commandant" items are twentieth-century). govinfo, usmcu.edu and loc.gov were not tried again (earlier lanes found usmcu.edu 403).
- The Navy Register (`registerofcommi00unit`, Marine Corps section) lists officers, their service in years and months, and the station of each (Marine Barracks, Washington, D.C., and the others). It gives no enlisted strength. Officers at Washington were not counted here.
- OR III-1 (`warofrebellion01unit`) has nothing on the Washington Marines (grep: only statutes). OR I-2's Washington garrison returns of April-May were not reachable (archive.org copy restricted). The "about 200 Marines" on 18 April in an earlier lane (NPS-CDW) is a secondary site and is not carried here.
- Secondary figures in the earlier note (63 officers and 1,775 men per the Adjutant and Inspector's report of 2 Nov 1860, from a search summary) are unread and unverified.

### Suggested use for `fb_us_marines_washington_barracks`
Strength: unknown, bounded. A sourced bracket: the yard guard alone was about 40 (24 Apr), the battalion drawn from the barracks 16 Jul was 350 (mostly recruits) after the corps was enlarged by 1,152 in April to June, and the whole Corps was about 1,775 enlisted pre-increase (unverified) and 2,500 privates by 4 Jul (Welles). State the barracks figure as "not found; between about 40 (yard guard) and 350 (battalion detail, July)", and flag it "assumed".

## Findings outside my scope
- The commandant was Colonel John Harris, not Archibald Henderson (Henderson died in January 1859). The brief's name is wrong; Welles's Dec 1861 report and ORN I-4 (24 Apr) both name Harris.
- Reynolds's battalion count differs in print: "three hundred and fifty officers and enlisted men" (his own report) against "12 officers and 336 men" (Welles). Consistent within a rounding.
- Livermore's "186,751 on the rolls" for the Union and the Union calls of 1861 are in the earlier note; nothing here changes them.
- Walker's 24 July letter is also a source for the Confederate arms shortage (261,000 stand contracted or ordered; 61,200 contracted at home, 200,000 ordered abroad), which bears on the Confederate "armed" share.
