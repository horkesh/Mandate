I went to see Mr. Lincoln on the subject of our difficulties, and Mr. Lincoln stated to me most distinctly that when Mr. Baldwin and one or two other gentlemen came here from the Virginia convention he had made the proposition to Mr. Baldwin and his colleagues distinctly that if the Virginia convention would adjourn without doing anything he would withdraw the troops from Fort Sumter. - Date: "early in the year 1861" (no month). The Globe contains no "late April" and no date for the Baldwin meeting. Hendricks replies he "do[es] not allude to this part of the history ... for the purpose of any criticism" and carries on.
- Same page: Grimes and Hendricks exchange on the Harvey telegram and on Cabinet divisions over reinforcing Sumter (Hendricks: "as late as April" Cabinet officers opposed reinforcement; Grimes: the President and some Cabinet members favoured it). ## 3. Custis will (26 March 1855; probated 1857): period printing

**Verdict: partly. One full printing of the will found on archive.org (1892, retrospective, WEAK on its own). *United States v. Lee* (STRONG, 1882) recites only the Arlington devise, not the emancipation clause. No 1850s newspaper or Congress-document printing reached.**

### 3b. The words (Decker, pp. 80-81; OCR, my spelling corrections marked)
Opening: "I, George Washington Parke Custis, of Arlington House, in the county of Alexandria and State of Virginia ... do make and ordain this instrument of writing as my last will and testament."

Executors (p. 81): "And I do constitute and appoint as my executors Lieut. Col. Robert Edward Lee, Robert Lee Randolph, of Eastern View, Rt. Rev. Bishop Meade, and George Washington Peter." Lee is named first of four; the will does not make him sole executor.

Emancipation (p. 81): "And upon the legacies to my four granddaughters being paid, and my estates that are required to pay the said legacies being clear of debt, then I give freedom to my slaves, the said slaves to be emancipated by my Executors in such manner as to my executors may seem most expedient and proper, the said emancipation to be accomplished in not exceeding five years from the time of my decease." (OCR "execiators", "paj-" corrected.)

Signed: "the twenty-sixth day of March, eighteen hundred and fifty-five." Witnesses: Martha Custis Williams, M. Eugene Webster (OCR "Webstee").

Estate: Arlington House (1,100 acres), the mill and adjacent lands to his daughter Mary Ann Randolph Lee for life, then to the eldest grandson George Washington Custis Lee "to him and his heirs forever, he ... taking my name and arms"; White House (New Kent) to W. H. F. Lee; Romancock (King William) to Robert Edward Lee (the grandson); $10,000 to each of four granddaughters; Smith's Island and lands in Stafford, Richmond and Westmoreland to be sold to pay the legacies; Washington relics to stay at Arlington. To Lt. Col. R. E. Lee personally: only "my lot in square No. 21, Washington city".

### 3c. United States v. Lee (STRONG on what it recites)
`unitedstatesrep02ottogoog` (U.S. Reports vol. 106, "Otto" 16), 106 U.S. 196, pp. 198-199: "The plaintiff offered evidence establishing title in himself by the will of his grandfather, George Washington Parke Custis, who devised the Arlington estate to his daughter, the wife of General Robert E. Lee, for life, and after her death to the plaintiff." (OCR "Cnstis" corrected.) The case does not print the will, the slaves or the executor clause. The index heads it "Kaufman v. Lee"; the running head reads "United States v. Lee".

### 3d. What the will shows: ownership versus control
- Ownership: the enslaved people are Custis's. The will calls them "my slaves". They are not bequeathed to Lee or to any heir; no clause gives Robert E. Lee (the son-in-law) any property in a person except the Washington lot. The will does not list them by number or name.
- Control: the emancipation power sits with the executors, jointly (four named), who choose the manner ("most expedient and proper"). Emancipation is conditional ("upon the legacies ... being paid, and my estates ... clear of debt") and has an outer limit: not exceeding five years from Custis's death (Custis died 10 Oct. 1857, so by Oct. 1862; the Decker and Munford texts give no death date, so that date is not from these sources). Lee therefore held the enslaved people as co-executor under a duty to free them, with discretion over timing and a mandate to work the White House and Romancock estates to raise the legacies ("to be worked to raise the aforesaid legacies").
- What the will does not show: that Lee personally owned them, or that he was sole executor, or that he alone controlled timing. Whether Lee qualified as executor, hired the people out, or what other executors did is not in the will; those facts need the probate record or contemporary letters (not read here).
- For the "slaveholder" trait: the will supports "controlled, as executor, enslaved people owned by Custis's estate, with a bounded duty to free them"; it does not support "owner" from this document alone.
