Lane: leads-L2-people. Read 2026-11-16. Every text below was downloaded as archive.org OCR and read in the OCR; page numbers are the printed running heads unless marked (inferred). Labels: STRONG (official or contemporary), SECONDARY-PD (period reference work), WEAK (memoir or retrospective). Anything seen only in a search snippet is not used as evidence.

## 1. Ward Hill Lamon (`lamon_ward_h`)

Name as printed: "Ward H. Lamon" (Senate Executive Journal, Official Register, Lincoln's letter heading); "Ward Hill Lamon" on the title page of LAMON-REC (full middle name from the memoir heading "WARD HILL LAMON").

Birth: 6 January 1828, in Frederick County, Virginia, "about two miles north of Winchester". LAMON-REC, Memoir, p. xxiii: "WARD H. LAMON was born in Frederick County, about two miles north of Winchester, in the state of Virginia, on the 6th day of January, 1828." WEAK, and it is the ONLY period source read for the date and place (family memoir). Appletons and the Directory do not cover him. Unverified beyond that.

Death: 7 May 1893. LAMON-REC, Memoir, p. xxxvi: "He died at eleven o'clock on the night of May 7th, 1893". WEAK, same single source. The memoir does not name the place. Martinsburg, W. Va. is given in modern and later works only (seen in search snippets, unread): treat the death place as unverified from a period source.

Offices and posts, with dates:
- District attorney for the Eighth (Illinois) district from 1856, per LAMON-REC p. xxiv: "Was elected District Attorney for the eighth District in 1856" (WEAK).
- Marshal of the District of Columbia. STRONG. SEJ vol. XI, p. 439 (page head "JULY 13, 1861.] EXECUTIVE JOURNAL. 439"): "I nominate Ward H. Lamon, of Illinois (heretofore appointed in recess of the Senate, April 6, 1861), to be marshal of the United States for the District of Columbia, in place of William Selden, removed. ABRAHAM LINCOLN. WASHINGTON, July 9, 1861." The message was read in the Senate on Saturday 13 July 1861 and the nomination referred to the Judiciary Committee (p. 443, same date: "Ordered, That the nominations of ... Ward H. Lamon ... be referred to the Committee on the Judiciary"). Confirmed Saturday 27 July 1861, SEJ p. 485: "Resolved, That the Senate advise and consent to the appointment of Ward H. Lamon, agreeably to the nomination." Vote 21 yeas, 14 nays (yeas include Anthony, Browning, Chandler, Cowan, Foot, Harlan, Harris, Johnson of Missouri, Kennedy, King, Lane of Indiana, Latham, Morrill, Pearce, Polk, Powell, Saulsbury, Sherman, Simmons, Trumbull, Willey; nays Clark, Collamer, Dixon, Fessenden, Foster, Grimes, Hale, Pomeroy, Sumner, Ten Eyck, Wade, Wilkinson, Wilmot, Wilson).
- So the facts for the cast row: recess appointment 6 April 1861 (a text search of vol. XI finds Lamon's name only in the July 1861 entries, none in the March 1861 special session, which agrees with a recess commission); nominated 9 July (dated), read 13 July; confirmed 27 July 1861. He was therefore NOT a confirmed marshal on 5 March - 5 April 1861; from 6 April he held a recess commission. WEAK pointer, not used as evidence: a 1968 secondary work says he was sworn in 12 April 1861 (snippet only; unread).
- Status on 30 Sept 1861: REG-1861, section "JUDICIARY - District Courts", "DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA": "Ward H. Lamon, Marshal" (STRONG for the office on that date). Page number not recovered (the OCR drops the running heads and scrambles the birth-state and appointed-from columns; the leaf is in the Judiciary part, after "Edward C. Carrington, Attorney").
- His resignation offered 31 Jan 1862 and refused by Lincoln is in LAMON-REC pp. xxvi-xxvii (WEAK); not checked against another source.

Military title "Colonel" in 1861: not a commission recorded in any STRONG source I read.
- LAMON-REC p. xxix: Lincoln's letter heading "Washington, D. C., June 25, 1861. Col. W. H. Lamon: My Dear Sir, -- I spoke to the Secretary of War yesterday, and he consents, and so do I, that as fast as you get Companies, you may procure a U. S. officer, and have them mustered in." Printed in the memoir (WEAK as printed; the letter itself is contemporary, so confirm against Nicolay and Hay or Basler before relying on the wording). Same memoir, p. xxix: "In May, 1861, Lamon was authorized to organize and command a regiment of volunteer Infantry, and subsequently his command was increased to a brigade"; p. xxx: "from May, 1861, to December of that year ... He held the commission of Colonel during the war." (WEAK, daughter's memoir).
- Reading: by 25 June 1861 Lincoln addresses him as "Col."; whether this is a commission or an authorisation to raise a Virginia (Unionist) regiment and brigade is not settled by a STRONG source here. No Official Records or Official Army Register line was found. Recommend: carry "Marshal of the District of Columbia (recess appointment 6 April 1861; confirmed 27 July 1861)" as the office and treat "Colonel" as a courtesy or raising-authority title from May-June 1861, not a commissioned rank, unless an OR or Register entry is found later.
- Not checked: that Lamon went to Charleston in the last days of March 1861 (LAMON-REC pp. xxx and the chapter; WEAK here, and another lane's L3-sumter covers it).

## 2. John Brown Baldwin (`baldwin_john_b`)

Name as printed: "John B. Baldwin" (VAJ roll, p. 4); "John Brown Baldwin" (WADD p. 360).

Birth: 11 January 1820, "near Staunton" (Augusta County), Virginia. WADD p. 360 (SECONDARY-PD): "John Brown Baldwin was the oldest son of Judge Briscoe G. Baldwin, and was born near Staunton, January 11, 1820." (OCR reads "ji"; the digits are consistent with 11 only by inference: read it as 11 with that OCR doubt flagged.) Single reference work read. Modern references (snippets, unread) give the same date and Staunton.

Death: 30 September 1873. WADD p. 360: "his death, which occurred September 30, 1873, caused universal lamentation in the county." Place not stated by Waddell (county only); Staunton is in modern references only (unverified from a period source).

Offices and posts:
- Member, Virginia House of Delegates for Augusta, one term, early (before 1859; year not given). WADD p. 360. Colonel of the 160th militia regiment (p. 360). Candidate for Court of Appeals judge 1859, defeated by William J. Robertson (p. 360).
- Delegate to the Virginia Convention of 1861 from Augusta County, a Union man. WADD p. 282: "The election was held on the 4th of February, and Alexander H. H. Stuart, John B. Baldwin and George Baylor were chosen, as Union men, to represent Augusta county." VAJ roll of 13 Feb 1861, p. 4, under "Augusta": A. H. H. Stuart, John B. Baldwin, George Baylor (STRONG). Augusta's Union resolutions of 26 Nov 1860 were drafted by a committee of thirteen that included him (WADD p. 281).
- Member of the Convention's Committee on Federal Relations (21 members, named by the President on 16 Feb 1861). VAJ p. 43: "Messrs. Conrad of Frederick, Wise, Scott of Fauquier, Preston, Harvie, Macfarland, McComas, Montague, Price, Southall, Willey, Bruce, Boyd, Barbour of Culpeper, Williams, Rives, Moore, Blow, Johnston of Lee and Scott, Baldwin, Jackson." On 11 March 1861 he presented a minority "substitute for the report of the Committee on Federal Relations" (VAJ p. 99, "Mr. Baldwin presented a substitute..."; printed as the "SUBSTITUTE For the Report of the Committee on Federal Relations, presented by Mr. Baldwin, March 11, 1861" in the Journal's appendix).
- 15 April 1861: voted with the negative (43 votes) on Holcombe's motion to suspend the standing order and go at once into secret session on the report of the committee that had seen Lincoln (VAJ pp. 319-320, vote lists; page number partly OCR-garbled). The public Journal does not print the 17 April ordinance vote (secret session), so his vote on the ordinance is not established here.
- Inspector General of Virginia State troops (spring 1861; no date given) and colonel of the 52nd Virginia Infantry. WADD p. 330 (list of officers): "Baldwin, John B. -- Inspector-General of State troops; colonel of Fifty-second infantry. Disabled by sickness." WADD p. 287 (diary, 7 Aug 1861): "The Fifty-second Virginia regiment was organized at that time. The field and staff officers were, John B. Baldwin, Colonel; M. G. Harman, Lieutenant-Colonel ..." So from about 7 Aug 1861 he was a colonel; in March-April 1861 he held no military office that is dated by a strong source.
- Elected to the (first) Confederate Congress, Nov 1861: WADD p. 290 (diary, 11 Nov 1861): "Baldwin was elected to Congress in this district. ... John N. Hendren was elected a member of the State Convention in Baldwin's place." VAJ p. 322 (14 Nov 1861): "John N. Hendren, of Augusta, who was elected to fill the vacancy occasioned by the resignation of Mr. Baldwin ... appeared and took their seats." STRONG for the seat change.
- Speaker of the Virginia House of Delegates from 1865. WADD p. 344: "John B. Baldwin, of Augusta, was elected speaker of the House of Delegates"; WADD p. 357: "John B. Baldwin in 1865, to 1867". (A modern speakers' list in a search snippet gives 1865-1869; unread, so the end year is unverified.)

## 3. John Minor Botts (`botts_john_m`)

Name as printed: "BOTTS, John Minor" (APPL I, p. 325; BDAC-1950 p. 869).

Birth: 16 September 1802, Dumfries, Prince William County, Va. APPL I, p. 325: "BOTTS, John Minor, statesman, b. in Dumfries, Prince William co., Va., 16 Sept., 1802". BDAC-1950 p. 869: "born in Dumfries, Va., September 16, 1802". BDAC-1928 p. 718: "Septemi)er 19, 1^2" (OCR garbled, reads like 16 with damage). Two independent printed works agree on 16 Sept 1802 (the directory may have drawn on the same earlier compilations). Note: the Congress's current online biography is blocked and was not read; I make no claim about it.

Death: Appletons and the Directory DISAGREE.
- APPL I, p. 325: "d. in Culpepper, Va., 7 Jan., 1869".
- BDAC-1950 p. 869: "died in Richmond, Va., January 8, 1869; interment in Shockoe Hill Cemetery." BDAC-1928 p. 718 (OCR garbled): "died in Richmond, Ya., January 8, 1809 [1869]; interment in Shockoe Hill Cemetery." The two Directory editions agree with each other (8 Jan, Richmond). Appletons (7 Jan, Culpeper) is the outlier. Unresolved: no newspaper obituary was read. Use the Directory (official) as primary and note the discrepancy.

Offices (all per BDAC-1950 p. 869 unless noted): Virginia House of Delegates 1833-1839; U.S. Representative (Whig) from Virginia, 26th and 27th Congresses (4 March 1839 - 3 March 1843); defeated for re-election 1842; elected to the 30th Congress (4 March 1847 - 3 March 1849); defeated 1848 and 1850; resumed practice in Richmond 1852; delegate to the Southern Loyalists' Convention 1866. APPL gives the same terms ("from 2 Dec., 1839, till 3 March, 1843" and "from 6 Dec., 1847, till 3 March, 1849"; he was "defeated by Mr. Seddon" in 1843).

1861 status: a private citizen holding no office. Not a delegate to the Virginia Convention (his name is not in the VAJ roll or elsewhere in the Journal; searched). APPL I, pp. 325-326: "He continued his practice, and remained in Richmond till the beginning of the civil war; but, being devoted to the union, and having used all his efforts, without avail, to prevent Virginia from seceding, he retired to his farm near Culpepper Court-House, where he remained most of the time during the war". Arrested "in March, 1862" by order of Gen. Winder and held eight weeks. SECONDARY-PD. No 1861 Botts letter or speech from a strong source was read; his own 1866 book is WEAK and was not opened.

## 4. George William Summers (`summers_george_w`)

Name as printed: "SUMMERS, George William" (BDAC-1950 p. 1883, inferred); "George W. Summers" (VAJ roll p. 5); "G. W. SUMMERS" (signature, VAJ Doc. No. 11, p. 6).

Birth: 4 March 1804, near Alexandria, Va. BDAC-1950: "born near Alexandria, Va., March 4, 1804". BDAC-1928 p. 1586: "born near Alexandria, Fairfax County, Va., March 4, 1804" (the 1928 text adds the county). Two Directory editions, same compilers; Appletons has no entry. Single source family; no independent check.

Death: 19 September 1868, Charleston, (West) Va. BDAC-1950: "died in Charleston, W. Va., September 19, 1868; interment in Walnut Grove Cemetery, Putnam County, W. Va." BDAC-1928 reads "September 10, 1868" in the OCR, which I take as an OCR misreading of 19 (the 1950 OCR is clean, but the two differ in the OCR of this digit; unverified which is right). Treat 19 Sept as probable, 10 Sept as a flag.

Page cites: BDAC-1950 p. 1883 is inferred (the running head for that page is not in the OCR; the entry sits between the heads for pp. 1882 and 1884). BDAC-1928 p. 1586 (head present).

Offices: Virginia House of Delegates 1830-1832 and 1834-1836; U.S. Representative (Whig) from Virginia, 27th and 28th Congresses (4 March 1841 - 3 March 1845); defeated 1844 for the 29th; delegate to the Virginia constitutional convention of 1850; unsuccessful Whig candidate for governor 1851; judge of the eighteenth judicial circuit 1852-1858 (BDAC-1950; the 1928 OCR reads "1802-1868", garbled); "member of the peace conference held at Washington, D. C., in 1861"; "delegate to the secession convention in 1861 at Richmond".

Peace Conference (STRONG, VAJ Doc. No. 11, "Commissioners' Report", p. 6, transmitted to the Convention by Gov. Letcher): the report to the Governor on "the Conference which convened in Washington City on the 4th day of February last" is signed "JOHN TYLER, G. W. SUMMERS, W. C. RIVES, JAMES A. SEDDON". Doc. No. 12 is a letter of Judge John W. Brockenbrough (the fifth commissioner) of 2 March 1861 addressed "to the Hon. John Tyler ... and Hon. George W. Summers ... as two of the Commissioners representing the State of Virginia in the late Peace Conference at Washington, and also as members of the State Convention". The report records that the plan was adopted by the Conference and that Virginia's majority voted no on section 1 and on sections 5 and 7.

Convention delegate for Kanawha: VAJ roll, 13 Feb 1861, p. 5: "Kanawha: George W. Summers, Spicer Patrick." (STRONG; the other member is Patrick.) He answered the roll on the first day.

Convention roles in the window (all VAJ, STRONG):
- 13 Feb 1861 (p. 7): nominated John Janney of Loudoun for permanent President ("Mr. Summers nominated Mr. John Janney"); Janney won 70 to 54 (names counted in the OCR list; the printed "70" is OCR "TO"), pp. 7-8; Summers was named with Flournoy to escort him to the chair (p. 8). Summers also helped escort the temporary President, Cox, to the chair (p. 3).
- He is NOT on the Committee on Federal Relations of 16 Feb (names listed above at VAJ p. 43); this is consistent with his being a Peace Conference commissioner. (Inference; the Journal gives no reason.)
- 5 March 1861 (p. 87, session of Tuesday 5 March): the Governor's communication transmitting the Commissioners' report was, "on motion of Mr. Summers, ... laid upon the table, and ordered to be printed [Doc. No. XI]"; he also presented Brockenbrough's letter and moved it laid on the table and printed [Doc. No. XII] (same page).
- 11 March (p. 99): "On motion of Mr. Summers, the report from the Commissioners ... in the Conference, which convened in the City of Washington, on the 4th day of February, 1861, was taken up".
- 8 April 1861 (p. 145): in the election of the three-man committee to wait on the President of the United States, "Mr. Scott, of Fauquier, nominated Mr. William Ballard Preston ... Mr. Summers nominated Mr. Alex. H. H. Stuart, of the county of Augusta. Mr. Holcombe nominated Mr. George W. Randolph ... All of whom were unanimously elected." (Preston presented the committee's report on 15 April, Doc. XVII; the report itself was not read.)
- 15 April 1861: voted with the 43 in the negative on Holcombe's motion to suspend the standing order and go into secret session at once (VAJ, negative list ending "Alex. H. H. Stuart, Chapman J. Stuart, George W. Summers, Campbell Tarr, Waitman T. Willey, Benjamin Wilson - 43"). The 17 April ordinance vote is in the secret journal and is not printed in this volume; his vote on it is not established here.
- He cast votes on the many roll calls through March-April; no committee chairmanship was found.

## Findings outside my scope

- The Virginia Convention Journal (`journalofactspro00virg`) holds usable roll-call lists (the 15 April negatives, the 13 Feb Janney ballot, the Committee on Federal Relations of 16 Feb), the Commissioners' report (Doc. 11) and the Brockenbrough letter (Doc. 12), and Doc. XVII (Preston's committee report of 15 April). Useful for the Virginia cast and the Richmond situation.
- The Senate Executive Journal vol. XI (a text that is on archive.org even though congress.gov is blocked) lists the whole set of Lincoln's July 1861 marshal and other nominations with recess-appointment dates (e.g., Thomas H. Fisher, E. Va. marshal, appointed 4 April; David A. Rawlings, E. Mo., 1 April; David L. Phillips, S. Ill., 30 March), a ready source for other cast rows' offices.
- Appletons has no entries for Lamon, J. B. Baldwin or G. W. Summers; the 1950 Directory has no entry for Lamon or Baldwin. Do not cite Appletons for those three.
- Two items worth a separate check: (a) whether Lincoln's 25 June 1861 letter to "Col. W. H. Lamon" appears in Nicolay and Hay or in the Collected Works with the same text; (b) the roll of the Virginia secession-ordinance vote of 17 April 1861 (not in the public Journal; I make no claim about how Summers or Baldwin voted on it).
