## Method and coverage limits

Read the main CODEX standing, command-line, writing and R44-R49 rules, R59, the lane hand-over, both requested briefs and the original resource brief, and decisions 0087, 0096 and 0097. The later two decisions were read from the main checkout because this research base predates them. The two R51 held lists were obtained with `git show cbc6a10:<path>` and retained unchanged in `evidence-r59/input-held-*.csv`. No other run's worktree was written.

The first manual searches concerned Lincoln and the cabinet, Scott and commanders, and governors. `evidence-r59/counts.json` records the search cohorts and their held-cell counts. Several requested names, including Davis and most of his cabinet, have **no judgment row in this particular held list**. This does not certify all of their existing judgments or expand the assignment to cells R51 did not hold. Hunter is included as a cabinet search name and has a held reputation row.

The primary discovery texts were Chase's correspondence, contemporary documents printed by Warden and Morgan Dix, the Sherman brothers' letters, the New-York Times of 14 January 1861, the Congressional Globe issue dated 16 January 1861, OR I-1, and McClellan's European report transmitted in February 1857. Selected passages were read, not entire books. The Globe issue contains proceedings of earlier sitting days, so its cover date was not automatically assigned to a speech. None of its passages is used for a positive CSV decision.

No retained evidence on this base establishes a frequency ranking of **individual judgment-cell reads** in the reference run. No new campaign was started to obtain one. After the priority searches, the remaining rows received an **alphabetical surname screen**, recorded in `evidence-r59/surname-screen.json`. A hit count is only discovery: it includes indexes, later documents, other people with the same name, and retrospective narrative. Many residual HOLDs have no additional cell-specific passage beyond this screen. They are explicitly labelled that way in the CSV. This is a bounded third pass, not a claim to have read every hit or exhausted the newspapers and archives for 189 cells.

Every positive decision rests on a manually read document and a visually checked printed page. Downloads and scans are outside the worktree, in `C:/Pyrrhic/mandate-research-sources/r59-sources/`. `evidence-r59/sources.json` supplies URLs, hashes, page images and reading limits. Unsuccessful downloads, unread finding aids and the blocked newspaper endpoint are separated from evidence. The Bates diary was not fetched or used.

**R59's cutoff is applied literally:** a document must precede 5 March 1861 in this pass, including innate fields which the older brief would otherwise allow through July. Its own-words exception says **a value**. Accordingly Chase's 1847 argument and Julian's 14 May 1850 speech are retained as primary leads but do not alone clear their held `trait:antislavery` cells here. The new Sumner and Wilson trait legs are other people's judgments. [I] This conservative reading avoids silently widening R59. The lead can decide whether a further pass should use the older conduct exception for traits.

## Important HOLDs

- **Lincoln's reputation remains HOLD**, with the average runtime default under 0097. The new Chase letter assesses judgment. It is not a resubmission of Andrew's rally speech or a request to reopen the rejected reputation CHANGE. Lincoln's regard toward Welles and Blair also remains held.
- **Bates, Blair and Welles:** CHASE-D pp489-490 expresses Stanton's preference for cabinet colleagues. That is not Bates's competence, Blair's own regard for other men, Welles's own order value, or Welles's regard toward Seward. Those cells remain held.
- **Cameron's other fields:** opposition to his Treasury appointment does not establish his own party or career values, nerve, regard, or a pre-start tendency to make policy.
- **McClellan:** Davis's 25 February 1857 transmission, printed p3 of the European report, states completion of the report, without appraising the man. The praise in Sherman Letters p115 belongs to May 1861. Neither clears start-day reputation. **Sherman:** John Sherman's 6 January 1861 letter, pp92-93, reports Scott's support for a return to army employment. Patronage alone does not establish the coded low reputation.
- **Banks:** Briggs's 17 March 1860 letter, CHASE-D pp481-483, attacks him in the nomination contest. [I] This is adverse evidence against an uncomplicated high rating, but one factional letter does not establish a replacement general reputation band. No CHANGE to low or below is proposed.
- **Governors:** no new qualifying pre-start leg was established for their held cells. Existing after-start relationship leads were not backdated. Neither an inaugural nor an office title is treated as proof of organising ability. The alphabetic screen is the full extent of the new search for some governor rows, as the CSV records.
- **Dana:** the R51 rights hold stands. An unattributed editorial, a mention in a meeting, or a later memoir is not an independently attributed pre-start appraisal of his gifts or his antislavery trait.
