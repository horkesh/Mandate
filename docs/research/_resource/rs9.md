## The division that does the work

The wings are told apart in the record by two divisions, both in the Congressional Globe, 36th Congress, 2nd session:

1. House, 28 Feb 1861 (Globe p.1285): passage of the joint resolution forbidding federal interference with slavery in the States, 133-65 (and the reconsideration vote, 128-65). Yeas include C. F. Adams, Pendleton, Vallandigham, Sickles, McClernand. Nays include Gooch, Stevens, Wade (Ohio), the Washburns and Blair.
2. Senate, 2 Mar 1861: passage of the same amendment, 24-12 (Globe p.1403): nays Bingham, Chandler, Clark, Doolittle, Durkee, Foot, King, Sumner, Trumbull, Wade, Wilkinson, Wilson. And the adjournment vote 17-31 (Globe p.1318) that Crittenden resisted: the 17 yeas are the same men plus Collamer, Grimes, Hale, Harlan, Morrill. Seward, Crittenden, Douglas, Johnson of Tennessee, Lane are in the 31 nays.

A third test, the House vote of 9 Jan on Adrain's resolution approving Anderson and pledging support "in all constitutional measures to enforce the laws" (Globe p.280, 124-53), separates the Union Democrats (Sickles, McClernand yea) from the peace Democrats (Pendleton, Vallandigham nay).

Most Republican senators voted alike on Crittenden's resolutions (15 and 19 Jan), so those votes do not place a man in a wing. Cameron and Fessenden stay HOLD for that reason.

## Notes for the lead

- Moderates is a weak label on the winter record: Lincoln, Seward and Adams hold it by conciliatory words and votes on the amendment, but Lincoln's own December letters hold firm on territorial extension.
- Holt, Dix, Johnson, McClernand, Sickles and Tod support "war_democrats" only as the Union side of the Democracy in the winter: none of them had yet been asked to support a war.
- Butler's pre-start Democratic record is the seceding wing at Baltimore, which does not support war_democrats: HOLD.
