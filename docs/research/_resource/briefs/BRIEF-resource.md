## The rule for a contemporary leg
- A **contemporary leg** is a citation of a PRIMARY document of the time: a letter, telegram, speech, order, official report, journal, newspaper of the day, or a diary written at the time; OR a contemporary document that a later work prints verbatim (dated by the document, cited to the printing: only the document's own words count, never the later author's comment around it).
- **Date boundary.** Class `start` (values, reputation, boldness, candour, regard, regard toward another person, traits such as candid or political_patron): the document must be dated BEFORE 5 March 1861. Class `innate` (competence, nerve, and the dispositions slow, rank_proud, joint_minded, organiser, micromanages, intrigues, quarrelsome, ambitious, insubordinate, disdains_superiors, shifts_blame, self_doubting, holds_grudges): the document may be dated up to 31 July 1861.
- **Whose words.** A man's own words are a leg when they ARE the conduct that shows the trait (a speech showing his value; a letter protesting his rank for rank_proud). They are NOT a leg when he assesses his own quality (his candour, competence, organising). A **reputation** is how OTHERS regarded him: it needs someone else's contemporary judgment of him, not his own words. An order assigning a man a task shows the assigner's expectation, not the man's gift: not a leg for the trait the task needs.
- NOT legs: memoirs and retrospective diaries (Welles's Diary, Grant, Sherman, McClellan's Own Story, Chesnut, Greeley's Recollections, Weed, Dana, Jones's Rebel War Clerk's Diary written later, Battles and Leaders), reference works (Appletons, the Biographical Directory), modern pages, and anything dated after the boundary.
- A cell may keep a direction only if the contemporary leg supports that direction. If the leg points elsewhere, propose the supported direction and say so. If you find no contemporary leg after a reasonable search, the decision is HOLD (the cell will be held back), and you say what you searched.
