# Charles P. Stone: January 1861 authority

R52 research, 19 November 2026. **The required contemporary commission, order or muster roll has not been found in the sources read.** A stronger official retrospective record was found, but it does not identify the original appointing instrument or settle its legal authority. Decision 0097's starting bloc of none remains in force.

## Official record found

The Senate pension report for Jeannie Stone adopts a House report and prints an Adjutant-General's Office statement dated **15 February 1888**, signed R. C. Drum. Its stated basis is the office's records. This is **Senate Report 1054, 50th Congress, 1st session, ordered printed 23 April 1888**, accompanying H.R. 401; it is not an 1861 commission and not House Report 1054. [Senate Report 1054, pp 1-3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-02523_00_00-395-1054-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-02523_00_00-395-1054-0000.pdf).

The statement separates these records:

| Record as printed | Date stated | Printed page |
|---|---|---|
| Regular Army resignation | 17 November 1856 | 2 |
| Colonel, 14th Infantry | 14 May 1861 | 2 |
| Colonel and inspector-general, District of Columbia militia | 1 January 1861 | 2 |
| Muster into service, on Scott's special application | 2 January 1861 | 2 |
| Organizing the District volunteers under Scott's orders | 2 January-16 April 1861 | 2 |
| Brigadier-general, United States volunteers | 17 May 1861 | 2 |

These are dates **reported by the 1888 office statement**, not new transcriptions of the original instruments. The last signature is on p 3. The widow's appeal, dated 26 December 1887 on p 2, is a separate source from the Adjutant-General's statement. Her account must not be attributed to his office.

[I] This is material evidence against treating Stone as simply an inactive civilian throughout January-March. It supports preserving his distinct District militia service in the dossier. It still does not establish which legal appointment he held on 5 March or whether that appointment meets the project's federal-commission definition.

## What remains unproved

The report does not reproduce the January commission, an 1861 appointment order, the original muster roll, an order number, or the statute invoked. Scott's request and operational supervision do not, by themselves, identify the appointing power. The report's volunteer-record heading cannot substitute for an original instrument showing whether this was federal service, a District militia office, or a distinct combination. Nor does the later Regular Army appointment resolve the January question. [Senate Report 1054, pp 2-3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-02523_00_00-395-1054-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-02523_00_00-395-1054-0000.pdf).

Searches/readings included the retained OR I-2 Stone correspondence and commands, the previously identified register/Cullum trail, the Rebellion Record's January diary-entry lead, contemporary-newspaper searches and the congressional pension file. No original January appointment/muster instrument was read. An OCR page numeral in the Rebellion Record lead was not certified; it is not used here as a new dated authority. This is a bounded negative search result, not a claim about all surviving archives.

[I] Proposed ruling: **retain none** under 0097 and keep the January District appointment/muster claim explicitly qualified by its 1888 source. A positive federal starting bloc requires the contemporary instrument requested in R52. The smallest further search is the Adjutant-General's underlying service/muster file or District militia appointment record, using the 1888 statement's 1/2 January dates; an archive identifier has not been established here.
