# James D. Bulloch on 5 March 1861

R52 research, 19 November 2026. **Contemporary shipping reports place a master named Bulloch with Bienville immediately before and after 5 March. They do not prove his exact location, legal employment contract or private Confederate commitment on that day.** Decision 0097's starting bloc of none remains in force.

## Contemporary employment evidence

| Read source | What it says | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| New-York Times, 2 March 1861, p 8, Marine Intelligence, New-York Friday 1 March, Cleared; Sailed | Bienville is listed under Bulloch for Havana and New-Orleans, with Livingston, Crocheron & Co. named in the clearance entry; Bienville also appears among the vessels sailed. | It establishes the reported master and commercial voyage on 1 March, not a 5 March coordinate or a payroll contract. |
| New-York Times, 25 March 1861, p 8, Marine Intelligence, New-York Saturday 23 March, Arrived | Bienville, Bulloch, from New-Orleans 15 March and Havana 18 March, with merchandise and passengers to Livingston, Crocheron & Co. | It brackets the continuing commercial association after the requested date; it is not a Confederate appointment. |

Sources: [2 March 1861, p 8 scan](https://archive.org/download/sim_new-york-times_the-new-york-times_1861-03-02_10_2947/page/n7.jpg), [25 March 1861, p 8 scan](https://archive.org/download/sim_new-york-times_the-new-york-times_1861-03-25_10_2966/page/n7.jpg). Both complete page headings and the relevant shipping entries were visually checked against their retained OCR. The notices give the surname; identification with James D. Bulloch and continuity through 5 March are inferences, not additional words in the shipping entries.

[I] The best supported start-day description is **probably continuing as Bienville's commercial master on the Havana/New-Orleans service associated with Livingston, Crocheron & Co.** The two named notices support continuity across the interval. They do not prove that he was at a particular port on 5 March. Naming the firm as clearing/receiving agent does not independently establish which legal entity employed or paid him. An employment contract, ship's articles or original muster/crew record for this voyage was not read.

## Commitment on the requested day

No contemporary letter, oath, acceptance, government order or service offer read in this pass establishes a Confederate commitment by 5 March 1861. The shipping notices establish commercial activity; they cannot establish political neutrality, Union loyalty or Confederate allegiance. [New-York Times, 2 March 1861, p 8; 25 March 1861, p 8, scans above].

Bulloch's later account describes his Bienville employment and March New-York difficulties at pp 27-28, his offer through Benjamin after the Sumter news at pp 32-35, and an unofficial Louisiana discussion placed only in March at p 43. These passages were read in the **1884 New-York edition** of [The Secret Service of the Confederate States in Europe, vol 1](https://archive.org/details/secretserviceofc01bull), not assumed from the provenance's generic 1883 title. They are a later participant's account, already marked weak by the project's policy. A reference to an unspecified point in March cannot establish commitment by 5 March. The account is not substituted for a contemporary letter.

The pack's `BULLOCH SS-p62-63` citation was also read: those pages discuss the Alabama contract and the Lairds retrospectively. They are not a March 1861 appointment or an offer of service. The 1884 imprint does not supply an exact composition day for that narrative. [Bulloch, vol 1, pp 62-63](https://archive.org/details/secretserviceofc01bull).

[I] Proposed ruling: **retain none** under 0097. Record the commercial-master inference with its two contemporary page citations and leave exact start-day location, legal employer and Confederate commitment unestablished. None is an evidence hold, not a positive claim of neutrality. No later commission or memoir offer is backdated into the starting bloc.
