# S94: Johnston's Harpers Ferry withdrawal and the papers authorizing it

23 November 2026. Research only, `sprint/research-military`, base `c94a05b0`. No pack, code, accepted decision, trait, map, date trigger or coverage verdict changes. Proposed representations below are **[I]**, for the lead's review.

The government preferred to retain Harpers Ferry but repeatedly allowed Johnston to leave if the military situation required it. The strongest read operational authorization is **Cooper to Johnston, June 13**, not Davis's June 13 letter to Beauregard. Lee had already given conditional instructions on June 1 and 7. This is a sequence of mission clarification, conditional discretion and the commander's own decision, not a dated relocation instruction (OR I-II pp.897-898,910,922-925).

There is a material chronology conflict. Johnston's **June 15** letter acknowledges Cooper's June 13 reply and says he reported the removal to Davis the previous day. His **October 14** report places the army's departure on June 15 and a courier's authorization on June 16. Those statements cannot establish a single clean receipt-before-departure timeline. Keep them separate; do not invent a June 14 receipt, replace the contemporary letter with the later report, or make permission effective on its issue date (OR I-II pp.470,472,929-930).

## 2. The move, property and headquarters

The dated sequence below separates instructions, contemporary location evidence and Johnston's later account.

- **June 13-14 preparations, in the October report.** Johnston says Hill's troops went by railway to Winchester and towards Romney on June 13; heavy baggage and remaining public property followed by railway during June 13 and 14, and the Potomac bridges were destroyed. These are reported acts; Cooper's June 13 instruction to take rolling stock and destroy the road, bridge, platforms and trestle work is a separate statement of authorized scope (OR I-II pp.470-472,924).
- **Main-column route, in the October report.** It gives departure on the morning of June 15 towards Winchester and a bivouac four miles beyond Charlestown. On June 16, intelligence about a crossing at Williamsport and withdrawal from Romney led to a flank movement to the Martinsburg turnpike at Bunker Hill. Johnston says he then resumed his original Winchester plan. The paper does not supply an exact day of arrival at Winchester. Do not turn this into an uninterrupted railway journey for the whole army, or substitute July's Ashby's Gap/Piedmont junction route for June's withdrawal (OR I-II pp.472-473).
- **Contemporary qualification.** The June 15 headquarters heading is Camp Long Meadow. Its report of a removal already communicated to Davis the previous day conflicts with a simple June 15-departure/June 16-permission account. The read pages do not locate Long Meadow precisely or prove it is the four-mile bivouac. Preparatory removals, troop-column departure and a completed evacuation need not share a clock; their exact reconciliation remains held (OR I-II pp.472,929-930).
- **Bunker Hill.** Johnston writes from Headquarters Camp Bunker Hill on June 17, describing his redirection after June 16 intelligence and giving the position as twelve miles ahead of Winchester. Cooper addresses him there on June 18 and acknowledges delivery of the June 17 letter. These prove dated headquarters/dispatch addresses, not permanent stationing or exact message arrival times for the reply (OR I-II p.934).
- **Winchester.** The October report places the army there after Bunker Hill. Cooper's June 19 address is *near* Winchester. Johnston's own June 24 and June 26 headquarters headings are Winchester. Those observations establish the location without manufacturing a June 17,18 or19 arrival date (OR I-II pp.472,940,948,956).
- **Property left and later recovery.** Johnston reports sending Steuart's Maryland battalion back for public property said to remain. He separately reports instructions to Jackson to destroy B&O rolling stock that could not be brought away and bring usable stock to Winchester. Cooper's June 18 letter seeks gun-stocks, gun-barrels and tilt-hammers still unremoved, if they can be recovered. These pages do not supply a complete inventory, locomotive count, proof every instruction was executed or a basis to claim that all armory machinery was destroyed in June (OR I-II pp.472,934-935).
- **Winchester was not a motionless month-long station.** Johnston's July 8 letter, headed Winchester, says he returned from Darkesville the previous night after waiting there for an opportunity to fight. July 7 is the inferred return date **[I]**. His later report describes the forward movement after Patterson's July 2 crossing. The July 17 junction telegram is addressed Winchester; Johnston reports receipt about 1 a.m. July 18. Issue date, receipt and his decision to march remain distinct (OR I-II pp.472-473,478,967).

These dates are research observations and consistency checks. None is proposed as a simulation trigger. No ammunition amount, enemy estimate, nominal regiment count or population expectation is converted into a known strength or a model threshold.

## 3. Carrying the exchange through the grammar [I]

This section is a proposal, not an implementation or a new accepted decision. [Grammar receipts](S94/grammar-inputs.json) pin twelve read-only inputs from `C:/Pyrrhic/sprint-july` at `0e7078cb`. No July pack/research/run input or S92 work was imported. Existing types and function names below refer to those pinned files, not this research branch's older runtime.

**Johnston's answer to a hold goal.** Use an identified, received order to retain Harpers Ferry as the model antecedent; do not pretend a new original historical hold paper was recovered here. His answer should name that order in `ReportBody.reportAnswers`, retain author/post/objective and stated reasons, and reach the government through ordinary message delivery. `advice` / `memorandum`, order identity and typed claims already exist in `knowledge/report.ts`; the plan cycle's `toIssuer` in `tick/plans.ts` already correlates plan papers with their goal order. The historical content is the choice of mission and the mobile alternative expressed in E01-E03/E05/E09, especially June 6, not a disagreement inferred from his eventual position.

**Missing at this first seam:** a typed commander proposal to replace a hold objective or seek a bounded mobile mission. Existing `AdviceBody.adviceRecommends` and weighted reason strings do not carry executable `PlanGoal` terms. `choose` selects only kernel-generated candidates; `planner/methods.ts` routes moving candidates to the current goal objective. A holding alternative does not generate a voluntary withdrawal to another town. `PlanCounter` is the record of the **principal's** edit answered by the commander; its name is not evidence of an existing commander-to-principal goal counterproposal. Do not smuggle Winchester into a free-text token or invent a candidate in the seat.

**The government's received answer.** A concrete replacement goal can already travel as `OrderCommand` and replace `planGoal` through `arriveOrder`/`setGoal` when it reaches Johnston. Alternatively a principal can approve/edit a submitted, valid plan via the existing `review` path; that path requires the relevant submitted plan and cannot conjure one from an unrelated memorandum. Government selection of a concrete Winchester objective would be representable, but it is a narrower act than the conditional discretion in E10/E13/E17. It must be labelled as that representation choice, not described as the literal June 7 letter.

**Missing at this second seam:** a received, order-correlated permission to abandon a fixed point under a stated military condition while retaining the broader valley mission, with its scope, supersession and acknowledgment visible. `PlanGoal` has one objective, posture, risk, deadline, movement permissions and review flags; no conditional freedom to select a fallback objective. `goalApproveAsDrafted` permits execution of a plan inside the current goal; it does not release the commander to invent another goal. `delegate` hands over an open situation decision, not general operational discretion (`log/commands.ts`). A type name or an advice string cannot supply that authority.

**Johnston's own withdrawal plan.** Once an appropriately authorized concrete destination exists, retain the normal kernel-drafted route, commander choice, review/approval and operation march. For a concrete move to friendly ground and its defense, consider the existing `relieve` semantics only if their garrison requirement fits the chosen model situation; do not label every retirement an attack or assume a `hold` automatically marches the army. `planner/axes.ts` also provides a no-route holding candidate; `planner/edits.ts` rejects an objective edit of such a holding axis and directs the caller to a new goal. A voluntary relocation needs a moving candidate, not that stationary alternative. No posture/value, deadline or travel duration is fitted in S94.

The subsequent diversion to Bunker Hill should be Johnston reacting to his received threat information within his permission, rather than Davis selecting every leg. After a valid move, existing `seats/posts.ts::headquartersOf` derives a field headquarters from its main directly commanded force. Let physical movement and normal message routing determine the address and latency; do not overwrite the headquarters on a date or move a seat without its force. This does **not** predict an earlier July delivery or certify a July junction: those would require implementation and a fresh bounded check in a later request.

**Additional limitations:** ordinary march/review infrastructure does not itself prove a representation of baggage evacuation, rolling-stock recovery, demolition, delaying action or threat-driven replanning under a continuing discretionary mission. Name those gaps where implementation needs them; do not claim `withdraw` in the combat operation stages is a ready voluntary strategic evacuation command. No new scuttling/demolition mechanic is proposed as a player lever here. Preservation and sensitive-history rules still govern any later implementation.

## Proposed rulings for the lead [I]

1. **Retain the conditional paper chain.** Use June 6 as the clearest counterproposal and Lee June 7 / Cooper June 13 as distinct government answers, with received-paper prerequisites. Keep prior June 1 authority and the June 18 reaffirmation. Reject a date-only move, a blanket hold-at-any-cost reading, a direct Davis-to-Johnston paper synthesized from Davis-to-Beauregard, and retroactive consent made effective on issue date.
2. **Preserve the chronology dispute and bounded route evidence.** Keep the contemporary June 15 acknowledgment beside the October report's June 15 departure/June 16 courier. Retain Harpers Ferry–Charlestown–Bunker Hill–Winchester as described, with separate rail removals and later location headings. Reject a fabricated exact Winchester arrival, a complete property-destruction inventory, or uninterrupted Winchester residence through July.
3. **Scope the later build around the two missing authority seams.** A typed commander counterproposal and receipt-scoped discretionary mission need an explicit design choice. Existing order/report/plan/march infrastructure can support the surrounding sequence, but a concrete replacement goal is only a disclosed approximation of conditional discretion. Reject trait fitting, arbitrary risk/deadline tuning, free-text authority, direct force relocation and manufactured early July receipt. Implement nothing in this research lane.
