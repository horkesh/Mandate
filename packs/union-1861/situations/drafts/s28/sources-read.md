# S28 readings and limits

Facts in these drafts come from the selected pages below. The page citations in each definition and its index entry narrow the claim to the dated document. Downloaded copies, manuscript images, selected extracts, search results and logs are retained at `C:/Pyrrhic/mandate-research-sources/S28/`. Existing caches in S4, S6, S7 and S13 were read only. The manifest records hashes and page mappings; a whole-file hash does not mean the whole volume was read.

| Source | Pages used or searched | What the reading establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Nicolay and Hay, *Complete Works of Abraham Lincoln*, II (1894) | 78–79, 129–130, 209–211, 213, 227–228, 239 | Dated replies, compensated-emancipation recommendation, confiscation objections and explanation, July draft, Greeley reply and September suspension. Page 213’s July date is editorial, not a proved cabinet timestamp. The confiscation bill and separate Militia Act are not conflated. |
| *Official Records*, I-3 | 466–467, 469–470, 485–486 | Frémont’s proclamation and Lincoln’s two stages of response. Only the emancipation clause is authored; the shooting and reprisal proposals are excluded. These pages do not supply a request to Lincoln that he leave the clause unchanged. |
| Frémont to Lincoln, 8 September 1861, LoC mal.1156100 | pp. 1–4, four-image item; complete LoC transcription read | Frémont explicitly maintains that the emancipation clause was right and necessary and explains why he will not retract it voluntarily. He also requests an open correction if Lincoln still disagrees. This supplies advocacy for retaining the clause. The separate shooting proposal is excluded. The complete transcription was read, but manuscript pagination and handwriting were not separately inspected. |
| *Official Records*, I-5 | 193 | Cameron’s 11 September instruction to detain specified Maryland legislators. Its assertions are not independent proof of their intent. |
| *Official Records*, III-2 | 321–322 | Stanton’s order dated 8 August 1862. The surrounding explanatory account is not an independently dated presidential order. |
| *Statutes at Large*, XII | 1264–1265, 1267–1268 | Hunter revocation and preliminary emancipation proclamation. These are separate dates and instruments. No final January 1863 proclamation is supplied. |
| Welles, *Diary*, I (1911) | 142–144, entry dated 22 September 1862 | Blair’s advice to delay, Lincoln’s stated resolution and Seward’s suggested earlier suspension. This selection does not establish the precise July victory condition. It is an edited diary published later; the dated entry is not a verbatim cabinet transcript. |
| Carpenter, *Six Months at the White House* (1866) | 20–23 | Later reported conversation supplies the “victory” account, while Lincoln cannot recall the July day and the September chronology differs. Kept as a lead and qualification, not a qualifying contemporary document for `wait-for-victory`. |
| Haupt, *Reminiscences* (1901) | 83, 86–87 (external PDF extracts 89, 92–93) | Memoir and reproduced wires prompted a search for the actual railroad instructions. The draft below uses the period instruction, not the memoir’s arrest threat or claim that it changed the campaign. |
| *Official Records*, I-12, part III | 638 | Halleck’s 23 August instruction against military interference with railroad management, with the attack exception and consultation requirement. The neighboring medical instruction is a different document. This clears only the administrative option. |
| Chase to Lincoln, 16 May 1862, SMC 501 | pp. 1–2, recto and verso, both manuscript images inspected | A contemporary secretarial copy explicitly urges non-revocation of Hunter’s order and recommends letting it stand on the commander’s responsibility. Chase says he cannot judge the military necessity from there. His predicted support is attributed, not a measured bloc change. The catalogue’s modern essay and the envelope image are not evidence for the draft. |
| House Executive Documents 133 and 143, 37th Congress, second session | 133 pp. 1–2; 143 pp. 1–3 | Search of the War Department correspondence and Hunter’s June enlistment report. The later enlistment explanation alone does not establish a request to retain his May freedom declaration. Chase’s separate letter supplies that recommendation. |
| *Official Records*, I-4 | 178–185 read; 179–181 cited | Walker’s withdrawal instruction, Polk’s argument for remaining at Columbus and Davis’s endorsement. Polk’s claims of necessity and support remain attributed. The edition does not settle the intraday receipt order of the separate papers. |
| Richardson, *Messages and Papers of the Confederacy*, I (1906) | 63–65, 75–77; 135–140, 142–146; 188–196, 198–199, 201–206, 212–213; 219–225; 231–233, 235–238, 249–250, 266–267 | Selected dated messages, proclamations and vetoes. Draft citations identify the exact pages used. Reports marked “received” retain that date qualification. Editorial biographies, historical narrative and index assertions are discovery aids only. This is not Davis’s later *Rise and Fall*, which was not adopted as evidence. |
| *Official Records*, IV-1 | 1061–1065, 1067, 1072, 1081–1085, 1094–1098, 1125–1126 searched/read; 1067, 1072, 1081, 1095–1097 cited | Conscription approval-day telegram, Georgia organization exchange and April statutes. The 16 April law’s reproduction in General Orders 30 of 28 April cannot become an order received on 16 April. The separate exemption act is approved 21 April. |
| Confederate public acts, first Congress, second session | 61–62; 77–79 | Acts approved 27 September and 11 October 1862. Read printed headers in the OCR and the complete provisions, not just search hits. The October approval date controls over the OCR-corrupted marginal year. Statutory eligibility is distinct from an individual exemption or completed call. |
| *Official Records*, I-19, part II | 590–593 read; 590–592 cited | Lee’s 3 and 4 September letters to Davis: proposed movement, readiness and ammunition concerns, requested Richmond works and conditional Pennsylvania extension. No result of the proposed offensive enters an earlier paper. |
| *Official Records*, I-15 | 734, 739, 754 read; 754’s final document cited | Davis’s 14 June inquiry to Smith at Vicksburg about the Arkansas, defenses and possible aid. The neighboring Moore letter’s proposed reprisal is excluded, not converted into an option. No Smith reply or completed ship is inferred. |
| *Official Records*, I-16, part II, corrected copy | 768–771 | Smith to Davis, 21 August, pp. 768–769, supplies the western support request. The August 22 Davis telegram and the delegation’s later papers are separate documents. Use corrected archive identifier `cu31924077730228` and its printed-page map; the older S6 files are excluded for this draft. |
| Richardson, *Messages and Papers of the Confederacy*, II (1906) | 211, 214–215, 218–232 selected; 227–232 cited | Benjamin’s confidential no. 3 to Slidell, 12 April 1862, contains conditional negotiating authority, the recognition alternative and request for a diplomatic dispatch route. Duty concessions and subsidies are permissions contingent on inquiry and negotiation, not an agreed French policy. Benjamin’s forecasts about cotton values, recognition and peace are not outcomes. |

Initial direct attempts to read Frémont’s 8 September letter, Library of Congress `mal.1156100`, returned HTTP 403. An alternate retrieval then returned the complete LoC transcription, retained in `fremont-transcription.json`; that reading, rather than a search snippet, supplies the new option. The four-image item is cited as a whole because image-by-image pagination was not inspected. Further Seward searches located references to Stanton’s July 22 notes and later reproductions, but an inspected period document for the precise victory condition was not acquired in this bounded search. Those uninspected leads add no historical fact. Absence here means “not established in these selected readings,” not “no advocate ever existed.”
