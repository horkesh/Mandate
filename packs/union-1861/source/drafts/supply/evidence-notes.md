# S24 evidence and adoption limits

These are research drafts for the lead, August 1861 through November 1862. Source keys resolve in `sources.csv`; `source-pages.csv` gives printed and retained PDF pages and scan hashes. Statements below describe the cited author's record. Retrospective reports do not establish contemporary knowledge. `[I]` marks a proposed interpretation or adoption ruling.

## Railways: extent is not availability

Ashcroft supplies thirty-seven published company gauge baselines. Its own Alexandria, Loudoun and Hampshire entry distinguishes a 170-mile scheme from 37.5 miles in use and six more nearly ready for track. Norfolk and Petersburg's earnings entry still refers to 1859. Publication in 1862 therefore supplies neither an August 1861 opening balance nor a monthly operating census. Directory endpoint descriptions, including Newbern-Morehead's printed ninety-five miles and the printed state labels for Paris, Canton and Charlotte, are preserved as attributed entries pending corroboration. (AS62 pp.107-108, 115, 137, 146.)

McCallum's May 26, 1866 report separates successive military reaches: O&A to Manassas in late March, Warrenton Junction in April and Culpeper in August; a few trains to Rapidan do not establish continuous service there. The nested mileages have different termini and must not be added. Seven engines and 295 cars were lost in the late-August withdrawal. November service was to Bealton and Warrenton, not a restored continuous Culpeper supply line. (MC66 pp.4-5.)

Aquia-Fredericksburg opened in April, was abandoned September 7, had repairs recommenced November 18 and reopened to Falmouth November 28. The February wharf improvements on the same page belong to 1863 and are excluded. The eastern Norfolk-Petersburg section was changed from sixty to 56.5 inches after occupation July 22; connecting track at Suffolk followed in August. The July 1862-June 1863 aggregate freight is outside this window's usable period. (MC66 pp.5-6.)

B&O distinguishes its Washington Branch from its repeatedly interrupted main stem. The branch's cars delivered at Washington frequently exceeded 400 per day during the pressure of October 1861-March 1862, but the statement combines army and civilian supplies. It is not every day's movement, tons per car or a capacity ceiling. Main-stem bridge interruptions and June boat transfers remain dated observations. Sandy Hook canal-coal transfer supplied the company's locomotives, not an army's food inventory. The volume's fiscal/title year is 1862, while its transportation header actually prints October 1, 1864. That unresolved chronology is preserved; the printed header is not silently emended or certified as the true composition date. (BO62 pp.5, 25-26, 37-38, 55-57.)

Richmond and Danville's report gives military freight earnings, worn track and inadequate Junction transfer accommodation. Earnings are dollars, not cargo weight; four miles of worn flat bar and twenty-two miles needing heavy-rail renewal do not establish a percentage throughput reduction. (RD62 pp.231, 237-239.) Myers's October 3 letter asks for borrowed cars back and objects to cars used as storehouses; his causal estimate remains an officer's claim. (OR42 pp.108-109.)

[I] Keep gauge incompatibility, missing connecting track, a broken same-gauge bridge and inadequate terminal facilities as different transfer problems. The draft contains no universal break-of-gauge penalty. Directory gauge cannot override the later conversion of a particular military section.

## Potomac and James: departmental depots and afloat reserves

Van Vliet dates the Potomac supply closure October 19, 1861 to latter February 1862, forcing the army and Washington onto the single-track Baltimore route. His Alexandria embarkation began the evening of March 16 or morning of March 17; Clarke's first troop sailing on March 18 is a different event. The reported 405 vessels and 86,278 tons describe the campaign's employed fleet and vessel tonnage, not simultaneous cargo deadweight. (OR11A pp.157-158, 167.)

Perryville was a transportation organization point, with a 1,500-wagon order. Fort Monroe, Cheeseman's Creek and Ship Point had different duties; Ship Point was a subsistence depot while Cheeseman's handled forage and quartermaster stores. Yorktown, Eltham, Cumberland Landing and White House followed the advance. Clarke's May 14-19 subsistence stages and Van Vliet's May 20 quartermaster establishment at White House are retained separately. (OR11A pp.157-159, 162, 167-168.)

White House's over-500-ton daily forage/subsistence requirement and over-600-ton daily total handled and forwarded are army-level statements, not tested rail or wharf maxima. Twenty-five cars landed on May 23; the two engines expected that evening are a forecast. Clarke dates rail operation May 25, followed by forward issuing depots at Dispatch, Savage and Orchard. Rain damage and difficult wagon movement remain dated constraints. (OR11A pp.159, 162-163, 169.)

June 18's 800,000-ration dispatch near City Point was an afloat reserve under gunboats. June 23 orders to designate vessels with oats, corn, hay and 500,000-600,000 rations for the James do not prove a June 23 receipt there, or an independent second ration stock. The later White House reserve instruction was for three-four days afloat. Ingalls's flotilla withdrawal and arrival at Haxall's on June 30 are separate movements. (OR11A pp.160, 165, 169.)

Clarke reports White House shore stores burned on June 28 and forward stores destroyed on June 29. Ingalls's claim that no valuable public property was left cannot erase that departmental loss account. On the James, Long Wharf accommodated only medium-draft vessels and was inadequate for the army; Westover offered better discharge facilities. Fifty-sixty days' rations were held ashore and afloat for some time, not necessarily at one instant ashore. Westover subsistence closed August 15 evening; Ingalls dates the Harrison quartermaster closure August 16 morning. (OR11A pp.165, 170-172.)

Confederate James evidence includes the March 29 troop crossing/steamer availability, April 9's report of ammunition forwarded two days earlier, and July 1's conflict over Army/Navy boat allocation. `[I]` April 7 is a relative-date inference, not a certified receipt at an invented landing. Nothing here opens a Union supply line to Richmond city. (OR11C pp.412, 434, 627.)

The autumn depot sequence includes Aquia, Fairfax Station, Rockville, canal supply at Seneca, the broken-bridge railhead at Monocacy, Hagerstown, Sandy Hook and Harpers Ferry. November's advancing terminals and the consolidated 600,000-ration reserve remain distinct records. Clarke's report heading covers service to November 9 while the account also names November 10-11 events; those printed dates are retained with that scope inconsistency, not repaired. (OR11A pp.172-174.)

## Tennessee and Cumberland: reach, boat and cargo scope

At Pittsburg Landing, high water contracted landing space even while a large fleet discharged. Sherman requested corn, hay, coal and landing organization; requests do not establish receipt. (OR10A pp.25, 27.) Darr, the chief commissary, describes June stores via Eastport and thirty-forty tons per trip in sixteen-inch-draft boats at Colbert's Shoals, damaged vessels, a later wagon detour and a weak bridge. He relays the engineers' account concerning two engines; this is not an independent bridge load test. Vessel draft is not measured channel depth. (OR16A pp.602-604.)

Symonds's Nashville supply testimony distinguishes rail and river and even gives different accounting intervals on the same page. July's roughly 300,000 complete-ration equivalent cannot be added to its meat, bread and coffee/sugar component totals. Bingham describes light-draft Clarksville relays over Harpeth Shoals, loads declining from about eighty tons to eight-ten for some vessels, and continued exceptional navigation after the river was practically closed. His final use around August 18 is qualified testimony. Neither witness warrants a universal calendar closure. (OR16A pp.339, 629.)

Bingham's five-million-pound grain requisition and half-million received are an undated example, not a paired daily stock return. Local corn obtained from Williamson County and near Nashville is procurement, not standing crop output or all stock held together. Payment, coercion and civilian consequences require separate review before any mechanical use. (OR16A pp.628-630.)

For the Confederate upper Cumberland, Hillyer identifies the Charter, Commerce and Umpire and their cargoes, temporary floating storage during a rapid rise, the Carthage dispatch and the thirty-day reserve for two regiments with explicit exceptions. The combined 130 barrels of molasses, pork and vinegar must not become 130 of each. His defensive account is attributed, not an independently reconciled balance. (OR7 pp.874-878.)

## Mississippi and depot stores on both sides

Meigs reports transport down to Memphis and Helena, while the failed Vicksburg operation required retention of the ram fleet. `[I]` Neither statement opens through Union supply past Vicksburg. Kasson's June 12 expectation of a shift from Tennessee River transport to Mississippi/Memphis-and-Charleston supply is a plan. The June 25 La Fayette depot/bridge guarding order reports a break that day; it is not proof of restored through rail. (OR32 pp.794, 797; OR17B pp.7, 33.)

Sherman's September Memphis arrangements distinguish the quartermaster cotton shed from brewery ordnance storage and magazines still being built. The November 12 order directs the First Division to Morton's Fort Pickering subsistence warehouse and the Second to Hinsdill's wharf-boat. His November 17 assessment says all but corn must come from above; his praise of the base remains opinion, and his description of local acquisition is not a free-supply rule. (OR17B pp.201, 344-345, 351.)

Villepigue's April 6 Fort Pillow report gives a military road of not more than twenty-nine miles to Mason's Depot on the Memphis and Ohio, with a Hatchie flat-boat crossing slightly out of condition. He reports about sixty days' rations for 5,000 men on hand and an intention to double them. Proposed transfer of about 12,000 pounds of powder after reservations and at least 15,000 pounds then required are different stages. The printed small-arms reserve is 149,000 cartridges; listed components sum to 141,500. The 7,500 difference remains unresolved. Neither future collection of nearby food nor unpaid contractor obligations is completed receipt. (OR10B pp.394-396.)

Confederate Nashville's precise surrender stock was explicitly unavailable to Northrop pending Shaaff's returns. Forrest's meat removed in 700-800 wagon loads remains an attributed range without a pounds-per-wagon conversion. The orders for Atlanta manufacture, Chattanooga distribution and March western depots/target reserves are not completed inventories. Columbus in those March instructions is Mississippi, not Kentucky. (OR41B p.1035; OR7 pp.427, 430, 915, 918-919.)

Noland's Thoroughfare withdrawal account distinguishes gross pork receipt, processing loss, destinations, net beef and meat left or burned. The pork components sum one pound below the printed subtotal; printed subtotal plus balance is one pound above gross receipt. Preserve all printed numbers. Beef consumed is 5,092 and the printed subtotal 1,057,352, confirmed in the scan against erroneous OCR. Forty-five dispatched cars and seven furnished but unmoved cars are different categories. The promise of at least 200,000 pounds daily was not fulfilled and cannot become rail capacity. (OR41B pp.1038-1040.)

## Quartermaster returns and units

Meigs's clothing account names nine principal purchasing/manufacturing depots and a June 30 national suit/cloth aggregate subsequently drawn down. The aggregate is not allocated to each depot. The appended Return No.1 explicitly records purchases at principal depots, with separate annual and July-September columns. `quartermaster-returns.csv` transcribes selected July 1-September 30 wagon and ambulance purchases, including the aggregate rows, checked against the page image. Purchases do not establish stock held, serviceable teams or deliveries to armies; aggregate and local rows overlap. (OR32 pp.803-804, 810-811.)

[I] Preserve the source's tons, bushels, barrels, pounds, cartridges, vehicles, rations and days of supply. No ration composition, barrel weight, ton standard, wagon load, troop denominator or direction-specific graph rate is invented. Empty model fields mean unestablished. They are unsafe for direct import because loader defaults could turn missing evidence into a working edge or depot.

## Map needs and work left for adoption

`map-joins.csv` compares every candidate edge, named railroad and depot with the pinned source map. It records 150 joins. `map-needs.csv` lists thirty held needs: aggregate corridors requiring their existing intermediate segments, absent company identities, collapsed Aquia and Vienna segments, unresolved landings, canal representation, blocked Vicksburg continuity and missing reach/terminal detail. Seven depot region fields remain blank. A matching present-day pack identity proves correspondence only, not historical date, operation or control.

[I] The adoption order is: resolve places and company identities; preserve dated possession and operations in an agreed temporal schema; choose units and direction; obtain missing rated capacity or deliberately author and label a design parameter; review sensitive uses; then consider promotion. No live map, World, stock flow, signed item or calibration band is changed here. `unresolved-inputs.csv` makes fifteen evidence gaps explicit.
