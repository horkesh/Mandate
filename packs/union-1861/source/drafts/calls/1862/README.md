# Calls and service terms in 1862

Data drafts for S15, 20 November 2026. Nothing here is loaded, imported or generated. The CSVs describe sources; they do not add levy orders, forces, people, exemptions or clocks to the world. Historical citations resolve through the [S15 source register](../../../../../../docs/research/2026-11-20-s15-sources.md). Proposed interpretations carry **[I]**.

`union-state-quotas.csv` keeps the existing nine columns of the 1861 call draft. `aggregate` means the published state quota in men; blank regiments and rank-and-file cells are unknown, not zero. Months remain calendar terms, not invented 90/270-day conversions. Virginia (Western) and the District of Columbia retain the source jurisdictions. No state force is inferred for a self-governing Native nation.

`call-catalogue.csv` distinguishes a call from legislative authority, exemption law, regulations and an executive age limit. Its date is the catalogue event; `document_date` preserves the July 1 letter behind the conventionally July 2 call. A blank quantity means the text did not prescribe a national numerical intake. The April and September Confederate laws authorize conscription; neither supplies an observed number enrolled.

`service-terms.csv` records retention and restrictions on accepting short service. Existing men retained beyond twelve months are not new recruits. A legal service term is different from an individual muster date, actual attendance, expiry, discharge or reenlistment. The ninety-day Confederate transition was disputed by officials; no uniform discharge date is fabricated.

`exemption-classes.csv` is a legal research classification, with conditions and citations. It is **not** an executable exemption decision table. A particular person's entitlement, a stock of eligible residents, or a numerical intake requires further evidence. All rows await review. The slavery-policing exemption appears as a restriction on which white men were compelled to serve, with its coercive purpose stated in the research note; it does not make enslaved people a resource or produce a player lever. No gate signature, situation wording or placement is supplied.

`union-retrospective-credits.csv` is quarantined comparison evidence. The Official Records editors give men furnished under each call without a dated observation window in those footnotes. The values must **not** become arrivals on July 2 or August 4. Explicit zeroes reproduce “none furnished”; Nebraska has a furnished entry but no stated quota. No Nebraska zero quota is invented.

## Source discrepancies and revisions

The twenty-three printed state/district quota rows sum to **334,835 [I]** for each nominal 300,000 call. That is the sum of the table, not a repaired national call. The July credits plus Nebraska sum to **421,465 [I]**, and August's to **87,588 [I]**, matching the editors' totals. Quota and credit are different measures. [OR32 pp.188, 291, footnotes]

August 4's General Orders 94 and August 9's General Orders 99 specify August 15 for the volunteer deficiency draft; the August 5 circular prints August 18. Later instructions allow postponement and excess-volunteer credits. The catalogue preserves the texts rather than choosing one universal draft day. [OR32 pp.291-292, 295-296, 333-335, 462-464]

The initial quotas are not a final immutable ledger. On August 26, Delaware is told a drafted quota of 2,000 and a volunteer baseline of 1,440, unlike the earlier 1,720 rows. Illinois's August 8 credit calculation is expressly corrected the next day. These revisions are in the [research note](../../../../../../docs/research/2026-11-20-s15-manpower.md); adopting the initial table requires preserving dated adjustments. [OR32 pp.337-338, 462]

**[I] Proposed ruling:** use the catalogue as research input to a future levy design, keeping a separate dated quota/credit ledger and contract-retention path. Reject immediate full levies on enactment, merging the two Union calls, converting men to regiments without an establishment source, and adding retained Confederate men twice. The lead must choose an implementation contract; none is added here.
