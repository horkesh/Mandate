# Foreign and blockade observations for S15

Research drafts, 20 November 2026. **Nothing here is loaded or generated.** These inputs support later design of the existing Britain/France pressure clocks through the Secretary of State, under 0023. No new playable faction, situation, signature or clock equation is supplied. `pressure-quantities.csv` uses the same one-observation principle as the economy drafts. A report date, accounting window and contemporary receipt date are different. Customs exports do not enumerate clandestine Confederate trade. British cotton arrivals use a separate perimeter. Public poor relief is not unemployment; private/public relief cannot be added without eliminating overlap. December 1862 relief and the Navy's 1 December report are explicitly after the November endpoint. Treasury trade appendices dated **26 December** are later than the Treasury narrative's 4 December report date.

`diplomatic-sequence.csv` separates incidents, sending, receipt, informal presentation, formal presentation, private proposals, decisions and implementation. Only the specific Washington receipt/presentation dates actually supported by Adams are populated; other receipt dates remain unknown. Private European correspondence is not automatic presidential knowledge. A public speech lacks the authority of Cabinet approval. The October 31 record says a request was **about to be sent**, preserving the source's wording about that stage rather than inventing a verified dispatch date. The French dating conflict in S5 is not resolved by recopying it.

## Monthly named-vessel series

`named-vessel-register-1862.csv` transcribes the **printed 1862 groups** in the named-vessel section of the Navy's 1863 retrospective table, pp.562-582. `named-vessel-monthly-1862.csv` counts those entries [I], with a separate bin for records lacking a month. Every record has its page, date precision and a stable selected-row ordinal. Explicit ditto dates are expanded and marked [I]; a blank date is not filled from the preceding vessel. Names receive punctuation/spacing normalization only. Publication is 1863; no contemporary receipt date is established.

The source title includes vessels captured **or destroyed**, for blockade violation **or in battle**. The file therefore measures **named-vessel register records**, not national blockade-only captures. It includes released prizes and combat captures and can repeat a hull/name. Cargo-only rows within the named section (cotton, coal and molasses) are excluded. Unnamed/property tables on pp.583-586 are excluded: some rows describe several boats or non-vessel property, so their row count is not a vessel count. Source-year grouping is preserved; notably p.581's T entries stand under 1863 and are not silently reassigned to 1862.

**[I]** The monthly series is a substantial retrospective observation set with a declared subset, not an interception rate, a complete capture census, a count of condemned vessels, or a sum to force equal to the previous year's 543 cumulative seizures. Actual monthly blockade-only totals remain **HOLD** pending event classification, missing-date resolution and inclusion of unnamed craft. Attempt and cargo-volume denominators remain absent.
